# Lease Types Reference

Comprehensive guide to residential and commercial lease structures, terms, and legal considerations.

## Residential Leases

### Fixed-Term Lease (Standard)
- **Duration**: Typically 12 months (6-24 months common)
- **Rent**: Fixed amount, specified due date (usually 1st of month)
- **Renewal**: Automatic renewal or new lease negotiation
- **Termination**: Requires notice (30-60 days typical)
- **Early Termination**: Usually requires penalty or lease buy-out
- **Security Deposit**: 1-2 months rent, held in escrow
- **Best For**: Stable tenants, predictable cash flow, landlord protection

**Standard Clauses:**
- Monthly rent amount and due date
- Late fee structure (typically $50-100 or 5% of rent)
- Grace period (3-5 days typical)
- Security deposit terms and return conditions
- Maintenance and repair responsibilities
- Utilities responsibility
- Occupancy limits
- Subletting and assignment restrictions
- Pet policy (deposits, fees, restrictions)
- Landlord access rights
- Renewal or notice requirements

### Month-to-Month Lease
- **Duration**: No fixed end date, renews monthly
- **Flexibility**: Either party can terminate with notice (30-60 days)
- **Rent**: Often 5-15% higher than fixed-term
- **Increases**: Can increase rent with proper notice
- **Best For**: Transitional housing, uncertain tenure, flexibility for both parties

**Advantages:**
- Maximum flexibility for tenants and landlords
- Easy to adjust to market conditions
- Lower commitment for both parties

**Disadvantages:**
- Less stability and predictability
- Higher rent premiums
- More frequent turnover
- Harder to qualify for mortgage (lenders prefer fixed-term)

### Lease with Option to Purchase (Rent-to-Own)
- **Structure**: Standard lease + purchase option agreement
- **Option Fee**: Non-refundable 1-5% of purchase price
- **Rent Credit**: Portion of rent (10-30%) credited toward purchase
- **Purchase Price**: Fixed at contract signing or formula-based
- **Option Period**: 1-3 years typical
- **Maintenance**: Tenant often responsible (as future owner)
- **Best For**: Credit repair situations, delayed closing, seller financing

**Financial Example:**
```
Purchase Price: $300,000
Option Fee: $9,000 (3%, non-refundable)
Monthly Rent: $2,000
Rent Credit: $400/month (20% of rent)
Option Period: 2 years

Total Rent Paid: $48,000
Total Credits: $9,600
Net Cost to Purchase: $290,400 (if exercised)
```

**Tenant Benefits:**
- Lock in purchase price in rising market
- Build equity while renting
- Time to improve credit score
- Test out neighborhood/property

**Landlord Benefits:**
- Higher monthly rent
- Motivated tenant (better care of property)
- Option fee income if not exercised
- Potential sale at predetermined price

### Sublease
- **Definition**: Tenant leases to third party (subtenant)
- **Landlord Permission**: Usually required per master lease
- **Liability**: Original tenant remains liable to landlord
- **Duration**: Cannot exceed master lease term
- **Rent**: Can charge more or less than master lease rent
- **Best For**: Job relocation, temporary absence, college students

### Co-Tenancy / Roommate Agreements
- **Structure**: Multiple tenants on one lease
- **Liability**: Joint and several liability (all responsible for full rent)
- **Individual Leases**: Some landlords offer separate leases per room
- **Subletting**: Usually requires all tenants' consent
- **Best For**: Students, young professionals, cost sharing

## Commercial Leases

### Gross Lease (Full Service Lease)
- **Tenant Pays**: Rent only
- **Landlord Pays**: Property taxes, insurance, maintenance, utilities, janitorial
- **Rent**: Higher than net leases (landlord covers all expenses)
- **Adjustments**: May include expense stops or escalation clauses
- **Best For**: Small office tenants, medical offices, professional services
- **Typical Use**: Class A office buildings, medical buildings

**Expense Stop Example:**
```
Base Rent: $30/sqft NNN
Operating Expenses Year 1: $10/sqft (landlord pays)
Expense Stop: $10/sqft

Year 2 Operating Expenses: $11/sqft
Tenant pays: $1/sqft over the expense stop
Effective Rate Year 2: $31/sqft
```

### Single Net Lease (N)
- **Tenant Pays**: Base rent + property taxes
- **Landlord Pays**: Insurance, maintenance, repairs
- **Rare**: Uncommon in modern commercial real estate
- **Best For**: Freestanding retail, small commercial properties

### Double Net Lease (NN)
- **Tenant Pays**: Base rent + property taxes + insurance
- **Landlord Pays**: Structural maintenance and repairs
- **Common**: Industrial properties, some retail
- **Risk**: Tenant assumes tax and insurance risk

### Triple Net Lease (NNN)
- **Tenant Pays**: Base rent + property taxes + insurance + maintenance (CAM)
- **Landlord Pays**: Structural repairs only (roof, foundation, exterior walls)
- **Rent**: Lower base rent (tenant pays most expenses)
- **Common**: Retail, single-tenant buildings, national chains, sale-leaseback
- **Best For**: Credit tenants, long-term investments, passive investors

**NNN Lease Breakdown Example:**
```
Property: 10,000 sqft retail
Base Rent: $15/sqft/year = $150,000/year
Property Taxes: $3/sqft/year = $30,000/year
Insurance: $1/sqft/year = $10,000/year
CAM (maintenance): $4/sqft/year = $40,000/year

Total Tenant Cost: $23/sqft/year = $230,000/year
Monthly Payment: $19,167
```

**CAM (Common Area Maintenance) Includes:**
- Parking lot maintenance and striping
- Landscaping and snow removal
- Exterior lighting
- Common area utilities
- Property management fees
- Pest control
- Security
- Trash removal

### Absolute Net Lease (Absolute NNN)
- **Tenant Pays**: Everything including structural repairs and replacements
- **Landlord Pays**: Nothing (true passive investment)
- **Risk**: Tenant assumes all property risks including roof, HVAC, parking lot
- **Common**: Corporate sale-leaseback (Walgreens, CVS, Dollar General)
- **Credit**: Requires strong tenant credit (investment grade)

### Modified Gross Lease
- **Definition**: Negotiated split of expenses between landlord and tenant
- **Common Split**: Tenant pays utilities and janitorial, landlord pays taxes/insurance/maintenance
- **Flexibility**: Each lease is customized based on negotiation
- **Best For**: Multi-tenant office buildings, flexible office space

**Example Modified Gross:**
```
Landlord Pays:
- Property taxes
- Building insurance
- Structural maintenance
- HVAC maintenance
- Landscaping

Tenant Pays:
- Utilities (electric, gas, water)
- Interior janitorial
- Interior improvements
- Business insurance
```

### Percentage Lease
- **Structure**: Base rent + percentage of gross sales
- **Base Rent**: Lower than market (or zero in some cases)
- **Percentage**: 5-15% of gross sales (varies by industry)
- **Breakpoint**: Sales threshold where percentage rent begins
- **Reporting**: Tenant must provide verified sales reports
- **Common**: Shopping malls, retail centers, restaurants
- **Best For**: Retail landlords, startup retailers, sales-dependent businesses

**Percentage Lease Calculation:**
```
Base Rent: $5,000/month = $60,000/year
Percentage: 7% of gross sales
Natural Breakpoint: $60,000 / 0.07 = $857,143

Scenario 1: Annual Sales = $750,000
Rent = $60,000 (base only, sales below breakpoint)

Scenario 2: Annual Sales = $1,200,000
Percentage Rent = $1,200,000 × 0.07 = $84,000
Rent = $84,000 (greater than base rent)

Scenario 3: Annual Sales = $1,500,000
Percentage Rent = $1,500,000 × 0.07 = $105,000
Rent = $105,000
```

**Typical Percentage Rates by Industry:**
- Restaurants: 6-8%
- Apparel: 6-7%
- Jewelry: 8-10%
- Convenience stores: 4-5%
- Department stores: 2-3%
- Supermarkets: 1.5-3%

### Ground Lease
- **Definition**: Land only, tenant owns/builds improvements
- **Term**: Very long (50-99 years)
- **Use**: Major developments, hotels, office towers
- **Reversion**: Building becomes landlord's property at lease end
- **Financing**: Complex (lender issues with land ownership)
- **Best For**: Long-term development projects, institutional investors

## Essential Lease Clauses

### Rent and Payment Terms
- Base rent amount and calculation method
- Rent payment due date and grace period
- Acceptable payment methods
- Late fee structure and application
- NSF (bounced check) fees
- Rent escalation provisions
- Rent abatement periods

### Security Deposit (Residential)
- Amount (typically 1-2 months rent)
- Holding account (separate escrow required in most states)
- Interest requirements (some states mandate)
- Use restrictions (damages, unpaid rent, cleaning)
- Itemized deduction requirements
- Return timeline (14-30 days typical)
- Walk-through inspection procedures

### Lease Term and Renewal
- Start and end dates
- Automatic renewal provisions
- Renewal notice requirements (30-180 days)
- Renewal rent adjustment (CPI, market rate, fixed %)
- Option periods and terms
- Holdover provisions and penalties

### Maintenance and Repairs
- Landlord responsibilities (structural, major systems)
- Tenant responsibilities (interior, minor repairs)
- Response time requirements
- Emergency repair procedures
- HVAC maintenance obligations
- Preventive maintenance schedules
- Landscaping and snow removal

### Use and Occupancy
- Permitted uses (residential dwelling only, specific business)
- Occupancy limits (based on square footage or bedrooms)
- Home business restrictions
- Short-term rental prohibitions (Airbnb clauses)
- Zoning compliance
- HOA rule compliance

### Pets and Animals
- Pet policy (allowed/prohibited)
- Pet types and size restrictions
- Number of pets allowed
- Pet deposit (refundable) or fee (non-refundable)
- Monthly pet rent ($25-75/pet typical)
- Service animal exceptions (ADA/FHA compliance)
- Breed restrictions and liability

### Subletting and Assignment
- Landlord consent requirements
- Sublease approval process
- Assignment fees
- Continued tenant liability
- Sublease rent restrictions
- Documentation requirements

### Early Termination
- Notice requirements (30-90 days)
- Early termination fee (1-2 months rent typical)
- Job relocation provisions
- Military clause (SCRA compliance)
- Domestic violence provisions
- Uninhabitability exceptions
- Lease break process and timeline

### Insurance Requirements
- Renter's insurance requirement (residential)
- Commercial general liability (CGL) minimums
- Certificate of insurance delivery
- Named additional insured requirement
- Waiver of subrogation clause
- Loss payee designation
- Insurance renewal notification

### Default and Remedies
- Definition of default events
- Cure period (3-10 days typical)
- Late rent payment procedures
- Notice requirements
- Eviction process and timeline
- Attorney's fees and costs
- Abandoned property procedures

### Utilities and Services
- Utility responsibility (landlord vs. tenant)
- Separately metered vs. shared utilities
- Utility allowances (subsidized housing)
- Service level agreements
- Utility payment delinquency
- Connection and disconnection procedures

## Lease Negotiation Strategies

### Tenant Leverage Points
- Lease term length (longer = better rates)
- Market conditions (tenant's market vs. landlord's market)
- Tenant creditworthiness and financials
- Property vacancy rate
- Space commitment (more sqft = better rate)
- Tenant improvements (TI) allowance
- Free rent periods (1-6 months)
- Moving allowances
- Parking ratios and costs
- Signage rights
- Exclusive use clauses

### Landlord Protections
- Personal guarantees (for corporate tenants)
- Letters of credit
- Security deposits and prepaid rent
- Financial reporting requirements
- Estoppel certificates
- Subordination, non-disturbance, and attornment (SNDA) agreements
- Radius restrictions (prevent competing locations)
- Continuous operation clauses
- Co-tenancy clauses (requires anchor tenants)
