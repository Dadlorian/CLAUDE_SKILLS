# HIPAA Penalties and Enforcement Reference

## Civil Monetary Penalties

### Tiered Penalty Structure (45 CFR §160.404)

HITECH Act established tiered civil penalties based on level of culpability.

#### Tier 1: Did Not Know (Unknowing Violation)

**Culpability:** Individual did not know and, by exercising reasonable diligence, would not have known of the violation.

**Penalty Range (Per Violation):**
- Minimum: $100
- Maximum: $50,000

**Annual Maximum (Per Provision):** $1,876,814 (2024 adjusted amount)

**Examples:**
- Vendor software glitch causes inadvertent disclosure despite reasonable safeguards
- Honest mistake with no reasonable way to have prevented
- Unforeseen technical failure after proper due diligence

**Mitigation Factors:**
- Demonstrated reasonable diligence
- Prompt corrective action
- No prior violations
- Minimal harm to individuals

#### Tier 2: Reasonable Cause

**Culpability:** Violation due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect.

**Reasonable Cause:** Circumstances that would make it unreasonable for entity to comply with violated provision, despite ordinary business care and prudence.

**Penalty Range (Per Violation):**
- Minimum: $1,000
- Maximum: $50,000

**Annual Maximum (Per Provision):** $1,876,814

**Examples:**
- Unexpected environmental disaster affects compliance
- Sudden workforce shortage prevents timely action
- Vendor failure despite reasonable oversight
- Budget constraints limiting security measures (may or may not qualify)

**Mitigation Factors:**
- Circumstances beyond entity's control
- Good faith efforts to comply
- Prompt remediation when possible
- Cooperation with investigation

#### Tier 3: Willful Neglect - Corrected

**Culpability:** Violation due to willful neglect that is corrected within 30 days of discovery (or within period deemed appropriate by Secretary).

**Willful Neglect:** Conscious, intentional failure or reckless indifference to legal obligation.

**Penalty Range (Per Violation):**
- Minimum: $10,000
- Maximum: $50,000

**Annual Maximum (Per Provision):** $1,876,814

**Examples:**
- Known vulnerability not addressed, then corrected after 30 days
- Deliberate delay in implementing required safeguards, later corrected
- Ignoring repeated warnings, then fixing issue when discovered by OCR

**Mitigation Factors:**
- Corrected within 30 days
- Comprehensive remediation
- Cooperation with OCR
- Steps to prevent recurrence

**Note:** Even if corrected, penalty still mandatory.

#### Tier 4: Willful Neglect - Not Corrected

**Culpability:** Violation due to willful neglect that is NOT corrected within 30 days of discovery.

**Penalty Range (Per Violation):**
- Minimum: $50,000
- Maximum: $1,876,814 (2024 adjusted amount)

**Annual Maximum (Per Provision):** $1,876,814

**Mandatory:** Secretary MUST impose penalty. No prosecutorial discretion.

**Examples:**
- Failure to conduct risk analysis despite repeated warnings
- Known security vulnerabilities deliberately not addressed
- Ongoing non-compliance after OCR notification
- Refusal to implement required corrective action plan

**Aggravating Factors:**
- Extended period of non-compliance
- Multiple individuals affected
- Sensitive information compromised
- Repeated violations
- Obstruction of investigation

## Criminal Penalties

### Federal Criminal Violations (42 U.S.C. §1320d-6)

Criminal prosecution handled by Department of Justice (DOJ).

#### Tier 1: Wrongful Disclosure/Obtaining PHI

**Offense:** Person who knowingly obtains or discloses PHI in violation of HIPAA.

**Penalties:**
- Fine: Up to $50,000
- Prison: Up to 1 year
- Or both

**Examples:**
- Employee accessing patient records without authorization
- Knowingly disclosing PHI without authorization
- Obtaining PHI through false pretenses

#### Tier 2: False Pretenses

**Offense:** Obtaining or disclosing PHI under false pretenses.

**Penalties:**
- Fine: Up to $100,000
- Prison: Up to 5 years
- Or both

**Examples:**
- Impersonating healthcare provider to obtain records
- Using false credentials to access system
- Lying about reason for PHI request

#### Tier 3: Intent to Sell, Transfer, or Use for Commercial Advantage, Personal Gain, or Malicious Harm

**Offense:** Obtaining or disclosing PHI with intent to sell, transfer, or use for commercial advantage, personal gain, or malicious harm.

**Penalties:**
- Fine: Up to $250,000
- Prison: Up to 10 years
- Or both

**Examples:**
- Selling patient lists to marketers
- Stealing PHI for identity theft
- Disclosing PHI to harm individual (revenge)
- Using PHI for competitive advantage

### Who Can Be Prosecuted?

**Individuals:**
- Employees
- Officers
- Directors
- Contractors
- Anyone with access to PHI

**Not Organizations:**
Criminal prosecution targets individuals, not entities (though civil penalties apply to entities).

## State Attorney General Enforcement

### Authority (HITECH §13410(e))

State AGs can bring civil actions on behalf of state residents.

**Prerequisites:**
- HIPAA violation affecting state residents
- State AG provides notice to HHS
- HHS has right to intervene
- No concurrent federal action on same matter

**Penalties:**
- Up to $25,000 per violation
- Actual damages to residents
- Costs of investigation
- Attorney's fees

**Recent Trend:**
- Increased state AG activity
- Multi-state investigations
- Focus on large breaches
- Coordinate with HHS OCR

## OCR Resolution Agreements and Corrective Action Plans

### Resolution Agreements

Negotiated settlements between OCR and entity.

**Typical Components:**
1. Monetary settlement amount
2. Corrective Action Plan (CAP)
3. Monitoring period (typically 2-3 years)
4. Reporting requirements
5. No admission of liability

**Settlement Amounts:**
- Range from $10,000 to $16+ million
- Based on violation severity, number of individuals affected, entity size, compliance history

### Corrective Action Plans (CAPs)

Required remedial measures entity must implement.

**Common CAP Requirements:**

1. **Risk Analysis**
   - Comprehensive security risk assessment
   - Identify and remediate vulnerabilities
   - Document findings and actions

2. **Policies and Procedures**
   - Develop or revise HIPAA policies
   - Implement specific safeguards
   - Document all policies

3. **Training**
   - HIPAA privacy and security training
   - Role-specific training
   - Annual refresher training
   - Document all training

4. **Business Associate Management**
   - Identify all business associates
   - Execute compliant BAAs
   - Monitor BA compliance
   - Maintain BA inventory

5. **Breach Notification Procedures**
   - Implement breach response plan
   - Document breach assessment process
   - Establish notification procedures

6. **Monitoring and Reporting**
   - Regular compliance reports to OCR
   - Internal monitoring program
   - Incident tracking and reporting
   - Annual compliance attestation

7. **Audit and Accountability**
   - Implement audit logging
   - Regular log reviews
   - Document reviews

**Monitoring Period:**
- Typically 2-3 years
- May be extended if non-compliant
- Regular reporting to OCR required

## Notable Enforcement Actions

### Largest Settlements

#### 1. Anthem Inc. (2018) - $16 Million
- **Violation:** Data breach affecting 78.8 million individuals
- **Issues:**
  - Lack of security risk analysis
  - Insufficient access controls
  - No encryption of ePHI
  - Inadequate firewall protections
- **CAP:** 3 years monitoring

#### 2. Premera Blue Cross (2019) - $6.85 Million
- **Violation:** Breach affecting 10.4 million individuals
- **Issues:**
  - Failed to conduct enterprise-wide risk analysis
  - Inadequate risk management
  - Insufficient network security
  - Lack of intrusion detection
- **CAP:** 3 years monitoring

#### 3. Health Net (2011) - $5.5 Million + (2017) - $250,000
- **Multiple Violations:**
  - Lost unencrypted hard drives
  - Lack of risk analysis
  - No encryption of portable devices
  - Repeated violations

#### 4. New York-Presbyterian Hospital/Columbia University (2014) - $4.8 Million
- **Violation:** Breach affecting 6,800 individuals
- **Issues:**
  - Deactivated server accessible on internet
  - Insufficient risk analysis
  - Lack of PHI access controls
  - Inadequate information system activity review

#### 5. Cottage Health (2018) - $3 Million
- **Violation:** Breach affecting 62,000+ individuals
- **Issues:**
  - Failed to conduct comprehensive risk analysis
  - Insufficient risk management
  - Lack of business associate oversight
  - ePHI accessible via internet

### Trends in Enforcement

**Common Violations:**
1. Lack of security risk analysis (most common)
2. Insufficient access controls
3. Inadequate business associate management
4. No encryption of mobile devices/portable media
5. Untimely breach notification
6. Failure to implement audit controls
7. Inadequate risk management
8. Lack of workforce training

**Increasing Focus:**
- Ransomware attacks
- Business associate compliance
- Cloud service security
- Mobile device security
- Third-party vendor risk
- Repeated/systemic violations

## Factors Affecting Penalty Amounts

### Aggravating Factors (Increase Penalties)

1. **Scope and Duration**
   - Long-standing violations
   - Widespread non-compliance
   - Multiple provisions violated
   - Large number of individuals affected

2. **Harm to Individuals**
   - Identity theft
   - Financial loss
   - Medical harm
   - Emotional distress
   - Sensitivity of information

3. **Entity Characteristics**
   - Large organization (more resources)
   - Healthcare system vs. small practice
   - Financial condition (ability to pay)
   - Previous violations

4. **Compliance History**
   - Prior OCR investigations
   - Pattern of non-compliance
   - Previous resolution agreements
   - Repeated violations

5. **Culpability**
   - Intentional violations
   - Reckless disregard
   - Obstruction of investigation
   - Failure to cooperate

6. **Entity Response**
   - Delayed corrective action
   - Incomplete remediation
   - Continued non-compliance
   - Lack of cooperation

### Mitigating Factors (Reduce Penalties)

1. **Prompt Response**
   - Immediate corrective action
   - Comprehensive remediation
   - Proactive compliance improvements
   - Swift breach notification

2. **Cooperation**
   - Full cooperation with investigation
   - Transparent communication
   - Providing requested documentation
   - Accepting responsibility

3. **Compliance Efforts**
   - Strong compliance program
   - Regular training
   - Documented policies and procedures
   - Risk assessments conducted

4. **First-Time Violation**
   - No prior enforcement actions
   - Good compliance history
   - Isolated incident

5. **Limited Harm**
   - Small number of individuals affected
   - Non-sensitive information
   - No actual harm occurred
   - Successful mitigation

6. **Resource Constraints**
   - Small organization with limited resources
   - Financial hardship
   - Rural or underserved area
   - Critical access provider

## Waiver of Penalties

### Circumstances for Waiver (§160.410)

Secretary may waive penalty if:

1. **Violation Corrected**
   - Corrected within 30 days (or period deemed appropriate)
   - Failure to comply was due to reasonable cause
   - Not due to willful neglect

2. **Affirmative Defense**
   - Entity did not know and could not have known (reasonable diligence)

**Note:** Willful neglect violations cannot be waived, even if corrected.

## Statute of Limitations

### Civil Violations
- **Discovery:** 6 years from date of violation or date of last act of ongoing violation
- **Complaint Filing:** Must file within 180 days of when complainant knew or should have known of violation

### Criminal Violations
- Generally 5 years from offense
- May be extended in certain circumstances

## Breach Notification Penalties

### Per Individual Affected
Each individual not properly notified = separate violation.

**Example:**
- Breach affects 10,000 individuals
- Entity fails to notify
- Potential penalties: 10,000 violations × penalty amount

### Untimely Notification
- Each day beyond 60-day deadline may be separate violation
- Multiplied by number of individuals

**Example:**
- 30 days late notifying 5,000 individuals
- Potential: 30 days × 5,000 individuals = 150,000 violations

## Private Right of Action

**No Federal Private Right of Action:**
- Individuals CANNOT sue under HIPAA for damages
- Must file complaint with OCR
- OCR investigates and enforces

**State Law Options:**
- Some states allow private lawsuits for privacy violations
- Common law claims (negligence, breach of confidentiality)
- State breach notification laws may provide remedies

## Reporting to HHS

### When Required
- Breaches affecting 500+ individuals: Immediately via web portal
- Breaches affecting fewer than 500: Annually (within 60 days of year-end)
- All security incidents affecting ePHI should be evaluated

### Consequences of Not Reporting
- Tier 4 penalties (willful neglect - not corrected)
- Minimum $50,000 per violation
- Each affected individual = separate violation

## Best Practices to Minimize Penalties

1. **Proactive Compliance**
   - Conduct annual risk assessments
   - Implement strong safeguards
   - Regular training programs
   - Comprehensive policies and procedures

2. **Early Detection**
   - Robust monitoring systems
   - Audit log reviews
   - Incident detection capabilities
   - Employee reporting channels

3. **Prompt Response**
   - Immediate containment
   - Thorough investigation
   - Swift corrective action
   - Timely notifications

4. **Full Cooperation**
   - Transparent with OCR
   - Provide requested documentation
   - Implement recommended corrections
   - Accept responsibility

5. **Comprehensive Remediation**
   - Address root causes
   - Implement preventive measures
   - Document all actions
   - Monitor effectiveness

6. **Documentation**
   - Maintain compliance documentation
   - Document decision-making
   - Keep incident records
   - Retain for 6+ years

7. **Insurance**
   - Cyber liability insurance
   - Coverage for HIPAA penalties
   - Breach response services
   - Legal defense coverage

## Recent Enforcement Trends (2020-2024)

1. **Increased Penalties**
   - Higher settlement amounts
   - More Tier 3 and 4 penalties
   - Focus on repeat offenders

2. **Business Associate Enforcement**
   - Direct BA investigations
   - Joint CE/BA penalties
   - Subcontractor scrutiny

3. **Ransomware Focus**
   - Encryption emphasized
   - Backup requirements
   - Incident response preparedness

4. **Right of Access**
   - Focus on timely access
   - Reasonable fees only
   - Electronic format requests

5. **Mobile Device Security**
   - Encryption required
   - Remote wipe capabilities
   - BYOD policies
