# HIPAA Privacy Rule Reference

## Overview

The HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Part 160 and Part 164, Subparts A and E) establishes national standards for the protection of Protected Health Information (PHI). It applies to covered entities and their business associates.

## Covered Entities

### Definition
Organizations that must comply with HIPAA Privacy Rule:

1. **Health Plans**
   - Health insurance companies
   - HMOs (Health Maintenance Organizations)
   - Company health plans
   - Government health programs (Medicare, Medicaid, military)

2. **Healthcare Providers**
   - Doctors, clinics, psychologists, dentists, chiropractors
   - Nursing homes, pharmacies
   - Any provider who transmits health information electronically

3. **Healthcare Clearinghouses**
   - Entities that process nonstandard health information
   - Billing services, repricing companies
   - Community health information systems

## Protected Health Information (PHI)

### Definition
Individually identifiable health information that is:
- Transmitted or maintained in any form (electronic, paper, oral)
- Created or received by a healthcare provider, health plan, employer, or healthcare clearinghouse
- Relates to past, present, or future physical or mental health, healthcare provision, or payment

### 18 HIPAA Identifiers
PHI includes any of the following identifiers:

1. Names (full name, last name and initial)
2. Geographic subdivisions smaller than state (street address, city, county, zip code)
3. Dates (except year) related to the individual
4. Telephone numbers
5. Fax numbers
6. Email addresses
7. Social Security numbers
8. Medical record numbers
9. Health plan beneficiary numbers
10. Account numbers
11. Certificate/license numbers
12. Vehicle identifiers and serial numbers (including license plates)
13. Device identifiers and serial numbers
14. Web URLs
15. IP addresses
16. Biometric identifiers (fingerprints, voiceprints)
17. Full-face photographs and comparable images
18. Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code

### Exceptions to PHI
- De-identified health information (using Safe Harbor or Expert Determination method)
- Employment records held by covered entity in role as employer
- Education records covered by FERPA
- Health information that has not been created or received by a covered entity or business associate

## Permitted Uses and Disclosures

### Without Patient Authorization

**Treatment, Payment, and Healthcare Operations (TPO)**

**Treatment:**
- Providing, coordinating, or managing healthcare
- Consultation between healthcare providers
- Referrals between providers
- Example: Doctor sends patient records to specialist

**Payment:**
- Activities to obtain payment for healthcare services
- Billing, claims management, collections
- Utilization review, pre-authorization
- Example: Hospital submits claim to insurance company

**Healthcare Operations:**
- Quality assessment and improvement
- Competency assurance, training programs
- Business planning and development
- Customer service, complaint resolution
- Example: Hospital reviews patient outcomes for quality improvement

**Other Permitted Disclosures (No Authorization Required):**

1. **To the Individual** - Patients have right to access their own PHI

2. **Treatment, Payment, Healthcare Operations of Other Covered Entities**
   - With certain limitations

3. **Public Interest and Benefit Activities:**
   - Required by law (court orders, subpoenas)
   - Public health activities (disease reporting)
   - Victims of abuse, neglect, or domestic violence
   - Health oversight activities (audits, investigations)
   - Judicial and administrative proceedings
   - Law enforcement purposes (limited circumstances)
   - Decedents (to coroners, medical examiners, funeral directors)
   - Cadaveric organ, eye, or tissue donation
   - Research (with IRB waiver or de-identified data)
   - Serious threat to health or safety
   - Essential government functions (military, national security)
   - Workers' compensation

4. **Incidental Disclosures**
   - Byproduct of permitted use/disclosure
   - Limited to minimum necessary
   - Example: Other patients in waiting room overhear name called

## Required Authorizations

### When Authorization is Required
Patient must provide written authorization for:
- Marketing purposes
- Sale of PHI
- Psychotherapy notes (with limited exceptions)
- Any use/disclosure not otherwise permitted

### Valid Authorization Components
Must include:
1. Specific description of information to be used/disclosed
2. Name of person(s) authorized to make disclosure
3. Name of person(s) to whom disclosure may be made
4. Description of each purpose of disclosure
5. Expiration date or event
6. Signature of individual and date
7. Right to revoke authorization
8. Ability or inability to condition treatment on authorization
9. Potential for re-disclosure by recipient

### Invalid Authorizations
- Compound authorizations (combining authorization with other documents)
- Conditioning treatment on authorization (except limited circumstances)
- Expired authorizations
- Authorizations that have been revoked

## Individual Rights

### Right to Access (45 CFR 164.524)
**Patient Rights:**
- Inspect and obtain copy of PHI in designated record set
- Request specific format (electronic if available)

**Entity Obligations:**
- Respond within 30 days (one 30-day extension allowed)
- Provide access in format requested if readily producible
- Reasonable cost-based fees only (labor, supplies, postage)
- Cannot charge for search/retrieval in most cases

**Exceptions:**
- Psychotherapy notes
- Information compiled for legal proceedings
- Laboratory results when law prohibits access
- Information obtained under promise of confidentiality

### Right to Amend (45 CFR 164.526)
**Patient Rights:**
- Request amendment of inaccurate or incomplete PHI
- Receive written response within 60 days

**Entity May Deny If:**
- Information not created by entity (unless originator unavailable)
- Not part of designated record set
- Not available for inspection
- Already accurate and complete

**If Denied:**
- Must provide written denial with reason
- Patient can submit statement of disagreement
- Entity can respond to disagreement statement

### Right to Accounting of Disclosures (45 CFR 164.528)
**Patient Rights:**
- Request list of disclosures made in last 6 years
- First accounting in 12-month period must be free

**Must Include:**
- Date of disclosure
- Name of entity/person who received PHI
- Brief description of PHI disclosed
- Brief statement of purpose

**Exceptions (No Accounting Required):**
- Disclosures for treatment, payment, or healthcare operations
- Disclosures to the individual
- Disclosures pursuant to authorization
- Disclosures for facility directory or to persons involved in care
- Disclosures for national security or intelligence
- Disclosures to correctional institutions or law enforcement
- Disclosures that occurred before compliance date

### Right to Request Restrictions (45 CFR 164.522)
**Patient Rights:**
- Request restriction on uses/disclosures for TPO
- Request restriction on disclosures to health plans for services paid out-of-pocket in full

**Entity Obligations:**
- Not required to agree to restrictions (except out-of-pocket full payment restriction)
- If agrees, must comply with restriction
- May terminate restriction with notice (except out-of-pocket restriction)
- Must comply with out-of-pocket full payment restriction

### Right to Confidential Communications (45 CFR 164.522)
**Patient Rights:**
- Request communications by alternative means or locations
- Example: Call cell phone instead of home, mail to P.O. box

**Entity Obligations:**
- Must accommodate reasonable requests
- May require information on how payment will be handled
- May require specification of alternative address/method

### Right to Notice of Privacy Practices (45 CFR 164.520)
**Patient Rights:**
- Receive Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP) at first service delivery
- Post NPP in clear and prominent location
- Have NPP available upon request

**NPP Must Contain:**
- How PHI may be used and disclosed
- Individual rights
- Entity's legal duties
- Complaint procedures
- Effective date
- Contact information

### Right to Breach Notification (45 CFR 164.400-414)
**Patient Rights:**
- Be notified of breaches of unsecured PHI
- Notification within 60 days of breach discovery

**Notification Must Include:**
- Description of breach
- Types of information involved
- Steps individuals should take
- Entity's response and mitigation
- Contact procedures for information

## Minimum Necessary Standard

### Principle
Covered entities must make reasonable efforts to limit PHI use, disclosure, and requests to the minimum necessary to accomplish intended purpose.

### When Applied
- Internal uses (except treatment)
- Disclosures (except to patient or for treatment)
- Requests for PHI from other covered entities

### When NOT Applied
- Disclosures to or requests by healthcare provider for treatment
- Uses or disclosures made to the individual
- Uses or disclosures pursuant to valid authorization
- Disclosures to HHS for compliance review
- Required by law disclosures

### Implementation
- Identify roles/persons who need access
- Determine necessary categories of PHI for each role
- Implement policies limiting access to those categories
- Review disclosures on case-by-case basis when appropriate
- Design reasonable safeguards to limit incidental uses/disclosures

## Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP)

### Required Elements
1. **Header** - "THIS NOTICE DESCRIBES HOW MEDICAL INFORMATION ABOUT YOU MAY BE USED AND DISCLOSED AND HOW YOU CAN GET ACCESS TO THIS INFORMATION. PLEASE REVIEW IT CAREFULLY."

2. **Uses and Disclosures**
   - Treatment, payment, healthcare operations (with examples)
   - Other permitted/required uses (with examples)
   - Uses requiring authorization

3. **Individual Rights**
   - Right to request restrictions
   - Right to confidential communications
   - Right to inspect and copy
   - Right to amend
   - Right to accounting
   - Right to paper copy of notice
   - Right to breach notification

4. **Covered Entity Duties**
   - Maintain privacy of PHI
   - Provide notice of privacy practices
   - Abide by terms of current notice
   - Right to change privacy practices and notice
   - How changes will be communicated

5. **Complaints**
   - How to file complaints with entity
   - How to file complaints with HHS
   - No retaliation for complaints

6. **Contact Information**
   - Privacy official contact details

7. **Effective Date**

### Distribution Requirements
- Provide at first service delivery
- Make available upon request
- Post prominently at facilities
- Post on website if have one
- Email to patients who agree (healthcare providers)

### Acknowledgment of Receipt
- Healthcare providers must make good faith effort to obtain written acknowledgment
- Document efforts if cannot obtain
- Not required for health plans or clearinghouses

## Marketing and Fundraising

### Marketing Definition
Communication about product/service that encourages purchase or use.

### Requires Authorization
- Most marketing communications
- Communications paid for by third party

### Exceptions (No Authorization Needed)
- Face-to-face communications
- Promotional gifts of nominal value
- Treatment and case management communications
- Communications describing health-related products/services offered by entity

### Sale of PHI
Requires authorization except:
- Public health purposes
- Treatment and payment
- Sale, transfer, merger of covered entity
- Business associate services
- Patient access to their own PHI
- Research purposes (cost limited to preparation and transmission)

### Fundraising
- May use limited PHI (demographic, dates of service, department, treating physician, outcome)
- Must provide opt-out method
- Must honor opt-out requests

## Personal Representatives

### Definition
Person authorized to act on behalf of individual in making healthcare decisions.

### Treatment as Individual
Must treat personal representative as the individual regarding PHI, except:
- Abuse, neglect, or endangerment situations
- Not in best interest of individual

### Categories
1. **Adults and Emancipated Minors**
   - Person with legal authority (power of attorney, court-appointed guardian)

2. **Unemancipated Minors**
   - Generally parent, guardian, or person acting in loco parentis
   - State law exceptions (minor consent for treatment)

3. **Deceased Individuals**
   - Executor, administrator, or person with authority under state law

## Administrative Requirements

### Privacy Official
- Must designate privacy official responsible for policy development and implementation
- Must designate contact person for receiving complaints

### Workforce Training
- Train all workforce members on privacy policies and procedures
- Train new members within reasonable time
- Document training

### Safeguards
- Implement administrative, technical, and physical safeguards
- Protect PHI from unauthorized uses and disclosures
- Reasonably limit incidental uses and disclosures

### Complaints
- Must have process for individuals to complain
- Document complaints and dispositions
- No retaliation or intimidation

### Sanctions
- Apply sanctions against workforce members who violate policies
- Document sanctions

### Mitigation
- Mitigate harmful effects of known improper uses or disclosures
- Document mitigation efforts

### Refraining from Intimidation
- May not intimidate, threaten, coerce, discriminate, or retaliate
- Against individuals exercising rights or filing complaints

### Waiver of Rights
- May not require individuals to waive rights as condition of treatment, payment, enrollment, or benefits eligibility

### Documentation and Retention
- Maintain policies, procedures, and actions in written or electronic form
- Retain for 6 years from creation or last effective date
- Make available to workforce members
- Review and update as needed

## Common Violations

1. **Impermissible Uses and Disclosures**
   - Accessing patient records without authorization
   - Gossiping about patients
   - Improper disposal of PHI

2. **Lack of Safeguards**
   - Unattended computers with PHI displayed
   - Unsecured paper records
   - Conversations in public areas

3. **Individual Rights Violations**
   - Denying timely access to records
   - Excessive fees for copies
   - Failing to provide accounting

4. **Inadequate Training**
   - No documented training program
   - Untrained workforce members
   - Outdated training materials

5. **Missing or Inadequate NPP**
   - No notice provided
   - Outdated notice
   - Notice doesn't meet requirements

## Best Practices

1. Implement role-based access controls
2. Conduct regular privacy training
3. Audit access to PHI regularly
4. Maintain comprehensive policies and procedures
5. Document all privacy-related activities
6. Respond promptly to individual requests
7. Limit PHI to minimum necessary
8. Secure all forms of PHI (paper, electronic, oral)
9. Implement physical safeguards (locked files, secure disposal)
10. Maintain business associate agreements
11. Conduct periodic privacy risk assessments
12. Update Notice of Privacy Practices as needed
13. Track and respond to complaints
14. Implement sanctions for violations
15. Stay informed of regulatory updates and guidance
