# HITECH Act Reference

## Overview

The Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health (HITECH) Act was enacted as part of the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009 (ARRA). Title XIII of HITECH significantly strengthened HIPAA enforcement and expanded privacy and security protections.

## Legislative Context

- **Enacted:** February 17, 2009
- **Codification:** Public Law 111-5
- **Primary Purpose:** Promote adoption and meaningful use of health information technology
- **Privacy/Security Impact:** Strengthened HIPAA Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification rules

## Key HITECH Provisions

### 1. Business Associate Direct Liability (§13404)

**Revolutionary Change:** Business associates are directly liable for HIPAA Security Rule compliance.

**Before HITECH:**
- BAs only had contractual obligations to CEs
- No direct federal regulatory liability
- Enforcement through contract breach

**After HITECH:**
- BAs directly subject to Security Rule requirements
- BAs subject to civil and criminal penalties
- OCR can directly investigate and penalize BAs
- Omnibus Rule (2013) extended Privacy Rule to BAs

**Applicable Provisions:**
- Security Rule (45 CFR Part 164, Subpart C)
- Breach Notification Rule
- Selected Privacy Rule provisions

**Business Associate Requirements:**
- Implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards
- Conduct security risk assessments
- Maintain policies and procedures
- Train workforce
- Report breaches to covered entities
- Enter into subcontractor agreements (BA-to-BA)

### 2. Breach Notification Requirements (§13402)

**New Requirement:** Mandatory notification following breach of unsecured PHI.

**Individual Notification:**
- Within 60 days of breach discovery
- All affected individuals must be notified
- Specific content requirements

**Media Notification:**
- For breaches affecting 500+ individuals
- Prominent media outlets in affected jurisdictions
- Within 60 days of discovery

**HHS Secretary Notification:**
- Immediately for breaches affecting 500+ (via web portal)
- Annually for breaches affecting fewer than 500
- Public posting on HHS "Wall of Shame" website

**Business Associate Obligations:**
- Notify covered entity within 60 days of discovery
- Provide sufficient information for CE to conduct notifications

**Safe Harbor:**
- Encrypted PHI (per NIST standards) exempt from notification
- Properly destroyed PHI exempt from notification

### 3. Enhanced Enforcement and Penalties (§13410)

**Tiered Penalty Structure:**

**Tier 1: Unknowing**
- Minimum: $100 per violation
- Maximum: $50,000 per violation
- Annual cap: $1,876,814 per provision

**Tier 2: Reasonable Cause**
- Minimum: $1,000 per violation
- Maximum: $50,000 per violation
- Annual cap: $1,876,814 per provision

**Tier 3: Willful Neglect - Corrected**
- Minimum: $10,000 per violation
- Maximum: $50,000 per violation
- Annual cap: $1,876,814 per provision

**Tier 4: Willful Neglect - Not Corrected**
- Minimum: $50,000 per violation
- Maximum: $1,876,814 per violation
- Annual cap: $1,876,814 per provision

**Mandatory Penalties:**
- Willful neglect not corrected within 30 days: penalty MUST be imposed
- No prosecutorial discretion for Tier 4 violations

**Enforcement Distribution:**
- 75% of civil monetary penalties to OCR for enforcement
- 25% distributed to harmed individuals (when feasible)

### 4. State Attorney General Enforcement (§13410(e))

**Authority Granted:**
- State AGs can bring civil actions on behalf of state residents
- Enforce HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules
- Seek damages and injunctive relief

**Requirements:**
- Provide written notice to HHS Secretary
- HHS has right to intervene
- No action if HHS already instituted action

**Damages:**
- Up to $25,000 per violation
- Actual damages to residents
- Costs of investigation and litigation

**Impact:**
- Increased enforcement actions
- Multi-state investigations
- Focus on large-scale breaches

### 5. Accounting of Disclosures for Electronic Health Records (§13405)

**Enhanced Right:**
Extended accounting of disclosures requirement to include TPO disclosures made through EHR.

**Original Requirement (pre-HITECH):**
- Account for disclosures NOT for TPO
- Individual could request 6-year accounting

**HITECH Enhancement:**
- Accounting for TPO disclosures if made through EHR
- Three-year lookback period
- Suspended pending further rulemaking (not yet implemented)

**Current Status:**
- Proposed rule issued but not finalized
- Enhanced accounting not currently required
- Traditional accounting of disclosures still required

### 6. Marketing and Sale of PHI Restrictions (§13406)

**Marketing Restrictions:**

**Definition Expansion:**
- Marketing communication = encouragement to purchase or use product/service
- Requires authorization unless exception applies

**Exceptions (No Authorization Required):**
- Face-to-face communications
- Promotional gifts of nominal value
- Treatment and case management
- Health plan communications about plan benefits

**Financial Remuneration:**
- If covered entity receives financial remuneration, MUST state in authorization
- Individual must be informed of payment

**Sale of PHI:**

**General Rule:**
- Authorization required for sale, exchange, or other remuneration for PHI
- Authorization must state PHI may be further disclosed by recipient

**Exceptions (No Authorization Required):**
- Public health purposes
- Treatment and payment
- Sale, transfer, merger, or consolidation of all or part of CE
- Services to or for CE (e.g., BA services)
- Providing individual access to their own PHI
- Research purposes (payment limited to cost of preparation and transmission)
- Other purposes permitted by HHS

**Prohibition:**
- Cannot sell PHI without valid authorization
- Cannot receive direct or indirect remuneration without authorization

### 7. Fundraising Requirements (§13406)

**Enhanced Protections:**

**Limited PHI Use:**
May use/disclose only:
- Demographic information
- Dates of healthcare service
- Department of service
- Treating physician
- Outcome information
- Health insurance status

**Opt-Out Requirement:**
- Must provide clear and conspicuous opt-out opportunity
- Each fundraising communication must include opt-out
- Cannot require reason for opt-out
- Cannot condition treatment on refusal to opt-out

**Opt-Out Method:**
- Must be clear, conspicuous, and easy to execute
- Example: Reply email, toll-free number, website

**Honoring Opt-Out:**
- Must honor opt-out requests
- May not send further fundraising communications after opt-out

### 8. Minimum Necessary Strengthening (§13405(b))

**Guidance Required:**
- HHS must issue guidance on "minimum necessary" standard
- Within 18 months of enactment (not yet fully implemented)

**Enhanced Standard:**
- Covered entities must limit PHI to minimum necessary
- Applies to requests, uses, and disclosures
- Guidance to specify specific standards

**Current Status:**
- Traditional minimum necessary standard applies
- Enhanced guidance still pending

### 9. Access to PHI in Electronic Format (§13405(e))

**Enhanced Access Rights:**

**Electronic Copies:**
- If PHI maintained electronically, individual can request electronic copy
- CE must provide in electronic format requested if readily producible
- If not readily producible in requested format, must provide in readable electronic format agreed upon

**Transmission:**
- Individual may direct CE to transmit copy directly to designated person
- Request must be in writing, signed, and clearly identify designated person

**Fees:**
- Reasonable cost-based fees only
- Labor for copying (electronic or paper)
- Supplies for paper copies
- Postage
- Preparation of summary (if individual agrees to summary)
- Cannot charge for search/retrieval in most cases

**Timeframe:**
- Within 30 days of request
- One 30-day extension if needed

### 10. Restrictions on Disclosures to Health Plans (§13405(a))

**Individual Right to Restrict:**

**Scenario:**
Individual pays out-of-pocket in full for item or service and requests restriction on disclosure to health plan.

**Requirements:**
- Restriction applies ONLY to disclosure to health plan
- ONLY for payment or healthcare operations purposes
- Individual must pay in full out-of-pocket
- Provider MUST honor restriction request

**Not Applicable:**
- Treatment purposes (may still disclose for treatment)
- Required by law disclosures
- If health plan already has the information

**Practical Impact:**
- Patients can keep certain services private from insurers
- Common for sensitive services (mental health, reproductive health)
- Provider must ensure billing systems honor restriction

### 11. Notification of Security Breaches to Individuals (§13407)

**Vendor Requirements:**
Personal health record (PHR) vendors and related entities must notify individuals and FTC of breaches.

**Applicability:**
- Non-HIPAA covered entities
- PHR vendors
- PHR-related entities
- Third-party service providers to PHRs

**FTC Enforcement:**
- FTC has enforcement authority
- Breach notification rules published by FTC
- Similar notification requirements to HIPAA

### 12. Audits (§13411)

**OCR Audit Program:**

**Authority:**
- HHS must conduct periodic audits of covered entities and business associates
- Assess HIPAA compliance

**Focus Areas:**
- Privacy practices
- Security safeguards
- Breach notification procedures
- Policies and procedures

**Audit Protocol:**
- Random selection
- Complaint-based
- Risk-based selection
- May be announced or unannounced

**Entity Obligations:**
- Cooperate with auditors
- Provide requested documentation
- Allow facility access
- Respond to findings

**Frequency:**
- Ongoing program
- Periodic audit cycles
- Increased frequency based on compliance history

### 13. Whistleblower Protections (§13410(d)(2))

**Prohibition on Retaliation:**

**Protected Actions:**
- Filing HIPAA complaint with HHS
- Testifying in HIPAA investigation or proceeding
- Opposing practice reasonably believed to be HIPAA violation

**Prohibited Retaliation:**
- Termination
- Demotion
- Suspension
- Threats, harassment, or discrimination
- Any other adverse employment action

**Remedies:**
- Reinstatement
- Back pay
- Restoration of benefits
- Other damages

**Enforcement:**
- Complaint to HHS OCR
- May also have state law remedies

## HITECH Implementation Timeline

- **February 17, 2009** - HITECH Act enacted
- **August 2009** - Interim Final Breach Notification Rule
- **October 2009** - Interim Final Enforcement Rule
- **September 23, 2009** - Breach Notification compliance date
- **February 17, 2010** - Business associate compliance date
- **January 25, 2013** - Final Omnibus Rule published
- **March 26, 2013** - Compliance date for most Omnibus Rule provisions
- **September 23, 2013** - BA compliance with Privacy Rule

## Omnibus Rule (2013)

**Major Changes Implementing HITECH:**

1. **Business Associate Expansion**
   - Patient safety organizations
   - Health information organizations
   - E-prescribing gateways
   - Contractors and subcontractors
   - Direct liability for Privacy and Security Rules

2. **Breach Notification Changes**
   - "Harm" threshold replaced with "low probability of compromise"
   - Burden of proof on entity
   - Four-factor risk assessment required

3. **Individual Rights Enhancements**
   - Access to electronic copies
   - Restrictions on health plan disclosures for services paid out-of-pocket

4. **Marketing and Fundraising**
   - Stricter marketing authorization requirements
   - Sale of PHI restrictions
   - Enhanced fundraising opt-out

5. **Genetic Information Protection**
   - Genetic information = health information
   - Health plans cannot use/disclose for underwriting
   - Prohibition on genetic information collection for underwriting

## Enforcement Trends Post-HITECH

**Increased Activity:**
- More investigations
- Higher penalties
- Focus on systemic issues
- Business associate enforcement

**Notable Cases:**
- Anthem: $16 million (2015)
- Premera Blue Cross: $6.85 million (2019)
- Cottage Health: $3 million (2018)
- Memorial Healthcare: $5.5 million (2017)

**Focus Areas:**
- Lack of security risk analysis
- Insufficient access controls
- Inadequate business associate management
- Breach notification failures
- Mobile device security

## Meaningful Use Provisions

**Incentive Programs:**
- Medicare EHR incentive program
- Medicaid EHR incentive program
- Promote adoption of certified EHR technology

**Privacy/Security Tie-In:**
- Conduct security risk analysis (required for MU)
- Implement security updates
- Encrypt PHI
- Conduct patient education on privacy

**Sunset:**
- Incentive programs ended
- Replaced by MIPS (Merit-based Incentive Payment System)
- Privacy/security requirements continue

## Best Practices for HITECH Compliance

1. **Business Associate Management**
   - Comprehensive BA identification
   - Updated BAAs with HITECH provisions
   - Regular BA assessments
   - Subcontractor agreement requirements

2. **Breach Response**
   - Formal breach response plan
   - Risk assessment procedures
   - Notification templates and processes
   - Forensic investigation capabilities

3. **Encryption**
   - Encrypt all ePHI (at rest and in transit)
   - NIST-approved algorithms
   - Proper key management
   - Mobile device encryption

4. **Access Controls**
   - Honor out-of-pocket payment restrictions
   - Implement mechanisms in billing systems
   - Train staff on restriction requirements

5. **Individual Rights**
   - Electronic access procedures
   - Timely response processes
   - Appropriate fee structures
   - Direct transmission capabilities

6. **Marketing/Fundraising**
   - Review communications for marketing definition
   - Obtain proper authorizations
   - Implement opt-out mechanisms
   - Honor opt-out requests

7. **Enhanced Enforcement**
   - Proactive compliance programs
   - Regular risk assessments
   - Corrective action for identified issues
   - Documentation of compliance efforts
