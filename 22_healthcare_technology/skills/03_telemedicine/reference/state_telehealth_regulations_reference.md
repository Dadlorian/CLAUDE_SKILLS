# State Telehealth Regulations Reference

## Overview
Comprehensive reference for state-by-state telehealth regulations, licensure requirements, prescribing rules, and reimbursement policies.

---

## Key Regulatory Dimensions

### 1. Medical Licensure
States require providers to be licensed in the state where the patient is located at the time of the telehealth visit.

### 2. Prescribing Authority
State-specific rules on prescribing via telehealth, especially for controlled substances.

### 3. Reimbursement Parity
State laws requiring insurance parity between telehealth and in-person services.

### 4. Consent Requirements
State-specific informed consent requirements for telehealth services.

### 5. Provider-Patient Relationship
Requirements for establishing a provider-patient relationship before telehealth delivery.

## Interstate Medical Licensure Compact (IMLC)

### Overview
The IMLC is an agreement among participating states that streamlines the licensure process for physicians who want to practice in multiple states.

### Member States (as of 2025)
Full Members: Alabama, Arizona, Colorado, Delaware, Idaho, Illinois, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming

### How It Works
1. Physician applies for an IMLC letter of qualification through their state of principal licensure
2. Once qualified, physician can apply for expedited licensure in other IMLC states
3. Streamlined process reduces time and cost of multi-state licensure
4. Each state still issues a separate license

### Benefits
- Faster licensure process (30-90 days vs 6+ months)
- Lower administrative burden
- Reduced cost compared to traditional licensure
- Continued compliance with state-specific requirements

### Limitations
- Not all states participate
- Still requires separate license in each state
- Compact only applies to physicians (MD, DO), not other providers
- Must maintain primary state of licensure
- Fees still apply for each state license

### Application Process
1. Verify eligibility (board certified, no restrictions, etc.)
2. Apply through IMLC portal in state of principal licensure
3. Pay compact fee ($700 initial, $250 annual renewal)
4. Obtain letter of qualification
5. Apply for licenses in desired member states
6. Maintain annual renewal in all states

## State-by-State Summary

### Alabama

**Licensure**: Alabama license required for patients located in Alabama

**Prescribing**:
- Telemedicine relationship acceptable for prescribing
- Controlled substances: In-person exam required for initial prescription of Schedule II
- Ongoing prescribing allowed after initial in-person visit

**Reimbursement**:
- Medicaid: Covers live video, store-and-forward, remote patient monitoring
- Private insurance: Parity law requires coverage equivalent to in-person
- Originating site: No restrictions (home allowed)

**Consent**: Written or verbal consent required, documented in medical record

**Special Provisions**:
- IMLC member state
- Telehealth definition includes synchronous and asynchronous
- No geographic restrictions

---

### Alaska

**Licensure**: Alaska license required or practicing under specific exemptions

**Prescribing**:
- Telemedicine visit acceptable for prescribing
- Controlled substances: Physician-patient relationship required (can be via telemedicine)
- Alaska Prescription Drug Monitoring Program (PDMP) check required

**Reimbursement**:
- Medicaid: Covers synchronous video, RPM
- Private insurance: No parity law (varies by payer)
- Originating site: Patient's home allowed

**Consent**: Informed consent required, including risks and limitations

**Special Provisions**:
- Rural focus with telehealth encouragement
- Cross-coverage exemptions for out-of-state providers

### Arizona

**Prescribing**:
- Valid provider-patient relationship required

**Reimbursement**:

- Originating site: Patient's home allowed

**Consent**: Informed consent required before telehealth services

**Special Provisions**:
- IMLC member state

- Specific rules for behavioral health services

### California

**Licensure**: California license required (NOT an IMLC member)

**Prescribing**:
- In-person exam generally required before prescribing
- Exceptions: Emergencies, cross-coverage, specialist consultations
- Controlled substances: Strict requirements, generally requires in-person
- Medical Board guidance should be followed carefully

**Reimbursement**:
- Medi-Cal: Synchronous video, asynchronous telehealth (specific specialties)
- Private insurance: Parity law with some exceptions
- Originating site: Home allowed for commercial; restricted for Medi-Cal

**Consent**: Verbal consent required before each visit, documented in record

**Special Provisions**:
- NOT an IMLC member (must obtain California license separately)
- Complex regulatory environment
- Detailed telehealth practice standards
- Specific requirements for mental health services

## Prescribing Controlled Substances

### Ryan Haight Online Pharmacy Consumer Protection Act (Federal)

**Overview**: Federal law restricting online prescribing of controlled substances.

**Key Provisions**:
- In-person medical evaluation required before prescribing controlled substances
- Telemedicine exceptions exist but are narrow
- DEA registration required
- Violations subject to criminal penalties

**Exceptions**:
1. **Public Health Emergency**: DEA may issue special registration during PHE
2. **Special Registration**: Telemedicine can occur at DEA-registered site
3. **Consultation**: Covering provider consultation
4. **Indian Health Service**: Specific exemptions
5. **Emergency Situations**: Limited 72-hour prescribing

**COVID-19 Flexibilities**:
- DEA allowed audio-visual telemedicine for controlled substance prescribing
- No in-person visit required during Public Health Emergency
- Flexibilities extended multiple times (check current status)
- Permanent rule-making in progress

### State-Specific Controlled Substance Rules

**States Allowing Telemedicine for Controlled Substances** (after relationship established):

**States Requiring In-Person for Initial Controlled Substance Prescription**:

**Best Practices**:
1. Verify state-specific requirements for patient location
2. Check PDMP before prescribing controlled substances
3. Document medical necessity and clinical rationale
4. Follow state prescribing limits (day supply, dosage)
5. Ensure proper provider-patient relationship
6. Maintain detailed records
7. Stay updated on regulatory changes

## Reimbursement Parity Laws

### States with Strong Parity Laws (Coverage and Payment)
Arizona, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Georgia, Hawaii, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia

### Key Parity Components

**Coverage Parity**: Requires insurers to cover telehealth services to same extent as in-person

**Payment Parity**: Requires equal reimbursement rates for telehealth and in-person services

**Originating Site**: Many states allow patient's home as originating site

**Modality**: Some states cover audio-only visits, others require video

**Provider Types**: Parity applies to all licensed providers in most states

## Consent Requirements

### Typical Consent Elements

**Information to Disclose**:
1. Nature of telehealth services
2. Limitations of telehealth (e.g., cannot perform physical exam)
3. Risks (technical issues, privacy concerns)
4. Provider credentials and licensure
5. Patient rights (refuse, discontinue services)
6. Privacy and security measures
7. Emergency protocols
8. Alternatives to telehealth

**Documentation**:
- Written consent (signature) or verbal consent (documented)
- Obtained before first telehealth encounter
- Renewed periodically or per state requirements
- Maintained in medical record

**Special Populations**:
- Minors: Parental consent required (age varies by state)
- Mental health: Additional consent requirements in some states
- Substance abuse: 42 CFR Part 2 consent requirements

## Provider-Patient Relationship Requirements

### Establishing Relationship

**States Allowing Telemedicine Establishment**:
- Majority of states now allow relationship via synchronous audio-visual telemedicine
- Some require initial in-person visit for controlled substances
- Relationship must involve real-time interaction (not asynchronous only)

**States Requiring In-Person**:
- Few states still require in-person for all services
- Some require in-person for specific services (e.g., controlled substances)
- Check current state requirements as policies evolve

**Best Practices**:
1. Use synchronous audio-visual communication for initial visit
2. Verify patient identity and location
3. Obtain thorough medical history
4. Discuss treatment plan and obtain consent
5. Document establishment of relationship
6. Ensure continuity of care plan
7. Provide access to in-person care when needed

## Special Considerations

### Behavioral Health Services

**Less Restrictive Rules**:
- Many states have more flexible rules for behavioral health telehealth
- Audio-only visits often permitted
- Broader originating site options
- Parity protections strong

**Substance Abuse Treatment**:
- 42 CFR Part 2 consent requirements
- Special privacy protections
- Medication-assisted treatment (MAT) via telehealth permitted
- DEA flexibilities for buprenorphine prescribing

### School-Based Telehealth

**State Support**:
- Many states encourage school-based telehealth programs
- Parental consent requirements
- Medicaid coverage in many states
- Reduces barriers to care for children

### Correctional Telehealth

**Unique Environment**:
- State and federal prisons increasingly using telehealth
- Reduces security risks and transportation costs
- Specialized platforms for secure environments
- Reimbursement varies

## Credentialing and Privileging

### Multi-State Credentialing Challenges

**Requirements**:
- Each hospital/health system has own credentialing process
- Verification of licensure in all states
- Malpractice insurance coverage across states
- Privileging for specific procedures/services

**Credentials Verification Organization (CVO)**:
- Third-party verification services
- Streamline multi-state credentialing
- Centralized primary source verification

**Credentialing by Proxy**:
- Some states allow credentialing based on another hospital's process
- Typically requires existing privileges at originating site
- Reduces duplication but maintains quality standards

## Multi-State Practice Decision Tree

```
Are you licensed in the patient's state?
│
├─ YES: Proceed with telehealth visit
│   └─ Verify compliance with state prescribing rules
│
└─ NO: Can you obtain expedited license?
    │
    ├─ IMLC Member State + You're IMLC Qualified
    │   └─ Apply for expedited license (30-90 days)
    │
    ├─ Exemption Available? (Consultation, Cross-coverage)
    │   └─ Verify exemption applicability and document
    │
    └─ Standard Licensure Required
        └─ Apply for state license (3-6+ months)
```

## Compliance Best Practices

### Multi-State Telehealth Compliance Checklist

**Before Providing Care**:
- [ ] Verify provider licensed in patient's state
- [ ] Confirm insurance coverage and payer requirements
- [ ] Obtain informed consent per state requirements
- [ ] Verify patient location at time of visit
- [ ] Review state prescribing rules (especially controlled substances)
- [ ] Ensure HIPAA compliance and BAA in place
- [ ] Verify credentialing/privileging if applicable

**During Visit**:
- [ ] Document patient location (city, state)
- [ ] Confirm patient identity
- [ ] Provide required disclosures
- [ ] Follow state-specific clinical standards
- [ ] Document thoroughly per state requirements

**After Visit**:
- [ ] Complete documentation in timely manner
- [ ] Transmit prescriptions per state rules
- [ ] Code and bill appropriately for patient's state
- [ ] Maintain records per state retention requirements
- [ ] Report adverse events if required

**Ongoing**:
- [ ] Maintain current licensure in all states
- [ ] Monitor regulatory changes
- [ ] Renew licenses and certifications
- [ ] Update malpractice insurance coverage
- [ ] Conduct periodic compliance audits

## Risk Management

### Malpractice Considerations

**Multi-State Coverage**:
- Ensure malpractice policy covers all states where practicing
- Verify coverage for telemedicine services specifically
- Understand tail coverage requirements
- Report telemedicine practice to insurer

**Standard of Care**:
- Same standard applies to telemedicine as in-person
- Document limitations of virtual examination
- Know when to refer for in-person evaluation
- Maintain competency in telemedicine modalities

**Documentation**:
- Thorough documentation is critical defense
- Include patient location, consent, clinical findings
- Document technical limitations if applicable
- Clear treatment plan and follow-up

### Licensure Violations

**Consequences**:
- State medical board discipline
- Fines and penalties
- License suspension or revocation
- Criminal charges (in extreme cases)
- Malpractice exposure

**Prevention**:
- Maintain rigorous licensure tracking
- Use software to verify patient location
- Block services in states without license
- Regular compliance audits
- Staff training on multi-state rules
