# Telehealth Consent Reference

## Overview
Comprehensive guide to telehealth consent requirements, consent form elements, state-specific requirements, and best practices for obtaining and documenting patient consent.

---

## Consent vs Authorization

### Consent for Treatment (General)

**Definition**: Patient agreement to receive medical treatment

**Legal Basis**:
- State law (not federal HIPAA requirement)
- Common law (battery/assault if treatment without consent)
- Medical ethics

**Forms**:
- **Implied Consent**: Patient's actions imply consent (e.g., rolling up sleeve for BP check)
- **Verbal Consent**: Spoken agreement (documented in chart)
- **Written Consent**: Signed consent form (required for invasive procedures, recommended for telehealth)

---

### HIPAA Authorization

**Definition**: Permission to use or disclose PHI for purposes beyond treatment, payment, operations

**When Required**:
- Marketing
- Research
- Sale of PHI
- Psychotherapy notes disclosure (beyond treating provider)
- Employer health surveillance

**Not Required for**:
- Treatment purposes (telehealth visits)
- Payment (insurance billing)
- Healthcare operations (quality improvement)

**Note**: Telehealth consent is treatment consent, not HIPAA authorization

---

## Telehealth Consent Requirements

### Federal Requirements

**HIPAA**:
- Does NOT require separate consent for telehealth
- Treatment is treatment, regardless of modality
- However, Notice of Privacy Practices must be provided

**Ryan Haight Act** (Controlled Substances):
- Patient consent implied by seeking treatment
- In-person exam requirement (with exceptions) supersedes consent discussion

**No Federal Telehealth Consent Law**:
- Federal government does not mandate telehealth-specific consent
- State laws and medical board regulations may require

---

### State Requirements

**States Requiring Informed Consent for Telehealth**:
- Alabama, Alaska, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Virginia, Washington, Wisconsin, Wyoming

**States Not Explicitly Requiring** (as of 2025):
- Connecticut, Illinois, Iowa, Missouri, Nebraska, North Carolina, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Vermont, West Virginia

**Note**: Even if not legally required, obtaining consent is best practice

---

### Payer Requirements

**Medicare**:
- Does not require separate telehealth consent
- Assumes consent if patient schedules and attends telehealth visit

**Commercial Insurance**:
- Generally follows state requirements
- Some payers may request documented consent for audits
- Best practice: Always obtain documented consent

**Medicaid**:
- State-specific Medicaid programs may have consent requirements
- Varies by state

## Essential Elements of Telehealth Consent

### Information to Disclose

**1. Nature of Telehealth**:
- Definition: Healthcare provided remotely via video, audio, or asynchronous communication
- Modality: Specify video, phone, or other technology
- How it works: Description of technology platform

**2. Benefits of Telehealth**:
- Convenient access to care
- Reduced travel time and cost
- Ability to access specialists remotely
- Continuity of care (especially during illness or quarantine)

**3. Risks and Limitations**:
- **Technical Issues**: Internet connectivity, platform failures, audio/video quality
- **Security Risks**: Potential for unauthorized access (despite safeguards)
- **Limitations of Virtual Exam**: Cannot perform hands-on physical examination
- **Diagnostic Limitations**: Some conditions require in-person evaluation
- **Emergency Limitations**: Not appropriate for emergencies, may need to call 911 or go to ER
- **Prescription Limitations**: Some states restrict prescribing via telehealth

**4. Privacy and Security**:
- HIPAA protections apply
- Platform is encrypted and secure
- Patient responsibilities (private location, secure connection)
- Recording policies (if applicable)
- Who may be present during visit (patient and provider sides)

**5. Alternatives to Telehealth**:
- In-person visit option always available
- Patient can decline telehealth at any time
- Phone visit as alternative if video not possible

**6. Provider Credentials and Licensure**:
- Provider's name and credentials
- Licensure in patient's state
- Provider's physical location during visit

**7. Patient Responsibilities**:
- Be in private location
- Use secure internet connection (not public WiFi)
- Have necessary information ready (medications, symptoms, etc.)
- Notify provider of anyone else present
- Seek emergency care if needed

**8. Cost and Insurance**:
- Co-pays or fees for telehealth visit
- Insurance coverage (if different from in-person)
- Out-of-pocket costs if not covered

**9. Documentation and Records**:
- Visit will be documented in medical record
- Patient can access records
- Records stored securely

**10. Right to Withdraw**:
- Patient can decline or stop telehealth service at any time
- Can request in-person visit instead
- No penalty for declining

## Consent Form Templates

### Standard Telehealth Consent Form

```
TELEHEALTH CONSENT FORM

Patient Name: _______________________________  DOB: ____________

I, ________________________, hereby consent to receive telehealth services from [Provider/Organization Name].

I understand that:

1. TELEHEALTH DEFINITION
   Telehealth involves the use of electronic communications to enable healthcare providers at different locations to share individual patient medical information for the purpose of improving patient care. The information may be used for diagnosis, therapy, follow-up, education, and/or care management.

2. TECHNOLOGY
   Telehealth services will be provided using [specify platform: video, audio, asynchronous messaging]. The technology used will incorporate network and software security protocols to protect the confidentiality of patient identification and data and will include measures to safeguard the data to ensure its integrity against intentional or unintentional corruption.

3. BENEFITS OF TELEHEALTH
   ☐ Improved access to care
   ☐ More convenient (less travel time and costs)
   ☐ Ability to access specialists
   ☐ Continuity of care

4. POSSIBLE RISKS
   ☐ Technical difficulties or failures could prevent or interrupt the telehealth visit
   ☐ Security protocols could fail, causing a breach of privacy
   ☐ Physical examination may be limited or not possible
   ☐ Some conditions may require in-person visit for proper diagnosis and treatment

5. PRIVACY AND SECURITY
   ☐ I understand that my healthcare information may be shared with other individuals for scheduling and billing purposes
   ☐ I understand that the telehealth platform is encrypted and HIPAA-compliant
   ☐ I will take reasonable precautions to ensure my privacy (private location, secure connection)
   ☐ Dissemination of any patient-identifiable images or information for purposes other than this telehealth visit is not permitted

6. RIGHTS
   ☐ I have the right to withhold or withdraw my consent to the use of telehealth at any time
   ☐ I have the right to request an in-person visit instead of telehealth
   ☐ I understand that insurance or payment may be different for telehealth vs in-person visits

7. ALTERNATIVE OPTIONS
   ☐ I understand that I can choose to have an in-person visit instead
   ☐ I understand that for emergencies, I should call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room

8. RECORDING
   ☐ Telehealth visits will NOT be recorded, OR
   ☐ Telehealth visits may be recorded for [specify purpose: quality assurance, medical education, etc.]
   ☐ I consent / do not consent to recording (initial one): _______

9. PATIENT LOCATION
   I will be located in the following state during the telehealth visit: ________________

10. ACKNOWLEDGMENT
    I have read and understand the information provided above regarding telehealth services. I have had the opportunity to ask questions and all of my questions have been answered to my satisfaction.

Patient Signature: _________________________ Date: ___________

Provider/Witness: _________________________ Date: ___________


PATIENT CONTACT INFORMATION (for telehealth visit):
Phone Number: _________________________
Email Address: _________________________
Preferred Communication Method: _________________________
```

```
ADDITIONAL CONSENT FOR TELETHERAPY/TELEPSYCHIATRY

In addition to the standard telehealth consent, I understand:

1. CRISIS MANAGEMENT
   ☐ In case of a mental health crisis or emergency, I will call 911, go to the nearest emergency room, or call the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline at 988
   ☐ My therapist/psychiatrist may not be immediately available in a crisis

2. EMERGENCY CONTACTS
   Emergency Contact Name: _______________________
   Relationship: _________________________________
   Phone: _______________________________________

3. LOCATION DISCLOSURE
   ☐ I understand I must disclose my physical location at the start of each session
   ☐ If I change locations during a session, I will notify my provider

4. CONFIDENTIALITY LIMITATIONS
   ☐ I understand that my provider may need to break confidentiality if I am a danger to myself or others, or if abuse/neglect is suspected

5. SECURE ENVIRONMENT
   ☐ I will participate in sessions from a private, quiet location
   ☐ I will notify my provider if anyone else is present or enters the room during the session

6. CANCELLATION POLICY
   ☐ I understand the cancellation policy: [specify policy, e.g., 24-hour notice required]

Patient Signature: _________________________ Date: ___________
```

## Obtaining Consent

### Timing

**Before First Telehealth Visit**:
- Best practice: Obtain consent before or at first telehealth visit
- Can be obtained electronically (email, patient portal, e-signature)
- Can be obtained verbally with documentation (if state law allows)

**Renewal**:
- Generally, one-time consent is sufficient for ongoing care
- Consider annual renewal or re-consent if services change significantly
- Re-consent if significant policy changes (e.g., new recording policy)

---

### Methods

**Electronic Consent**:
- Patient portal with e-signature
- Email with DocuSign or similar
- SMS with text-to-sign
- Video platform integrated consent
- Advantages: Convenient, easy to track, automatically stored
- Requirements: Must meet state e-signature laws (UETA, E-SIGN Act)

**Paper Consent**:
- Mailed to patient with return envelope
- Signed in-person before first telehealth visit
- Scanned or faxed copy acceptable
- Advantages: Traditional, some patients prefer
- Disadvantages: Slower, requires handling paper

**Verbal Consent**:
- Obtain verbally at start of first telehealth visit
- Document in medical record: "Patient provided verbal consent for telehealth after discussion of risks, benefits, and alternatives. Patient's questions were answered."
- Some states may not accept verbal-only consent
- Best for established patients in ongoing care

---

### Documentation

**What to Document**:
- Date and time consent obtained
- Method (written, electronic, verbal)
- Who obtained consent (provider, staff member)
- That risks, benefits, alternatives discussed
- That patient had opportunity to ask questions
- Patient's decision (consent given or declined)
- Copy of signed form (if written/electronic)

**Where to Document**:
- Scanned consent form in EHR
- Consent management module in EHR
- Separate consent tracking database
- Progress note documenting verbal consent

## Special Consent Scenarios

### Urgent or Emergency Telehealth

**Implied Consent**:
- In true emergencies, consent may be implied
- Patient's act of seeking urgent care implies consent
- Document: "Patient presented for urgent telehealth visit, implied consent obtained"

**Abbreviated Consent**:
- Brief verbal consent: "I'm going to conduct this visit via video. Do you consent to telehealth for this visit?"
- Document: "Verbal consent for telehealth obtained"
- Send full written consent for signature afterward

---

### Interpreter Services

**Language Barrier Considerations**:
- Provide consent form in patient's primary language (if available)
- Use qualified interpreter to explain consent
- Document use of interpreter: "Consent discussed via [language] interpreter"
- Interpreter signs as witness to consent process

---

### Substance Abuse Treatment (42 CFR Part 2)

**Additional Consent Required**:
- 42 CFR Part 2 requires specific consent for disclosure of substance abuse treatment information
- Separate from general telehealth consent
- Must include specific elements:
  - Name of patient
  - Name of program
  - Purpose of disclosure
  - How much and what kind of information
  - Name of person/organization to whom disclosure will be made
  - Expiration date or event
  - Right to revoke
  - Prohibition on re-disclosure

## Consent for Recording

### When Recording May Occur

**Educational Purposes**:
- Medical student or resident education
- Provider self-improvement
- Quality assurance review

**Legal/Compliance Purposes**:
- Documentation of visit (in addition to written note)
- Quality audits
- Billing disputes

**Research**:
- Clinical research studies

---

### Recording Consent Requirements

**Explicit Consent Required**:
- Must obtain specific consent to record
- General telehealth consent is NOT sufficient
- State laws vary (some states require all-party consent for recording)

**Information to Provide**:
- Purpose of recording
- Who will have access to recording
- How long recording will be retained
- How recording will be stored (encrypted, secure)
- Patient's right to decline recording (and still receive care)
- Patient's right to request deletion of recording

**Documentation**:
- Separate checkbox on consent form: "I consent to recording of telehealth visits for [purpose]"
- Or separate recording consent form

## State-Specific Requirements

### California

**Requirements**:
- Verbal consent required before each telehealth visit
- Must document in medical record
- Information to provide:
  - Types of services appropriate for telehealth
  - Provider's name, contact info, specialty
  - Limitations of telehealth
  - Potential risks
  - Procedures for follow-up and emergency care

**Documentation**: "Verbal consent for telehealth obtained and documented per California law"

---

### New York

**Requirements**:
- Informed consent required before telehealth services
- Written consent recommended (verbal acceptable if documented)
- Information to provide:
  - Nature of telehealth services
  - Limitations and risks
  - Privacy and security measures
  - Right to refuse
  - Provider identity and location

---

### Texas

**Requirements**:
- Informed consent before telemedicine visit
- Verbal consent acceptable (written preferred)
- Must document in medical record
- Information to provide:
  - Risks and benefits
  - Security measures
  - Limitations of virtual exam
  - Alternatives

**Texas Medical Board Rules**: Detailed telemedicine consent requirements

---

### Florida

**Requirements**:
- Informed consent before telehealth
- Written consent for initial telehealth visit (verbal for follow-ups if documented)
- Information to provide:
  - Modality of visit
  - Potential for others to see/hear
  - Limitations of telehealth
  - Provider credentials

---

## Consent Refusal

### If Patient Declines Telehealth

**Options**:
- Offer in-person visit instead
- Offer phone visit (audio-only) if acceptable to patient
- Reschedule for when patient is more comfortable
- Provide educational resources about telehealth

**Documentation**:
- "Patient declined telehealth consent. In-person visit scheduled for [date]."
- Do not pressure or coerce patient
- Respect patient's autonomy

---

### Partial Consent

**Example**: Patient consents to video visit but not to recording
- Honor patient's preference
- Proceed with visit without recording
- Document: "Telehealth consent obtained. Patient declined consent for recording."

---

## Minors and Consent

### Parental Consent

**General Rule**: Parent/guardian consent required for minors (<18)

**Exceptions** (State-Specific):
- **Emancipated Minors**: Can consent for own care
- **Mature Minor Doctrine**: Some states allow mature minors to consent
- **Specific Services**: Some states allow minors to consent for certain services without parental consent:
  - STI testing and treatment
  - Pregnancy services
  - Substance abuse treatment
  - Mental health services (age varies by state)

---

### Assent

**Definition**: Minor's agreement to participate in care (in addition to parental consent)

**Age**: Typically for minors 7-17 years old

**Process**:
- Explain telehealth in age-appropriate language
- Ask if minor agrees to participate
- Document assent in addition to parental consent

## Best Practices

### Design Clear, Readable Forms
- Use plain language (avoid legal jargon)
- Short sentences and bullet points
- Adequate white space
- Large enough font (12-14 point)
- Organized by topic
- Highlight key information

### Make Process Easy
- Electronic signature options
- Mobile-friendly forms
- Integration with scheduling/check-in
- Reminders to complete consent before visit
- Help available if questions

### Train Staff
- Staff can explain consent to patients
- Staff can answer common questions
- Escalate complex questions to providers
- Consistent process across staff members

### Document Thoroughly
- Date, time, method of consent
- Signed form (if applicable)
- Verbal consent documented in note
- Store securely in EHR

### Review Periodically
- Annual review of consent forms
- Update for regulatory changes
- Update for technology changes
- Patient feedback on clarity

---

## Audit and Compliance

### Monitoring Consent Compliance

**Metrics**:
- % of telehealth visits with documented consent
- Consent form completion rate
- Time to obtain consent (efficiency)

**Auditing**:
- Monthly random chart audit (10-20 charts)
- Check for consent documentation
- Review consent form completeness
- Identify gaps and remediate

**Corrective Actions**:
- Re-train staff on consent process
- Improve electronic consent workflow
- Automate reminders and tracking
- Update policies as needed
