# Patient Education Program Guide

## Program Design and Content Strategy (Weeks 1-4)

### Curriculum Development Process

**Step 1: Needs Assessment**
- Identify patient population (age, education, health literacy)
- Survey patients on learning preferences
- Assess current knowledge gaps
- Identify readiness for behavior change

**Step 2: Learning Objectives**

For Diabetes Education:
```
Knowledge Objectives (Patient will know):
- How insulin and blood glucose work
- Signs of hypo/hyperglycemia
- Medication types and purposes
- Nutrition principles for diabetes
- Exercise benefits and safety
- Foot care and complications prevention
- Sick day management

Skills Objectives (Patient will be able to):
- Test blood glucose and record results
- Administer insulin or oral medications
- Count carbohydrates
- Plan healthy meals
- Recognize and treat low blood glucose
- Monitor feet for problems

Behavior Objectives (Patient will):
- Take medications as prescribed
- Exercise 150 min/week
- Maintain healthy diet
- Monitor blood glucose regularly
- Attend follow-up appointments
- Report concerning symptoms early
```

**Step 3: Content Outline**

```
Module 1: Understanding Diabetes (Week 1-2)
├─ What is diabetes?
├─ Types of diabetes
├─ How blood glucose regulation works
├─ Risk factors and complications
└─ Living well with diabetes

Module 2: Medications and Glucose Monitoring (Week 2-3)
├─ Medication types and purposes
├─ How to take medications correctly
├─ Blood glucose monitoring
├─ Record keeping and pattern recognition
└─ When to call provider

Module 3: Nutrition (Week 3-4)
├─ Food groups and portions
├─ Carbohydrate counting
├─ Meal planning
├─ Eating out and special occasions
└─ Addressing emotional eating

Module 4: Physical Activity (Week 4-5)
├─ Benefits of exercise
├─ Safe exercise practices
├─ Starting an exercise program
├─ Preventing low blood glucose during exercise
└─ Building a sustainable routine

Module 5: Preventing Complications (Week 5-6)
├─ Foot care
├─ Eye exams
├─ Kidney health
├─ Heart health
└─ Regular screening

Module 6: Living Well (Week 6-7)
├─ Stress management
├─ Sleep and blood glucose
├─ Sick day management
├─ Traveling with diabetes
└─ Working with your care team
```

### Content Creation Best Practices

**Plain Language Standards:**
- Average sentence: 10-15 words
- Average paragraph: 2-3 sentences
- 6th-8th grade reading level
- Define medical terms clearly
- Use active voice
- Concrete examples from daily life

**Example - Clear vs. Medical Language:**

Medical: "Hyperglycemia is an elevated blood glucose level resulting from inadequate insulin secretion or insulin resistance."

Clear: "High blood sugar happens when your body doesn't have enough insulin or can't use insulin properly."

## Budget and Financial Sustainability (Week 17)

### Cost Structure

**Fixed Costs (Annual):**
```
Staffing (salaries + benefits):        $300,000-$400,000
Platform/LMS licensing:                 $50,000-$100,000
Facility rent (classroom):              $30,000-$50,000
Equipment (monitors, supplies):         $10,000-$20,000
Marketing and materials:                $15,000-$25,000
Administrative overhead:                $20,000-$30,000
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total Fixed Annual Cost:               $425,000-$625,000
```

**Variable Costs (Per Participant):**
```
Educational materials:                 $10-$20
Device supplies:                        $5-$10
Food for classes:                       $3-$5
Incentives/certificates:                $2-$5
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total Per-Participant Cost:            $20-$40
```

### Revenue Models

**Option 1: Fully Funded (No Cost to Patient)**
- Funded by health system
- Reduces costs through improved outcomes
- Improves patient satisfaction
- Enhances community reputation

**Option 2: Nominal Fee ($50-$150)**
- Low patient barrier
- Covers materials and supplies
- Increases commitment/adherence
- Generates revenue for expansion

**Option 3: Insurance Reimbursement**
- Billable as healthcare service
- CPT codes for diabetes education
- Patients' insurance pays
- May require patient copay

**Option 4: Hybrid Model**
- Insurance covers most
- Health system subsidizes difference
- Patient minimal or no cost
- Sustainable funding model
