# Shared Decision-Making Reference

## Core Concepts and Frameworks

### Definition of Shared Decision-Making (SDM)

**SDM is a collaborative approach where:**
1. Healthcare provider shares clinical evidence
2. Patient shares values, preferences, and concerns
3. Both collaboratively make healthcare decisions
4. Decision is documented and implemented

**Key Elements:**
- Bidirectional information exchange
- Patient involvement in decision-making
- Value and preference elicitation
- Support for informed choice
- Respect for patient autonomy

### Historical Development

**Three Models of Decision-Making:**

1. **Paternalistic Model**
   - Provider makes decision
   - Limited patient input
   - "Doctor knows best"
   - Historical approach

2. **Informative Model**
   - Provider gives information
   - Patient decides independently
   - Limited discussion
   - Early patient autonomy approach

3. **Shared Decision-Making Model** (Current Best Practice)
   - Mutual deliberation
   - Information exchange
   - Value exploration
   - Joint decision-making
   - Supported choice

### Evidence for SDM Effectiveness

**Clinical Outcomes:**
- Improved patient adherence (15-20% improvement)
- Better health outcomes in some conditions
- Reduced unnecessary procedures
- Improved disease management

**Patient Outcomes:**
- Higher patient satisfaction
- Increased confidence in decision
- Better understanding of options
- Reduced decisional conflict
- Improved quality of life

**Healthcare System Outcomes:**
- Lower costs in some conditions (reduced overtreatment)
- Reduced malpractice claims
- Improved provider satisfaction
- Better resource allocation

## SDM Process Model

### Six-Step SDM Process

```
Step 1: Recognize a Decision Needs to be Made
├─ Patient presents with condition
├─ Treatment/management needed
├─ Multiple reasonable options exist
└─ Decision point identified by provider or patient

Step 2: Establish and Maintain Rapport
├─ Warm greeting and initial listening
├─ Understanding patient's perspective
├─ Building trust relationship
└─ Creating safe space for discussion

Step 3: Share Information About the Condition
├─ Disease pathophysiology explained
├─ Prognosis explained
├─ Risk factors discussed
├─ Complications addressed
└─ Plain language used

Step 4: Discuss Treatment Options
├─ All reasonable options presented
├─ Evidence for each option
├─ Benefits and harms outlined
├─ Realistic expectations set
└─ Provide decision aids

Step 5: Help Patient Explore Values and Preferences
├─ What matters most to you?
├─ What are your goals?
├─ What concerns do you have?
├─ What's your risk tolerance?
└─ Family/cultural considerations

Step 6: Reach a Decision and Plan Implementation
├─ Summarize deliberation
├─ Confirm patient understanding
├─ Document patient choice
├─ Plan follow-up and monitoring
└─ Provide written summary
```

## Decision Aids and Tools

### Types of Decision Aids

**Condition-Specific Decision Aids:**
- Patient education about specific conditions
- Options unique to that condition
- Evidence specific to condition
- Outcomes and timing
- Example: "Treating early prostate cancer: Your choices"

**Preference Elicitation Tools:**
- Help patient clarify what matters
- Weighted preference questions
- Visual analogs and scales
- Strength of preference assessment

**Comparison Tools:**
- Side-by-side option comparison
- Benefit and harm visualization
- Cost and time commitment comparison
- Interactive decision support systems

### Decision Aid Components

**Effective Decision Aids Typically Include:**

1. **Condition Information**
   - Clear disease explanation
   - Natural history and prognosis
   - Prevalence and risk factors

2. **Option Descriptions**
   - Non-treatment option
   - Medical management
   - Surgical/procedural options
   - Lifestyle approaches
   - Watchful waiting/monitoring

3. **Benefits and Harms**
   - Specific probabilities (e.g., "1 in 100")
   - Time frame for outcomes
   - Common vs. rare side effects
   - Long-term considerations

4. **Research Evidence**
   - Clinical trial data
   - Guidelines and recommendations
   - Quality of evidence (GRADE assessment)
   - Where evidence is lacking

5. **Values Clarification**
   - Questions about priorities
   - Life goals and preferences
   - Risk tolerance assessment
   - Family impact consideration

6. **Implementation Support**
   - How to prepare
   - What to expect
   - Follow-up plan
   - When to contact provider

## Value and Preference Elicitation

### Exploring Patient Values

**Key Questions:**

1. **Life Goals and Priorities**
   - "What's most important to you in life right now?"
   - "What are your top 3 health goals?"
   - "How do health decisions affect your life?"

2. **Functional Preferences**
   - "What activities are most important to continue?"
   - "How much would you be willing to compromise on function?"
   - "What level of independence is essential?"

3. **Side Effect Tolerance**
   - "What side effects concern you most?"
   - "How much are you willing to tolerate for benefit?"
   - "What would make a side effect unacceptable?"

4. **Risk Attitudes**
   - "How do you feel about uncertain outcomes?"
   - "Would you prefer guaranteed smaller benefit or chance at larger benefit?"
   - "What's your comfort with preventive vs. treating actual disease?"

5. **Treatment Burden Tolerance**
   - "How much time are you willing to dedicate to treatment?"
   - "What about frequency of appointments/medications?"
   - "Concerns about cost or access?"

6. **Life Expectancy vs. Quality of Life**
   - "What matters more: living longer or better quality of life?"
   - "Would you trade length for independence?"
   - "What would make life feel worth living?"

### Preference Elicitation Methods

**Visual Analog Scales:**
```
How important is reducing symptoms vs. extending life?

Reducing symptoms ←─────────────→ Extending life
Completely       |         |         |         | Completely
disagree         25%      50%       75%       agree
```

**Ranking Exercise:**
```
Rank these from most to least important:
1. _____ Living as long as possible
2. _____ Having high quality of life
3. _____ Minimizing medication burden
4. _____ Avoiding side effects
5. _____ Maintaining independence
```

**Time Trade-Off:**
```
"If you could live 10 more years in current health state
 vs. 6 years in perfect health, which would you choose?"
```

**Standard Gamble:**
```
"Would you accept a treatment with 70% chance of cure
 and 30% chance of death to treat this condition?"
```

## Implementing SDM in Clinical Practice

### Organizational Readiness

**Assessment Questions:**
- Does leadership support SDM?
- Are providers trained in SDM?
- Are decision aids available?
- Is patient portal capable?
- Is time built into visits?
- Are outcomes measured?

### Provider Training

**SDM Competencies:**
1. **Communication Skills**
   - Active listening
   - Eliciting preferences
   - Explaining evidence clearly
   - Addressing emotions

2. **Clinical Knowledge**
   - Condition pathophysiology
   - Treatment options
   - Benefits and harms
   - Evidence quality

3. **Values Exploration**
   - Open-ended questioning
   - Non-judgmental listening
   - Identifying what matters
   - Respecting differences

4. **Documentation and Shared Decision**
   - Documenting preference
   - Recording decision
   - Written summary provision
   - Follow-up planning

**Training Methods:**
- Online modules and CME
- Role-playing and coaching
- Audit and feedback
- Peer learning
- Champion provider mentorship

### Workflow Integration

**Visit Structure with SDM:**
```
1. Establish rapport (2 min)
   └─ Build connection, understand perspective

2. Assess understanding (2 min)
   └─ What patient already knows

3. Identify decision needs (2 min)
   └─ Decision required and options

4. Share evidence (5 min)
   └─ Benefits and harms discussed

5. Explore preferences (3 min)
   └─ Values and concerns

6. Reach agreement (2 min)
   └─ Confirm preference and document

Total: 16 minutes (expandable as needed)
```

## Measuring SDM Implementation

### Process Measures

**Documentation:**
- Proportion of decisions documented as "shared"
- Use of decision aids
- Time spent on preference discussion
- Patient preparation completion

**Patient Involvement:**
- Patient perception of involvement (CollaboRATE scale)
- Decisional conflict assessment
- Treatment decision satisfaction

### Outcome Measures

**Clinical Outcomes:**
- Adherence to chosen treatment
- Clinical efficacy of chosen option
- Complication rates
- Symptom improvement

**Patient Outcomes:**
- Knowledge about condition and options
- Adherence and satisfaction
- Quality of life after decision
- Regret or satisfaction with choice

**Experience Measures:**
- Patient satisfaction with decision process
- Provider satisfaction with SDM
- Time and resource requirements
- Utilization changes

### Validated Instruments

**Decisional Conflict Scale:**
- Measures uncertainty about decision
- 16 items, scored 0-100
- Used pre and post SDM
- Target: <25 (low conflict)

**CollaboRATE:**
- Measures perceived shared decision-making
- 3 items, quick assessment
- Patient perspective
- Target: >90% high collaboration rating

**Acceptability of Decision Scale:**
- Measures satisfaction with decision
- 4 items assessment
- Used post-decision
- Target: High satisfaction

## Challenges and Solutions

### Common Challenges

| Challenge | Root Cause | Solution |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Time constraints | No time built into schedule | Allocate adequate appointment time |
| Provider knowledge | Lack of evidence training | CME and decision aid review |
| Patient literacy | Low health literacy | Simplified aids, teach-back method |
| Cultural factors | Language/cultural barriers | Translated aids, cultural brokers |
| Technology barriers | Unfamiliar with tools | Training and support available |
| Resistance to change | Habit and comfort | Champion advocates, evidence presentation |

### Adoption Strategies

1. **Build the Case**
   - Share evidence on effectiveness
   - Present patient and provider satisfaction data
   - Discuss malpractice risk reduction

2. **Start Small**
   - Pick one condition to pilot
   - Use champions to demonstrate
   - Build on success

3. **Provide Support**
   - Training programs
   - Decision aid availability
   - Template documentation
   - Technical support

4. **Measure Progress**
   - Track adoption rates
   - Collect satisfaction feedback
   - Monitor outcomes
   - Share results
