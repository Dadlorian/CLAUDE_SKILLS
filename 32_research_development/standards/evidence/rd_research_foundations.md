# R&D Research Foundations
## Evidence-Based Research & Innovation Practices

### Seminal Papers

#### Research Methodology

**Statistical Power and Effect Sizes**:
- Cohen, J. (1992). "A power primer." *Psychological Bulletin*, 112(1), 155-159.
  - Defines effect sizes (small d=0.2, medium d=0.5, large d=0.8)
  - Power = 0.8 standard for sample size calculations

**Reproducibility Crisis**:
- Open Science Collaboration (2015). "Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science." *Science*, 349(6251), aac4716.
  - Only 36% of 100 studies replicated
  - Sparked reproducibility reforms across sciences

**Preregistration**:
- Nosek, B.A., et al. (2018). "The preregistration revolution." *PNAS*, 115(11), 2600-2606.
  - Reduces p-hacking and HARKing (Hypothesizing After Results Known)
  - ClinicalTrials.gov, OSF preregistration

**Meta-Analysis Methods**:
- Borenstein, M., et al. (2009). *Introduction to Meta-Analysis*. Wiley.
  - Gold standard for systematic reviews
  - Fixed-effect vs random-effects models

#### Innovation Management

**Stage-Gate Process**:
- Cooper, R.G. (2008). "Perspective: The Stage-Gate® Idea-to-Launch Process." *Journal of Product Innovation Management*, 25(3), 213-232.
  - 5 stages, 5 gates with go/kill criteria
  - Used by 60%+ of leading product developers

**Disruptive Innovation**:
- Christensen, C.M. (1997). *The Innovator's Dilemma*. Harvard Business Press.
  - Explains why market leaders fail (focus on sustaining vs disruptive)
  - Examples: Kodak (digital), Nokia (smartphones)

**Open Innovation**:
- Chesbrough, H.W. (2003). "The Era of Open Innovation." *MIT Sloan Management Review*, 44(3), 35-41.
  - Inbound (acquire external IP) and outbound (license out)
  - Procter & Gamble "Connect + Develop": 50% of innovation from outside

**Lean Startup**:
- Ries, E. (2011). *The Lean Startup*. Crown Business.
  - Build-Measure-Learn loop
  - Minimum Viable Product (MVP)
  - Pivot or persevere based on data

#### Data Management

**FAIR Principles**:
- Wilkinson, M.D., et al. (2016). "The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management." *Scientific Data*, 3, 160018.
  - Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable
  - 8000+ citations, global adoption

**Reproducible Research**:
- Wilson, G., et al. (2017). "Good enough practices in scientific computing." *PLOS Computational Biology*, 13(6), e1005510.
  - Version control, data management, workflow automation
  - Practical advice for researchers

### Benchmarking Studies

#### R&D Investment Returns

**Industrial R&D**:
- Jaruzelski, B., & Dehoff, K. (2010). "The Global Innovation 1000." *Strategy+Business*, Booz & Company.
  - Finding: R&D spending level less important than *how* money is spent
  - 3 strategies: Need Seekers (customer-driven), Market Readers (fast followers), Technology Drivers (R&D-led)

**Pharmaceutical R&D**:
- DiMasi, J.A., et al. (2016). "Innovation in the pharmaceutical industry." *Journal of Health Economics*, 47, 20-33.
  - Cost per approved drug: $2.6 billion (including failures)
  - Time: 10-15 years from discovery to approval
  - Success rate: 12% from Phase I to approval

**Software R&D**:
- Standish Group (2015). "Chaos Report."
  - Only 29% of software projects successful (on time, on budget, with features)
  - Agile projects 2x more likely to succeed than waterfall

#### Technology Transfer

**University Tech Transfer**:
- AUTM (2022). "Licensing Activity Survey."
  - 1,080 startups formed from university research (2021)
  - $3.8 billion in licensing revenue
  - MIT, Stanford, Columbia: Top 3 for startups

**TRL Validation**:
- Mankins, J.C. (2009). "Technology readiness assessments: A retrospective." *Acta Astronautica*, 65(9-10), 1216-1223.
  - TRL 1-9 scale developed at NASA (1970s-1980s)
  - Widely adopted (DoD, DOE, EU)
  - Criticisms: Subjective, linear (reality is iterative)

#### Research Impact

**Citation Analysis**:
- Hirsch, J.E. (2005). "An index to quantify an individual's scientific research output." *PNAS*, 102(46), 16569-16572.
  - h-index: h papers with ≥h citations
  - Widely used (and criticized) for researcher evaluation

**Altmetrics**:
- Priem, J., et al. (2010). "Altmetrics: A manifesto." altmetrics.org/manifesto
  - Beyond citations: social media, news, policy documents
  - Faster indicators (citations take years)
  - Examples: Altmetric Attention Score, PlumX

### Industry Best Practices

#### FAANG R&D Practices

**Google Research** (from published papers):
- Sculley, D., et al. (2015). "Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems." *NIPS*.
  - ML systems have unique technical debt (data dependencies, configuration)
  - Emphasize testing, monitoring, versioning

**Netflix** (Tech Blog):
- Chaos Engineering: Deliberately inject failures to test resilience (Chaos Monkey tool)
- A/B testing at scale: 1000+ experiments running simultaneously
- Data-driven decision making: Every feature tested

**Amazon**:
- "Working Backwards": Start with press release, work backward to product
- Two-pizza teams: Small autonomous teams (6-10 people)
- Bias for action: High-velocity decision making

#### Corporate R&D Labs

**Bell Labs** (historical, Lucent/Nokia):
- 9 Nobel Prizes, Unix, C language, transistor, laser, solar cell
- Model: Long-term research (10-20 years) + applied development
- Decline: Breakup of AT&T, shift to short-term focus

**IBM Research**:
- 6 Nobel Prizes, DRAM, hard disk, relational database, Watson
- Current focus: Hybrid cloud, AI, quantum computing
- 3000+ researchers, 19 labs worldwide

**Microsoft Research**:
- Founded 1991, 1000+ researchers
- Notable: Kinect, PageRank precursor, ML frameworks (CNTK)
- Model: Publish freely (8000+ papers), patent selectively

**DeepMind** (Alphabet):
- AlphaGo (2016), AlphaFold (2020, 2021)
- Hybrid: Academic publishing + Google product integration
- Example of successful "moonshot" research lab

### Funding Landscape

#### U.S. Federal R&D Funding (FY2023)

**By Agency**:
1. NIH: $45 billion (biomedical)
2. DOD: $16 billion (defense)
3. DOE: $8 billion (energy, physics)
4. NSF: $7.5 billion (basic research, education)
5. NASA: $7 billion (space, aeronautics)
6. USDA: $3 billion (agriculture)

**By Type**:
- Basic research: $40B (30%)
- Applied research: $40B (30%)
- Development: $55B (40%)

**Trends**:
- Increasing focus on "use-inspired basic research" (Pasteur's Quadrant)
- Cross-cutting priorities: AI/ML, quantum, climate, biotech

#### European R&D Funding

**Horizon Europe** (2021-2027): €95.5 billion
- Pillar I: Excellent Science (€25B) - ERC grants, fellowships
- Pillar II: Global Challenges (€54B) - Missions (cancer, climate, cities)
- Pillar III: Innovative Europe (€14B) - EIC (European Innovation Council)

**Success Rates**: 10-15% (highly competitive)

#### Private Funding

**Venture Capital in Deep Tech** (2023):
- Biotech/Pharma: $30B
- AI/ML: $25B
- Climate tech: $20B
- Quantum: $2B
- Total deep tech: ~$100B (down from $150B in 2021)

**Challenges**:
- Long development timelines (7-12 years vs 2-4 for software)
- Capital intensity ($50M+ to Series B common)
- Technical risk (will it work?)
- Regulatory risk (FDA, EPA approval)

**Successful Deep Tech VCs**: DCVC, Lux Capital, Flagship Pioneering, Khosla Ventures

### Key Metrics

**R&D Efficiency**:
- **R&D Intensity**: R&D spend / Revenue (pharma: 15-20%, software: 10-15%, manufacturing: 2-5%)
- **RODI** (Return on Development Investment): Revenue from new products / R&D spend
- **Innovation Accounting** (Lean Startup): Validated learning per dollar spent

**Researcher Productivity**:
- **Publications/year**: 1-3 for faculty, varies widely
- **h-index**: Varies by field (10-20 mid-career, 30-50 senior)
- **Grant success rate**: NIH R01 ~20%, NSF ~25%, ERC ~13%

**Technology Transfer**:
- **Invention disclosures**: 100-300/year (top research universities)
- **Patents filed**: 30-50% of disclosures
- **Licenses executed**: 50-150/year
- **Startups formed**: 10-30/year
- **Licensing revenue**: $50M-$200M/year (top universities)

**Clinical Trials**:
- **Phase I→II**: 63% success rate
- **Phase II→III**: 31%
- **Phase III→Approval**: 58%
- **Overall (Phase I→Approval)**: 12%
