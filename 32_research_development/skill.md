# Research & Development Domain Skill
## Elite Professional R&D Expertise - From Discovery to Commercialization

### Philosophy

This skill embodies world-class Research & Development practices from:
- **Bell Labs** - Legendary innovation culture and fundamental research
- **DARPA** - High-impact breakthrough research methodologies
- **Google X / Alphabet** - Moonshot innovation frameworks
- **MIT Media Lab** - Interdisciplinary research excellence
- **CERN** - Large-scale collaborative research
- **Fraunhofer Institutes** - Applied research and technology transfer
- **Corporate R&D Labs**: IBM Research, Microsoft Research, Meta AI Research
- **Academic Research**: Nature, Science, Cell publication standards
- **Innovation Frameworks**: Stage-Gate, Agile Innovation, Open Innovation

---

## Domain Overview

Research & Development is the systematic creative work undertaken to:
1. **Discover new knowledge** (Basic/Fundamental Research)
2. **Apply knowledge** to create new products/processes (Applied Research)
3. **Develop prototypes** and validate concepts (Development)
4. **Transfer technology** to production and commercialization
5. **Manage innovation** as a strategic organizational capability

### Critical Success Factors
- **Rigorous methodology**: Reproducible, peer-reviewed, statistically valid
- **IP protection**: Patents, trade secrets, publication strategy
- **Collaboration**: Cross-functional, interdisciplinary, open innovation
- **Data integrity**: FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable)
- **Ethics**: IRB approval, responsible innovation, research integrity

## Core Competencies

### 1. Research Methodology Excellence

#### Experimental Research
- **Controlled experiments**: Independent/dependent variables, confounding factors
- **Statistical power analysis**: Sample size determination (G*Power, R pwr package)
- **Randomization**: Block randomization, stratified sampling
- **Blinding**: Single-blind, double-blind, triple-blind protocols
- **Replication**: Direct replication, conceptual replication

#### Observational Research
- **Cohort studies**: Prospective, retrospective analysis
- **Case-control studies**: Matching, odds ratios
- **Cross-sectional studies**: Prevalence research
- **Longitudinal studies**: Panel data analysis

#### Qualitative Research
- **Grounded theory**: Coding, theoretical saturation
- **Phenomenology**: Lived experience analysis
- **Ethnography**: Participant observation, field notes
- **Case study research**: Multiple case analysis, pattern matching

#### Mixed Methods
- **Convergent design**: Quantitative + qualitative integration
- **Explanatory sequential**: Quant → Qual explanation
- **Exploratory sequential**: Qual → Quant validation
- **Embedded design**: One method within another

**References**:
- Creswell & Creswell: "Research Design" (5th edition)
- Shadish, Cook & Campbell: "Experimental and Quasi-Experimental Designs"
- Yin: "Case Study Research" (6th edition)

### 2. Innovation Management

#### Innovation Types
- **Incremental innovation**: Continuous improvement (Kaizen, PDCA)
- **Radical innovation**: Breakthrough technology (Stage-Gate)
- **Disruptive innovation**: Clayton Christensen framework
- **Open innovation**: Chesbrough model, crowdsourcing
- **Reverse innovation**: Emerging market → developed market

#### Innovation Processes
- **Stage-Gate Process** (Cooper):
  - Discovery → Scoping → Build Business Case → Development → Testing & Validation → Launch
  - Gates with clear go/kill criteria
  - Portfolio management across stages

- **Agile Innovation** (Design Thinking + Lean Startup):
  - Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test (repeat)
  - Build-Measure-Learn loops
  - Minimum Viable Products (MVP)

- **Innovation Funnels**:
  - Wide exploration (1000 ideas)
  - Rigorous filtering (100 concepts)
  - Focused development (10 projects)
  - Commercial success (1-2 winners)

#### Innovation Metrics (RODI Framework)
- **Return on Development Investment**: Revenue from new products / R&D investment
- **Time to Market**: Concept → Commercial launch duration
- **Innovation Pipeline Health**: Balance across stages
- **Patent Quality**: Citations, forward citations, family size
- **Technology Readiness Level (TRL)**: NASA/DoD 1-9 scale

**References**:
- Cooper: "Winning at New Products" (5th edition)
- Ries: "The Lean Startup"
- Christensen: "The Innovator's Dilemma"

#### FAIR Data Principles
- **Findable**: DOIs, rich metadata, indexed repositories
- **Accessible**: Open/controlled access, authentication/authorization
- **Interoperable**: Standard formats (HDF5, netCDF), ontologies

#### Data Governance
- **Metadata standards**: Dublin Core, DataCite, domain ontologies
- **Data quality**: Completeness, accuracy, consistency, validity
- **Provenance tracking**: PROV-O ontology, workflow systems
- **Sensitive data**: Anonymization, de-identification (HIPAA Safe Harbor)

  - t-tests, ANOVA, ANCOVA

- **Bayesian methods**:
  - Prior selection, MCMC (Stan, PyMC)

- **Machine learning**:
  - Cross-validation, hyperparameter tuning

- **Sequential analysis**: Adaptive designs, interim analyses
- **Non-inferiority/equivalence**: TOST, confidence interval methods

## Research Workflow Patterns

### Pattern 1: Hypothesis-Driven Research

#### Phase 1: Literature Review & Gap Identification
1. **Systematic search**: PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, Google Scholar
2. **Screening**: PRISMA flowchart, inclusion/exclusion criteria
3. **Critical appraisal**: CASP checklists, risk of bias assessment
4. **Synthesis**: Narrative review, meta-analysis (Cochrane methods)
5. **Gap identification**: Research questions, specific aims

#### Phase 2: Hypothesis Formulation
1. **Theory grounding**: Conceptual framework, logic models
2. **Hypothesis structure**: Null (H0) vs alternative (H1)
3. **Variable definition**: Operationalization, measurement
4. **Prediction specification**: Directional, magnitude estimates

#### Phase 3: Experimental Design
1. **Design selection**: RCT, quasi-experimental, observational
2. **Power analysis**: Effect size, alpha, power, sample size
3. **Randomization**: Allocation concealment, randomization software
4. **Controls**: Positive, negative, vehicle controls

#### Phase 4: Data Collection
1. **Protocol adherence**: SOPs, training, monitoring
2. **Quality control**: Calibration, blanks, replicates
3. **Documentation**: Lab notebooks, metadata, provenance
4. **Blinding maintenance**: Code breaking procedures

#### Phase 5: Statistical Analysis
1. **Data cleaning**: Outlier detection, missing data handling
2. **Assumption testing**: Normality, homoscedasticity, independence
3. **Primary analysis**: Pre-specified in protocol/registration
4. **Secondary analysis**: Exploratory, hypothesis-generating
5. **Sensitivity analysis**: Robustness checks, alternative models

#### Phase 6: Interpretation & Dissemination
1. **Results interpretation**: Clinical/practical significance
2. **Limitation acknowledgment**: Threats to validity
3. **Manuscript preparation**: ICMJE authorship, CONSORT/STROBE reporting
4. **Peer review**: Response to reviewers, revisions
5. **Data/code sharing**: Repository deposit, supplementary materials

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] Pre-registration completed (OSF, clinicaltrials.gov)
- [ ] Statistical analysis plan finalized before data lock
- [ ] All data quality checks passed
- [ ] Results reported per guidelines (EQUATOR network)
- [ ] Data/code deposited in public repository

### Pattern 2: Exploratory Data Science Research

#### Phase 1: Data Acquisition & Understanding
1. **Data source identification**: APIs, databases, web scraping
2. **Data profiling**: Pandas-profiling, Great Expectations
3. **Schema understanding**: Data dictionaries, ERDs
4. **Legal/ethical review**: Data use agreements, IRB

#### Phase 2: Data Preparation
1. **Data cleaning**: Dedupe, missing imputation, outliers
2. **Feature engineering**: Domain knowledge, transformations
3. **Data integration**: Joins, entity resolution, harmonization
4. **Train/validation/test split**: Stratified, time-based

#### Phase 3: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
1. **Univariate analysis**: Distributions, summary statistics
2. **Bivariate analysis**: Correlations, chi-square, t-tests
3. **Multivariate analysis**: PCA, clustering, dimensionality reduction
4. **Visualization**: Matplotlib, Seaborn, Plotly, ggplot2

#### Phase 4: Model Development
1. **Baseline model**: Simple heuristics, linear models
2. **Model selection**: Cross-validation, grid search, AutoML
3. **Hyperparameter tuning**: Bayesian optimization, Optuna
4. **Ensemble methods**: Stacking, boosting, bagging

#### Phase 5: Model Evaluation & Interpretation
1. **Performance metrics**: Precision, recall, F1, AUC-ROC
2. **Validation**: k-fold CV, time series CV, nested CV
3. **Interpretability**: Feature importance, SHAP, LIME
4. **Fairness assessment**: Bias metrics, disparate impact

#### Phase 6: Reproducibility & Documentation
1. **Version control**: Git, DVC for data/models
2. **Environment**: Docker, Conda environment.yml
3. **Pipeline**: Airflow, Prefect, Kedro
4. **Documentation**: Jupyter notebooks, Sphinx docs, model cards

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] Reproducible environment (requirements.txt, Dockerfile)
- [ ] Automated pipeline from raw data to results
- [ ] Model performance exceeds baseline by meaningful margin
- [ ] Fairness metrics within acceptable thresholds
- [ ] Code peer-reviewed and documented

### Pattern 3: Innovation Sprint (Design Thinking + Lean Startup)

#### Phase 1: Empathize (Week 1)
1. **User research**: Interviews (15-20), shadowing, surveys
2. **Stakeholder mapping**: Primary, secondary, tertiary users
3. **Journey mapping**: Current state, pain points, jobs-to-be-done
4. **Synthesis**: Affinity diagrams, thematic analysis

#### Phase 2: Define (Week 1)
1. **Persona creation**: Archetypes, goals, frustrations
2. **Problem framing**: Point-of-view statements, "How Might We" questions
3. **Success metrics**: OKRs, KPIs, leading/lagging indicators
4. **Scope definition**: Must-have, nice-to-have, out-of-scope

#### Phase 3: Ideate (Week 2)
1. **Divergent thinking**: Brainstorming (100+ ideas), SCAMPER, TRIZ
2. **Convergent thinking**: Dot voting, feasibility-impact matrix
3. **Concept sketching**: Storyboards, wireframes, mockups
4. **Assumption mapping**: Leap-of-faith assumptions, riskiest first

#### Phase 4: Prototype (Week 2-3)
1. **Low-fidelity prototypes**: Paper prototypes, Wizard of Oz
2. **Medium-fidelity**: Clickable prototypes (Figma, InVision)
3. **High-fidelity**: Functional MVPs, coded prototypes
4. **Technical feasibility**: Proof-of-concept, spike solutions

#### Phase 5: Test (Week 3-4)
1. **Usability testing**: Think-aloud protocol, task success rate
2. **A/B testing**: Hypothesis, sample size, statistical significance
3. **Pilot deployment**: Limited user base, instrumentation
4. **Feedback collection**: Surveys (NPS, CSAT), interviews, analytics

#### Phase 6: Iterate or Pivot (Week 4)
1. **Validated learning**: Did we prove/disprove assumptions?
2. **Metrics review**: Are we moving success metrics?
3. **Decision**: Persevere, pivot, or perish
4. **Next sprint planning**: Prioritized backlog, refined hypotheses

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] 15+ user interviews completed
- [ ] 3+ prototype iterations tested
- [ ] Key assumptions validated with data
- [ ] MVP achieves product-market fit indicators
- [ ] Decision made with data, not opinion

### Pattern 4: Technology Readiness Advancement (TRL 3 → TRL 6)

#### TRL 3 → 4: Lab Validation
1. **Component testing**: Individual subsystems in lab environment
2. **Interface definition**: APIs, protocols, data formats
3. **Performance characterization**: Benchmarking against requirements
4. **Documentation**: Technical specifications, test reports

#### TRL 4 → 5: Relevant Environment Integration
1. **System integration**: Combine components into working system
2. **Simulated environment**: Representative conditions
3. **End-to-end testing**: Full functionality demonstration
4. **Risk mitigation**: Identified risks addressed

#### TRL 5 → 6: Relevant Environment Demonstration
1. **Pilot deployment**: Real operational environment (subset)
2. **Field testing**: Performance under actual conditions
3. **User feedback**: Operational user evaluation
4. **Reliability testing**: MTBF, fault tolerance, degradation
5. **Manufacturing readiness**: Transition to production planning

#### Documentation Requirements
- **TRL 3**: Technical specifications, lab test results
- **TRL 4**: Component test reports, integration plans
- **TRL 5**: System test reports, risk assessment
- **TRL 6**: Field test reports, user acceptance, manufacturing plan

#### Gate Reviews
- **Technical review**: Experts assess technical maturity
- **Business review**: Market, IP, competition assessment
- **Risk review**: Technical, market, IP, regulatory risks
- **Go/No-go decision**: Continue, pivot, or kill

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] All technical requirements met with margin
- [ ] Demonstrated in operationally relevant environment
- [ ] User acceptance achieved
- [ ] Manufacturing feasibility confirmed
- [ ] Business case validated (TAM, SAM, SOM)

## Quality Standards for R&D

### Research Integrity
- **Reproducibility**: Raw data, analysis code, exact software versions
- **Transparency**: Pre-registration, protocol publication, negative results
- **Ethics**: IRB/IACUC approval, informed consent, data privacy
- **Authorship**: ICMJE guidelines, CRediT taxonomy
- **Conflict of interest**: Financial disclosures, competing interests

### Data Quality (ALCOA+ Principles)
- **Attributable**: Who collected the data, when, why
- **Legible**: Clear, readable, permanent records
- **Contemporaneous**: Recorded at time of activity
- **Original**: First recording or certified copy
- **Accurate**: Error-free, verified, validated
- **Complete**: All data present, missing data explained
- **Consistent**: Chronological, sequential, no contradictions
- **Enduring**: Preserved, backup, disaster recovery
- **Available**: Retrievable throughout retention period

### Statistical Rigor
- **Pre-specification**: Analysis plan before data collection
- **Appropriate methods**: Match design, assumptions checked
- **Multiple testing correction**: Bonferroni, FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg)
- **Effect sizes**: Not just p-values, confidence intervals
- **Transparency**: All analyses reported, not just significant

### Innovation Quality
- **Novelty**: Prior art search, freedom to operate
- **Utility**: Solves real problem, value proposition
- **Feasibility**: Technical, economic, regulatory
- **Scalability**: Can it be manufactured/delivered at scale?
- **Sustainability**: Environmental, social, governance (ESG)

## Getting Started with R&D Projects

When beginning a research or innovation project:

1. **Define clear objectives**: Specific aims, hypotheses, success criteria
2. **Conduct thorough literature review**: What's known, what's novel
3. **Assess feasibility**: Technical, resources, timeline, ethics
4. **Plan rigorously**: Experimental design, statistical power, contingencies
5. **Establish workflows**: Data management, version control, collaboration
6. **Build the team**: Complementary skills, clear roles, communication norms
7. **Secure resources**: Funding, equipment, materials, access
8. **Document everything**: Protocols, data, decisions, learnings
9. **Iterate and adapt**: Agile sprints, pivot when data dictates
10. **Disseminate findings**: Publications, patents, products, presentations
