# Technology Transfer Skill

## Purpose
Translate research discoveries into commercial products through licensing, startup formation, and partnerships, navigating IP, valuation, and the "valley of death."

## Core Competencies

### Technology Readiness Levels (TRL 1-9)

**TRL 1-3: Basic Research**
- TRL 1: Basic principles observed
- TRL 2: Technology concept formulated
- TRL 3: Proof of concept (lab)
- Funding: NSF, NIH R01, ERC

**TRL 4-6: Applied Research**
- TRL 4: Lab validation
- TRL 5: Relevant environment validation
- TRL 6: Prototype demonstration
- Funding: NIH R21, SBIR Phase I/II, corporate partnerships

**TRL 7-9: Development**
- TRL 7: System prototype (operational environment)
- TRL 8: System complete and qualified
- TRL 9: Proven in operational environment
- Funding: Venture capital, corporate partnerships, SBIR Phase II

### Pathways to Market

**Pathway 1: Licensing to Existing Company**
- University TTO negotiates license
- Company develops, manufactures, sells
- University receives: Upfront fee + royalties (2-5% typical) + milestone payments
- Timeline: 1-3 years negotiation, 3-10 years to product

**Pathway 2: Startup Formation**
- License IP from university (exclusive or non-exclusive)
- Found company, recruit team
- Raise seed funding ($500k-$2M), Series A ($5-15M), Series B+ ($20-100M+)
- Exit: Acquisition (5-10 years) or IPO (rare, 10-15 years)

**Pathway 3: Strategic Partnership**
- Sponsored research agreement (SRA) with corporation
- Joint development, shared IP
- Option to license or acquire startup
- Timeline: 2-5 years, lower risk than pure startup

**Pathway 4: Consortia**
- Pre-competitive research (multiple companies)
- Shared IP, members get non-exclusive licenses
- Examples: Semiconductor Research Corporation, CERN

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Invention Disclosure (Weeks 1-4)
**Researcher**: Submit disclosure to TTO
- What: Technology description, data, prototypes
- Who: Inventors, collaborators
- When: Before ANY public disclosure (poster, paper, talk)
- Why: Public disclosure kills patentability in most countries

**TTO**: Assess commercial potential
- Prior art search (Google Patents, Espacenet)
- Market size (TAM, SAM, SOM)
- Competitive landscape
- Decision: File patent, copyright, trade secret, or publish defensively

### Phase 2: IP Protection (Months 2-18)
**Provisional Patent** (12-month window):
- Lower cost ($5k-$10k with attorneys)
- Establishes priority date
- Not examined, not published
- Must file non-provisional within 12 months

**Non-Provisional / PCT**:
- Full patent application ($15k-$30k)
- Examined by USPTO/EPO
- Published 18 months after filing
- Granted in 2-4 years (if successful)

**Patent Strategy**:
- Broad claims (cover many embodiments)
- File continuation applications (improve claims)
- File in key markets (US, EU, China, Japan)

### Phase 3: Market Assessment (Months 6-12)
**Customer Discovery** (Steve Blank / NSF I-Corps):
- Interview 100+ potential customers
- Identify: Pain, current solution, willingness to pay
- Iterate value proposition

**Business Model Canvas**:
- Value proposition: What problem solved, how much better?
- Customer segments: Who will buy first? (early adopters)
- Revenue model: License, subscription, per-use, razor/blade
- Channels: Direct sales, distributors, OEMs

**Market Sizing**:
- TAM (Total Addressable Market): All potential revenue
- SAM (Serviceable Addressable): Realistic target
- SOM (Serviceable Obtainable): 3-5 year goal

### Phase 4: Business Development (Months 12-24)
**Licensing** (if Pathway 1):
- Identify potential licensees (100+ contacts → 10 interested → 2-3 serious)
- NDA, material transfer agreement (MTA)
- Pitch deck, data package
- Negotiate term sheet (6-12 months typical)

**Startup Formation** (if Pathway 2):
- Incorporate (Delaware C-corp standard)
- Founder equity split (vesting schedules: 4 years, 1-year cliff)
- License from university (typically 5-10% equity + low royalty)
- Build founding team (CEO, CTO, CSO)

**Funding**:
- **Friends & Family**: $50k-$250k
- **Angel**: $250k-$1M
- **Seed VC**: $1M-$3M
- **Series A**: $5M-$15M (TRL 6-7, proof-of-concept in market)
- **Series B**: $20M-$50M (scale-up, revenue traction)

### Phase 5: Development & Scale-Up (Years 2-7)
**Product Development**:
- V1: Barely works (early adopters only)
- V2: Works reliably (early majority)
- V3: Feature-complete (mass market)

**Regulatory** (if applicable):
- FDA (medical devices, drugs): 510(k), PMA, IND, NDA
- EPA (chemicals, pesticides): TSCA, FIFRA
- FCC (telecommunications): Equipment authorization

**Manufacturing**:
- Prototype: Lab-scale, hand-assembled
- Pilot: 100-1,000 units, semi-automated
- Commercial: 10,000+ units, fully automated, supply chain

**Sales & Marketing**:
- Early: Direct sales, founder-led
- Growth: Sales team, channel partners
- Scale: Marketing automation, inside sales

## Valuation Methods

**Pre-Revenue (TRL 4-6)**:
- **Scorecard**: Compare to similar startups (stage, team, market, tech)
- **Berkus Method**: $0.5M each for: idea, prototype, team, strategic relationships, product rollout
- **Risk-Factor Summation**: Adjust from base value ($2M typical) by 12 risk factors

**Revenue-Generating (TRL 7-9)**:
- **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)**: NPV of future cash flows
- **Comparable Companies**: Multiples (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA)
- **Venture Capital Method**: Exit value / desired return → current value

**Example**: Biotech Series A
- Market: $5B TAM, target 5% → $250M peak sales
- Exit: 3x revenue multiple → $750M acquisition (Year 10)
- VC wants 10x return → $75M post-money valuation
- Raise $10M → VC owns 13.3%

## Success Metrics

**TTO Metrics**:
- Invention disclosures: 100-300/year (top universities)
- Patents filed: 30-50% of disclosures
- Licenses executed: 50-150/year
- Startups formed: 10-30/year
- Licensing revenue: $50M-$200M/year (MIT, Stanford, Columbia top tier)

**Startup Metrics** (SaaS example):
- **ARR** (Annual Recurring Revenue): $1M (Series A), $10M (Series B)
- **Growth**: 100%+ YoY (early), 50%+ (growth)
- **Retention**: 90%+ net revenue retention
- **CAC Payback**: <12 months
- **LTV/CAC**: >3x

## Common Pitfalls

**Valley of Death** (TRL 4-6):
- Too advanced for grants, too early for VCs
- Solutions: SBIR/STTR, angel investors, proof-of-concept funds

**IP Issues**:
- Public disclosure before filing → patentability lost
- Inventors join startup → conflict of interest
- Third-party IP (e.g., using reagents with reach-through claims)

**Team**:
- All-technical team (need business co-founder)
- Absentee founders (professors too busy)
- Founder disputes (equity, roles, vision)

**Market**:
- Technology looking for a problem
- Overestimate willingness to pay
- Ignore competitors (incumbents fight back)

## Key Terms

**Licensing Agreement**:
- **Exclusive**: One licensee (typical for startups)
- **Non-exclusive**: Multiple licensees (lower royalties)
- **Field-of-use**: Exclusive for one application, non-exclusive for others

**Term Sheet Components**:
- Upfront fee: $10k-$500k (licensee pays to get started)
- Milestone payments: $100k-$5M (tied to development stages)
- Royalties: 2-5% of net sales (varies by industry)
- Minimum annual royalties: $50k+ (or license terminates)
- Diligence: Licensee must actively develop (or return rights)

**Equity Terms**:
- **Preferred stock**: VCs get (liquidation preference, anti-dilution)
- **Vesting**: Earn equity over time (typically 4 years, 1-year cliff)
- **Option pool**: 10-20% reserved for employees
- **Valuation**: Pre-money vs post-money

## Detailed Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Biotech Startup from Academic Research

**Origin**: Discovered novel diabetes drug target at university

**Phase 1: Invention Disclosure (Months 1-3)**
- Researchers submit disclosure to TTO
- TTO assesses: Large market ($10B+), differentiated mechanism, patentable
- Decision: File provisional patent

**Phase 2: IP Protection (Months 3-18)**
- File provisional patent (establishes priority)
- Conduct freedom-to-operate (FTO) analysis: Identified 3 existing patents but licenses available
- File non-provisional US patent + PCT (international)
- Cost: $50k total

**Phase 3: Market Assessment (Months 6-12)**
- NSF I-Corps program (free 7-week training)
- 100+ customer interviews: Pharma companies, clinicians, patients
- Key finding: $500M market for new diabetes treatments
- Value prop: First-in-class mechanism, better safety profile

**Phase 4: Company Formation (Months 12-18)**
- Incorporate in Delaware as C-corp
- Founder team: Lead researcher (CEO), postdoc (CTO), MBA (COO)
- License from university: Exclusive license, 5% equity + 2% royalties
- Seed funding: Friends & family raise $1M

**Phase 5: Development (Years 1-5)**
- Year 1: Lead compound identification, in-vitro testing
- Year 2: IND-enabling studies (FDA good laboratory practice)
- Year 3: IND Application filed; Phase I clinical trial (safety, dosing)
- Year 4: Phase II trial (efficacy in diabetes patients)
- Year 5: Series B fundraising ($15M) based on positive Phase II results

**Outcome**:
- Acquisition by major pharma ($250M + $100M milestones) in Year 7
- University receives: 5% equity stake ($17.5M) + milestone royalties
- Founders: $50M+ personal wealth

### Case Study 2: Materials Science Tech to Startup

**Origin**: Novel battery chemistry developed in university lab

**Challenges**:
- Technology at TRL 4 (lab validation)
- Manufacturing scalability unknown
- Significant development investment needed ($5M+)

**Solution Path: Strategic Partnership**
- Year 1: Partner with large energy company (Pathway 3)
- Partnership agreement: Co-develop, joint IP (cross-license)
- Funding: $2M from partner over 2 years
- Researchers: Dedicate 50% time (other 50% remains at university)
- Timeline: 3-year partnership, then decide exclusive licensing

**Benefits**:
- De-risks technology (partner has manufacturing expertise)
- Researchers maintain academic positions
- University retains co-ownership
- Clear path to commercialization

## Deep Dive: Valuation Methods

### Scorecard Method (Pre-Revenue)
**Used when**: Early TRL (3-5), no revenue, minimal data

**Comparable companies grid**:
```
Factor             Weight  Company A  Company B  Avg
Stage              20%     TRL 4      TRL 4      TRL 4
Team               25%     Experienced Experienced
Market             20%     $5B TAM    $2B TAM    $3.5B
Product            20%     High risk  Med risk   Med-High
IP                 15%     1 patent   3 patents
Total              100%
```

**Scoring**:
- Assign score 0-10 for each company
- Weight factors
- Weighted score for target company
- Benchmark: Similar stage startups valued at $5M
- Target score of 7.5 → Valuation $6.5M (adjusted for local market)

### Berkus Method
**Used when**: Pre-revenue, some evidence of feasibility

Formula: Each element worth $500k-$1M
1. **Idea** ($500k): Unique, solvable problem
2. **Prototype** ($500k): Working proof of concept
3. **Team** ($500k): Experienced, complementary skills
4. **Strategic relationships** ($500k): Key customers, partners interested
5. **Product rollout** ($500k): Go-to-market plan, pilot customer

Total possible: $2.5M + add $500k-$3M for market traction

**Example**:
- Idea: $1M (strong, large market)
- Prototype: $500k (working but rough)
- Team: $500k (first-time entrepreneur, hired experienced COO)
- Relationships: $750k (letters of interest from 3 potential customers)
- Rollout: $250k (basic plan)
- **Total base**: $3.5M

## Funding Strategy

### Friends & Family ($50k-$500k)
**Timeline**: 2-4 weeks
**Investors**: Personal network, angel investors
**Structure**: Often simple notes or SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)
**Terms**: Light on governance, flexible terms
**Pitch**: "Help us get MVP working + early customer validation"

### Angel Investors ($250k-$1M)
**Timeline**: 4-12 weeks
**Investors**: Wealthy individuals, often with industry experience
**Structure**: Preferred equity, convertible notes
**Terms**: Equity stake (5-10%), seat on board, governance rights
**Value beyond capital**: Mentorship, connections, customer introductions
**Pitch**: "We've validated product-market fit; now scaling"

### Seed VC ($1M-$3M)
**Timeline**: 8-16 weeks
**Investors**: Seed-stage venture funds
**Structure**: Standard Series Seed equity round
**Terms**:
- Post-money valuation: $4M-$10M (for $1-2M raise)
- Investor equity: 10-25%
- Board seat, preference rights, anti-dilution
**Pitch**: "Proven team, clear market, path to Series A"
**Typical metrics**:
- Early product traction (100+ users, $10k MRR)
- Founder commitment (leaving day job)
- Experienced advisor/mentor

### Series A ($5M-$15M)
**Timeline**: 12-20 weeks
**Investors**: Growth-stage venture funds
**Typical at**: Years 1-2 post-seed
**Metrics investors want**:
- **Revenue**: $500k-$2M ARR
- **Growth**: 5-10% month-over-month
- **Customer acquisition**: Clear path to profitable CAC
- **Retention**: 90%+ net revenue retention
- **Team**: Experienced VP Sales, CFO on board or hired
**Valuation**: Typically 3-5x seed valuation
**New capital raised**: $5-15M (founder dilution: ~25%)

## Regulatory Approval Pathways

### FDA Device Approval (US)

**510(k) - Predicate Device**:
- **Timeline**: 60 days to decision
- **Requirements**: Substantially equivalent to existing cleared device
- **Cost**: $10k-$100k
- **Risk**: Fastest, lowest cost path
- **Example**: New blood pressure monitor similar to existing ones

**PMA - Premarket Approval**:
- **Timeline**: 180 days-2 years
- **Requirements**: Proven safety & efficacy (clinical data)
- **Cost**: $100k-$1M+
- **Risk**: Rigorous, comprehensive review
- **Example**: Novel surgical robot; first-in-kind diagnostic

### IND Application (Investigational New Drug)
**For clinical trials in humans**

Timeline: 30 days FDA review (IND becomes effective if no objections)
Submission includes:
- Chemistry/manufacturing info
- Pharmacology/toxicology data
- Previous human experience
- Protocol for proposed studies

Cost: $50k-$200k for IND application

### NDA / BLA Approval
**NDA** (New Drug Application):
- Timeline: 10 months standard review, 6 months priority
- Requirements: Complete Phase I/II/III data, manufacturing info, labeling
- Cost: $1M+ (clinical trials are 80% of cost)

## Go-to-Market Strategies by Product Type

### B2B SaaS
**Customer journey**:
1. Problem: Recognize need
2. Awareness: Webinar, blog, conference
3. Consideration: Free trial, demo, case study
4. Purchase: Sales call, negotiation
5. Adoption: Onboarding, training

**Sales model**: Self-serve (SMB) + Sales-assisted (enterprise)
**CAC**: $1k-$20k (varies by segment)
**Payback period**: 6-12 months
**Key metric**: Net revenue retention >120%

### Hardware / Physical Product
**Customer journey**:
1. Retail shelf presence OR online store
2. Reviews, demonstrations, expert endorsement
3. Limited availability initially (exclusivity)
4. Gradual channel expansion

**Distribution**: Direct (store, website) + Retail (Best Buy, Amazon) + OEM
**CAC**: $100-$1k per unit
**Key metric**: Return rate <5%, warranty claims <2%

### Licensed Technology
**Customer journey**:
1. Identify licensee (pharma company, manufacturer)
2. NDA + data package review
3. Negotiation (royalties, territories, milestones)
4. Diligence (6-12 months)
5. Closing + ongoing royalty tracking

**Term**: 5-15 years typically
**Upfront fee**: $10k-$500k
**Royalties**: 2-8% of net sales
**Minimum royalties**: Ensure licensee commercializes

## Intellectual Property Strategy

### Patent Filing Strategy

**Domestic vs International**:
- US only: ~$15k (if not planning international)
- US + PCT (65 countries): ~$30k-$50k upfront
- US + selective countries (EU, China, Japan): ~$50k-$100k
- Cost to maintain patent: $500-$2k/year (per patent)

**Patent lifecycle decision points**:
- Year 1.5: Convert provisional to non-provisional (commit to cost)
- Year 2.5: Decide international filing (PCT deadline)
- Year 5-7: Assess commercial potential; decide which countries to maintain
- Year 20: Patent expires; assess evergreen strategy (continuation patents)

### Trade Secrets vs Patents
**Patent**: Public (published 18 months after filing), protected 20 years
**Trade secret**: Secret (not published), protected indefinitely if kept confidential

**Tradeoffs**:
- Use patent if: Competitive advantage expires <20 years, plan to license, want to block competitors
- Use trade secret if: Can keep confidential forever, want to prevent reverse engineering
- Use both: File patent for core + keep implementation details secret

## Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Overestimating Market Size
**Problem**: Tech is cool but no real customer need
**Solution**: Talk to 50+ potential customers before company formation
**Question**: "Would you pay $X for this?" not "Is this a cool idea?"

### Mistake 2: Underestimating Development Cost
**Problem**: "Prototype works in lab; should scale in 6 months" (actually takes 3 years)
**Solution**:
- Get experienced manufacturing partner advice upfront
- Add 50% contingency to cost estimates
- Track actual costs vs. forecast

### Mistake 3: Weak IP
**Problem**: Third-party patent blocks commercialization
**Solution**:
- Conduct FTO (freedom-to-operate) analysis early
- Identify licensing options (what if we need to cross-license?)
- Monitor competing patents during development

### Mistake 4: Choosing Wrong Licensing Partner
**Problem**: Licensee has no experience in market; product dies
**Solution**:
- Require minimum development milestones with penalties
- Include diligence clause: Must actively pursue (or rights revert)
- Track early commercialization metrics
