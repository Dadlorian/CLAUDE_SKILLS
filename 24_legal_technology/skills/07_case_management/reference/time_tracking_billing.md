# Time Tracking and Billing in Legal Practice

## Overview

Time tracking and billing are fundamental to law firm economics. Effective time capture, accurate billing, and efficient collection processes directly impact firm profitability and cash flow. This reference covers time tracking methods, billing practices, technology solutions, and best practices for optimizing the billing and collection cycle.

## Time Tracking Fundamentals

### Billing Models

#### Hourly Billing
**Standard Hourly**
- Attorney/staff bill at hourly rate
- Time recorded in increments (0.1, 0.25, 0.5 hour)
- Billable rate × hours = fee
- Most common billing model

**Blended Rates**
- Single rate for all timekeepers on matter
- Simplifies billing for client
- Firm manages resource allocation for profitability

**Volume Discounts**
- Reduced rates for high-volume clients
- Tiered pricing based on annual spend
- Incentivizes increased work

#### Alternative Fee Arrangements (AFAs)
**Flat/Fixed Fees**
- Single fee for defined scope of work
- Predictable cost for client
- Firm bears risk of scope creep
- Common for routine matters (incorporation, will, simple contract review)

**Contingency Fees**
- Fee is percentage of recovery
- No recovery = no fee (except costs)
- Common in personal injury, class action
- Percentage varies (20-40% typical)
- May have tiered structure (increases if case goes to trial)

**Capped Fees**
- Hourly billing up to maximum amount
- Client protected from runaway costs
- Firm incentivized to be efficient

**Collars**
- Minimum and maximum fee
- Protects both client and firm
- Shares risk

**Success Fees/Bonuses**
- Base fee plus bonus for successful outcome
- Incentive alignment
- Complex to structure

**Retainer Models**
- **Security Retainer**: Held in trust, applied to invoices
- **Evergreen Retainer**: Replenished as used
- **True Retainer**: Fee for availability, non-refundable
- **Monthly Retainer**: Fixed monthly fee for ongoing services

**Subscription/Membership**
- Regular monthly fee for defined services
- Predictable revenue for firm
- Increased access for client
- Growing model for small firms

### Time Capture Methods

#### Manual Time Entry
**Description**
- Attorney/staff enter time after activity
- Narrative description of work
- Matter association and task code

**Advantages**
- Complete control over description
- Thoughtful time entry
- No technology overhead

**Disadvantages**
- Relies on memory (inaccurate)
- Time leakage (forgotten entries)
- Procrastination (batch entry later)
- Interrupts workflow

#### Timer-Based Entry
**Running Timers**
- Start timer when beginning activity
- Stop timer when complete
- Automatic time capture
- Add description and codes after

**Multiple Simultaneous Timers**
- Track multiple matters concurrently
- Switch between matters easily
- Separate timers for phone calls, emails, drafting

**Advantages**
- Accurate time capture
- No reliance on memory
- Captures all time (less leakage)
- Real-time recording

**Disadvantages**
- Requires remembering to start/stop timer
- Interruptions may distort time
- Administrative overhead (starting/stopping)
- Multiple timers can be confusing

#### Automated/Activity-Based Tracking
**Document-Based**
- Tracks time spent in documents
- Auto-associates with matter (from document metadata)
- Generates time entry automatically
- Smokeball is primary example

**Calendar-Based**
- Converts calendar appointments to time entries
- Uses appointment duration
- Auto-generates narrative from appointment title
- Requires manual review and confirmation

**Email-Based**
- Tracks time reading and writing emails
- Suggests matter association
- Can auto-generate time entries
- Requires rules to determine billing vs. non-billing

**Activity Monitoring**
- Software tracks all computer activities
- AI/ML suggests time entries
- Captures context (documents, emails, websites)
- Carpe Diem is leading example

**Advantages**
- Minimal user effort
- Comprehensive time capture
- No time leakage
- Captures forgotten time

**Disadvantages**
- Privacy concerns (monitoring)
- Requires review and cleanup
- May capture non-billable time
- Initial setup and training

#### Mobile Time Entry
**Mobile App Features**
- Timers on smartphone
- Voice-to-text time entry
- Quick entry templates
- Offline entry with sync

**Benefits**
- Capture time anywhere
- Immediate entry (court, travel, client site)
- Voice entry while driving
- Calendar integration

#### Bulk/Batch Time Entry
- Enter multiple time entries at once
- Useful for reconstructing day
- Copy/paste similar entries
- Risky (memory-dependent)

### Time Entry Components

#### Required Information
1. **Date**: Date service performed
2. **Timekeeper**: Attorney or staff member
3. **Matter**: Client and matter
4. **Hours**: Time spent (in firm's increment)
5. **Description**: Narrative of work performed
6. **Task Code**: Categorization of activity (UTBMS or firm codes)

#### Optional Information
- **Activity Code**: Detailed LEDES task code
- **Expense Code**: Associated expense category
- **Billing Status**: Billable, non-billable, no-charge
- **Approval Status**: Draft, submitted, approved
- **Rate Override**: Different rate than default

### Time Rounding and Increments

#### Common Increment Schemes
- **0.1 hour (6 minutes)**: Most common, precise
- **0.25 hour (15 minutes)**: Common for smaller firms
- **0.5 hour (30 minutes)**: Less common, less precise
- **Variable**: Different increment for different activities

#### Rounding Rules
- **Round Up**: Always round partial increment up (favors firm)
- **Round to Nearest**: Round to nearest increment (fair)
- **Actual Time**: Bill exact time in smallest increment (most accurate)
- **Minimum Time**: Minimum charge (e.g., 0.3 hours for any entry)

#### Ethical Considerations
- Must be reasonable and explained to client
- Disclose rounding practices
- Don't "pad" time
- Must reflect actual time spent
- Consider ABA Model Rule 1.5 (reasonable fees)

## Billing Practices

### Work-in-Progress (WIP) Management

#### WIP Defined
- Unbilled time entries and expenses
- Recorded but not yet invoiced
- Asset on firm's books (accounts receivable once billed)

#### WIP Tracking
- **WIP by Attorney**: Individual timekeeper WIP
- **WIP by Matter**: All WIP on specific matter
- **WIP by Client**: All WIP across client's matters
- **Aged WIP**: Time since entry recorded
  - Current (0-30 days)
  - 31-60 days
  - 61-90 days
  - Over 90 days (stale WIP, less likely to collect)

#### WIP Problems
- **Stale WIP**: Old unbilled time (over 90 days)
  - Client may dispute
  - Difficult to remember/justify
  - Lower collection rate
- **WIP Write-Offs**: Uncollectible WIP
  - Client disputes
  - Excessive time
  - Unrecoverable work
- **Locked WIP**: Prevent changes after billing

### Pre-Bill Review Process

#### Purpose
- Review time for accuracy and appropriateness
- Edit narratives for clarity and client consumption
- Apply write-downs or adjustments
- Ensure compliance with client billing guidelines
- Check for billing errors or duplicates

#### Pre-Bill Generation
- System generates draft invoice from WIP
- Includes all unbilled time and expenses
- Organized by timekeeper, task, or chronologically
- Shows proposed charges

#### Review Steps
1. **Accuracy Check**
   - Verify all time entries correct
   - Check for duplicates
   - Confirm task codes accurate
   - Ensure expense amounts correct

2. **Appropriateness Review**
   - Excessive time for task?
   - Duplicative work by multiple attorneys?
   - Tasks that should be written down?
   - Internal/administrative time billed?

3. **Narrative Review**
   - Clear and understandable?
   - Confidentiality protected (no privileged details)?
   - Professional tone?
   - Compliant with client guidelines (block billing limits)?

4. **Rate Review**
   - Correct rates applied?
   - Client-specific rates honored?
   - Discount agreements applied?

5. **Write-Downs and Adjustments**
   - Reduce excessive time
   - Adjust rates if needed
   - Apply courtesy discounts
   - Bundle similar tasks

6. **Client Guidelines Compliance**
   - LEDES format requirements
   - Task code requirements (UTBMS)
   - Rate caps
   - Block billing prohibitions
   - Required approval for expenses over threshold

7. **Final Approval**
   - Partner approval
   - Client approval (if required)
   - Authorization to generate final invoice

### Invoice Generation

#### Invoice Components
**Header Information**
- Firm name and logo
- Firm address and contact information
- Invoice number (unique)
- Invoice date
- Client name and billing address
- Matter name and number
- Billing period
- Payment terms
- Remittance instructions

**Fee Detail**
- Time entries (by attorney, date, description, hours, rate, amount)
- Expenses (description, amount)
- Organized by timekeeper or chronologically
- Subtotals by timekeeper or task

**Summary**
- Total fees
- Total expenses
- Previous balance (if any)
- Payments received
- Current amount due
- Total amount due (including previous balance)
- Retainer application (if applicable)

**Footer**
- Payment instructions
- Bank account information (for wire transfer)
- Credit card payment option
- Thank you message
- Contact for questions

#### Invoice Formats

**Standard Invoice**
- Firm's custom template
- Branded with firm logo and colors
- Detailed time entries
- Organized for readability

**LEDES Invoice (Electronic Billing)**
- **LEDES 1998B**: Standard e-billing format (most common)
- **LEDES 2000**: Enhanced format with more fields
- **LEDES 2006**: Includes legal coding (UTBMS)
- Text file format (pipe-delimited or tab-delimited)
- Submitted to corporate client's e-billing platform

**Client-Specific Format**
- Corporate clients may require specific format
- Outside counsel guidelines specify requirements
- May require specific grouping or detail level

### Electronic Billing (E-Billing)

#### E-Billing Platforms
**Common Platforms**
- **Collaborati (LawManager)**: Largest platform
- **Serengeti Tracker**: Thomson Reuters platform
- **Allegient**: TyMetrix (Wolters Kluwer)
- **Brightflag**: AI-powered platform
- **SimpleLegal**: Modern platform for in-house teams
- **Legal Tracker**: Thomson Reuters
- **Onit BillBlast**: Enterprise platform

#### E-Billing Process
1. Generate LEDES file from practice management system
2. Upload to e-billing platform
3. Platform validates invoice (rate caps, task codes, guidelines)
4. Address any validation failures (resubmit)
5. Client reviews invoice in platform
6. Client approves or requests adjustments
7. Resubmit adjusted invoice if needed
8. Await payment through platform

#### UTBMS Codes (Uniform Task-Based Management System)
**Task Codes**
- Standardized codes for legal activities
- Example: L300 (Legal Research)
- Required by many corporate clients
- Different code sets for different practice areas (litigation, transactions, etc.)

**Expense Codes**
- Standardized expense categories
- Example: E101 (Court Fees)
- Facilitates expense tracking and analysis

**Activity Codes**
- More granular than task codes
- Example: L310 (Research-statutes/regulations)

### Payment Processing

#### Payment Methods
**Check**
- Traditional payment method
- Requires deposit and posting
- Slower than electronic

**Credit Card**
- Fast processing (2-3 business days to bank)
- Convenience fee (2.5-3.5% typically)
- PCI compliance required
- LawPay, AffiniPay most common processors

**ACH/eCheck**
- Electronic bank transfer
- Lower fees than credit card (0.5-1%)
- Takes 3-5 business days
- Requires bank account information

**Wire Transfer**
- Immediate transfer
- Used for large payments
- Bank fees apply
- Requires providing wire instructions

**Online Payment Portal**
- Client portal payment option
- Credit card or ACH
- Convenient for client
- Automated posting to account

**Payment Plans**
- Installment payments
- Automated recurring charges
- Reduces collections burden

#### Trust Account Application
- Payment held in trust applies to invoices
- System applies payment to oldest invoices first (or per client instruction)
- Generates trust to operating transfer
- Updates trust ledger

#### Payment Application
- Post payment to client account
- Apply to specific invoices
- Handle partial payments
- Generate receipt
- Update accounts receivable

### Collections

#### Collections Process
**Payment Reminders**
- First reminder: 15-30 days after invoice
- Second reminder: 45-60 days
- Final reminder: 75-90 days
- Increasingly firm tone

**Collection Calls**
- Personal outreach to client
- Discuss payment issues
- Negotiate payment plan if needed
- Address any billing disputes

**Collections Actions**
- Suspend work on matter
- Cease representation (if ethically permissible)
- Collections attorney/agency
- Lawsuit for fees (rare)
- Fee arbitration (some jurisdictions)

#### AR Aging
**Age Buckets**
- Current (0-30 days)
- 31-60 days
- 61-90 days
- 91-120 days
- Over 120 days (highest risk)

**Metrics**
- Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)
- Collection rate (collected/billed)
- Write-off rate
- AR turnover

## Best Practices

### Time Entry Best Practices
1. **Enter Time Daily**: Don't wait until end of week/month
2. **Be Specific**: Descriptive narratives, avoid "research" or "review file"
3. **Contemporaneous Entry**: Enter time as activity occurs or immediately after
4. **Accurate Time**: Record actual time spent, don't inflate or round excessively
5. **Proper Coding**: Use correct matter, task codes
6. **Review Before Submitting**: Check for errors before finalizing

### Billing Best Practices
1. **Regular Billing Cycles**: Bill monthly or more frequently
2. **Prompt Billing**: Don't let WIP age beyond 60 days
3. **Thorough Pre-Bill Review**: Catch issues before client sees invoice
4. **Clear Narratives**: Client should understand value received
5. **Itemize Appropriately**: Balance detail with readability (avoid excessive block billing)
6. **Comply with Guidelines**: Honor client billing requirements
7. **Transparent Pricing**: Discuss fees upfront, provide estimates

### Collection Best Practices
1. **Retainers**: Require retainer for new clients
2. **Evergreen Retainers**: Replenish as used to maintain cushion
3. **Payment Terms**: Net 15 or Net 30, clearly stated
4. **Early Follow-Up**: Contact client at 30 days if unpaid
5. **Payment Plans**: Offer installment options before collection
6. **Suspend Work**: Don't continue working if significantly past due
7. **Write-Off Promptly**: Write off truly uncollectible to clean up books

## Metrics and KPIs

### Time Tracking Metrics
- **Billable Hours Target**: Annual or monthly billable hour goals
- **Billable Percentage**: Billable hours / total hours
- **Utilization Rate**: Billable hours / available hours
- **Time Entry Timeliness**: Same-day entry rate
- **Time Leakage**: Estimated lost billable time

### Billing Metrics
- **Realization Rate**: Billed / recorded time (measures write-downs)
- **Collection Realization**: Collected / billed (measures collection effectiveness)
- **Total Realization**: Collected / recorded time (overall effectiveness)
- **Billing Cycle Time**: Days from period end to invoice sent
- **WIP Aging**: Average age of unbilled time

### Financial Metrics
- **Revenue per Lawyer**: Total revenue / number of lawyers
- **Effective Rate**: Total fees / total hours
- **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)**: Average days to collect payment
- **AR Turnover**: Annual revenue / average AR balance
- **Write-Off Rate**: Write-offs / gross billings

## Ethical Considerations

### ABA Model Rule 1.5 - Fees
**Reasonable Fees**
- Factors: time, labor, novelty, skill required, results obtained, experience, relationship
- Fee agreements should be reasonable
- Communicate basis of fee (hourly, flat, contingency)

**Written Fee Agreements**
- Scope of representation
- Basis of fee
- Expenses
- Required in some states, best practice everywhere

**Contingent Fees**
- Must be in writing
- State percentage and how calculated
- Expenses paid by client or firm
- Cannot be used in criminal or some family law matters

**Fee Splitting**
- Can split fees with another lawyer outside firm only if client consents and division proportional to services or lawyers assume joint responsibility

### Billing Ethics
- **No Padding**: Cannot bill for time not actually spent
- **No Double Billing**: Cannot bill multiple clients for same time
- **No Task Inflation**: Describe tasks accurately
- **Reasonable Rates**: Rates must be reasonable for market and experience
- **Expense Markup**: Disclose any markup on expenses to client
