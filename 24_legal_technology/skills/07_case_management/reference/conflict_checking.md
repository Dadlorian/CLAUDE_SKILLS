# Conflict of Interest Checking

## Overview
Conflict of interest checking is a critical compliance function in law firm case management. Proper conflict checking prevents ethical violations, protects client confidentiality, and mitigates legal liability.

## Types of Conflicts

### Direct Conflicts
- **Adverse Party Conflict**: Firm represents party A against party B; client requests representation against party A
- **Opposing Counsel Conflict**: Firm's attorney previously represented opposing counsel's client
- **Related Party Conflict**: Potential conflict due to family relationships or business interests

### Indirect Conflicts
- **Related Matter Conflict**: Client involved in matter related to existing client matter
- **Successive Conflict**: Former client adverse to prospective client
- **Issue Conflict**: Same issue or similar legal question in concurrent matters

### Imputed Conflicts
- **Firm-Wide Conflict**: One attorney has conflict; entire firm may be imputed
- **Screening Issues**: Limited screening available in some circumstances
- **Lateral Hire Conflicts**: New attorney from other firm brings conflicts

## Conflict Checking Process

### Pre-Engagement Conflict Check
**Timing**: Before engagement letter issuance
**Responsible Party**: Office manager or managing partner

#### Process Steps
1. **Party Information Collection**: Identify client, adverse parties, related parties
2. **Database Search**: Search existing client database
3. **Matter Search**: Search existing matter database
4. **Attorney Search**: Review attorney calendars and personal client lists
5. **Related Party Search**: Identify business relationships and family connections
6. **Document Review**: Review engagement letter and related documents
7. **Conflict Determination**: Make conflict/no-conflict determination
8. **Documentation**: Record conflict check in matter file
9. **Escalation**: Escalate to managing partner if unclear
10. **Approval**: Obtain approval before engagement letter issuance

### Periodic Conflict Review
**Timing**: Ongoing during matter; quarterly comprehensive review
**Responsible Party**: Conflict coordinator or managing partner

#### Review Activities
- New party identification
- Adverse party changes
- Related matter emergence
- Attorney staffing changes
- Client relationship changes

### Ongoing Monitoring
**Trigger Events**:
- New parties join litigation
- Settlement or dismissal of related matters
- New attorney joins firm
- Attorney leaves firm
- Client staff changes
- Opposing counsel changes

## ABA Model Rules & Conflict Standards

### ABA Model Rule 1.7 - Concurrent Conflicts
**Current Client Conflict**: Representation of one client directly adverse to another client in same or related matter

**Exception**: May represent if:
1. Lawyer reasonably believes representation will not be adversely affected
2. Clients give informed consent
3. Confirmation in writing

### ABA Model Rule 1.9 - Successive Conflicts
**Former Client Conflict**: Cannot represent new client against former client if:
1. Same or substantially related matter, and
2. Lawyer gained confidential information from former client

### ABA Model Rule 1.10 - Imputation of Conflicts
**Firm-Wide Imputation**: Conflict of one lawyer generally imputed to all lawyers in firm
**Exceptions**: 
- With proper screening procedures
- With former client consent
- Limited circumstances (financial interest, property interest)

### ABA Model Rule 1.6 - Confidentiality
Even after matter conclusion, must maintain client confidentiality and cannot use confidential information against former client

## State-Specific Variations

### Multiple Representation Rules
- **ABA Standard**: Generally prohibited without consent
- **State Variations**: Some states permit with greater restrictions
- **Check Local Rules**: Verify state bar rules for practice jurisdiction

### Screening Procedures
- **ABA Model Rule 1.10(a)**: Screening may be effective in some circumstances
- **State Variations**: Some states prohibit screening for direct conflicts
- **Documentation**: Proper screening documentation essential

### Trust Accounting Conflicts
- **Funds Management**: Cannot commingle client funds
- **Disputed Fees**: Special handling of disputed fee disputes
- **Lien Issues**: Consideration of attorney liens and property rights

## Conflict Checking Procedures

### Standard Operating Procedure

#### 1. Initial Conflict Check
- Client name and all variations (nicknames, DBA, corporate entities)
- Adverse parties
- Related parties (business partners, family members)
- Jurisdiction and venue
- Related matters or issues

#### 2. Search Strategy
- Search by client name (exact and similar)
- Search by adverse party name
- Search by phone number and email
- Search by business address
- Search by case name (litigation)
- Search by related parties

#### 3. Review Standards
- False positives (similar names, common names)
- Time-gap analysis (matters closed years ago)
- Business relationship analysis (no conflict if both party to contract, etc.)
- Lawyer-specific analysis (only certain lawyers affected)

#### 4. Documentation
- Record of who performed check
- Date and time of check
- Search terms used
- Results found
- Conflict determination
- Any exceptions or waivers
- Approval authority

#### 5. Resolution
- **No Conflict**: Document and proceed
- **Potential Conflict**: Further investigation
- **Confirmed Conflict**: Decline engagement or obtain consent
- **Imputed Conflict**: Determine scope and screening options

## Managing Conflicts

### Conflict Waiver Procedures
**When Applicable**: Both current clients, informed consent, not prohibited by law

**Process**:
1. Full disclosure to all parties
2. Explanation of potential implications
3. Opportunity for separate counsel review
4. Written informed consent from all parties
5. Limited engagement (may need termination later)
6. Ongoing monitoring for scope changes

### Chinese Wall Screening
**When Applicable**: Successive conflicts, limited circumstances
**Requirements**:
- Physical separation of files
- Limited communication between attorneys
- Restricted access to confidential information
- Written screening procedures
- Documentation of screening
- Regular monitoring

### Conflict Resolution Options
1. **Decline Engagement**: Simplest option
2. **Refer Client**: Recommend alternative counsel
3. **Obtain Waiver**: With full informed consent
4. **Withdraw**: If conflict emerges during representation
5. **Limited Engagement**: Engagement that avoids conflict issue

## Common Mistakes

1. **Incomplete Database**: Missing parties or adverse party information
2. **Limited Search**: Only searching obvious party names
3. **Inadequate Training**: Staff unfamiliar with conflict procedures
4. **Skip the Process**: Rushing conflict check due to time pressure
5. **Poor Documentation**: Not documenting conflict check
6. **Scope Creep**: Matter expands to new parties without conflict re-check
7. **Name Confusion**: Not searching related entities or business names
8. **Inadequate Screening**: Poor screening procedures
9. **Retention Issues**: Discarding old conflict records
10. **One-Time Check**: Not monitoring for emerging conflicts
