# Trust Accounting & Client Funds Management

## Overview
Trust accounting ensures proper management of client funds, compliance with ethical rules, and prevention of commingling. All law firms holding client funds must maintain separate trust accounts and comply with state bar requirements.

## Legal Framework

### ABA Model Rule 1.15 - Safekeeping Property
**Key Requirements**:
- Trust account for client funds
- Separate from operating account
- Promptly deliver client funds
- Account accounting for funds
- Regular reconciliation
- Identification of deposits
- Protection against commingling

### State Bar Requirements
**Varies by Jurisdiction**:
- Trust account establishment rules
- Minimum balance requirements
- Interest on Lawyer Trust Account (IOLTA)
- Audit and reporting requirements
- Quarterly reconciliation
- Trust account name requirements
- Authorized signatories
- Investment restrictions

### Common State Requirements
1. **Account Naming**: "Law Firm Name - Client Trust Account"
2. **IOLTA Participation**: Interest accrues to IOLTA program
3. **Quarterly Reconciliation**: Monthly or quarterly bank reconciliation
4. **Annual Audit**: Annual audit by independent accountant
5. **Records Retention**: 5-7 year retention of records
6. **Overdraft Protection**: Prohibition on overdraft protection
7. **Interest**: Interest goes to IOLTA program

## Trust Account Structure

### Types of Trust Accounts

#### General Client Trust Account
- Holds general client funds
- Short-term deposits for billing
- Settlement funds
- Retainers
- Advance costs

#### Specific Trust Accounts
- Client-specific trust accounts
- Used for large settlements or specific matters
- Separate accounting for each account
- Required if requested by client
- Used for long-term client funds

#### IOLTA Account
- Interest-bearing trust account
- Interest goes to IOLTA program (legal services provider)
- Used for small client balances
- Improves return on small balances
- Reduces number of non-interest accounts

### Account Setup
**Requirements**:
- Designated bank account
- Trust account designation in account name
- Authorized signatories (typically multiple)
- Fee structure (avoid fees if possible)
- Check order for deposits and withdrawals
- Separate accounting ledger

## Trust Fund Management

### Deposit Procedures
1. **Identification**: Identify source and client
2. **Recording**: Record in trust ledger
3. **Deposit Slip**: Record check number or deposit identification
4. **Timely Deposit**: Deposit within 1-3 business days
5. **Segregation**: Maintain separate records per client
6. **Documentation**: Keep all supporting documentation

### Withdrawal Procedures
1. **Authorization**: Verify billing and withdrawal authority
2. **Ledger Entry**: Record withdrawal in trust ledger
3. **Check**: Issue check with clear memo
4. **Documentation**: Supporting documentation for withdrawal
5. **Accounting**: Record in client account
6. **Reconciliation**: Verify against ledger

### Client Fund Tracking
**Per-Client Ledger**:
- Client name
- Matter reference
- Deposit date and amount
- Withdrawal date and amount
- Current balance
- Supporting documentation

**Tracking Requirements**:
- Separate entry for each transaction
- Daily/weekly updates
- Monthly reconciliation
- Client statement generation
- Interest allocation

## Accounting Methods

### Cash Basis Accounting
**Method**: Record transactions when money moves
**Use**: Most law firms use cash basis
**Advantages**:
- Simple and straightforward
- Easy reconciliation
- Clear cash position
- Required by many state bars

**Limitations**:
- May not reflect true financial position
- Timing issues between work and billing

### Accrual Basis Accounting
**Method**: Record transactions when earned/incurred
**Use**: Some larger firms and accounting departments
**Advantages**:
- More accurate financial picture
- Better matching of income and expenses
- Useful for financial planning

**Limitations**:
- More complex accounting
- Requires professional accounting help
- May not reflect cash position

## Reconciliation & Compliance

### Monthly Bank Reconciliation
**Process**:
1. Obtain bank statement
2. Compare to ledger
3. Identify outstanding items
4. Verify all deposits recorded
5. Verify all checks cleared
6. Identify discrepancies
7. Make reconciling adjustments
8. Document reconciliation

**Key Items to Check**:
- All deposits recorded in ledger
- All checks cleared at bank
- No outstanding checks over 30 days
- Interest credited properly
- Bank fees recorded
- No unusual transactions
- Ledger balance = bank balance

### Quarterly Reconciliation
**Enhanced Review**:
1. Complete monthly reconciliation
2. Compare ledger to client statements
3. Verify interest allocation
4. Review for unusual transactions
5. Confirm all accounts reconcile
6. Document and retain records
7. File compliance report if required

### Annual Audit
**Required in Many Jurisdictions**:
- CPA audit of trust accounts
- Verification of compliance
- Confirmation of fund safeguarding
- Documentation of reconciliation
- Verification of IOLTA compliance
- Report to state bar

**Audit Scope**:
- Account balances and transactions
- Client ledger accuracy
- Reconciliation procedures
- Withdrawal authorization
- Interest accounting
- IOLTA compliance

## Billing Integration

### Trust to Operating Transfer
**Process**:
1. Time entry and expense recording
2. Invoice generation
3. Retainer application (if applicable)
4. Invoice issuance
5. Client payment or trust withdrawal
6. Reconciliation and accounting

**Documentation**:
- Invoice tied to trust withdrawal
- Trust withdrawal authorization
- Client billing detail
- Reconciliation documentation

### Retainer Management

#### Advance Retainers
- Upfront payment for work to be performed
- Held in trust account
- Applied to invoices as work is done
- Unused portion refunded or applied

#### Evergreen Retainers
- Automatically replenished to fixed amount
- Common for ongoing counsel relationships
- Client is billed when retainer is depleted
- Retainer amount refreshed for future period

#### Specific Retainers
- Held for specific matter
- Applied only to that matter
- Unused portion returned after matter closes
- Separate accounting and tracking

### Cost Advance Management
- Client reimburses court costs, expert fees, etc. in advance
- Held in trust account
- Spent on client's behalf
- Reconciled at billing time
- Reimbursement/refund as needed

## IOLTA & Interest Management

### IOLTA Program Overview
- Interest On Lawyer Trust Account (IOLTA)
- Interest from small client balances goes to legal services program
- Improves return on client balances
- May provide tax benefits
- Supports legal services for low-income clients

### IOLTA Compliance
- **Account Setup**: Deposit qualifying client funds
- **Interest Allocation**: Interest accrues to IOLTA
- **Reporting**: Report accounts to state bar
- **Restrictions**: Limited to funds that wouldn't otherwise generate interest
- **Compliance**: Annual certification and reporting

### Interest Accounting
- Interest earned on combined balance
- Allocated to IOLTA program (not to clients)
- Excluded from trust liability
- Documented and reported separately
- Tax implications (no income to firm)

## Compliance & Controls

### Internal Controls
1. **Segregation of Duties**: Different people handle deposits vs. withdrawals
2. **Dual Signatures**: Checks require multiple signatories
3. **Monthly Review**: Manager review of reconciliation
4. **Audit Trail**: Documentation of all transactions
5. **Access Controls**: Limited access to accounts
6. **Approval Process**: Formal approval for all withdrawals

### Compliance Procedures
1. **Written Policies**: Written trust accounting policies
2. **Training**: Staff training on trust accounting procedures
3. **Monitoring**: Regular monitoring and review
4. **Audit**: Annual independent audit
5. **State Reporting**: Compliance with state bar requirements
6. **Documentation**: Maintain all supporting documentation

### Risk Management
- **Unauthorized Access**: Prevent unauthorized deposits/withdrawals
- **Commingling**: Prevent mixing of client and operating funds
- **Misappropriation**: Prevent theft or misuse of client funds
- **Fraud**: Prevent embezzlement or fraud
- **Neglect**: Prevent lost or forgotten client funds
- **Interest Misapplication**: Ensure proper interest accounting

## Common Trust Accounting Issues

### Commingling Violations
- Mixing client and operating funds
- Operating account for client funds
- Client funds in wrong account
- Improper interest accounting
- Fee problems and ethics violations

### Reconciliation Failures
- Failure to reconcile monthly
- Outstanding items not reviewed
- Discrepancies not investigated
- Incorrect ledger entries
- Audit failures

### Documentation Issues
- Missing deposit documentation
- No withdrawal authorization
- Poor client identification
- Unclear interest accounting
- Inadequate supporting records

### Compliance Failures
- Failure to perform annual audit
- Inadequate internal controls
- Missing written policies
- Staff training inadequate
- State reporting non-compliance

## Best Practices Summary

### Account Management
1. Separate trust and operating accounts
2. Proper account designation
3. Authorized signatories identified
4. Fee-free checking if possible
5. IOLTA compliance

3. Clear documentation
4. Client identification
5. Interest accounting

### Reconciliation
1. Monthly bank reconciliation
