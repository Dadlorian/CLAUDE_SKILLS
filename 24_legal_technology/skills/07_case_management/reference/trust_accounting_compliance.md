# Trust Accounting Compliance for Law Firms

## Overview

Trust accounting is one of the most heavily regulated aspects of legal practice. Attorneys hold client funds in trust and have strict ethical and legal obligations to safeguard these funds, maintain proper records, and comply with state bar rules. Violations can result in professional discipline, including disbarment. This reference covers trust accounting fundamentals, compliance requirements, technology solutions, and best practices.

## Legal and Ethical Framework

### ABA Model Rule 1.15 - Safekeeping Property

#### Segregation Requirement
- Client funds must be kept separate from lawyer's own funds
- Held in separate bank account(s)
- Designated as attorney trust account or client funds account
- Cannot commingle lawyer's funds with client funds (except minimal amount for bank fees)

#### Deposit Timing
- Promptly deposit client funds upon receipt
- "Promptly" typically means within 1-5 business days (varies by state)
- Funds become property of lawyer only when earned or authorized

#### Notification and Accounting
- Promptly notify client of receipt of funds
- Promptly deliver funds or property when client entitled
- Provide full accounting when requested
- Upon termination of representation, promptly refund unearned fees

#### Dispute Handling
- If dispute over ownership of funds, keep disputed portion in trust until resolved
- Cannot unilaterally withdraw disputed funds

#### Record Keeping
- Keep complete records of trust account funds
- Preserve records per state rules (typically 5-7 years minimum)
- Records must show all transactions and current balance for each client

### State-Specific Trust Account Rules
- All states have trust accounting rules (based on Model Rule 1.15)
- Significant variations between states
- Some states have detailed regulations and bookkeeping requirements
- Consult state bar rules for specific jurisdiction

### IOLTA (Interest on Lawyers' Trust Accounts)
**Purpose**
- Generate interest from small, short-term client deposits
- Interest funds legal aid and access to justice programs
- Lawyer doesn't keep interest (goes to state IOLTA program)

**Requirements**
- Accounts that don't generate meaningful interest for client must be in IOLTA account
- Interest paid to state IOLTA foundation
- Reporting requirements to state bar
- Specific bank account designation

**Exemptions**
- Large amounts or long-term deposits may be placed in separate interest-bearing account for client's benefit
- Investment of client funds (with client permission)

## Trust Accounting Fundamentals

### Types of Trust Accounts

#### Pooled Trust Account (IOLTA)
- Holds funds for multiple clients in single account
- Individual client ledgers track each client's balance
- Interest goes to state IOLTA program
- Most common trust account type

#### Separate Client Trust Account
- Dedicated account for single client's funds
- Used for large or long-term deposits
- Interest accrues to client's benefit
- Requires client's permission and separate accounting

### Trust Account Transactions

#### Deposits (Receipts)
**Retainers**
- Advance payment held in trust
- Earns out as work performed
- Transfer to operating when earned

**Settlement Funds**
- Personal injury settlements
- Litigation settlements
- Hold until authorized distribution

**Cost Advances**
- Client funds to pay case costs
- Held until costs incurred
- Reimburse operating account for costs paid

**Third-Party Funds**
- Opposing party settlements
- Escrow funds
- Stakeholder funds (real estate closings)

**Unearned Fees**
- Advance fee payments
- Held until earned
- Some jurisdictions require all fees in trust initially

#### Disbursements
**To Operating Account**
- Transfer earned fees from trust to operating
- Reimburse costs paid from operating
- Only when authorized (fee earned, cost incurred)

**To Client**
- Refund of unused retainer
- Distribution of settlement funds
- Return of client property

**To Third Parties (on behalf of client)**
- Court filing fees
- Expert witness fees
- Medical providers (from settlement)
- Liens and third-party claims

**Between Clients (Reallocations)**
- Correct deposit errors
- Transfer between client matters (with authorization)

#### Trust-to-Trust Transfers
- Between different trust accounts for same client
- Pooled to separate interest-bearing account
- Requires clear documentation

### Client Ledgers

#### Individual Client Ledger
- Separate ledger for each client/matter
- Tracks client's portion of pooled trust account
- Shows:
  - Beginning balance
  - Deposits (receipts)
  - Disbursements (payments)
  - Ending balance
- Must always equal share of pooled trust account balance

#### Ledger Components
**Transaction Records**
- Date
- Description
- Receipt or disbursement amount
- Running balance
- Check number (if applicable)
- Source/payee

**Supporting Documentation**
- Deposit slips
- Bank statements
- Check copies
- Wire confirmations
- Authorization (client permission for disbursements)

### Three-Way Reconciliation

#### The Three Components
1. **Bank Balance**: Trust account balance per bank statement
2. **Book Balance**: Trust account balance per firm's books
3. **Client Ledger Total**: Sum of all individual client ledger balances

#### Reconciliation Process
**Monthly Reconciliation (minimum)**
1. Reconcile bank statement to book balance
   - Starting balance + deposits - checks - fees = ending balance
   - Identify outstanding checks
   - Identify deposits in transit
   - Reconcile to bank statement ending balance

2. Add up all individual client ledger balances
   - Should equal book balance exactly
   - Discrepancies indicate error

3. Verify three-way tie:
   - Bank balance (adjusted for outstanding items) = Book balance = Total client ledgers

**If Doesn't Reconcile**
- Identify error immediately
- Common issues:
  - Deposit posted to wrong client
  - Disbursement not recorded
  - Math errors in ledgers
  - Duplicate or missing transactions
  - Bank fees not recorded

## Compliance Requirements

### State Bar Trust Accounting Rules

#### Common Requirements
**Account Setup**
- Must be in financial institution approved by state bar
- Account titled "Attorney Trust Account," "Client Funds Account," or similar
- Attorney as signatory
- FDIC insured (or equivalent)
- No offset or liens allowed (bank cannot take funds for attorney's debts)

**Overdraft Protection**
- Many states PROHIBIT overdraft protection (to prevent masking errors)
- Bounced check immediately notifies attorney of problem
- Some states require overdraft reporting to state bar

**Bank Reporting**
- Some states require banks to notify state bar of overdrafts or dishonored checks

**Record Retention**
- Check registers or equivalent
- Bank statements
- Deposit slips
- Canceled checks or images
- Client ledgers
- Reconciliation records
- Typically 5-7 years minimum (varies by state)

**Reconciliation Frequency**
- Monthly reconciliation required (minimum)
- Some states require quarterly or annual certification
- Written reconciliation records required

#### State-Specific Variations
**California**
- Rule 1.15 (Trust Accounts)
- Quarterly reconciliations required
- Specific record keeping rules

**New York**
- 22 NYCRR Part 1200, Rule 1.15
- Detailed bookkeeping rules
- Attorney Registration with IOLTA

**Florida**
- Rule 5-1.1 (Trust Accounts)
- Specific account requirements
- Regular trust account audits for some violations

**Texas**
- Rule 1.14 (Trust Account)
- Specific reconciliation requirements
- Annual trust account declaration

### IOLTA Compliance

#### Reporting Requirements
- Annual IOLTA report to state bar
- Report interest paid to IOLTA foundation
- Certify compliance with trust accounting rules

#### Interest Rates
- Financial institution must pay comparable rate
- Rate comparability certification

#### Eligible Institutions
- Must be approved IOLTA financial institution
- Bank agrees to report overdrafts to state bar
- Pays interest to state IOLTA foundation

### Audit Requirements
- Random audits by state bar (some states)
- Required audits after disciplinary complaints
- Self-audits (best practice)
- Third-party audits (if required)

## Best Practices

### Account Management
1. **Use Dedicated Account**: Never use operating account for client funds
2. **Minimal Commingling**: Keep only minimal amount of firm funds (for bank fees)
3. **Prompt Deposits**: Deposit client funds within 24-48 hours
4. **Clear Documentation**: Document every transaction with description and authorization
5. **Client Notification**: Notify client promptly of deposits and disbursements

### Transaction Processing
1. **Proper Authorization**: Obtain client permission before disbursements
2. **Verify Balances**: Ensure sufficient client balance before writing checks
3. **Accurate Ledger Posting**: Post to correct client ledger immediately
4. **Dual Signatures**: Consider requiring two signatures for trust checks (risk management)
5. **Check Security**: Use secure checks with anti-fraud features

### Reconciliation
1. **Monthly Reconciliation**: Reconcile every month without fail
2. **Timely Reconciliation**: Complete within 30 days of month-end
3. **Three-Way Reconciliation**: Always perform three-way reconciliation
4. **Resolve Discrepancies Immediately**: Don't wait if doesn't balance
5. **Document Reconciliation**: Keep written reconciliation records
6. **Management Review**: Have partner or manager review reconciliations

### Record Keeping
1. **Comprehensive Records**: Keep all deposit slips, checks, statements
2. **Electronic Backups**: Backup trust account data regularly
3. **Long Retention**: Keep records beyond minimum requirement
4. **Organized Filing**: Easy retrieval for audits or client requests
5. **Audit Trail**: Complete transaction history

### Segregation of Duties
1. **Separate Responsibilities**: Different people handle deposits, disbursements, reconciliation
2. **Oversight**: Partner review of trust account activities
3. **Limited Signatories**: Restrict who can sign trust checks
4. **Access Control**: Limit system access to trust account functions

### Client Communication
1. **Regular Statements**: Send trust account statements to clients
2. **Advance Notice**: Notify before disbursing or transferring earned fees
3. **Final Accounting**: Provide detailed accounting at matter conclusion
4. **Dispute Resolution**: Handle disputed funds properly (maintain in trust)

## Common Violations and How to Avoid

### Commingling
**Violation**: Mixing client funds with lawyer's own funds
**How to Avoid**:
- Use separate trust account exclusively for client funds
- Don't deposit operating account funds into trust
- Transfer earned fees promptly (don't leave in trust unnecessarily)

### Misappropriation
**Violation**: Using client funds for unauthorized purposes
**How to Avoid**:
- Never borrow from trust account
- Ensure client balance before disbursement
- Obtain client authorization for all disbursements
- Regular reconciliation to detect errors immediately

### Improper Advances
**Violation**: Paying costs from trust when client hasn't provided funds
**How to Avoid**:
- Require adequate retainer or cost advance
- Don't pay costs from trust if client balance insufficient
- Pay costs from operating account and bill client (don't advance from trust)

### Failure to Return Unearned Fees
**Violation**: Keeping unearned fees in trust or operating after representation ends
**How to Avoid**:
- Promptly calculate earned vs. unearned fees at conclusion
- Refund unearned portion immediately
- Provide final accounting to client
- Don't keep funds "in case" of future work

### Failure to Maintain Records
**Violation**: Inadequate record keeping, failure to reconcile
**How to Avoid**:
- Maintain client ledgers faithfully
- Keep all supporting documentation
- Reconcile monthly without exception
- Retain records for required period (check state rules)

### Overdrafts
**Violation**: Negative trust account balance (indicates misappropriation)
**How to Avoid**:
- Verify client balance before disbursement
- Don't disburse more than client's balance
- Reconcile regularly to catch errors
- Don't use overdraft protection (most states prohibit)

## Auditing and Monitoring

### Self-Audits
**Quarterly Self-Audit**
- Review trust account transactions
- Verify reconciliation completion
- Test client ledger accuracy
- Check for unusual transactions
- Review outstanding items

**Annual Comprehensive Audit**
- Full year review
- Test sample of transactions
- Verify client ledger accuracy
- Confirm compliance with state rules
- Management review and sign-off

### State Bar Audits
**Random Audits**
- Some states conduct random audits
- Must provide records within specified time
- Prepare by maintaining excellent records

**Complaint-Triggered Audits**
- Client complaints may trigger audit
- Disciplinary investigation
- Full cooperation required

**Preparation**
- Maintain audit-ready records
- Know where all records located
- Keep reconciliations current
- Organize records logically

### Red Flags
- Reconciliation discrepancies
- Negative client balances
- Overdrafts or bounced checks
- Stale or unclaimed funds
- Missing documentation
- Frequent journal entries (corrections)
- Unidentified deposits

## Consequences of Violations

### Professional Discipline
- Disbarment (most serious violations)
- Suspension (temporary loss of license)
- Censure or reprimand (formal discipline)
- Probation with monitoring
- Restitution to clients
- Ethics training requirement

### Criminal Liability
- Theft or embezzlement charges
- Fraud charges
- Felony convictions possible

### Civil Liability
- Client malpractice claims
- Breach of fiduciary duty
- Conversion claims

### Reputational Harm
- Public discipline records
- Loss of client trust
- Difficulty obtaining malpractice insurance
- Damage to professional reputation

## Special Situations

### Flat Fee Retainers
**Earned Upon Receipt vs. Advance Payment**
- Some states allow flat fees earned upon receipt (can go directly to operating)
- Many states require deposit to trust, earn out as work performed
- Must be clear in fee agreement
- Check state ethics opinions

### Evergreen Retainers
- Require client to replenish when balance falls below minimum
- Convenient for ongoing representation
- Monitor balance and request replenishment promptly

### Settlement Funds
- Deposit to trust immediately upon receipt
- Determine all liens and obligations
- Obtain client authorization for distribution
- Disburse per settlement agreement and client instruction
- Provide detailed accounting to client

### Disputed Funds
- Cannot disburse if ownership disputed
- Maintain in trust until resolution
- May need to interplead (court action to determine rightful owner)
- Document dispute and resolution

### Real Estate Closings
- Escrow funds held in trust
- Detailed settlement statement
- Verify all amounts before disbursing
- Wire fraud risks (verify all wiring instructions)

### Unclaimed or Abandoned Funds
- Client cannot be located or doesn't respond
- Hold in trust (cannot escheat to firm)
- May need to turn over to state unclaimed property
- Document efforts to locate client
