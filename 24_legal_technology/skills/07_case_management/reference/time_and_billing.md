# Time & Billing Management

## Overview
Time and billing systems capture attorney time, track expenses, and generate client invoices. Accurate time tracking and billing is critical for firm financial management, profitability analysis, and client relationships.

## Time Tracking

### Time Entry Methods

#### Online Time Entry
**Web-Based Timekeeping**:
- Real-time time entry through web interface
- Matter and task selection
- Description of work performed
- Time classification and billing codes
- Status tracking (submitted, approved, locked)

**Advantages**:
- Immediate time capture
- Easy billable hour recording
- Automatic backup
- Searchable time entries
- Easy review and approval

#### Mobile Time Entry
**Mobile Applications**:
- Native mobile apps (iOS/Android)
- Mobile web access
- Offline time entry capability
- Photo/document attachment
- Real-time synchronization

**Advantages**:
- Time capture in field or court
- Reduced data entry delay
- Convenient for mobile workers
- Push notifications
- Quick submission

#### Timer-Based Tracking
**Stopwatch Functionality**:
- Start/stop timer during work
- Automatic time calculation
- Pause and resume
- Time adjustment capability
- Integration with matter/task

**Advantages**:
- Accurate time capture
- No estimation needed
- Continuous tracking
- Easy time adjustment
- Prevents time jumping

#### Bulk Time Entry
**Batch Entry Method**:
- Manual bulk entry for past dates
- Use when daily entry not feasible
- Typically limited to supervisors
- Documentation of entry date vs. work date
- Less preferred than daily entry

### Time Entry Standards

#### Billing Codes
**Time Code Categories**:
- **Hourly**: Standard hourly rate billing
- **Non-Billable**: Work tracked but not billed (marketing, training, admin)
- **Fixed Fee**: Work applied to fixed fee matter
- **Contingency**: Work on contingency matter (tracked for contingency calculation)
- **Pro Bono**: Pro bono work (tracked separately)

**Specific Codes by Practice Area**:
- **Litigation**: Research, draft, deposition, discovery, trial preparation
- **Corporate**: Drafting, negotiation, due diligence, closing
- **Transactional**: Document review, drafting, negotiation, closing

#### Time Entry Requirements
- **Matter**: Matter code (required)
- **Task**: Task code (required)
- **Hours**: Hours worked (required)
- **Description**: Brief description of work (required)
- **Billing Code**: Time code/classification
- **Date**: Date work performed

#### Billing Description Standards
**Good Descriptions**:
- "Draft response to interrogatories #1-25"
- "Review client contract and provide comments"
- "Attend settlement conference with opposing counsel"
- "Research issue re: statute of limitations"

**Poor Descriptions**:
- "Work on case"
- "Legal work"
- "Various tasks"
- "As discussed"

### Quality Standards
1. **Timeliness**: Enter time daily or at least weekly
2. **Accuracy**: Record actual time worked
3. **Detail**: Sufficient detail for client to understand
4. **Appropriateness**: Charge appropriate billing code
5. **Matter Selection**: Correct matter assignment
6. **Review**: Regular review for accuracy

## Billing Models

### Hourly Billing
**Standard Model**:
- Time-based billing at set hourly rate
- Typical rates: $75-$600+ per hour depending on attorney level
- Invoice every month or upon completion
- Trust account deposits

**Rate Structure**:
- **Partner Rate**: Senior attorney billing rate
- **Associate Rate**: Mid-level attorney billing rate
- **Counsel Rate**: Of-counsel billing rate
- **Paralegal Rate**: Paralegal support rate
- **Law Clerk Rate**: Law student or clerk rate

**Advantages**:
- Straightforward for client and firm
- Easy to track and predict
- Aligns incentives with hours worked
- Easy to calculate profitability

**Disadvantages**:
- Incentivizes quantity over efficiency
- Creates time pressure perception
- Client cost uncertainty
- Disconnects attorney behavior from results

### Fixed Fee Billing
**Model**: Single fee for defined scope of work
**Typical Applications**:
- Document review
- Contract review
- Lease review
- Trademark application
- Will preparation
- Simple transaction

**Advantages**:
- Cost certainty for client
- Client budget control
- Encourages efficiency
- Incentivizes quality

**Disadvantages**:
- Scope definition critical
- Risk of unprofitable work
- Requires detailed scope management
- May require cost controls

### Retainer Billing
**Monthly Retainer Model**:
- Monthly fixed fee for ongoing services
- Time-based reduction from retainer
- Annual retainer fees
- Typical for ongoing counsel relationships

**Advantages**:
- Predictable revenue
- Established client relationships
- Client convenience
- Flexibility in service provision

**Disadvantages**:
- Limited scope and fees
- Requires detailed scope management
- Client may underestimate value
- Billing disputes over scope

### Contingency Fee Billing
**Model**: Percentage fee upon successful outcome
**Typical Applications**:
- Personal injury cases
- Collection matters
- Some commercial disputes
- Some employment matters

**Fee Structure**:
- Usually 30-40% of recovery
- May vary by case complexity
- Client responsible for costs/expenses
- Cost recovery from settlement/judgment

**Advantages**:
- Aligns interests with client
- Client only pays if successful
- Encourages case quality
- Risk-based fee

**Disadvantages**:
- Significant financial risk
- Requires case evaluation
- Complex accounting
- Ethical limitations apply

### Blended Fee Models
**Combination Approaches**:
- **Retainer + Hourly**: Monthly retainer with hourly above retainer
- **Retainer + Contingency**: Retainer plus percentage of recovery
- **Fixed + Hourly**: Fixed amount plus hourly above fixed
- **Flat + Contingency**: Flat fee plus contingency premium

## Billing Process

### Time Collection & Review
**Daily Process**:
1. Attorney enters time daily
2. Task and matter coding
3. Billing description added
4. Time entry submitted
5. Matter manager reviews
6. Managing attorney approves
7. Time locked for billing

**Quality Review**:
- Verify matter coding
- Check billing description quality
- Confirm billing code appropriateness
- Review for time reasonableness
- Check for duplicate time entries

### Adjustment & Write-Off Process
**Write-Off Decisions**:
- Excessive time (more than reasonable)
- Unnecessary time (scope management)
- Client issues (disputes, payment issues)
- Business decisions (cost reduction)
- Litigation/trial adjustments

**Process**:
1. Identify time to adjust
2. Document reason for adjustment
3. Obtain approval (partner, billing manager)
4. Adjust in system
5. Document in matter file
6. Report to accounting

### Invoice Preparation
**Pre-Invoice Review**:
1. Compile all time and expenses
2. Calculate billing amount
3. Apply retainer (if applicable)
4. Calculate taxes (if applicable)
5. Determine payment terms
6. Final review and approval

**Invoice Generation**:
1. Select period (monthly, custom)
2. Generate draft invoice
3. Review for accuracy
4. Add client-specific instructions
5. Finalize and transmit

**Invoice Contents**:
- Client name and address
- Invoice date and invoice number
- Period covered
- Itemized time entries (or summary by category)
- Expenses and disbursements
- Subtotal
- Applicable taxes
- Payment terms
- Payment instructions
- Trust account information (if applicable)

### Expense & Disbursement Management

#### Expense Types
- **Court Costs**: Filing fees, service fees, court reporter fees
- **Investigation**: Investigator fees, expert report costs
- **Travel**: Airfare, hotels, meals, mileage
- **Expert Fees**: Expert witness retainers and reports
- **Third-Party Costs**: Court reporter transcripts, deposition copies
- **Technology**: Software subscriptions, document hosting

#### Expense Tracking
- Require receipts
- Proper matter coding
- Client approval for large expenses
- Regular review
- Timely billing
- Trust account for advance costs

#### Reimbursement Methods
- Add to client invoice
- Separate expense invoice
- Trust account deduction (for advance costs)
- Direct payment (for third-party providers)

## Billing Analysis & Reports

### Key Metrics
- **Billable Hours**: Total billable hours by attorney
- **Realization Rate**: Hours billed vs. hours worked
- **Collection Rate**: Actual collections vs. billed amount
- **Average Billing Rate**: Average rate per billable hour
- **Matter Profitability**: Revenue minus cost by matter
- **Budget vs. Actual**: Actual hours vs. budget hours

### Reports
- **Attorney Production Report**: Billable hours by attorney
- **Matter Profitability Report**: Revenue and cost by matter
- **Aging Report**: Outstanding invoices by age
- **Collection Report**: Collection rate and cash flow
- **Budget Report**: Budget utilization by matter
- **Client Report**: Billing by client

### Benchmarking
- **Utilization Benchmarks**: Industry standard utilization (70-80%)
- **Rate Benchmarks**: Rate comparisons by market and level
- **Profitability Benchmarks**: Matter and client profitability targets
- **Collection Benchmarks**: Collection rate targets (95%+)

## Best Practices

### Time Entry Best Practices
1. **Daily Entry**: Enter time same day or next day
2. **Detailed Description**: Clear description of work
3. **Proper Coding**: Correct matter and task coding
4. **Honest Reporting**: Accurate time reporting
5. **Regular Review**: Supervisor review of time entries
6. **Quality Standards**: Maintain consistent quality

### Billing Best Practices
1. **Timely Invoicing**: Invoice within 5 business days of billing period
2. **Clear Invoices**: Clear, understandable invoice descriptions
3. **Regular Review**: Partner review before invoice issuance
4. **Payment Follow-up**: Prompt payment follow-up (within 30 days)
5. **Cost Transparency**: Transparent billing and cost information
6. **Fee Alignment**: Align fees with scope and value

### Profitability Best Practices
1. **Accurate Tracking**: Accurate time and expense tracking
2. **Budget Management**: Track hours against budget
3. **Efficiency Focus**: Focus on productivity and efficiency
4. **Cost Control**: Active cost and expense management
5. **Regular Analysis**: Regular profitability analysis
6. **Continuous Improvement**: Continuous process improvement

## Common Billing Issues

### Time Entry Issues
1. **Batch Entry**: Delayed, batch time entry
2. **Vague Descriptions**: Unclear work descriptions
3. **Wrong Matter Coding**: Time charged to wrong matter
4. **Excessive Time**: Excessive or unreasonable hours
5. **Missing Entries**: Lost or forgotten time entries

### Billing Issues
1. **Billing Disputes**: Disputes over billing accuracy or appropriateness
2. **Collection Delays**: Slow payment collection
3. **Budget Overruns**: Budgets exceeded without client awareness
4. **Billing Errors**: Invoicing errors or omissions
5. **Client Communication**: Poor communication about billing

### Profitability Issues
1. **Low Realization**: Low billing realization (excessive write-offs)
2. **Poor Collection**: Low collection rates
3. **Unprofitable Matters**: Matters with poor margins
4. **Cost Overruns**: Unexpected cost increases
5. **Inadequate Analysis**: Poor profitability visibility
