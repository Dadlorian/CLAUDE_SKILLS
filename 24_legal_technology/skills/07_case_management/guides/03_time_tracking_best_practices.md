# Time Tracking Best Practices Guide

## Overview

Accurate, consistent time tracking is fundamental to law firm profitability. Poor time tracking leads to revenue leakage, inaccurate billing, client disputes, and reduced profitability. This guide provides practical strategies and best practices for maximizing billable time capture and maintaining accurate time records.

## The Time Leakage Problem

### Common Causes of Lost Time
- Delayed entry (forgetting what was done)
- Interrupted work (forgot to track short tasks)
- Minimal tasks not tracked (brief emails, quick calls)
- Context switching not captured
- Write-downs due to poor descriptions
- Reluctance to bill for legitimate work

### Impact
**Revenue Loss**
- Industry average: 10-30% time leakage
- For attorney billing $300/hour working 2,000 hours/year
- 10% leakage = $60,000 lost revenue per year
- For 10-attorney firm: $600,000 annually

**Profitability Impact**
- Reduced realization rates
- Missed billable hour targets
- Undervalued work
- Competitive disadvantage

## Time Entry Methods

### Method 1: Real-Time Timers

**How It Works**
- Start timer when beginning billable task
- Stop when complete or interrupted
- Add description and matter after
- Multiple timers for multi-tasking

**Advantages**
- Most accurate time capture
- No reliance on memory
- Captures all time including interruptions
- Reduces write-downs (actual time recorded)

**Disadvantages**
- Requires remembering to start timer
- Can be disruptive to workflow
- Timer fatigue (forgetting to start/stop)

**Best Practices**
1. Make timers easily accessible (toolbar widget, hotkey)
2. Use descriptive timer names while running
3. Review and edit descriptions at end of day
4. Set reminders if timer runs too long (over 3-4 hours)
5. Use mobile timer when away from desk

**Tools**
- Clio timers (desktop and mobile)
- PracticePanther timer widget
- MyCase timers
- Standalone: Toggl, Timely (with PM integration)

### Method 2: Contemporaneous Manual Entry

**How It Works**
- Enter time immediately after completing task
- Record while details fresh
- Use time entry shortcuts

**Advantages**
- Accurate (if done immediately)
- More detailed descriptions
- Less disruptive than timers

**Disadvantages**
- Relies on discipline
- Easy to procrastinate
- Can still miss brief tasks

**Best Practices**
1. Set reminders every 2-3 hours to enter time
2. Keep PM system open in browser tab
3. Enter time before switching to next task
4. Use templates for recurring tasks
5. Keep notepad for quick time notes if away from computer

### Method 3: Reconstructive Daily Entry

**How It Works**
- Reconstruct day at end of day (or next morning)
- Review calendar, emails, documents
- Estimate time for each task

**Advantages**
- One time entry session per day
- Can review entire day for completeness
- Less interruption during work

**Disadvantages**
- Relies heavily on memory
- Tends to under-estimate time
- May miss brief tasks
- Less accurate descriptions

**Best Practices**
1. Do it same day (not next day or week)
2. Use calendar to reconstruct day
3. Review sent emails for activities
4. Check document modified times
5. Set aside dedicated time (30 minutes at end of day)
6. Use time tracking notes/journal during day

### Method 4: Automated Time Tracking

**How It Works**
- Software monitors computer activity
- Tracks documents worked on, emails, websites
- Suggests time entries based on activity
- Requires review and confirmation

**Examples**
- **Smokeball**: Automatically tracks document time
- **Carpe Diem**: Comprehensive activity tracking
- **Timely**: AI-powered time tracking
- **RescueTime**: Activity monitoring

**Advantages**
- Captures all computer activity
- No user action required during work
- Discovers forgotten time
- Most comprehensive capture

**Disadvantages**
- Privacy concerns (staff monitoring)
- Requires review and cleanup
- May capture non-billable time
- Doesn't track offline work

**Best Practices**
1. Review suggested entries daily
2. Edit descriptions for clarity
3. Delete or mark non-billable
4. Supplement with offline activities
5. Be transparent with staff about monitoring

## Time Entry Best Practices

### Timing

**Same-Day Entry**
- Enter time same day as work performed
- 90%+ same-day entry should be goal
- Accuracy degrades quickly after 24 hours

**Real-Time Entry**
- Enter immediately or use timers
- Most accurate method
- Captures all time

**Never**
- Don't wait until end of week or month
- Don't batch enter old time
- Don't estimate entire day/week at once

### Description Quality

**Good Descriptions**
- Specific: "Drafted motion to dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction"
- Task-oriented: "Researched Delaware LLC formation requirements"
- Value-focused: "Negotiated settlement terms with opposing counsel"
- Client-readable: Avoid jargon if client will see invoice

**Poor Descriptions**
- Vague: "Legal research" (what specifically?)
- Generic: "Review file" (what did you review and why?)
- Block billing: "Drafted pleading; researched case law; conferred with client" (separate entries for each task)

**Best Practices**
1. Be specific about what was done
2. Include document name if applicable
3. Avoid abbreviations client won't understand
4. Keep descriptions concise (1-2 sentences)
5. Use firm's description templates/standards

### Capturing All Time

**Small Tasks Add Up**
- 6-minute call: 0.1 hours
- 5 calls per day: 0.5 hours
- 5 days per week: 2.5 hours per week
- 50 weeks: 125 hours per year = $30,000+ at $250/hour

**Commonly Missed Activities**
- Brief phone calls
- Quick emails (especially on mobile)
- Calendar review and planning
- Travel time (if billable)
- Document filing and organization (if billable)
- Text messages with client
- Court time (waiting)

**Strategies to Capture**
1. Set minimum time increment (0.1 or 0.2 hours)
2. Track all activities, even brief ones
3. Use mobile app for on-the-go time entry
4. Batch similar small tasks if appropriate
5. Review day for gaps (did I miss anything?)

### Handling Interruptions

**Context Switching**
- Attorney works on Task A
- Interrupted for urgent Task B
- Returns to Task A
- Both tasks should be tracked

**Best Practices**
1. Pause timer on Task A when interrupted
2. Start timer on Task B (urgent matter)
3. Resume timer on Task A when returning
4. OR: Note interruption and add time entry later
5. Use multiple timers for complex days

### Billing vs. Non-Billable

**Billable Activities**
- Legal research
- Document drafting and review
- Client communication
- Court appearances
- Depositions and hearings
- Negotiations
- Case strategy and planning

**Non-Billable (Typically)**
- Administrative tasks (filing, organizing)
- Internal firm meetings
- Continuing legal education
- Marketing and business development
- Billing and time entry itself
- Correcting own errors

**Gray Areas (Depends on Agreement)**
- Travel time
- Email review and triage
- File review for background
- Training junior associates
- Internal consultations

**Best Practices**
1. Track all time (billable and non-billable)
2. Mark appropriately in system
3. Non-billable time shows full workload
4. Helps identify inefficiencies
5. Provides data for management decisions

## Technology Strategies

### Practice Management System

**Configuration**
- Set default time increment (0.1 hour common)
- Set rounding rules (up, nearest, actual)
- Configure task codes (UTBMS if needed)
- Create description templates
- Set up matter-specific billing rules

**Workflows**
- Timer shortcuts on desktop
- Mobile app always logged in
- Email integration (quick time from email)
- Calendar integration (convert appointments to time)

### Mobile Time Tracking

**Use Cases**
- Court appearances (enter time while waiting)
- Client meetings (timer during meeting, enter after)
- Travel time
- Weekend work
- Emergency situations

**Best Practices**
1. Install PM system mobile app
2. Set up quick timer access
3. Use voice-to-text for descriptions
4. Take advantage of offline mode
5. Review mobile entries on desktop for completeness

### Integration and Automation

**Email-to-Time**
- Some systems allow email-based time entry
- Email time details to specific address
- Automatically creates time entry
- Good for on-the-go entry

**Calendar-Based Time**
- Convert calendar appointments to time entries
- Appointment duration = time entry
- Edit description as needed
- Good for meetings, hearings, depositions

**Document-Based Time** (Smokeball, etc.)
- Tracks time in documents automatically
- Associates with matter based on document
- Minimal user effort

## Firm Culture and Management

### Setting Expectations

**Billable Hour Targets**
- Set realistic annual targets (1,500-2,000 hours typical)
- Monthly/quarterly checkpoints
- Provide feedback on progress

**Time Entry Standards**
- Same-day entry required
- Minimum description requirements
- Billing vs. non-billable guidelines
- Rounding policies

**Consequences and Rewards**
- Recognize attorneys meeting targets
- Address chronic late or poor time entry
- Bonus structures tied to hours and realization

### Training

**New Attorney Training**
- Extensive time entry training
- Practice with mock entries
- Review and feedback on first invoices
- Mentoring on appropriate billing

**Ongoing Training**
- Annual refresher on firm standards
- New technology training (PM system updates)
- Best practices sharing

### Monitoring and Feedback

**Regular Reports**
- Daily time entry report (who entered time?)
- Unbilled time aging (how old is WIP?)
- Billable hours progress (vs. target)
- Realization rates (written down time)

**One-on-One Review**
- Monthly review of time entries
- Feedback on description quality
- Identify patterns (under-billing, over-billing)
- Coaching and support

### Write-Down Analysis

**Causes of Write-Downs**
- Excessive time for task
- Unnecessary work
- Inefficient work
- Partner discretion (client relationship)
- Poor description (client challenged)

**Reducing Write-Downs**
1. Accurate time entry (don't inflate)
2. Efficient work (don't bill for learning)
3. Clear descriptions (justify time)
4. Client communication (manage expectations)
5. Budget awareness (stay within budget)

## Advanced Strategies

### Activity-Based Costing

**Concept**
- Track time by activity type, not just matter
- Identify most profitable activities
- Allocate resources to high-value work

**Implementation**
- Use task codes consistently
- Analyze time by activity
- Shift to profitable activities
- Delegate low-value work

### Budgeting and Tracking

**Matter Budgets**
- Estimate time at outset
- Track time against budget
- Alerts when nearing budget
- Discussion with client before exceeding

**Benefits**
- Predictable fees for client
- Discipline for firm
- Identifies inefficiencies
- Supports alternative fee arrangements

### Delegation

**Billable Hour Pyramid**
- Partners should do partner-level work
- Associates do research and drafting
- Paralegals do document preparation
- Staff do administrative tasks

**Time Tracking Impact**
- Track delegated time to matter
- Partner may spend less time, but matter still profitable
- Leverage enables higher capacity

## Metrics and KPIs

### Individual Metrics
- **Billable Hours**: Total billable time recorded
- **Billable Percentage**: Billable / total hours
- **Utilization Rate**: Billable / available hours
- **Time Entry Timeliness**: % same-day entry
- **Realization Rate**: Billed / recorded time

### Firm Metrics
- **Average Billable Hours**: Per attorney
- **Total Billable Hours**: Firm-wide
- **Time Leakage**: Estimated lost time
- **Write-Down Rate**: Write-downs / gross time
- **Collection Rate**: Collected / billed

### Targets (Industry Benchmarks)
- Billable hours: 1,500-2,000/year (attorney)
- Billable percentage: 65-75%
- Same-day entry: 90%+
- Realization rate: 90%+
- Collection rate: 95%+

## Common Pitfalls

### Procrastination
- Waiting until end of day, week, month
- Solution: Same-day entry requirement, timers

### Under-Billing
- Not tracking small tasks
- Solution: Track everything, mobile app, timers

### Poor Descriptions
- Vague, generic descriptions
- Solution: Templates, training, review

### Technology Avoidance
- Not using available tools
- Solution: Training, ease of access, mandate usage

### No Accountability
- No monitoring or consequences
- Solution: Regular reports, feedback, performance reviews
