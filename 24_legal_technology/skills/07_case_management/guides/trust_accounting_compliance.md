## Trust Account Fundamentals {#fundamentals}

### What is a Trust Account?

A trust account (also called an escrow account or client trust account) is a special bank account used to hold client funds that belong to the client but are temporarily in the law firm's possession.

**Key Characteristics**

- Holds funds belonging to clients, not the firm
- Established in firm name but for client benefit
- Separate from firm operating account
- Subject to strict regulatory oversight
- Must be reconciled regularly
- Cannot be used for firm operations

### Who Must Maintain a Trust Account?

Most lawyers are required to maintain a client trust account:

- All practicing attorneys except in-house counsel
- Required by state bar ethical rules
- Non-negotiable professional obligation
- Failure to comply is serious violation
- Can result in suspension or disbarment

### Types of Client Funds Requiring Trust Account

**Funds That Go Into Trust**

1. **Client Advance Retainers**
   - Money paid by client for future services
   - Held until earned by firm
   - Earned when services delivered

2. **Disputed Funds**
   - Settlement funds in dispute
   - Funds in escrow pending agreement
   - Interparty held funds

3. **Third-Party Funds**
   - Settlement proceeds
   - Loan proceeds held on client's behalf
   - Earnest money deposits

4. **Costs and Expenses Advances**
   - Client pays for anticipated court costs
   - Client pays for expert witness fees
   - Client prepays for service costs

5. **Security Deposits**
   - Deposits held as security
   - Released when matter concludes
   - Returned with accounting

**Funds That Stay in Operating Account**

- Earned fees for work completed
- Amounts earned from retainer
- Reimbursable expenses already paid
- Any amount rightfully earned by firm

## Legal and Regulatory Requirements {#requirements}

### ABA Model Rules

**Rule 1.15: Safekeeping Property**

Key requirements:

```
(a) Obligation to hold funds:
"A lawyer shall hold property of clients or third persons 
that is in a lawyer's possession in connection with a representation 
separate from the lawyer's own property."

(c) Specific requirements for client funds:
- Client funds shall be kept in a separate account
- Account shall be maintained in accordance with law
- Lawyer shall keep client funds separate from own
- Lawyer shall promptly pay or deliver funds to client
- Account shall be kept at a financial institution authorized 
  to receive client funds
- Lawyer shall maintain complete records of client funds

(d) Lawyer's property:
- Lawyer may maintain own property in trust account
- Only property reasonably necessary for trust account operation
- Law office supplies, security deposits, etc.

(e) Dispute resolution:
- If dispute over client funds:
  - Hold funds in trust until dispute resolved
  - Notify all parties
  - Seek appropriate resolution
  - May seek court determination if necessary
```

### State Bar Requirements

**Typical State Requirements**

```
ACCOUNT STRUCTURE:
- Separate account for client funds (operating account separate)
- Account in jurisdiction where law practice is
- Account name must identify as trust/client account
- Firm name and address on account
- Account records clearly identified

DEPOSITS AND WITHDRAWALS:
- Deposits made promptly (often within 5 days)
- Deposits in client's name/matter number
- Deposits clearly identified as to purpose
- Withdrawals only for entitled purposes
- No commingling with firm funds

RECORD KEEPING:
- Ledger card for each client
- Individual entries for deposits/withdrawals
- Running balance for each client
- Date, amount, description for each transaction
- Monthly reconciliation required
- Annual reconciliation to bank statement

REPORTING:
- Annual certification of compliance
- Some states require annual audit
- Written reconciliation required
- Documentation of balance sheet

PROHIBITED PRACTICES:
- Using client funds for firm operations
- Comingling client and firm funds
- Making loans to clients from trust account
- Depositing firm funds in trust account
- Using interest without proper authorization
- Failing to account for funds
- Improper book entries
```

### IOLTA (Interest on Lawyer Trust Accounts)

**What is IOLTA?**

IOLTA programs allow law firms to deposit client funds that are small or temporary in interest-bearing accounts, with interest going to legal services for the poor.

**IOLTA Requirements**

```
ACCOUNT SETUP:
- Separate interest-bearing account
- Deposits small amounts or short-term deposits
- Compliant with IOLTA regulations
- Financial institution must offer interest
- Account clearly identified as IOLTA account

INTEREST HANDLING:
- Interest paid to IOLTA program
- Not paid to individual clients
- Used for legal services to poor
- Some states allow firm to retain small amounts
- Annual report to IOLTA program

PARTICIPATION:
- Participation usually mandatory
- Opt-out requires written notice
- Program varies by state
- Pooled accounts often used
- Single interest check per month (typically)

COMPLIANCE:
- Account must be properly designated
- Records must clearly show IOLTA account
- Interest calculations must be correct
- Reports must be timely filed
- Annual audit may be required
```

## Trust Account Structure and Setup {#setup}

### Trust Account Establishment

**Initial Setup Checklist**

```
ACCOUNT ESTABLISHMENT:
□ Determine account holder name
  - Typically: "[Firm Name], Client Trust Account"
  - Must clearly identify as trust/escrow account
  - Ensure state bar approval of account name

□ Select financial institution
  - Select FDIC-insured bank
  - Verify institution accepts client trust funds
  - Review account requirements
  - Check fees and minimum balances
  - Verify online banking capability
  - Ensure regular statements available

□ Account type selection
  - Interest-bearing vs. non-interest bearing
  - Pooled vs. individual account
  - IOLTA account (if applicable)
  - Overdraft protection (NO - prohibited)

□ Authorized signatories
  - Typically attorney and office manager
  - Require dual signatures (some states)
  - Document authorized users
  - Establish withdrawal authority limits
  - Periodic review and update

□ Bank documentation
  - Provide FDIC compliance forms if required
  - Provide state bar requirements to bank
  - Establish monthly reporting
  - Set up online banking access
  - Secure API for CMS integration if applicable

□ Record systems
  - Establish ledger system
  - Create general ledger accounts
  - Set up client subsidiary ledgers
  - Establish reconciliation procedures
  - Create monthly closing procedures

□ Control procedures
  - Require itemized statements
  - Require monthly reconciliation
  - Require supervisory review
  - Document procedures
  - Establish audit requirements
```

### Account Naming and Legal Structure

**Proper Account Naming**

```
CORRECT EXAMPLES:
"John Smith, Attorney, Client Trust Account"
"Smith Law Firm, Client Trust Account"
"Smith Law Firm, IOLTA Account"
"Smith Law Firm, Client Escrow Account"

INCORRECT EXAMPLES:
"John Smith, Client Funds" (too vague)
"Smith Law Firm" (looks like operating account)
"Client Funds for Smith" (improper formatting)
"Firm Name Trust" (confusing to identify as client funds)

NAMING REQUIREMENTS:
- Must clearly identify as client/trust account
- Must show firm name
- May show attorney name
- Should not suggest operating account
- Must be consistent year to year
- State bar may have specific requirements
```

### Multiple Trust Accounts

**When to Use Multiple Trust Accounts**

```
SINGLE ACCOUNT (Common):
- All client funds in one account
- Single monthly reconciliation
- Simplest to manage
- Easiest to audit
- Suitable for most small/medium firms

MULTIPLE ACCOUNTS (Sometimes Necessary):
1. Separate IOLTA account
   - Small/temporary funds earning interest
   - Interest goes to legal services programs

2. Separate Escrow Account
   - Funds held in true escrow (two-party dispute)
   - Funds not firm's responsibility
   - Different accounting rules
   - Still requires trust account compliance

3. Practice Area Accounts
   - Real estate closings (may be separate)
   - Settlement funds (may be separate)
   - Client advance accounts (if permitted)
   - Complex portfolio/management situations

GENERAL RULE:
- Use single account unless specific reason for multiple
- State bar rules generally prohibit multiple operating accounts
- Additional accounts add complexity and compliance burden
- Document reason for multiple accounts
- Account must be properly designated and reconciled
```

## Client Fund Deposits and Handling {#deposits}

### Deposit Procedures

**Receiving Client Funds**

```
IMMEDIATE RECEIPT (Within 24 hours):
1. Client or third party delivers funds
   - Check, wire transfer, or cash
   - Document receipt date and amount
   - Get receipt acknowledgment if appropriate

2. Record transaction
   - Enter in deposit log
   - Identify client/matter number
   - Record source of funds
   - Note purpose/type of funds
   - Document date received

3. Verification
   - Verify amount matches documentation
   - Verify check properly endorsed
   - Verify proper authorization
   - Verify no duplicate deposits
   - Confirm amount reasonable for matter

4. Temporary hold if needed
   - Verify funds before depositing
   - Check for stop payment indicators
   - Verify wire actually received
   - Hold cash separately until verified
   - Document reason for any holds

DEPOSIT TO TRUST ACCOUNT (Within 5 business days):
1. Consolidate daily deposits
   - Combine multiple deposits from same day
   - Create deposit list
   - Prepare bank deposit

2. Make deposit
   - Deposit to trust account (never operating)
   - Keep deposit receipt
   - Record bank confirmation
   - Record date deposited

3. Record in accounting system
   - Post to trust account general ledger
   - Post to client subsidiary ledger
   - Assign to client matter
   - Identify purpose of deposit
   - Document in system

4. Reconcile to client invoice/agreement
   - Verify deposit matches agreement terms
   - Verify amount matches invoice
   - Verify proper matter assignment
   - Document in matter file
```

### Types of Client Deposits

**Retainer Deposits**

```
RECEIPT AND ACCOUNTING:
1. Client retainer received for future services
   - Example: $5,000 retainer for litigation
   - Deposited to trust account
   - Not earned by firm initially

2. Record in accounting
   - Debit Trust Account: $5,000
   - Credit Client Liability: $5,000
   - Creates liability on firm's balance sheet
   - Firm owes work for client or refund of unused

3. As work is performed
   - Each month, record earned fees
   - Example: 20 hours × $250/hour = $5,000 earned
   - Transfer from trust to operating account
   - Debit Client Liability: $5,000
   - Credit Revenue: $5,000

4. Invoicing
   - Client invoiced for work performed
   - Retainer credit applied
   - If retainer exhausted, invoice for additional
   - If retainer excess, refund or credit future work

EXAMPLE SCENARIO:
Month 1: Receive $5,000 retainer
- Trust Account: $5,000 (bank)
- Client Liability: $5,000 (books)

Month 1: Perform $3,000 worth of work
- Transfer $3,000 from trust to operating
- Revenue recognized: $3,000
- Trust Account: $2,000 (bank)
- Client Liability: $2,000 (books)

Month 2: Perform $2,500 worth of work
- Transfer $2,000 from trust to operating (retainer exhausted)
- Additional billing $500 (to operating account)
- Revenue: $2,500
- Trust Account: $0 (retainer used up)
- Invoice: $500 (for work beyond retainer)
```

**Cost Deposits**

```
RECEIPT AND ACCOUNTING:
1. Client deposits for anticipated costs
   - Example: $2,000 for expert witness
   - Deposited to trust account
   - Not firm income

2. Record in accounting
   - Debit Trust Account: $2,000
   - Credit Cost Liability: $2,000
   - Creates liability on firm's balance sheet
   - Firm owes expenses paid or refund

3. As costs are incurred
   - Expert witness paid from trust: $1,200
   - Remaining balance: $800 trust liability
   - Debit Cost Expense: $1,200
   - Credit Trust Account: $1,200

4. Reconciliation and refund
   - Costs incurred: $1,200
   - Remaining client funds: $800
   - Refund to client or credit against fees
   - Account closed when balance zero

EXAMPLE SCENARIO:
Month 1: Receive $2,000 cost deposit
- Trust Account: $2,000 (bank)
- Cost Liability: $2,000 (books)

Month 2: Expert witness charges $1,200
- Pay from trust account
- Trust Account: $800 (bank)
- Cost Liability: $800 (books)

Month 3: No additional costs
- Prepare cost reconciliation
- Refund $800 to client (or apply to fees)
- Trust Account: $0
- Cost Liability: $0
```

**Settlement Deposits**

```
RECEIPT AND ACCOUNTING:
1. Settlement proceeds received
   - Example: Settlement check for $50,000
   - Deposited to trust account
   - Firm's share must be determined

2. Record in accounting
   - Debit Trust Account: $50,000
   - Credit Settlement Liability: $50,000
   - Trust holds funds for client, attorney fees, and costs

3. Settlement distribution
   - Determine client's net: $35,000
   - Firm's attorney fees: $12,500 (25%)
   - Costs/expenses: $2,500
   - Total: $50,000

4. Distribution from trust
   - Pay client: $35,000 (trust to client)
   - Pay firm: $12,500 (trust to operating account)
   - Pay costs: $2,500 (trust to reimburse or trust out)
   - Account balance zero after distribution

DETAILED EXAMPLE:
Settlement Amount:                         $50,000
Deposited to trust account

Distribution:
Firm attorney fees (25% contingency):   ($12,500)
Costs reimbursement:                      ($2,500)
Client net proceeds:                      ($35,000)
_______________________________________
Total:                                        $0

Journal Entries:
Debit: Trust Account         $50,000
  Credit: Settlement Liability              $50,000

Debit: Settlement Liability  $50,000
  Debit: Trust Account (client payout)      $35,000
  Debit: Trust Account (costs)              $2,500
  Debit: Operating Account (firm fees)      $12,500
  Credit: Settlement Liability                           $50,000

Resulting in:
- Trust Account: $0
- Client account: settled
- Firm's operating account: increased by $12,500 fee
```

### Documentation Requirements

**Deposit Documentation**

```
REQUIRED FOR EACH DEPOSIT:
□ Source of funds clearly documented
  - Name of person/entity sending funds
  - Check number or wire transfer ID
  - Date funds received
  - Purpose of funds

□ Amount verification
  - Amount matches payment documentation
  - Amount matches matter/invoice
  - Amount reasonable for matter
  - Verification of multiple checks if applicable

□ Matter assignment
  - Matter number clearly assigned
  - Client name matched to documentation
  - Matter type confirmed
  - Partner/attorney confirmed

□ Deposit recording
  - Recorded in deposit log
  - Recorded in accounting system
  - Recorded in client subsidiary ledger
  - Dated when recorded

□ Reconciliation
  - Deposit slip retained
  - Bank confirmation obtained
  - Matched to accounting records
  - Verified in monthly reconciliation

RETENTION REQUIREMENTS:
- Deposit slips: minimum 7 years
- Bank statements: minimum 7 years
- Deposit documentation: minimum 7 years
- Client communications: per statute of limitation + 2 years
- Ledger cards: minimum 7 years
```

## Trust to Operating Account Transfers {#transfers}

### Earning and Transferring Retainer Funds

**Monthly Retainer Earnings Process**

```
RETAINER EARNED WORKFLOW:

1. Services Performed
   - Attorney records time entries
   - Time entries approved
   - Hours calculated at billing rate
   - Total monthly charges determined
   - Example: 30 hours × $250/hour = $7,500

2. Calculate Earned Amount
   - Compare charges to retainer balance
   - Earned amount = lesser of charges or balance
   - If charges < retainer balance:
     * Earned amount = total charges
     * Remaining retainer stays in trust
   - If charges > retainer balance:
     * Earned amount = remaining retainer balance
     * Retainer exhausted
     * Additional work billed to operating account

3. Transfer from Trust to Operating
   - Earned amount identified
   - Journal entry prepared
   - Debit: Operating Account
   - Credit: Trust Account
   - Amount transferred via internal transfer (often)
   - Or may be written check to operating account

4. Record in Accounting
   - Trust Account decreased by earned amount
   - Operating Account increased
   - Client retainer liability decreased
   - Revenue recognized for services

5. Invoice Preparation
   - Retainer credit applied
   - Additional billing if needed
   - Unbilled retainer shown on invoice
   - Matter cost updated

EXAMPLE CALCULATION:
Retainer balance (trust): $10,000
Month's work performed: $7,500
Earned amount: $7,500 (less than balance)
Transfer to operating: $7,500
Remaining retainer: $2,500

Retainer balance (trust): $2,500
Month's work performed: $3,500
Earned amount: $2,500 (balance exhausted)
Transfer to operating: $2,500
Additional billing from operating: $1,000
Remaining retainer: $0
```

**Expense Reimbursement from Client Advances**

```
EXPENSE ADVANCE WORKFLOW:

1. Client advance received
   - Client pays $5,000 for anticipated expert witness
   - Deposited to trust account
   - No fee component (not earned by firm)

2. Expenses incurred
   - Expert witness invoice: $3,200
   - Court filing fees: $500
   - Total expenses: $3,700

3. Transfer from trust to reimburse expenses
   - Trust account decreased by $3,700
   - Operating account increased
   - Expense accounts increased
   - Balance remaining: $1,300

4. Accounting treatment
   - Debit: Expense Accounts (expert witness, fees)
   - Credit: Trust Account (cash out)
   - Debit: Operating Account (cash in from transfer)
   - Credit: Client Liability (cost account)

5. Reconciliation and refund
   - Unused portion $1,300
   - Refund to client or apply to future costs
   - If refunding: check issued from trust account
   - Account reconciles to zero

DETAILED EXAMPLE:
Received: $5,000 cost advance
  Trust Account: +$5,000
  Cost Liability: +$5,000

Paid expert witness: $3,200
  Expert Expense: +$3,200
  Trust Account: -$3,200
  Cost Liability: -$3,200

Paid court fees: $500
  Court Fee Expense: +$500
  Trust Account: -$500
  Cost Liability: -$500

Remaining balance: $1,300
  Refund to client or apply to invoice credit
```

### Controlling Unauthorized Transfers

**Safeguards Against Improper Transfers**

```
REQUIRED CONTROLS:

1. Authorization Protocol
   - Retainer earnings: automatic, documented calculation
   - Expense reimbursement: expense receipt required
   - Other transfers: partner approval required
   - No transfers without supporting documentation

2. Documentation Requirements
   - Time entries for retainer earnings
   - Expense receipts for expense reimbursements
   - Calculation showing amount earned/incurred
   - Approval signatures
   - Date and description of transfer

3. Supervisory Review
   - Monthly review of all transfers
   - Verification of earnings calculations
   - Verification of expense documentation
   - Confirmation of proper client assignment
   - Investigation of unusual amounts

4. Prohibited Practices
   - NO transfer without work performed or expense incurred
   - NO transfer for firm operations from retainer
   - NO transfer for unrelated matters
   - NO transfer without proper documentation
   - NO transfer beyond amounts earned or expense incurred

5. Audit and Verification
   - Monthly reconciliation verifies all transfers
   - Spot-check of transfer documentation
   - Annual review of transfer pattern
   - Quarterly supervisory review of transfers
   - Investigation of any discrepancies

EXAMPLE VIOLATION SCENARIO:
Improper: Client retainer $5,000, no work performed, transfer $2,000 to operating
Consequence: Firm has misappropriated client funds, ethics violation

Proper: Client retainer $5,000, work performed $2,000, transfer $2,000
Consequence: Earned amount properly transferred, appropriate accounting
```

## Interest-Bearing and IOLTA Accounts {#interest}

### IOLTA Account Requirements

**IOLTA Program Setup**

```
IOLTA ACCOUNT ELIGIBILITY:
- Pooled account holding multiple client funds
- Deposits of small amounts (varies by state, typically <$10,000)
- Deposits of temporary funds (funds held <6 months)
- Interest-bearing account
- Interest paid to legal services program
- Individual client not entitled to interest

ACCOUNT SETUP:
1. Contact state IOLTA program
   - Obtain program requirements
   - Get list of IOLTA-approved banks
   - Obtain enrollment forms
   - Determine threshold amounts

2. Select participating bank
   - Bank must be IOLTA-approved
   - Must offer appropriate interest rate
   - Must provide proper statements
   - Must comply with IOLTA requirements
   - Must provide FDIC coverage verification

3. Establish account
   - Open account in IOLTA-approved name
   - Ensure proper account designation
   - Verify monthly statement will show interest
   - Establish online access if available
   - Verify dual authority requirements

4. Enroll with IOLTA program
   - Complete enrollment forms
   - Provide bank account information
   - Register for annual reporting
   - Provide contact information
   - Document required notifications
```

**IOLTA Accounting and Reporting**

```
MONTHLY IOLTA ACCOUNTING:

1. Deposits and Withdrawals
   - Track all deposits to IOLTA account
   - Track all withdrawals from account
   - Record in accounting system
   - Maintain client subsidiary ledgers
   - Reconcile to bank statement

2. Interest Calculation and Posting
   - Bank calculates interest monthly
   - Interest shown on bank statement
   - Firm records interest in accounting
   - Check received from bank monthly (typically)
   - Interest deposited to IOLTA program account

3. Journal Entries
   - Debit: Trust Account
   - Credit: IOLTA Liability
   - When interest received:
     Debit: Cash (IOLTA account)
     Credit: Interest Payable (IOLTA program)

4. Annual IOLTA Reporting
   - Annual statement filed with IOLTA program
   - Interest earned reported
   - Interest paid reported
   - Account balance reported
   - Certification of compliance

EXAMPLE MONTHLY CYCLE:
Starting balance (bank): $250,000
Deposits during month: $25,000
Withdrawals during month: ($15,000)
Ending balance (bank): $260,000
Interest earned (0.5% annual): $108
Interest check received: $108

Accounting entries:
- Daily deposits/withdrawals as they occur
- Month-end interest posting:
  Debit: IOLTA Cash Account: $108
  Credit: Interest Payable (IOLTA): $108
- Deposit interest check monthly to IOLTA program
```

### Non-IOLTA Interest-Bearing Accounts

**Earned Interest on Client Funds**

```
SITUATION: Client entitled to interest

Example: Long-term fund in trust earning interest
- Client funds held for extended period
- Account earns interest
- Client entitled to interest on their funds
- Firm may not use interest for operations

ACCOUNTING:
Interest accrues:
- Debit: Interest Receivable
- Credit: Client Interest Earned

Interest paid:
- Debit: Trust Account
- Credit: Interest Receivable

Posted to client:
- Debit: Client Liability (reduce payable)
- Credit: Interest Posted to Client

Client receives interest:
- Debit: Client Liability
- Credit: Trust Account (when paid)

DOCUMENTATION:
- Client agreement must address interest
- Interest earnings reported to client
- Client approval for use of interest
- Some states allow firm to retain small amounts
- Proper documentation required
```

## Reconciliation Procedures {#reconciliation}

### Monthly Reconciliation Process

**Complete Reconciliation Workflow**

```
MONTHLY RECONCILIATION SCHEDULE:

By 5th of Month: Obtain Bank Statement
- Request or download monthly statement
- Verify all transactions
- Identify any unusual activity
- Check for errors or discrepancies

By 10th of Month: Prepare Reconciliation
- List all trust account transactions from books
- List all bank statement transactions
- Identify deposits in transit
- Identify outstanding checks
- Identify bank errors or firm errors

By 15th of Month: Complete Reconciliation
- Prepare detailed reconciliation schedule
- Reconcile book balance to bank balance
- Investigate all differences
- Obtain explanations for variances
- Document all reconciling items

By 20th of Month: Review and Approval
- Supervisory review of reconciliation
- Verification of all transactions
- Investigation of unusual items
- Approval signature required
- File reconciliation in trust file
```


### Client Subsidiary Ledger Reconciliation

**Client Fund Accounting**

```
SUBSIDIARY LEDGER RECONCILIATION:

For each client with trust funds:
1. Prepare client subsidiary ledger showing:
   - Beginning balance
   - Deposits
   - Withdrawals
   - Ending balance

2. For each client, verify:
   - All deposits recorded
   - All withdrawals recorded
   - Balance is correct
   - No unauthorized transfers

3. Sum all client balances
   - Total of all client balances
   - Should equal trust account balance
   - Investigate any difference

4. Investigate variances
   - Unrecorded deposits or withdrawals
   - Incorrect amounts recorded
   - Deposits to wrong client
   - Lost or misplaced funds

EXAMPLE RECONCILIATION:
Client A balance:          $5,000
Client B balance:          $3,500
Client C balance:          $2,200
Trust account operating:   $1,300
_____________________________
Total:                    $12,000

Bank Balance verification:
Bank statement balance:   $12,000

Reconciliation: Complete, no variances
```

```
ANNUAL AUDIT ACTIVITIES:

1. External Audit (if required by state)

3. Auditor Report

4. Internal Audit (annual minimum)

5. State Bar Certification (if required)
   - File annual certification with bar
   - Certify trust account compliance
   - Provide audit report if required
   - Declare any violations
   - Certify controls in place
```

## Compliance Monitoring and Controls {#controls}

### Internal Controls Framework

**Key Controls Over Trust Accounts**

```
CONTROL OBJECTIVE: Ensure client funds handled properly

Control 1: Separate Account
- Trust account completely separate from operating
- No commingling of funds
- Different account numbers
- Different account names
- Different bank (optional but recommended)
- Verified at monthly reconciliation

Control 2: Dual Authorization
- Two people required for withdrawals (some states)
- Different authorization levels
- Joint signatures required
- Review of transaction before authorization
- Documentation of authorization

Control 3: Monthly Reconciliation
- Performed within 15 days of month-end
- Supervisor review required
- Signed and filed
- All variances investigated
- Documented explanations

Control 4: Subsidiary Ledgers
- Maintained for each client
- Updated with each deposit/withdrawal
- Monthly balance verification
- Sum reconciled to general ledger
- Accurate documentation

Control 5: Earnings Controls
- Time entries required for retainer transfers
- Approval required before transfer
- Amount calculated and verified
- Transfer documented
- Periodic supervisory review

Control 6: Audit Trail
- Transaction log maintained
- User access documented
- Changes recorded and reported
- System-generated confirmations
- Monthly management review

Control 7: Bank Reconciliation
- Professional bank reconciliation
- Performed timely
- Variances investigated
- Approved and filed
- Retained per record retention policy
```

### Detecting Violations

**Red Flags and Warning Signs**

```
RED FLAG: Account Never Reconciles
Warning: Possible unauthorized transfers, missing deposits
Action: Immediate investigation required

RED FLAG: Large Transfers Without Documentation
Warning: Possible misappropriation of funds
Action: Trace back to source, verify authorization

RED FLAG: Operating Account Funded from Trust Account
Warning: Clear violation, possible misappropriation
Action: Immediate notification to partner, bar counsel

RED FLAG: Funds Transferred to Non-Client Parties
Warning: Improper use of client funds
Action: Investigation and corrective action required

RED FLAG: Monthly Reconciliation Delayed or Skipped
Warning: Possible attempt to hide violations
Action: Understand reason, ensure controls in place

RED FLAG: No Subsidiary Ledgers for Some Clients
Warning: Possible hidden transactions
Action: Reconstruct ledgers, investigate

RED FLAG: Significant Variance Between Book and Bank
Warning: Possible error or unauthorized transfer
Action: Thorough investigation required

RED FLAG: Client Complains About Missing Funds
Warning: Critical - possible misappropriation
Action: Immediate investigation, possible bar report
```

## Common Violations and Prevention {#violations}

### Types of Trust Account Violations

**Commingling**

```
VIOLATION: Client funds mixed with firm operating funds

Example: 
- Firm deposits client retainers to operating account
- Firm funds and client funds in same account
- Creates confusion and liability

Prevention:
- Maintain separate trust account
- All client funds go to trust only
- Only firm money in operating account
- Monthly reconciliation verifies separation
- Staff training on proper deposits
- Automated routing (if possible)

Consequence: Disciplinary action, potential disbarment
```

**Misappropriation**

```
VIOLATION: Using client funds for firm operations

Example:
- Transfer client retainer to operating account before earned
- Use client expense fund for firm rent
- Use settlement proceeds for firm payroll

Prevention:
- Strict controls on trust transfers
- Earnings only transferred if work performed
- Expenses only transferred if incurred
- Dual authorization required
- Supervisory review monthly
- Monthly reconciliation detects
- Restricted access to trust account

Consequence: Serious disciplinary action, likely disbarment
```

**Failure to Maintain Records**

```
VIOLATION: Inadequate trust account documentation

Example:
- No subsidiary ledgers
- No monthly reconciliation
- No supporting documentation
- Unclear client identification
- Missing deposit records

Prevention:
- Establish proper accounting system
- Train staff on documentation
- Monthly reconciliation mandate
- Supervisory review required
- Audit trail maintained
- Regular compliance reviews
- Annual audit if state requires

Consequence: Disciplinary action
```

**Improper Interest Handling**

```
VIOLATION: Using client interest without authorization

Example:
- IOLTA account interest retained by firm
- Client entitled to interest improperly withheld
- No disclosure of interest earning
- No client agreement addressing interest

Prevention:
- Clear client agreement on interest
- Proper IOLTA account if eligible
- Client interest posted to client account
- Interest reporting to clients
- Annual interest reconciliation
- Proper accounting treatment

Consequence: Disciplinary action, restitution required
```

## Audit and Documentation {#audit}

### Trust Account Audit Requirements

**State Bar Audit Standards**

```
TYPICAL STATE REQUIREMENTS:

Many states require annual audit by:
- CPA firm certified and bonded
- Licensed in state of practice
- Experienced in law firm audits
- Compliant with professional auditing standards

Audit Scope:
- Verify trust account properly designated
- Verify dual authorization in place
- Review monthly reconciliations
- Spot-test transactions
- Verify subsidiary ledgers
- Confirm earnings handled properly
- Verify interest accounting
- Confirm no commingling
- Review compliance with state rules

Audit Report:
- Unqualified opinion on compliance
- Identification of any violations
- Recommendations for improvement
- Certification of controls

Filing:
- Send to state bar
- Maintain in office
- Retain per record retention policy
```

### Record Retention and Documentation

**Required Documentation and Retention**

```
DOCUMENT: Monthly Bank Statements
Retention: 7 years minimum
Purpose: Verify deposits/withdrawals, reconciliation basis

DOCUMENT: Cancelled Checks
Retention: 7 years minimum
Purpose: Verify withdrawals, authorization, payee

DOCUMENT: Monthly Reconciliations
Retention: 7 years minimum
Purpose: Verify account management, variance investigation

DOCUMENT: Subsidiary Ledgers (by client)
Retention: 7 years minimum (or per statute of limitations + 2 yrs)
Purpose: Verify client balances, funds tracking

DOCUMENT: Trust Account General Ledger
Retention: 7 years minimum
Purpose: Record all transactions and balances

DOCUMENT: Deposit Slips
Retention: 7 years minimum
Purpose: Verify deposits, reconciliation, tracing

DOCUMENT: Wire Transfer Confirmations
Retention: 7 years minimum
Purpose: Verify fund transfers, reconciliation

DOCUMENT: Annual Audit Report
Retention: 7 years minimum
Purpose: Evidence of compliance, bar requirements

DOCUMENT: Client Retainer Agreements
Retention: 7 years minimum (or longer per matter type)
Purpose: Verify authorization of trust deposits

DOCUMENT: Engagement Letters
Retention: 7 years minimum
Purpose: Document fee arrangements, interest handling

ORGANIZATION:
- File in trust account file
- Organized chronologically
- Indexed for easy retrieval
- Secure storage (locked cabinet/system)
- Backup copies if electronic
- Readily available for audit
```

## Conclusion

Trust account management is one of the most critical compliance functions in law practice. Strict procedures, detailed documentation, careful controls, and regular monitoring are essential to ensure client funds are handled properly and firm compliance with professional responsibility rules is maintained.

The stakes for trust account violations are extremely high, with serious disciplinary consequences up to and including disbarment. By implementing the comprehensive procedures, controls, and documentation standards outlined in this guide, law firms can ensure proper trust account management and protect both clients and the firm from violation of these critical professional responsibilities.

Regular training, supervision, and monitoring should be ongoing practices in every law firm to ensure that all staff understand the critical importance of trust account compliance and the firm's commitment to handling client funds with the utmost care and accuracy.
