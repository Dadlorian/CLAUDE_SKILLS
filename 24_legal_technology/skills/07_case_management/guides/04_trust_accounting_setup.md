# Trust Accounting Compliance Setup Guide

## Overview

Setting up compliant trust accounting is critical for law firms. Violations can result in professional discipline, including disbarment. This guide provides step-by-step instructions for establishing trust accounting procedures, selecting appropriate tools, and maintaining compliance with state bar rules.

## Prerequisites

Before setting up trust accounting:
- [ ] Review your state's trust account rules (check state bar website)
- [ ] Determine if you need IOLTA account, separate interest-bearing account, or both
- [ ] Verify bank is approved for attorney trust accounts in your state
- [ ] Understand record-keeping requirements (typically 5-7 years)
- [ ] Confirm reconciliation frequency (monthly minimum in most states)

## Step 1: Set Up Trust Bank Account

### Choose Financial Institution

**Requirements**
- Approved by state bar for attorney trust accounts
- FDIC insured
- Local branch for access (optional but helpful)
- Online banking with transaction export
- Check writing available

**Verify Bank Features**
- Reports overdrafts to state bar (required in many states)
- NO overdraft protection (prohibited in many states to ensure errors are immediately apparent)
- Competitive interest rates for IOLTA accounts
- Check imaging for records

### Account Application

**Account Title**
- Must identify as trust or client funds account
- Examples:
  - "[Law Firm Name] Attorney Trust Account"
  - "[Attorney Name] Client Funds Account"
  - "[Firm Name] IOLTA Account"

**Signatory**
- Attorney(ies) as signatory
- May add staff with attorney supervision
- Consider dual signature requirement for checks over certain amount

**IOLTA Designation**
- Inform bank account is IOLTA if applicable
- Bank must remit interest to state IOLTA program
- Bank will need state IOLTA foundation information

### Initial Deposit

**Small Amount**
- Deposit minimal firm funds for bank fees only
- Typically $50-$200
- Only exception to "no commingling" rule
- Document this deposit clearly

## Step 2: Choose Trust Accounting Software

### Option 1: Practice Management System Trust Accounting

**Clio**
- Client ledgers
- Deposits and disbursements
- Operating ↔ Trust transfers
- Three-way reconciliation
- Trust reports
- Check printing integration

**MyCase / PracticePanther**
- Similar features to Clio
- Integrated with billing
- Trust account reporting

**Pros**
- Fully integrated with PM system
- Automatic trust balance on matter dashboard
- Invoice payment from trust automated
- Training and support included

**Cons**
- May lack advanced features
- State-specific reporting may be limited
- Dependent on PM system

### Option 2: QuickBooks

**Setup**
- Trust account as separate "bank account" in QuickBooks
- Each client as "customer"
- Client sub-accounts for ledgers
- Custom reports for reconciliation

**Pros**
- Powerful accounting features
- CPA familiar with QuickBooks
- Detailed reporting

**Cons**
- Complex setup for legal trust accounting
- Not designed for legal use
- Manual integration with PM system
- Steep learning curve

### Option 3: Specialized Software

**CosmoLex**
- Practice management with integrated accounting
- Built for legal trust accounting
- Compliance-focused
- Bank integration

**Law Ruler**
- Trust accounting software
- Compliance reporting
- Three-way reconciliation

**Pros**
- Designed specifically for legal trust accounting
- Compliance features built-in
- State-specific reporting

**Cons**
- Additional cost
- Learning curve
- Integration with PM system may be needed

### Recommendation

For most small to mid-size firms: **Use Practice Management System trust accounting** (Clio, MyCase, PracticePanther)
- Simplest setup
- Integrated with matter management and billing
- Adequate for most firm needs
- Lower total cost

## Step 3: Configure Trust Accounting System

### Connect Bank Account

**Bank Feed Integration**
- Link trust bank account to PM system
- Automatically imports transactions
- Reduces manual entry
- Speeds reconciliation

**Manual Import**
- Download transactions from bank (CSV, OFX)
- Import to PM system
- Less automated but still efficient

### Set Up Client Ledgers

**Automatic Creation**
- Client ledger created when trust transaction entered
- Associated with matter
- Tracks that client's portion of pooled trust account

**Initial Setup**
- Review existing clients with trust balances (if migrating)
- Create opening balances
- Document source (migration, prior system)

### Configure Transaction Types

**Receipt Types**
- Retainer deposit
- Cost advance
- Settlement funds received
- Third-party funds
- Refund from vendor

**Disbursement Types**
- Payment to firm (earned fees)
- Payment to firm (cost reimbursement)
- Payment to client
- Payment to third party on behalf of client
- Transfer between matters (same client)

### Set Up Approval Workflows (if applicable)

**Approval Requirements**
- May require partner approval for:
  - Disbursements over certain amount
  - Payments to firm (earned fees)
  - Refunds to clients
- Configure approval workflow in system
- Notifications to approvers

## Step 4: Establish Procedures

### Trust Deposit Procedures

1. **Receipt of Funds**
   - Client check, wire, credit card, cash
   - Immediately place in secure location
   - Never deposit to operating account

2. **Recording**
   - Enter receipt in trust accounting system
   - Note: Client name, amount, source, purpose (retainer, settlement, cost advance)
   - Create client ledger entry

3. **Bank Deposit**
   - Deposit within 1-5 business days (check state rules)
   - Prepare deposit slip
   - Include copy with firm records
   - Scan or retain bank deposit receipt

4. **Confirmation**
   - Reconcile deposit in system when bank clears
   - Match to bank statement
   - Notify client of receipt (email or letter)

### Trust Disbursement Procedures

1. **Authorization**
   - Client authorization required (engagement letter, email, signed form)
   - Verify sufficient client balance
   - Verify purpose is permitted

2. **Recording**
   - Enter disbursement in system BEFORE writing check
   - Reduces client ledger balance
   - Prevents inadvertent overdraft

3. **Check Writing**
   - Use trust account checks only
   - Complete all information (payee, amount, purpose)
   - Secure checks to prevent unauthorized use
   - Consider dual signature for large amounts

4. **Documentation**
   - Scan or copy check
   - Retain check image from bank
   - File with matter documents
   - Note in matter record

5. **Trust to Operating Transfers** (Earned Fees)
   - Must be earned before transferring
   - Client authorization (advance or invoice approval)
   - Record transfer in both trust and operating systems
   - Typically by check from trust to operating, or electronic transfer

### Reconciliation Procedures (Monthly Minimum)

**Three-Way Reconciliation**

1. **Bank Reconciliation**
   - Obtain bank statement
   - Match all transactions to system
   - Identify outstanding checks
   - Identify deposits in transit
   - Reconcile bank balance to book balance

2. **Client Ledger Total**
   - Generate client ledger balance report
   - Sum all individual client balances
   - Should equal trust account book balance

3. **Verify Three-Way Tie**
   - Adjusted bank balance = Book balance = Sum of client ledgers
   - If doesn't tie, investigate immediately

4. **Document Reconciliation**
   - Print or save reconciliation report
   - Date and sign
   - Retain for required period (5-7 years)
   - Partner or manager review and sign

**Frequency**
- Monthly (minimum, required in most states)
- Some firms reconcile weekly or even daily
- Never less than monthly

## Step 5: Implement Internal Controls

### Segregation of Duties

**Separate Responsibilities**
- Different people handle:
  - Deposits (staff)
  - Disbursements (attorney or senior staff)
  - Reconciliation (different person than deposits/disbursements)
  - Review (partner or manager)

**Small Firms**
- If too small for segregation, implement compensating controls
- Partner reviews all transactions
- Surprise audits
- Dual signature for checks

### Access Controls

**System Access**
- Limit who can record trust transactions
- Restrict check signing
- Require passwords for trust account functions
- Audit log of all trust actions

**Check Security**
- Store blank checks securely (locked cabinet)
- Limit who has access
- Use security features (watermarks, micro-printing)

### Approval Requirements

**Mandatory Approvals**
- Partner approval for disbursements over $X (e.g., $1,000 or $5,000)
- Client approval for earned fee transfers
- Partner review of reconciliations

### Monitoring

**Regular Reviews**
- Partner reviews monthly reconciliation
- Spot-check transactions
- Review for unusual items
- Verify client balances match expectations

**Exception Reports**
- Negative client balances (error, should never happen)
- Large or unusual transactions
- Old or stale balances (unclaimed funds)
- Frequent transfers

## Step 6: Record Keeping

### Required Records (per state rules, typically):

**Transaction Records**
- All deposits and disbursements
- Date, amount, payee/payor, purpose
- Check numbers
- Client matter association

**Bank Records**
- Monthly bank statements
- Cancelled check images
- Deposit slips
- Wire confirmations

**Client Ledgers**
- Individual client balances
- Transaction history per client
- Running balance

**Reconciliations**
- Three-way reconciliation documentation
- Monthly reports
- Signed and dated by preparer and reviewer

### Retention Period
- 5-7 years minimum (check state rules)
- Some states require longer
- Keep electronic backups
- Consider permanent retention for audit defense

### Organization
- Organized by month and year
- Easy to retrieve for audits or client requests
- Electronic and/or paper
- Secure storage

## Step 7: Train Staff and Attorneys

### Training Topics

**All Attorneys**
- Trust accounting rules and ethics
- When funds must go to trust
- Client authorization requirements
- Prohibition on borrowing or advancing
- Importance of accurate records

**Staff Handling Trust Transactions**
- How to record deposits
- How to record disbursements
- How to perform reconciliation
- How to generate reports
- Security and confidentiality

**Partners/Managers**
- How to review reconciliations
- Red flags to watch for
- Approval procedures
- Audit preparation

### Ongoing Training
- Annual refresher
- New employee onboarding
- Updates when rules change
- CLE on trust accounting for attorneys

## Step 8: Establish Audit and Review Process

### Internal Audits

**Quarterly Self-Audit**
- Review recent reconciliations
- Test sample of transactions
- Check for unusual items
- Verify procedures followed

**Annual Comprehensive Audit**
- Detailed review of all trust activity
- Test controls
- Verify compliance
- Partner sign-off

### External Audits

**State Bar Audits**
- Random or complaint-triggered
- Must provide all records
- Prepare by maintaining excellent records

**Malpractice Insurer**
- Some insurers require or offer trust audits
- Demonstrates good risk management
- May reduce premiums

## Step 9: Handle Special Situations

### Unclaimed or Abandoned Funds

**Situations**
- Client cannot be located
- Client doesn't respond to refund notice
- Small balances client doesn't claim

**Procedures**
- Document attempts to contact client
- Hold funds indefinitely (cannot take for firm)
- May need to escheat to state unclaimed property
- Check state rules

### Disputed Funds

**When Occurs**
- Dispute over who entitled to funds
- Fee dispute with client
- Third party claims interest

**Procedure**
- Do not disburse disputed portion
- Hold until resolution
- May need interpleader action (court determines rightful owner)
- Clearly document dispute

### Nominal Amounts for Bank Fees

**Permissible**
- Minimal firm funds for bank fees only
- Typically $50-$200
- Only exception to no commingling rule

**Record Keeping**
- Clearly document this balance separately
- Ensure not used for other purposes
- Replenish if needed for fees

## Red Flags (Immediate Attention Required)

- Reconciliation doesn't balance
- Negative client balance
- Overdraft or bounced check
- Client complains about trust balance
- Unable to refund client (insufficient funds)
- Large unexplained transactions
- Failure to reconcile monthly
- Missing documentation
