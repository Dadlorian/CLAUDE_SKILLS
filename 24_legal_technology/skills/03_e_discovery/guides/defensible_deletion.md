# Defensible Deletion Guide

## Legal Framework for Deletion

### Regulatory Requirements
- NARA guidelines for federal records
- State-specific retention requirements
- Industry-specific regulations (healthcare, finance)
- Data protection regulations (GDPR, CCPA)
- Employment law requirements
- Contract-specific retention obligations

### Litigation Hold Implications
- Active legal hold prevents deletion
- Preserve documents relevant to anticipated litigation
- Proportionality considerations
- Ongoing hold evaluation
- Hold termination procedures

## Deletion Planning

### Pre-Deletion Assessment
1. Review all applicable retention policies
2. Identify regulatory requirements
3. Assess litigation risk
4. Determine hold status
5. Get legal approval for deletion

### Documentation Requirements
1. Retention policy version and approval
2. Legal hold status verification
3. Deletion criteria and basis
4. Custodian notification (if required)
5. Approval chain and signatures
6. Deletion date and method
7. Certification of completion

## Deletion Methods

### Technical Deletion
- Secure file deletion with overwriting
- Database record deletion
- Email system purging
- Backup media destruction
- Cloud system deletion

### Deletion Verification
- Confirm data removal
- Verify backup systems
- Check recovery impossibility
- Document verification process
- Create deletion certificate

### Media Destruction
- Certification of destruction
- Third-party verified destruction
- Certificate of destruction retention
- Proper documentation of process
- Chain of custody for media

## Phased Approach

### Phase 1: Hold Removal
1. Evaluate hold necessity
2. Get legal authorization
3. Notify custodians of hold removal
4. Document removal decision
5. Set effective date

### Phase 2: Deletion Planning
1. Identify documents eligible for deletion
2. Apply deletion criteria
3. Schedule deletion process
4. Prepare documentation
5. Brief technical team

### Phase 3: Execution
1. Apply final verification
2. Execute deletion process
3. Verify successful deletion
4. Generate deletion logs
5. Create backup verification

### Phase 4: Documentation
1. Create deletion certificate
2. Document all procedures
3. Maintain approval records
4. Preserve policy documents
5. Archive documentation

## Legal Considerations

### Defensibility Elements
- Clear retention policy existence
- Proper hold procedures
- Regular hold evaluation
- Documented hold removal process
- Technical execution verification
- Consistent application across organization
- Good faith compliance efforts

### Risk Areas
- Selective deletion
- Inadequate hold procedures
- Missing documentation
- Inconsistent application
- Lack of technical verification
- Insufficient legal review

### Spoliation Implications
- Avoiding appearance of destruction
- Documenting routine deletion
- Maintaining consistent practices
- Preserving documentary evidence
- Legal review at each stage

## Monitoring and Verification

### Ongoing Monitoring
- Track deletion progress
- Verify technical completion
- Monitor for recovery attempts
- Audit deletion logs
- Review stakeholder compliance

### Quality Assurance
- Sample verification of deletion
- Spot-check recovered data
- Review deletion logs
- Confirm proper authorization
- Validate backup removal
