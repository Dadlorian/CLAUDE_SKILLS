# Sedona Conference E-Discovery Principles

## Overview

The Sedona Conference is a nonprofit research and educational institute dedicated to the advanced study of law and policy in areas of antitrust law, complex litigation, and intellectual property rights. The Sedona Conference Cooperation Proclamation and Best Practices Commentary provide influential guidance on e-discovery, frequently cited by courts and adopted by practitioners.

## Core Principles

### The Sedona Principles (Third Edition, 2017)

These 14 principles represent best practices for electronic document production.

---

### Principle 1: Proportionality

**"An organization's decisions about the preservation, identification, and production of electronically stored information should be based on a practical assessment of the value and importance of the information in the context of the litigation, taking into consideration proportionality and party needs, as well as considerations of technical feasibility, and cost."**

**Key Concepts**:
- ESI decisions must consider proportionality
- Balance burden vs. benefit
- Align with FRCP 26(b)(1) proportionality factors
- Consider technical feasibility and cost
- Tailor approach to case needs and value

**Practical Application**:
- Assess case value early
- Consider amount in controversy
- Evaluate importance of issues
- Balance discovery burden with likely benefit
- Use sampling to assess value before full collection
- Negotiate reasonable scope with opposing counsel

---

### Principle 2: Cooperation

**"An attorney's zealous representation of a client is not compromised by conducting discovery in a cooperative manner. The failure of counsel or the parties to litigation to cooperate in creating a reasonable ESI discovery plan may result in unreasonable and disproportionate costs, delays, and litigation."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Cooperation does not compromise zealous advocacy
- Early cooperation saves time and money
- Reduces motion practice and disputes
- Courts favor and reward cooperation
- Transparency builds trust and efficiency

**Practical Application**:
- Discuss ESI issues early (Rule 26(f) conference)
- Share information about data sources and volumes
- Collaborate on search methodologies
- Negotiate reasonable protocols
- Resolve disputes informally when possible
- Document cooperative efforts (courts notice)

**Case Law Support**:
- *Da Silva Moore v. Publicis Groupe*: Cooperation praised in TAR approval
- *Mancia v. Mayflower Textile Services*: Cooperation on search terms
- *Rio Tinto v. Vale*: Transparent TAR methodology

---

### Principle 3: Early Case Assessment

**"As soon as litigation is reasonably anticipated, organizations should take reasonable steps to ensure that potentially relevant electronically stored information is not lost, destroyed, or altered."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Early assessment reduces costs and risks
- Identify key data sources and custodians
- Understand data landscape before full preservation
- Use ECA tools to assess volume and relevance
- Inform strategy and settlement discussions

**Practical Application**:
- Conduct preliminary data interviews
- Estimate data volumes and locations
- Sample data for relevance assessment
- Use analytics for early insights
- Develop case strategy based on early findings
- Inform settlement negotiations

**Tools**:
- Early case assessment software
- Processing samples for analysis
- Analytics and clustering
- Data mapping and interviews

---

### Principle 4: Preservation

**"An organization's actions should be reasonable and consistent with its own preservation obligations and not interfere with the preservation obligations of other parties."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Duty to preserve when litigation reasonably anticipated
- Reasonable steps, not perfection
- Balance preservation with business operations
- Document preservation efforts
- Over-preserve rather than under-preserve

**Practical Application**:
- Issue litigation hold notices promptly
- Identify key custodians and data sources
- Suspend auto-delete and retention policies
- Monitor compliance with holds
- Document all preservation steps
- Release holds when appropriate

**Reasonable Steps** (Not Required):
- Not required to preserve every document
- Not required to change business operations
- Not required to preserve inaccessible backup data (unless highly relevant)
- Not required to preserve duplicates (if originals preserved)

---

### Principle 5: Identification of Relevant ESI

**"The identification of potentially relevant electronically stored information should be conducted using methods that are appropriate for the particular matter, considering the parties' resources and capabilities, the importance of the case or issue, the amount in controversy, and the likely benefits and burdens of the various methods considered."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Tailor identification methods to case
- Consider resources and capabilities
- Use appropriate technology
- Balance cost and comprehensiveness
- Defensible process more important than perfect results

**Methods**:
- Keyword searching
- Predictive coding / TAR
- Concept clustering
- Sampling and extrapolation
- Targeted custodian identification
- Hybrid approaches

**Practical Application**:
- Assess data volume and complexity
- Select search methodology collaboratively
- Test and validate search terms
- Document methodology and results
- Be prepared to explain and justify

---

### Principle 6: Collection

**"The collection of potentially relevant electronically stored information should be done in a manner that is reasonably calculated to preserve the integrity of that information and protect the legitimate interests of other interested persons, while minimizing unnecessary disruption of an organization's ongoing operations."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Forensically sound collection when appropriate
- Preserve metadata and integrity
- Minimize business disruption
- Balance thoroughness with practicality
- Document collection methods

**Practical Application**:
- Use certified collection tools
- Preserve native files and metadata
- Maintain chain of custody
- Hash files for verification
- Document collection process
- Coordinate timing to minimize disruption

**Collection Methods** (Based on Need):
- **Forensic imaging**: High-stakes matters, suspected spoliation
- **Logical collection**: Most e-discovery matters
- **Remote collection**: Distributed custodians
- **Cloud collection**: SaaS and cloud data
- **Self-collection**: Low-stakes, trusted custodians

---

### Principle 7: Information Governance

**"An organization should have reasonable policies and procedures for managing its information and records."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Proactive information governance reduces e-discovery costs
- Policies should address creation, retention, and disposal
- Regular training and enforcement
- Data mapping and inventories
- Litigation readiness

**Components of Information Governance**:
- Records retention policies
- Data classification schemes
- Disposition procedures (defensible deletion)
- Litigation hold procedures
- Employee training
- Regular audits and compliance monitoring

**Benefits**:
- Reduced data volumes
- Lower e-discovery costs
- Faster response to litigation
- Regulatory compliance
- Risk mitigation

---

### Principle 8: Search and Review

**"Requesting parties and responding parties should cooperate in the identification of appropriate search and review techniques. Requesting parties should be willing to discuss and narrow the scope of the request, and responding parties should be willing to provide appropriate information about their ESI systems."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Collaboration on search methodology
- Transparency about data systems
- Iterative refinement of searches
- Testing and validation
- Sampling to assess results

**Practical Application**:
- Share information about data systems
- Collaboratively develop search terms
- Test search terms on sample sets
- Refine based on results
- Consider TAR and advanced analytics
- Document methodology and decisions

**Best Practices**:
- Start broad, refine iteratively
- Use multiple search methods (keywords, concepts, analytics)
- Validate search effectiveness
- Be transparent with opposing counsel
- Document all search iterations

---

### Principle 9: Production

**"Requesting parties should clearly indicate how they want electronically stored information to be produced. Responding parties should communicate in advance of production any limitations or conditions imposed on their production."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Clear production specifications
- Early agreement on format
- Communication of limitations
- Quality control before delivery

**Format Decisions**:
- Native vs. image production
- Metadata fields to include
- De-duplication approach
- Redaction method
- Load file format

**Rule 26(f) Discussion**:
- Form of production
- Metadata to include
- Naming conventions
- Delivery method
- Production schedule

---

### Principle 10: Privileged Information

**"A responding party may withhold electronically stored information on the ground of privilege or work product protection. The requesting party may seek to establish the waiver of privilege or work product protection, or challenge the assertion of privilege or work product protection."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Privilege applies to ESI
- Rigorous privilege review required
- Privilege logs must be provided
- Inadvertent disclosure procedures (Rule 26(b)(5)(B))
- Claw-back and quick-peek agreements

**Practical Application**:
- Implement thorough privilege review process
- Use TAR for privilege screening
- Create detailed privilege logs
- Negotiate claw-back agreements (Rule 26(f))
- Fed. R. Evid. 502(d) orders for protection
- Document privilege review methodology

**Fed. R. Evid. 502**:
- Section (b): Subject matter waiver limitations
- Section (d): Court orders protecting against waiver
- Section (e): Party agreements can prevent waiver

---

### Principle 11: Form of Production

**"Absent a specific request or agreement to the contrary, electronically stored information should be produced in a reasonably usable form that balances the producing party's burden and the requesting party's needs."**

**Key Concepts**:
- "Reasonably usable" standard
- Balance burden and need
- Default: format ordinarily maintained
- Requesting party can specify form
- Metadata preservation

**Common Forms**:
- **Native**: Databases, spreadsheets with formulas
- **PDF**: Standard documents, searchable
- **TIFF with load files**: Standard production format
- **Hybrid**: Native for complex, images for simple

**Metadata Considerations**:
- Define metadata fields to produce
- Standard vs. extended metadata
- Embedded metadata handling
- Privacy scrubbing if needed

---

### Principle 12: Cost-Shifting

**"In most situations, the reasonable costs of retrieving and reviewing electronically stored information should be borne by the responding party. In unusual situations, the court may require the requesting party to contribute to the cost of retrieving and reviewing electronically stored information."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Presumption: responding party bears costs
- Exception: inaccessible or unduly burdensome data
- Court applies multi-factor test (Zubulake factors)
- Cost-sharing for sampling before full production

**Zubulake Cost-Shifting Factors**:
1. Extent to which request is specifically tailored
2. Availability of information from other sources
3. Total cost of production
4. Ability of party to control costs
5. Resources available to each party
6. Relative benefits to parties
7. Importance of issues at stake

**Practical Application**:
- Estimate costs early
- Negotiate cost-sharing if appropriate
- Use sampling to demonstrate burden
- Consider alternatives to reduce costs
- Document costs for potential cost-shifting motion

---

### Principle 13: Emerging Technologies

**"Attorneys and parties should be knowledgeable about emerging technologies that may be used in their cases and be prepared to address their implications."**

**Key Concepts**:
- Duty of technology competence (ABA Model Rule 1.1)
- Stay informed about new technologies
- Understand AI, machine learning, blockchain
- Address new data sources (cloud, mobile, IoT)
- Ethical use of technology

**Emerging Areas**:
- Artificial intelligence and machine learning
- Ephemeral messaging (Snapchat, Signal)
- Collaboration tools (Slack, Teams, Zoom)
- Blockchain and cryptocurrency
- Cloud-native applications
- Social media and web scraping
- Internet of Things (IoT) data

**Technology Competence**:
- Understand basics of relevant technology
- Consult with experts when needed
- Stay current through CLE and training
- Recognize limitations and seek assistance

---

### Principle 14: Continuing Dialogue

**"Electronic discovery requires an ongoing dialogue between counsel and parties. Counsel should meet and confer on ESI issues throughout the litigation lifecycle."**

**Key Concepts**:
- E-discovery is not one-time event
- Continuous communication required
- Regular check-ins on status and issues
- Flexibility to adjust approach
- Escalate disputes early

**Practical Application**:
- Schedule regular ESI conferences
- Update on processing and review progress
- Discuss issues as they arise
- Adjust protocols based on experience
- Document all communications
- Resolve disputes collaboratively before court involvement

## Additional Sedona Conference Guidance

### Cooperation Proclamation (2008, Updated 2014)

**"Counsel and parties can and should cooperate in discovery."**

**Core Concepts**:
- Cooperation is consistent with zealous advocacy
- Early, open, and substantive dialogue
- Transparency about capabilities and burdens
- Creative solutions to reduce costs
- Good faith in all discovery activities

**Practical Steps**:
1. Communicate early and often
2. Be candid about capabilities and limitations
3. Seek to understand opposing party's needs
4. Propose reasonable solutions
5. Resolve disputes before court involvement
6. Document cooperative efforts

### Commentary on Proportionality in Electronic Discovery

**Key Points**:
- Proportionality is central to FRCP
- Consider all Rule 26(b)(1) factors
- Use technology to achieve proportionality
- Sampling to assess scope and cost
- Phased discovery approaches

### Best Practices Commentary on the Use of Search & Information Retrieval Methods in E-Discovery

**Topics**:
- Keyword search development and testing
- Concept searching and clustering
- Predictive coding and TAR
- Sampling and validation
- Transparency and cooperation on search methods

## Practical Implementation

### Rule 26(f) Conference Preparation

Use Sedona Principles to structure discussion:
1. **Preservation**: Describe preservation efforts (Principle 4)
2. **Scope**: Discuss proportionality (Principle 1)
3. **Search**: Propose collaborative search methodology (Principles 5, 8)
4. **Production**: Agree on format and metadata (Principles 9, 11)
5. **Privilege**: Negotiate claw-back agreement (Principle 10)
6. **Cooperation**: Commit to ongoing dialogue (Principles 2, 14)

### Discovery Plan Template

**Based on Sedona Principles**:

1. **Preservation** (Principle 4):
   - Steps taken to preserve ESI
   - Custodians and data sources preserved
   - Timing and scope of preservation

2. **Identification and Search** (Principles 5, 8):
   - Methodology for identifying relevant ESI
   - Search terms, concepts, or TAR approach
   - Validation and testing procedures
   - Iterative refinement process

3. **Production** (Principles 9, 11):
   - Form of production (native, TIFF, PDF)
   - Metadata fields to include
   - De-duplication approach
   - Delivery method and schedule

4. **Privilege** (Principle 10):
   - Privilege review procedures
   - Privilege log format and timing
   - Claw-back agreement
   - Fed. R. Evid. 502(d) order proposed

5. **Cost Management** (Principle 12):
   - Estimated volumes and costs
   - Cost allocation if applicable
   - Phased approach to manage costs

6. **Cooperation** (Principles 2, 14):
   - Commitment to ongoing communication
   - Regular status updates
   - Dispute resolution procedures
   - Points of contact

## Conclusion

The Sedona Conference Principles represent industry consensus on e-discovery best practices. They emphasize proportionality, cooperation, and the reasonable use of technology to manage ESI efficiently and defensibly. Courts regularly cite and rely on Sedona guidance, making familiarity with these principles essential for e-discovery practitioners. Applying Sedona Principles leads to more efficient, cost-effective, and defensible e-discovery processes that serve the interests of justice while managing the unique challenges of electronic information.
