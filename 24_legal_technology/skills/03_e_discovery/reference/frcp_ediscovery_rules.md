# Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP) - E-Discovery Rules

## Overview

The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure govern civil litigation in federal courts. Several rules specifically address electronic discovery (e-discovery), establishing procedures for the identification, preservation, collection, and production of electronically stored information (ESI).

## Key FRCP Rules for E-Discovery

### Rule 16 - Pretrial Conferences; Scheduling; Management

**Rule 16(b)(3)(B)(iii)** - Scheduling Order Must Include:
- Provisions for disclosure, discovery, or preservation of electronically stored information

**Practical Application**:
- Court may establish ESI protocols in scheduling order
- Deadlines for ESI production
- Limits on e-discovery requests
- Procedures for privilege claims
- Cost-shifting provisions

**Best Practices**:
- Propose reasonable ESI provisions
- Address TAR and advanced analytics
- Include dispute resolution procedures
- Define search methodologies
- Establish cooperation protocols

### Rule 26 - Duty to Disclose; General Provisions Governing Discovery

**Rule 26(a)(1)(A) - Initial Disclosures**

Must provide information about:
- Documents and ESI in party's possession, custody, or control
- That may be used to support claims or defenses

**Timing**: Within 14 days of Rule 26(f) conference (unless otherwise stipulated)

**Rule 26(b)(1) - Scope of Discovery - Proportionality**

Discovery must be proportional to the needs of the case, considering:
1. The importance of the issues at stake
2. The amount in controversy
3. The parties' relative access to relevant information
4. The parties' resources
5. The importance of discovery in resolving the issues
6. Whether the burden or expense outweighs the likely benefit

**2015 Amendment Impact**:
- Proportionality moved to Rule 26(b)(1) (previously only in 26(b)(2)(C))
- Courts more likely to limit disproportionate discovery
- Burden on requesting party to justify broad requests
- Cost-benefit analysis required

**Rule 26(b)(2)(B) - Specific Limitations on ESI**

**Not Reasonably Accessible ESI**:
- Party need not provide discovery of ESI from sources that are not reasonably accessible because of undue burden or cost
- Party must identify such sources even if not searching them
- Court may order discovery if requesting party shows good cause
- Court must limit discovery if burden outweighs likely benefit

**Examples of Not Reasonably Accessible**:
- Backup tapes
- Legacy systems no longer in use
- Disaster recovery systems
- Deleted or fragmented data
- Databases requiring significant programming
- Archived offline data

**Rule 26(b)(5)(B) - Privilege Log and Claw-Back**

**Inadvertent Production of Privileged Information**:
- Party who produced privileged information may notify receiving party
- Receiving party must promptly return, sequester, or destroy the information
- Must not use or disclose until privilege claim resolved
- Receiving party may promptly present to court under seal

**Fed. R. Evid. 502 Integration**:
- Federal Rule of Evidence 502 provides additional protections
- Agreements on privilege protections can be enforceable
- Quick-peek and claw-back agreements encouraged

**Rule 26(f) - Conference of the Parties; Planning for Discovery**

**Required Topics for ESI**:
Parties must confer and must discuss:

1. **Preservation of ESI**:
   - What information should be preserved
   - Form of preservation
   - Duration of preservation

2. **Production of ESI**:
   - Form of production (native, TIFF, PDF)
   - Metadata to be produced
   - De-duplication approach

3. **Privilege Protection**:
   - Procedures for asserting privilege
   - Claw-back agreements
   - Privilege log format and timing

4. **ESI Not Reasonably Accessible**:
   - Identification of such sources
   - Reasons for inaccessibility
   - Potential cost-sharing

**Timing**: At least 21 days before scheduling conference with court

**Deliverable**: Discovery plan submitted to court

**Best Practices**:
- Prepare thoroughly before conference
- Bring technical knowledge (IT staff or consultant)
- Propose specific ESI protocols
- Address search methodologies (including TAR)
- Seek cooperation and reasonableness
- Document all agreements

### Rule 34 - Producing Documents, Electronically Stored Information, and Tangible Things

**Rule 34(a) - Scope**

May request:
- Documents and ESI in responding party's possession, custody, or control
- Including writings, drawings, graphs, charts, photographs, sound recordings, images, and data compilations

**Rule 34(b) - Procedure**

**Request Requirements**:
- Describe with reasonable particularity
- Specify form for producing ESI (if desired)

**Response Requirements**:
- Must state objections
- Must produce documents or permit inspection
- Must produce as kept in usual course of business or organized to correspond to request categories
- If objecting to form, must state form to be used

**Rule 34(b)(1)(C) - Form of Production**

**Requesting Party May Specify Form**:
- Native format
- Reasonably usable form (searchable PDF, TIFF with text)
- Form ordinarily maintained
- Reasonably usable form

**If No Form Specified**:
- Produce in form ordinarily maintained
- Or in reasonably usable form

**Rule 34(b)(2)(B) - Organization of Documents**

Must produce:
- As kept in usual course of business, or
- Organized and labeled to correspond to categories in request

**Rule 34(b)(2)(C) - Metadata**

If producing in form ordinarily maintained:
- Must produce metadata
- Unless parties agree or court orders otherwise

**Rule 34(b)(2)(E) - ESI Production Requirements**

If ESI produced:
- Need not produce same ESI in more than one form
- Unless specifically requested and reasonable

### Rule 37 - Failure to Make Disclosures or to Cooperate in Discovery; Sanctions

**Rule 37(a) - Motion to Compel**

Available when:
- Party fails to respond to discovery request
- Response is evasive or incomplete
- Party fails to attend deposition

**ESI Context**:
- Court may compel search of "not reasonably accessible" sources
- Court must consider proportionality
- Court may allocate costs

**Rule 37(e) - Failure to Preserve ESI - SAFE HARBOR**

**(e)(1) - No Sanctions If**:
- ESI lost because of routine, good-faith operation of electronic information system
- Party took reasonable steps to preserve
- Information cannot be restored or replaced through additional discovery

**(e)(2) - Sanctions If Prejudice**:
Court may order measures no greater than necessary to cure prejudice, only if:
- ESI should have been preserved
- It is lost because party failed to take reasonable steps to preserve
- It cannot be restored or replaced through additional discovery
- Another party is prejudiced by loss

**Sanctions**:
- Additional discovery
- Adverse jury instruction
- Other sanctions

**(e)(3) - Severe Sanctions If Intent to Deprive**:
Court may impose severe sanctions, including:
- Presumption that lost information was unfavorable
- Adverse jury instruction
- Dismissal or default judgment

**Only if**:
- Party acted with intent to deprive another party of information's use in litigation

**2015 Amendment - Safe Harbor**:
- Previous version allowed sanctions for "negligence"
- New version requires prejudice or intent
- Encourages reasonable preservation efforts
- Recognizes reality of electronic data systems
- Focuses on good faith and reasonable steps

## Preservation Obligations

### Trigger for Preservation

**When Litigation is Reasonably Anticipated**:
- Receipt of demand letter
- Threat of litigation
- Investigation commenced
- Regulatory inquiry
- Knowledge of potential claim

**Duty Arises**:
- Before formal complaint filed
- Based on reasonable anticipation
- Subjective and objective analysis

### Reasonable Steps to Preserve

**What is Required**:
- Suspend auto-deletion/retention policies
- Issue litigation hold notices
- Identify custodians and data sources
- Preserve relevant ESI
- Monitor compliance
- Document preservation efforts

**What is NOT Required**:
- Preservation of every document
- Heroic measures or unreasonable expense
- Changing business operations
- Preservation of duplicates (if originals preserved)
- Retention of inaccessible data (unless highly relevant)

### Spoliation and Sanctions

**Spoliation**: Destruction or alteration of evidence

**Elements for Sanctions**:
1. Duty to preserve existed
2. Evidence was destroyed or altered
3. Destruction was culpable (negligent, reckless, or intentional)
4. Evidence was relevant
5. Prejudice to requesting party

**Sanctions Spectrum**:
- Curative measures (additional discovery, cost-shifting)
- Adverse inference instruction
- Preclusion of evidence
- Dismissal or default judgment (most severe)

## E-Discovery Best Practices Under FRCP

### 1. Early Case Assessment

- Identify custodians and data sources immediately
- Estimate data volumes and costs
- Assess proportionality
- Plan discovery strategy

### 2. Preservation

- Act immediately when litigation anticipated
- Issue comprehensive legal hold notices
- Suspend auto-delete and retention
- Document all preservation efforts
- Over-preserve rather than under-preserve

### 3. Rule 26(f) Conference

- Prepare detailed ESI plan
- Bring IT or e-discovery expertise
- Propose specific methodologies
- Seek cooperation and agreement
- Document all discussions

### 4. ESI Protocols

**Include**:
- Search methodology (keywords, TAR, analytics)
- Form of production (native, TIFF, PDF)
- Metadata fields to produce
- De-duplication approach (global, custodial)
- Privilege protection (claw-back, quick peek)
- Cost allocation
- Dispute resolution procedures

### 5. Proportionality Analysis

**Consider**:
- Case value (damages sought)
- Importance of issues
- Complexity of matter
- Cost of discovery vs. benefit
- Parties' resources
- Alternative methods

**Document**:
- Analysis of proportionality factors
- Decisions to limit or expand discovery
- Rationale for methodology chosen

### 6. Cooperation

- Communicate early and often with opposing counsel
- Share information about data sources
- Negotiate reasonable protocols
- Resolve disputes without court intervention
- Document cooperative efforts (courts favor cooperation)

### 7. Privilege Protection

- Implement quality privilege review
- Use privilege screening tools (TAR)
- Create detailed privilege logs
- Negotiate claw-back agreements
- Document privilege review process

### 8. Production Quality Control

- Validate productions before delivery
- Test load files and images
- Verify metadata accuracy
- Document production specifications
- Maintain production logs

### 9. Documentation

- Document all e-discovery decisions
- Maintain audit trails
- Preserve evidence of reasonable steps
- Create declarations if needed
- Support defensibility

## Common FRCP E-Discovery Issues

### Issue: Proportionality Disputes

**Resolution**:
- Burden on requesting party to justify broad requests
- Court weighs Rule 26(b)(1) factors
- Use sampling to demonstrate burden/utility
- Propose alternatives (keywords, TAR, date limits)
- Document proportionality analysis

### Issue: Form of Production

**Resolution**:
- Requesting party specifies form
- Producing party may object if unreasonable
- Default: form ordinarily maintained or reasonably usable
- Consider native for databases, spreadsheets with formulas
- TIFF/PDF acceptable for most documents

### Issue: Metadata

**Resolution**:
- Define metadata fields to be produced
- Standard fields: author, dates, recipients, file path
- Extended metadata: track changes, comments, hidden data
- Some metadata lost in TIFF production
- Agreement on metadata critical

### Issue: Not Reasonably Accessible Data

**Resolution**:
- Identify and describe such sources
- Explain burden and cost
- Requesting party must show good cause
- Court may order sampling
- Cost-shifting possible

### Issue: Preservation Failures

**Resolution**:
- Assess when duty triggered
- Determine reasonableness of steps taken
- Evaluate prejudice
- Consider intent
- Rule 37(e) safe harbor analysis

## Notable Case Law

### Zubulake v. UBS Warburg (S.D.N.Y. 2003-2004)

**Holdings**:
- Duty to preserve arises when litigation reasonably anticipated
- Litigation hold notices required
- Counsel must ensure preservation efforts followed
- Failure to preserve can result in sanctions

**Impact**:
- Established preservation standards
- Defined counsel's role in preservation
- Created framework for cost-shifting

### Pension Committee v. Banc of America (S.D.N.Y. 2010)

**Holdings**:
- Gross negligence in preservation warrants sanctions
- Written litigation hold notices required
- Must identify key players and preserve their documents
- Must communicate litigation hold to relevant employees

**Impact**:
- Heightened preservation standards
- Emphasis on documentation
- Sanctions for inadequate preservation efforts

### Victor Stanley v. Creative Pipe (D. Md. 2008)

**Holdings**:
- Keyword search methodology subject to scrutiny
- Must demonstrate reasonableness of search terms
- Disclosure of search terms may be required
- Inadequate privilege review can waive privilege

**Impact**:
- Transparency in search methodology
- Testing and validation of keywords
- Quality control in privilege review

### Da Silva Moore v. Publicis Groupe (S.D.N.Y. 2012)

**Holdings**:
- Predictive coding/TAR acceptable discovery method
- Cooperation and transparency required
- Methodology must be defensible
- Equal to or better than manual review

**Impact**:
- Validated TAR for e-discovery
- Encouraged cooperation on methodology
- Set standards for TAR protocols
