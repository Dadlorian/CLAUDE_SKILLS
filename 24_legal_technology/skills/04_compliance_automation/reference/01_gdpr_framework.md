# GDPR Framework Reference

## General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) - EU 2016/679

### Overview

The General Data Protection Regulation is the European Union's comprehensive data protection law that came into effect on May 25, 2018. It regulates the processing of personal data of individuals in the EU and EEA, with extraterritorial reach affecting organizations worldwide.

### Territorial Scope (Article 3)

GDPR applies to:
- Organizations established in the EU processing personal data
- Organizations outside the EU offering goods/services to EU residents
- Organizations outside the EU monitoring behavior of EU residents

### Key Principles (Article 5)

1. **Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency**
   - Processing must have legal basis
   - Data subjects must be informed
   - Processing must be fair and transparent

2. **Purpose Limitation**
   - Data collected for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes
   - Cannot be processed for incompatible purposes
   - Further processing for archiving, research, or statistics allowed with safeguards

3. **Data Minimization**
   - Adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary
   - Only collect data needed for the purpose
   - Avoid excessive data collection

4. **Accuracy**
   - Personal data must be accurate and kept up to date
   - Inaccurate data must be erased or rectified without delay
   - Reasonable steps to ensure accuracy

5. **Storage Limitation**
   - Kept only as long as necessary for the purpose
   - Longer retention for archiving, research, or statistics with safeguards
   - Clear retention periods required

6. **Integrity and Confidentiality (Security)**
   - Appropriate security measures
   - Protection against unauthorized or unlawful processing
   - Protection against accidental loss, destruction, or damage

7. **Accountability**
   - Controller responsible for compliance
   - Must be able to demonstrate compliance
   - Implement appropriate technical and organizational measures

### Legal Bases for Processing (Article 6)

Processing is lawful only if at least one applies:

1. **Consent** (Article 6(1)(a))
   - Freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous
   - Clear affirmative action required
   - Must be as easy to withdraw as to give
   - Separate from other terms and conditions

2. **Contract** (Article 6(1)(b))
   - Necessary for performance of contract
   - Necessary for pre-contractual steps

3. **Legal Obligation** (Article 6(1)(c))
   - Required by EU or Member State law
   - Must be a clear legal obligation

4. **Vital Interests** (Article 6(1)(d))
   - Necessary to protect life of data subject or another person
   - Used rarely, typically in emergency situations

5. **Public Task** (Article 6(1)(e))
   - Performance of task in public interest
   - Exercise of official authority

6. **Legitimate Interests** (Article 6(1)(f))
   - Legitimate interests of controller or third party
   - Must balance against data subject rights
   - Not available for public authorities
   - Requires legitimate interest assessment (LIA)

### Special Categories of Data (Article 9)

**Prohibited unless exception applies:**
- Racial or ethnic origin
- Political opinions
- Religious or philosophical beliefs
- Trade union membership
- Genetic data
- Biometric data for unique identification
- Health data
- Sex life or sexual orientation

**Exceptions include:**
- Explicit consent
- Employment, social security, social protection law
- Vital interests (when data subject cannot give consent)
- Legitimate activities of foundations, associations, etc.
- Made public by data subject
- Legal claims or judicial acts
- Substantial public interest
- Health or social care (professional secrecy)
- Public health
- Archiving, research, statistics

### Data Subject Rights

#### Right of Access (Article 15)
- Confirmation of processing
- Access to personal data
- Information about processing (purpose, categories, recipients, retention, rights)
- Copy of data (first copy free)
- Response within 1 month (extendable to 3 months)

#### Right to Rectification (Article 16)
- Correct inaccurate personal data
- Complete incomplete data
- Without undue delay

#### Right to Erasure - "Right to be Forgotten" (Article 17)
- Data no longer necessary for purpose
- Withdrawal of consent (where consent was basis)
- Objection to processing (no overriding grounds)
- Unlawful processing
- Legal obligation to erase
- Child's data collected for information society services

**Exceptions:**
- Freedom of expression and information
- Legal obligations
- Public health
- Archiving, research, statistics
- Legal claims

#### Right to Restriction of Processing (Article 18)
- Accuracy is contested
- Processing is unlawful but erasure opposed
- No longer needed but required for legal claims
- Objection pending verification

#### Right to Data Portability (Article 20)
- Receive personal data in structured, commonly used, machine-readable format
- Transmit to another controller
- Only applies to automated processing based on consent or contract
- Must be technically feasible

#### Right to Object (Article 21)
- Grounds relating to particular situation (for public task or legitimate interests)
- Absolute right for direct marketing
- Automated decision-making including profiling

#### Rights Related to Automated Decision-Making (Article 22)
- Right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal/significant effects
- Exceptions: necessary for contract, authorized by law, explicit consent
- Right to human intervention, express view, contest decision

### Controller Obligations

#### Data Protection by Design and Default (Article 25)
- Implement appropriate technical and organizational measures
- Consider state of the art, costs, nature, scope, context, risks
- Only process data necessary for each purpose
- Pseudonymization where possible
- Privacy-enhancing technologies

#### Records of Processing Activities (Article 30)
Required information:
- Name and contact details of controller/processor
- Purposes of processing
- Categories of data subjects and personal data
- Categories of recipients
- International transfers
- Retention periods
- Security measures description

**Exemption:** Organizations <250 employees (unless high risk, regular processing, or special categories)

#### Security of Processing (Article 32)
Implement appropriate measures:
- Pseudonymization and encryption
- Confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience
- Ability to restore availability and access
- Regular testing and evaluation
- Risk-based approach

#### Data Breach Notification (Articles 33-34)

**To Supervisory Authority (Article 33):**
- Within 72 hours of becoming aware
- Unless unlikely to result in risk to rights and freedoms
- Required information: nature of breach, categories and numbers affected, DPO contact, consequences, measures taken/proposed
- Can be phased notification if not all information available

**To Data Subjects (Article 34):**
- Without undue delay
- If high risk to rights and freedoms
- Clear and plain language
- Same information as to authority plus specific actions for data subjects
- **Exceptions:** Encryption or other measures render data unintelligible, subsequent measures eliminate high risk, disproportionate effort (public communication instead)

#### Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) (Article 35)
Required when likely to result in high risk, particularly:
- Systematic and extensive automated processing including profiling
- Large-scale processing of special categories or criminal data
- Systematic monitoring of publicly accessible area at large scale

**DPIA must contain:**
- Description of processing and purposes
- Assessment of necessity and proportionality
- Assessment of risks to data subjects
- Measures to address risks

**Consult supervisory authority if high residual risk**

#### Data Protection Officer (DPO) (Article 37)
Required for:
- Public authorities (except courts)
- Core activities consist of regular and systematic monitoring at large scale
- Core activities consist of large-scale processing of special categories or criminal data

**DPO requirements:**
- Expert knowledge of data protection
- Sufficient resources
- Independence
- Reports to highest management
- No conflict of interest
- Contact point for data subjects and supervisory authority

### International Data Transfers (Chapter V)

#### Adequacy Decisions (Article 45)
- European Commission determines adequate protection level
- Current adequate countries: Andorra, Argentina, Canada (commercial), Faroe Islands, Guernsey, Israel, Isle of Man, Japan, Jersey, New Zealand, South Korea, Switzerland, UK, Uruguay
- EU-US Data Privacy Framework (2023)

#### Appropriate Safeguards (Article 46)
- Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)
- Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)
- Approved codes of conduct or certification mechanisms
- Ad hoc contractual clauses approved by supervisory authority

**Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) required:**
- Assess laws and practices in destination country
- Evaluate if supplementary measures needed
- Document the assessment
- Based on Schrems II ruling

#### Derogations for Specific Situations (Article 49)
- Explicit consent (after informing of risks)
- Contract performance
- Legal claims
- Public interest
- Vital interests
- Public register
- Compelling legitimate interests (occasional, limited, safeguarded)

### Supervisory Authorities and Enforcement

#### Lead Supervisory Authority
- Main establishment determines lead authority
- One-stop-shop mechanism for cross-border processing
- Cooperation and consistency mechanisms

#### Powers of Supervisory Authorities (Article 58)
- Investigative powers (audits, access, information)
- Corrective powers (warnings, reprimands, orders, limitations, bans, fines)
- Authorization and advisory powers

#### Administrative Fines (Article 83)
**Tier 1 - Up to €10 million or 2% of global annual turnover:**
- Controller/processor obligations
- Certification body obligations
- Monitoring body obligations

**Tier 2 - Up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover:**
- Basic principles of processing (Article 5)
- Legal bases for processing (Article 6)
- Consent conditions (Article 7)
- Special categories of data (Article 9)
- Data subject rights (Articles 12-22)
- International transfers (Articles 44-49)
- Member State law obligations (Chapter IX)
- Non-compliance with SA orders

**Factors considered:**
- Nature, gravity, duration of infringement
- Intentional or negligent
- Actions to mitigate damage
- Degree of responsibility
- Previous infringements
- Cooperation with SA
- Categories of data affected
- How SA became aware
- Compliance with previous measures
- Approved codes of conduct or certification
- Aggravating or mitigating factors

### Common GDPR Compliance Challenges

1. **Data Discovery:** Finding all personal data across systems
2. **Legal Basis:** Determining appropriate legal basis for processing
3. **Consent Management:** Obtaining and managing valid consent
4. **Vendor Management:** Ensuring processors comply with GDPR
5. **International Transfers:** Navigating complex transfer mechanisms
6. **DSAR Response:** Responding to requests within 1-month deadline
7. **Breach Notification:** Meeting 72-hour notification requirement
8. **Documentation:** Maintaining comprehensive compliance records
9. **Third-Country Laws:** Addressing government access concerns (Schrems II)
10. **Resource Constraints:** Dedicating sufficient resources to compliance

### GDPR Compliance Checklist

- [ ] Identify all personal data processing activities
- [ ] Document legal basis for each processing activity
- [ ] Create and maintain Records of Processing Activities (RoPA)
- [ ] Update privacy notices and policies
- [ ] Implement consent management mechanisms
- [ ] Establish processes for all data subject rights
- [ ] Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) where required
- [ ] Appoint Data Protection Officer (DPO) if required
- [ ] Review and update data processor agreements
- [ ] Implement appropriate security measures
- [ ] Establish breach notification procedures
- [ ] Document international data transfer mechanisms
- [ ] Implement data protection by design and default
- [ ] Provide privacy training to employees
- [ ] Establish compliance monitoring and auditing
- [ ] Designate EU representative if required
- [ ] Register with supervisory authority if required
- [ ] Maintain compliance documentation and audit trail
