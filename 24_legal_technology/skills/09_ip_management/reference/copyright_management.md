# Copyright Management

## Overview

Copyright management encompasses registration, protection, licensing, and enforcement of original works of authorship including literary, musical, artistic, software, and audiovisual works.

## Copyright Fundamentals

### Types of Protected Works

**Literary Works**:
- Books, novels, short stories
- Poetry, scripts, plays
- Technical documentation
- Software code
- Database compilations

**Musical Works**:
- Musical compositions
- Lyrics and songwriting
- Instrumental arrangements
- Musical recordings (neighboring rights)

**Artistic Works**:
- Visual art (paintings, sculptures, drawings)
- Photography
- Graphic designs
- Illustrations
- Architectural works

**Audiovisual Works**:
- Motion pictures
- Television programs
- Video recordings
- Animations
- Streaming content

**Software and Digital Works**:
- Source code
- Object code
- User interfaces
- Database structures
- Digital media

### Copyright Protection Timeline

**United States**:
- **Works created 1978+**: Life of author + 70 years
- **Works made for hire**: 95 years from publication or 120 years from creation
- **Published before 1928**: Now in public domain
- **Works created before 1978**: Complex calculations, often already public domain

**International**:
- **WIPO Member countries**: Minimum life + 50 years (often life + 70)
- **Harmonization**: Most developed countries adopted life + 70
- **Berne Convention**: Mutual protection among 170+ signatory nations

### Copyright Ownership

**Automatic Protection**:
- Copyright exists upon creation
- No registration required (Berne Convention)
- Notice not required (post-1989 for US)
- Registration optional but provides legal advantages

**Work-for-Hire Doctrine**:
- Employer owns copyright if within scope of employment
- Written agreement required for independent contractors
- Significantly impacts ownership determination

**Joint Works**:
- Multiple authors create single indivisible work
- Each co-author owns equal undivided interest
- Requires written agreement for license restrictions

## Copyright Registration

### Registration Advantages

**Legal Benefits**:
- Creates public record of copyright claim
- Provides prima facie evidence of validity
- Enables statutory damages and attorney's fees recovery
- Establishes basis for registration certificate

**Statutory Damages** (Infringement):
- **Registered works**: $750-$30,000 per infringement
- **Willful infringement**: Up to $150,000 per infringement
- **Innocent infringement**: As low as $200 per infringement
- Without registration: Limited to actual damages and profits

**Enforcement Benefits**:
- Damages multiplier for registered works
- Attorney's fees recovery available
- Stronger litigation position
- International enforcement basis

### Registration Process (U.S. Copyright Office)

**Filing Requirements**:
- Form CO (online) or Form TX/PA/VA (paper)
- Application fee: $65 (online)
- Copy of work (deposit)
- Identification of claimant
- Detailed description

**Examination Timeline**:
- Standard examination: 4-6 months
- Expedited examination: 2-3 months (surcharge $800)
- Office action possible for unclear/incomplete submission
- Registration certificate issued upon approval

**Deposit Requirements**:
- **Published works**: Two copies of best edition
- **Unpublished works**: One complete copy or identifying portion
- **Software**: Partial source code acceptable (with redaction)
- **Online submission**: Digital deposit acceptable

**Costs**:
- Single registration: $65
- Multiple works bundle: Various fee structures
- Digital submission: Standard pricing
- Expedited examination: +$800

### International Copyright Registration

**Berne Convention Benefits**:
- Automatic protection in all 170+ signatory countries
- No registration required in most countries
- National treatment principle (same protection as nationals)
- Mutual enforcement obligations

**Optional National Registrations**:
- UK Copyright Service: £52-£78 (national registry)
- Various countries: Optional registry systems
- Value varies by enforcement mechanisms
- Often combined with Berne protection

**WIPO Services**:
- International Literary and Artistic Property System
- Document deposit services
- Certificate issuance
- Notarization services

## Copyright Licenses and Permissions

### License Types

**Exclusive Licenses**:
- Grant entire right to licensee
- Licensor cannot exercise right during term
- Superior enforcement position for licensee
- Typically highest royalty rates

**Non-Exclusive Licenses**:
- Licensor retains right and can license others
- More affordable for licensee
- Common for educational, promotional use
- Lower royalty rates typical

**Compulsory Licenses**:
- Statutory right to license without permission
- **Music reproduction**: Mechanical licenses, 10.5 cents per copy statutory rate
- **Cable television**: Retransmission licenses
- **Satellite television**: Licensing regime
- Royalty rates set by statute or Copyright Royalty Board

### License Provisions

**Key Licensing Terms**:
- Scope of rights (exclusive, non-exclusive, limited)
- Geographic territory (worldwide, regional, national)
- Duration (limited term, perpetual, renewable)
- Field of use (specific industries, formats)
- Royalty structure (flat fee, percentage, per-unit)
- Sublicense rights (permitted or restricted)
- Attribution requirements (credit notices)
- Warranty and indemnification

**Royalty Models**:
- Percentage of revenue: 5-15% typical for content
- Per-unit royalties: Fixed amount per copy
- Advance against royalties: Guaranteed minimum
- Flat fees: Fixed licensing cost
- Combination: Mix of above structures

## Copyright Protection Strategies

### Practical Protection Measures

**Registration**:
- Register all original works with Copyright Office
- Create registration evidence trail
- Facilitate enforcement actions

**Notice and Attribution**:
- Use copyright notice: © [Year] [Owner Name] All Rights Reserved
- Include notices on all published copies
- Maintain proper attribution
- Digital metadata embedding

**Licensing**:
- Use clear license agreements
- Define permitted and prohibited uses
- Specify attribution requirements
- Enable third-party enforcement

**Digital Protection**:
- Digital rights management (DRM)
- Watermarking and metadata
- Access controls and authentication
- Licensing management systems

**Contractual**:
- Work-for-hire agreements with creators
- Non-disclosure agreements
- Indemnification provisions
- Ownership transfer agreements

### Trade Secret Strategies

**Distinction from Copyright**:
- Copyright: Fixed expression of ideas
- Trade secrets: Ideas, methods, processes
- Software: May be both copyright and trade secret
- Customer lists: Trade secret (not copyrightable)

**Maintaining Trade Secret Status**:
- Limit distribution and access
- Implement security measures
- Require NDAs from recipients
- Avoid public disclosure
- Maintain secrecy for reasonable commercial value

## Copyright Enforcement

### Infringement Analysis

**Elements of Infringement**:
1. Valid copyright ownership/registration
2. Unauthorized reproduction, distribution, display, or performance
3. Substantial similarity between works
4. Access to original work by infringer

**Proof Requirements**:
- Ownership documentation
- Evidence of access
- Comparison showing similarity
- Causal connection (independent creation defense)

### Enforcement Options

**Informal Enforcement**:
- Cease and desist letter
- Demand for damages
- Injunction request
- Settlement negotiations

**Administrative**:
- Copyright Office takedown procedures
- DMCA Section 512 notices
- Platform reporting (YouTube, social media)
- ISP notification procedures

**Legal Actions**:
- Federal court infringement suit
- Damages: Actual damages + profits or statutory damages
- Preliminary injunctions: Prevent continued infringement
- Impounding of infringing materials
- Attorney's fees: Registered works in favorable cases

### DMCA Section 512 (Safe Harbor)

**Notice-and-Takedown Procedures**:
- Copyright owner sends notice to ISP/platform
- ISP removes infringing material
- Protects ISP from secondary liability
- Infringer can send counter-notice

**Effective Notice Requirements**:
- Physical or electronic signature
- Identification of copyrighted work
- Identification of infringing material
- Contact information
- Declaration under penalty of perjury
- Agent certification for organizations

## Copyright in Specific Contexts

### Software Copyright

**Protection**:
- Copyright automatically protects source and object code
- Registration recommended for enforcement benefits
- Dual protection: Copyright + patent possible

**Licensing Models**:
- Proprietary: Restrictive, exclusive licensing
- Open source: GPL, MIT, Apache licenses
- Hybrid: Commercial + free editions
- Subscription: SaaS licensing models

### Music Copyright

**Licensing Complexity**:
- Composition rights (songs)
- Recording rights (specific recordings)
- Mechanical licenses: Reproduction
- Performance licenses: PROs (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC)
- Synchronization licenses: Film/TV use

### Digital Media

**Special Considerations**:
- Streaming licensing (Spotify, Apple Music)
- Aggregation and distribution
- Metadata requirements
- Territory restrictions
- Royalty accounting

## Best Practices

1. **Proactive Registration**
   - Register all original works promptly
   - Maintain organized registration records
   - Consider expedited registration for valuable works

2. **Comprehensive Licensing**
   - Use clear, detailed license agreements
   - Specify permitted and prohibited uses
   - Include proper notice and attribution

3. **Digital Protection**
   - Implement DRM for sensitive works
   - Use watermarking and metadata
   - Monitor digital distribution channels

4. **Active Enforcement**
   - Monitor for unauthorized use
   - Swift response to infringement
   - Leverage DMCA takedown procedures
   - Pursue legal action when necessary

5. **Portfolio Management**
   - Maintain detailed ownership records
   - Track licensing agreements
   - Monitor expiration dates
   - Regular portfolio reviews
