## Introduction

Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests are powerful tools for obtaining government documents, exposing agency decision-making processes, and gathering evidence for advocacy campaigns. Effective FOIA management requires strategic planning, systematic tracking, and sophisticated document analysis.

### Why FOIA Matters

- **Government Transparency**: Access to decision-making documents
- **Evidence Gathering**: Support for advocacy positions with official documents
- **Accountability**: Identify inconsistencies, conflicts, or wrongdoing
- **Due Diligence**: Understand agency positions before formal advocacy
- **Public Record**: Create permanent record of government actions
- **Leverage**: Information can influence negotiations and settlement discussions

### FOIA Statistics

- Federal agencies receive 700,000+ FOIA requests annually
- Average processing time: 3-6 months (backlog often much longer)
- Success rate: 50-70% of requested documents released (full or partial)
- Litigation rate: 1-3% of requests eventually litigated
- Cost: $0 to requester but significant government resources

### Strategic Importance

FOIA is most effective as part of integrated advocacy strategy:
- Combine with policy research and analysis
- Integrate into media campaigns
- Support grassroots mobilization
- Strengthen negotiating position
- Build public and political pressure

## FOIA Fundamentals

### The FOIA Law and Process

#### Key FOIA Principles

**Public Right of Access**:
- Any person can request any federal record
- No need to demonstrate interest or justification
- No limit on number of requests from same person
- No fees for searches up to 20 hours
- Agencies must respond within 20 working days

**Exemptions and Limitations**:
- 9 statutory exemptions to FOIA (national security, privacy, deliberative process, etc.)
- Redaction of exempt information allowed
- Agencies must disclose non-exempt portions
- High bar to prove exemptions (courts strictly construe)
- Some exemptions discretionary; others mandatory

**Request Process**:
1. Identify relevant agency
2. Prepare specific request description
3. Submit request (written, email, or online)
4. Agency acknowledges receipt
5. Agency locates and reviews documents
6. Agency releases or withholds documents (with explanation)
7. Requestor can appeal if dissatisfied

### FOIA Exemptions Overview

Understanding exemptions critical for planning strategy:

#### Exemption 1: National Security
- Information properly classified in national defense
- Must follow Executive Order classification procedures
- Difficult to challenge; courts defer to agencies
- Not commonly used in domestic policy matters

#### Exemption 2: Internal Personnel Rules
- Information regarding internal government rules/procedures
- Often misused by agencies to block mundane procedures
- Courts increasingly skeptical of this exemption
- Can often be challenged successfully

#### Exemption 3: Information Prohibited by Other Law
- Information protected by other federal statutes
- Examples: Tax information, trade secrets protected by federal law
- Depends on specific statute

#### Exemption 4: Trade Secrets and Confidential Business Information
- Commercial information protected from public disclosure
- High bar to meet; must genuinely harm business
- Exemption 4 disputes frequently litigated
- Agencies often over-claim this exemption

#### Exemption 5: Deliberative Process Privilege
- Internal communications predecisional in nature
- Attorney-client communications
- Work product of lawyers
- Can be challenged if request made after decision finalized
- Facts in deliberative communications often releasable

#### Exemption 6: Privacy
- Personnel, medical, financial records
- Home addresses and phone numbers
- Frequently used exemption
- Must demonstrate public interest outweighs privacy
- Redaction allowed to protect individual privacy

#### Exemption 7: Law Enforcement
- Information about ongoing investigations
- Protecting confidential sources
- Tactics and procedures used in investigations
- Frequently claimed in FOIA requests

#### Exemption 8: Financial Institutions
- Information from financial institution regulators
- Rarely used; limited applicability

#### Exemption 9: Geological/Geophysical Information
- Information about mineral deposits/energy resources on public land
- Very narrowly applied

### FOIA Process Timeline

```
Federal FOIA Process Timeline

Day 1: Request Submitted
  ├─ Agency receives request
  ├─ Clerk logs request
  └─ Enters queue for processing

Days 1-5: Request Acknowledgment
  ├─ Agency sends acknowledgment letter
  ├─ Assigns case number
  ├─ Provides estimated completion date
  └─ Identifies component processing request

Days 5-20: Request Processing
  ├─ Search for responsive documents
  ├─ Initial review and inventory
  ├─ Legal review for exemptions
  ├─ Consultation with other agencies (if needed)
  └─ Preparation of response

Days 20-30: Response Released
  ├─ Documents released (fully or partially)
  ├─ Explanation of redactions provided
  ├─ Information on appeal process
  └─ Fee statement

Days 30+: Appeal Period
  ├─ Requestor can appeal adverse determination
  ├─ Appeal process adds 30-90 days typically
  ├─ Administrative appeal required before litigation
  └─ Can sue if dissatisfied with appeal

Days 90-1000+: Litigation (if necessary)
  ├─ File suit in federal district court
  ├─ Discovery and briefing
  ├─ Trial or settlement
  └─ Potential appeal
```

**Note**: This is the ideal timeline. Many agencies fall significantly behind, taking 6-18 months or longer.

## Strategic Planning and Request Development

### Identifying FOIA Opportunities

#### Research and Due Diligence

Before filing FOIA request:

**Prior Research**:
- Search agency website for information
- Review public statements and positions
- Identify press releases and announcements
- Check Congressional testimonies
- Review published reports and analysis

**Information Gaps**:
- Identify what information is NOT publicly available
- Determine what documents likely exist
- Assess likelihood of agency holding documents
- Estimate if information is likely responsive

**Strategic Assessment**:
```
FOIA Opportunity Assessment

Question 1: Does our issue/campaign need this information?
├─ How would it strengthen our position?
├─ What specific questions would it answer?
├─ How would it be used in advocacy?
└─ What's the probability of finding useful information?

Question 2: What documents likely exist?
├─ Internal memos discussing issue
├─ Meeting notes or records
├─ Financial records or budgets
├─ Communications with stakeholders
└─ Decision-making documents

Question 3: Will agency likely release?
├─ Are documents likely within FOIA exemptions?
├─ Is there public interest in disclosure?
├─ Has similar information been released?
├─ Is there political sensitivity?

Question 4: Do we have resources for FOIA process?
├─ Time to manage request and appeals
├─ Ability to review potentially large document productions
├─ Capacity for appeals if documents withheld
├─ Budget for potential FOIA litigation
└─ Personnel with FOIA expertise

Overall Assessment: Proceed / Deprioritize / Combine with other requests
```

### Request Formulation Strategy

#### Crafting Effective Requests

**Key Principles**:
- **Specificity**: Narrow, specific requests get better responses
- **Clarity**: Clear language reduces agency confusion
- **Narrowness**: Too broad requests often result in rejections
- **Reasonableness**: Estimate 20-30 hour search/review threshold
- **Feasibility**: Define reasonable time period and scope

**Request Structure**:

```
FOIA Request Template

[Recipient Agency/Component]
[Address]

FREEDOM OF INFORMATION ACT REQUEST

Dear FOIA Officer:

Pursuant to the Freedom of Information Act, 5 U.S.C. § 552, I request
copies of the following records:

[DESCRIPTION OF RECORDS]
Specific description of documents sought, including:
- Nature of documents (emails, memos, reports, etc.)
- Time period covered
- Specific subject matter or issue
- Known recipients or originators (if applicable)
- Any other identifying information

EXAMPLE:
"All emails between [Person A] and [Person B] regarding [Issue],
from January 1, 2023 to June 30, 2023, including attachments."

REQUEST DETAILS

Record Format: [Specify - electronic, paper, etc.]

Fee Waiver: [Request waiver if available and appropriate]

I request a waiver of fees under 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(4)(A)(iii) because
disclosure of these records would be in the public interest as they would
contribute to public understanding of [Agency] decision-making regarding
[Issue].

Public Contact: [Your email and phone]

Sincerely,

[Name]
[Address]
[Email]
[Phone]
```

#### Effective FOIA Language

**Specificity Examples**:

**Too Broad**:
"All documents regarding environmental policy"

**Better**:
"All emails between EPA Administrator and Office of Management and Budget,
January 1-March 31, 2023, discussing the proposed Clean Water Rule"

**Too Narrow/Impossible**:
"The email sent on March 15, 2023 at approximately 2:47 PM"

**Good**:
"All emails sent on March 15, 2023 between 2:00 PM and 3:00 PM by
[Agency FOIA officer name] regarding [Specific issue]"

### Multi-Request Strategy

#### Sequential Requests

Rather than one broad request, consider series:

**Phase 1: Initial Request**
- Core documents most likely to be released
- Establishes search pattern
- Gets baseline response
- Identifies additional request areas

**Phase 2: Follow-up Requests**
- Based on information in Phase 1 responses
- More targeted and specific
- Higher likelihood of release
- Builds on learning from Phase 1

**Phase 3: Appeal or Litigation**
- Focus on documents incorrectly withheld in Phase 1-2
- Based on analysis of redacted/withheld documents
- Use new information to support claims

#### Coalition and Coordinated Requests

**Coordinated Requesting**:
- Multiple organizations request same information
- Increases visibility and pressure
- Creates public record of broad interest
- Agencies more likely to release with multiple requests
- Combined appeals more persuasive

**Request Coordination**:
```
Coalition FOIA Strategy

1. Organize Coalition Meeting
   ├─ Identify shared information needs
   ├─ Assign lead organization
   ├─ Divide responsibilities
   └─ Coordinate timeline

2. Develop Shared Request Language
   ├─ Agree on specific request scope
   ├─ Develop identical or coordinated requests
   ├─ Document strategy and rationale
   └─ Assign tracking responsibility

3. File Requests
   ├─ Each organization files request
   ├─ Submit on same day if possible
   ├─ Coordinate media outreach
   └─ Request expedited processing if merited

4. Share Response
   ├─ Share documents as received
   ├─ Aggregate and organize
   ├─ Conduct joint analysis
   └─ Plan media and advocacy response

5. Coalition Appeals
   ├─ If documents improperly withheld
   ├─ File coordinated appeals
   ├─ Submit joint appeal arguments
   └─ Coordinate media response to appeals
```

#### Expedited Processing

Request expedited processing if merited:

**Criteria for Expedited Processing**:
- Compelling need involving public health/safety
- Involving substantial question of government action
- Information needed for urgency related to news story or deadline
- Public interest in timely disclosure

**Example Request**:
```
REQUEST FOR EXPEDITED PROCESSING

I request expedited processing of this FOIA request pursuant to 5 U.S.C.
§ 552(a)(6)(e) because:

[Statement of compelling need]

The information sought is related to [specific matter] which presents a
substantial question affecting public health/safety/government action, and
the public interest in obtaining the information would be diminished if not
processed quickly.

I am prepared to document the need upon request and to pay any applicable
fees.
```

#### Document Processing and Enhancement

**OCR (Optical Character Recognition)**:
- Convert scanned PDFs to searchable text
- Tools: Adobe Acrobat, Free Online OCR, Tesseract
- Critical for older scanned documents
- Enables text search and analysis

**Transcription**:
- Convert handwritten documents to text
- Tools: Automated (Google Docs, Azure) or manual
- Enables analysis of handwritten notes
- Time-consuming but often worthwhile for important docs

**Redaction Identification**:
- Mark redacted information
- Identify exemption cited
- Cross-reference with withholding log
- Note if redaction appears proper or improper

**Metadata Analysis**:
- Extract creation/modification dates
- Identify document creators and recipients
- Cross-reference with other documents
- Build timeline and relationship map

## Submission and Tracking

### Filing FOIA Requests

#### Request Submission Methods

**Email Submission**:
- Most common method
- Identify agency FOIA officer email
- Subject line: "FOIA Request"
- Attach or paste request text
- Request email confirmation
- Keep copy of sent email

**Online Portals**:
- Many agencies have online systems
- Examples: eOIP (IRS), FOIA.gov (cross-agency portal)
- Upload request document
- Receive confirmation and tracking number
- Often faster than email

**Mail Submission**:
- Send certified mail to FOIA officer
- USPS tracking provides proof of submission
- Slower but creates paper trail
- Useful if email not reliable

**Government-wide FOIA Portal** (FOIA.gov):
- Centralized portal for submitting requests
- Routes to appropriate agency
- Tracks request across government
- Increasingly used by federal agencies

**Multi-Agency Requests**:
- Submit identical requests to multiple agencies
- Can coordinate submission
- Increases visibility and pressure
- Some information held by multiple agencies

#### Request Submission Checklist

```
FOIA Request Submission Checklist

□ Request clearly describes records sought
□ Request is reasonably narrow and specific
□ Request is not overly burdensome
□ Request is sent to correct agency/component
□ Request includes requestor name and contact
□ Request properly formatted
□ Request submitted via official channel
□ Confirmation of receipt obtained
□ Tracking number received
□ Copy filed for records
□ Deadline calendar entry made (20 business days + processing)
□ Fee waiver request included (if appropriate)
□ Expedited processing requested (if appropriate)
□ Expectation set for response timeline
```

#### Response Timeline Management

**Calendar System**:
- Enter 20-business-day deadline in calendar

- Plan appeal strategy if deadline passes


- After 30 days late: Send follow-up letter

**Tracking Agency Performance**:
- Monitor whether agencies meet 20-day deadline

- Track partial vs. full releases

- Request response rate and timeliness
- Full release vs. partial release rate
- Appeal overturn rate
- Litigation success rate

## Response Processing and Analysis

### Reviewing and Analyzing Released Documents

#### Quality Assessment

When documents received:

**Initial Review**:
1. Count pages and documents received
2. Verify completeness (all pages present)
3. Check for misplaced documents
4. Identify duplicates
5. Assess document quality (readability, redactions)

**Redaction Analysis**:
- Identify all redacted portions
- Note exemption claimed for each redaction
- Assess whether redaction appears justified
- Identify patterns of redactions
- Flag potentially improper redactions

**Key Document Identification**:
- Read all documents carefully
- Highlight significant portions
- Extract key quotes and evidence
- Create document summary sheet
- Flag documents for media/advocacy use

## Appeals and Litigation

### Administrative Appeals

#### Filing an Appeal

When documents improperly withheld or inadequate search:

**Appeal Grounds**:
- Agency failed to conduct adequate search
- Agency improperly claimed exemption
- Agency failed to meet legal deadline
- Agency applied exemption too broadly
- Agency withheld material that should be public

**Appeal Letter Template**:
```
[Agency Appeal Official]
[Address]

ADMINISTRATIVE APPEAL OF FOIA DENIAL

RE: Appeal of [Agency] Response to FOIA Request [Case Number]

Dear [Official]:

I appeal the [Agency] denial of FOIA Request [Number], submitted
[Date], regarding [Description].

GROUNDS FOR APPEAL:

The agency improperly withheld or redacted the requested documents.
Specifically:

1. [Ground 1 - Inadequate Search]
   [Explanation and evidence]

2. [Ground 2 - Improper Exemption]
   [Explanation and evidence of why exemption not applicable]

3. [Ground 3 - Overly Broad Redaction]
   [Explanation of why redacted material should be disclosed]

LEGAL BASIS:

[Citations to FOIA statute and case law supporting appeal]

REQUEST FOR RELIEF:

I request that the agency:
- Conduct an additional search for responsive documents
- Release documents withheld under improper exemption claims
- Release redacted portions of documents
- Provide detailed explanation of withholdings

The public interest favors disclosure of these documents regarding
[Issue] because [explanation of public interest].

I am prepared to discuss this appeal and provide additional information.
Please contact me at [email/phone].

Sincerely,

[Name]
[Address]
[Email]
[Phone]
```

**Appeal Timeline**:
- File within 20 business days of adverse decision
- Agency has 20 business days to respond
- Actual timeline often longer (30-90 days typical)
- Appeal decision is final agency action
- After appeal, can file lawsuit

#### Appeal Success Rate

**Factors Affecting Appeal Success**:
- Strength of legal arguments
- Evidence of improper withholding
- Reasonableness of request
- Political factors and sensitivity
- Agency's track record

**Statistics**:
- Approximately 30-40% of appeals are successful (full or partial)
- Success rate varies by agency
- Success higher for procedural issues (inadequate search) than exemption disputes

### FOIA Litigation

#### When to Consider Litigation

**Factors**:**
- High-value documents likely withheld
- Significant public interest
- Clear legal basis for challenge
- Inadequate search or improper exemption likely
- Resources available for litigation

**Cost-Benefit Analysis**:
- Litigation costs: $5,000-$50,000+ (attorney time)
- Timeline: 1-3 years
- Success not guaranteed
- Potential to win important disclosure
- Establishes precedent
- Attorney fee recovery available if successful

#### Finding Counsel and Resources

**Free or Low-Cost Representation**:
- National Freedom of Information Coalition
- Government Accountability Project
- Public Citizen Litigation Group
- ACLU (certain cases)
- Law school clinics (educational institutions)
- Pro bono attorneys

**FOIA Litigation Precedent**:
- Research similar cases
- Learn from others' litigation strategy
- Build on established legal arguments
- Reference winning briefs and arguments

#### Litigation Process Overview

```
Federal FOIA Litigation Process

Administrative Appeal (if not already completed)
  └─ File administrative appeal (if not already done)
  └─ Wait for agency response (typically 30-90 days)
  └─ Preserve issue for litigation

Lawsuit Filing
  ├─ File complaint in federal district court
  │  └─ Identify grounds for challenge
  │  └─ Request preliminary injunction if appropriate
  │  └─ Request declaratory and injunctive relief
  │
  ├─ Court review of classified/withheld documents (in camera)
  │  └─ Agency submits declarations explaining withholdings
  │  └─ Judge reviews withheld documents
  │  └─ Judge rules on withholding propriety
  │
  └─ Standard of review: De novo (no deference to agency)
     └─ Agency must justify withholding
     └─ Exemptions strictly construed

Discovery and Motion Practice
  ├─ Limited discovery (case law dependent)
  ├─ Motions for summary judgment likely
  ├─ Agency may provide supplemental release (settlement)
  └─ Timeline: 6-18 months typical

Trial/Decision
  ├─ Often decided on summary judgment
  ├─ Bench trial if needed
  ├─ Judge rules on withheld documents
  └─ Release ordered for improperly withheld documents

Post-Decision
  ├─ Possible appeal (to appeals court)
  ├─ Attorney fee recovery available if successful
  ├─ Cost shifting if substantial legal issue
  └─ Potential Supreme Court review (rare)
```

## Best Practices and Case Studies

### FOIA Best Practices

**1. Strategic Planning**
- Define information needs clearly
- Assess likelihood of agency cooperation
- Plan for appeals and litigation
- Coordinate with coalition partners
- Time requests strategically

**2. Request Quality**
- Draft requests carefully with specificity
- Include fee waiver if appropriate
- Request expedited processing if justified
- Provide clear contact information
- Keep copies of all submissions

**3. Document Management**
- Create organized system immediately
- Process documents as received
- Maintain chain of custody
- Preserve originals
- Back up all documents

**4. Analysis and Use**
- Conduct thorough document review
- Extract key evidence and quotes
- Timeline events and communications
- Create accessible summaries
- Publicize findings strategically

**5. Follow-Up**
- Monitor deadlines closely
- File appeals if warranted
- Consider litigation for important cases
- Track agency performance
- Share learning with others

### Case Study 1: Corporate Decision-Making

**Organization**: Environmental advocacy group

**Campaign**: Challenge fossil fuel subsidy policies

**FOIA Objective**: Obtain documents showing agency decision-making on subsidy policies

**FOIA Strategy**:

1. **Initial Request**
   - Target: Interior Department
   - Request: "All emails between [Designated Official] and fossil fuel industry representatives regarding subsidy policies, 2020-2023"
   - Result: 150 pages of documents, 30% redacted

2. **Appeal**
   - Challenged redactions claiming deliberative process exemption
   - Argued public interest in corporate interaction with government
   - Result: Additional 40 pages released; some redactions upheld

3. **Document Analysis**
   - Identified 15 key meetings with industry
   - Extracted evidence of close coordination
   - Documented timing of policy changes relative to industry meetings
   - Created timeline of decision-making

4. **Campaign Use**
   - Press release highlighting industry coordination
   - Publication of documents on DocumentCloud
   - Major environmental outlet ran story
   - Congressional staff briefing with documents
   - Advocacy campaign highlighting industry influence
   - Follow-up FOIA request based on initial findings

**Outcome**:
- Media coverage highlighting industry influence
- Congressional interest and investigation
- Policy change reducing subsidies
- Increased public awareness of corporate influence
- Established precedent for future FOIAs on topic

### Case Study 2: Government Overreach Documentation

**Organization**: Civil liberties nonprofit

**Campaign**: Challenge government surveillance policies

**FOIA Objective**: Obtain surveillance program documents

**FOIA Strategy**:

1. **Coalition Building**
   - 12 organizations with shared interest
   - Developed coordinated request language
   - Each filed identical/similar requests
   - Increased visibility and pressure

2. **Multi-Agency Approach**
   - Filed requests with FBI, NSA, DOJ, and DHS
   - Coordinated timing of submissions
   - Created visibility across agencies
   - Generated broader disclosure

3. **Systematic Requests**
   - Phase 1: Broad request for program overview
   - Phase 2: Specific requests for individual cases
   - Phase 3: Appeals and follow-up requests
   - Built comprehensive picture over time

4. **Aggressive Appeals**
   - Appealed all improper redactions
   - Successfully challenged overboard exemptions
   - Won releases on major documents
   - Established precedent for future requests

5. **Litigation Strategy**
   - For key withheld documents, pursued litigation
   - Court found exemptions improperly applied
   - Won significant disclosure through litigation
   - Set precedent for surveillance document disclosure

**Campaign Integration**:
- Books and reports based on FOIA documents
- Media investigations highlighting government practices
- Legislative advocacy for policy changes
- Public speaking and awareness
- Litigation supporting civil liberties

**Outcome**:
- Over 10,000 pages of surveillance documents disclosed
- Multiple congressional investigations initiated
- Policy changes restricting surveillance
- Public awareness of government practices
- Litigation precedent supporting transparency
- Long-term impact on surveillance policy

## Conclusion

FOIA requests are powerful tools for obtaining government documents, investigating decision-making, and supporting advocacy campaigns. Success requires strategic planning, disciplined execution, systematic document management, and integration with broader advocacy efforts.

- Systematic tracking and organization
- Thorough document analysis
- Strategic integration with advocacy campaign
- Willingness to appeal or litigate if necessary
- Coalition coordination and shared effort
- Professional and persistent follow-up
