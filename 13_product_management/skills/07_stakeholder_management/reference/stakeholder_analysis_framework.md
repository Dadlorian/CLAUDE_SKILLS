# Stakeholder Analysis Framework

## Overview

Stakeholder analysis is a systematic process for identifying, understanding, and categorizing individuals and groups who have an interest in or influence over your product decisions. This framework provides structured methods for mapping and analyzing stakeholders.

## The Stakeholder Identification Process

### Step 1: Comprehensive Brainstorm

List all individuals and groups who could be affected by or influence your product decisions.

#### Internal Stakeholders

**Executive Leadership**
- CEO and C-Suite
- Board members
- VP of Product
- VP of Engineering
- VP of Sales
- VP of Marketing
- VP of Finance
- VP of Operations

**Product & Design**
- Product managers
- Design leaders
- User researchers
- Content strategists
- Analytics leads

**Engineering**
- Engineering leaders (VPs, Directors)
- Technical architects
- Platform/infrastructure leads
- Security and compliance engineers
- QA and testing leads

**Go-to-Market**
- Sales leadership and account executives
- Customer success managers
- Support and operations teams
- Marketing teams
- Solutions consultants

**Business Functions**
- Finance and budgeting teams
- Human resources
- Legal and compliance
- Procurement and vendor management

**Key Contributors**
- Team members on your roadmap
- Individual contributors with influence
- Subject matter experts

#### External Stakeholders

**Customers**
- Key account customers
- Enterprise customers
- Small/medium customers
- Segments of users
- Power users or beta customers

**Partner Organizations**
- Technology partners
- Integration partners
- Channel partners
- Strategic alliance partners
- Vendors and service providers

**Market & Industry**
- Industry analysts (Gartner, Forrester, etc.)
- Competitors
- Industry associations
- Press and media
- Academia and researchers

**Regulatory & Compliance**
- Regulatory agencies
- Standards bodies
- Privacy and security bodies
- Industry oversight bodies

### Step 2: Stakeholder Categorization

Organize stakeholders by type:

```
INTERNAL STAKEHOLDERS
├── Leadership (Decision Authority)
├── Functional Heads (Resource Owners)
├── Team Members (Implementers)
└── Influencers (Connectors)

EXTERNAL STAKEHOLDERS
├── Customers (Users & Payers)
├── Partners (Ecosystem)
├── Industry (Competition & Influence)
└── Regulatory (Compliance)
```

## The Power/Interest Matrix (Mendelow Matrix)

### Framework

The Power/Interest Matrix categorizes stakeholders based on two dimensions:

**Power:** Their ability to influence decisions and allocate resources
**Interest:** Their level of concern about your initiative

```
                    HIGH INTEREST
                         |
HIGH POWER    QUADRANT 1 | QUADRANT 2
              MANAGE     | KEEP
              CLOSELY    | SATISFIED
        _____|___________|_____
                         |
LOW POWER     QUADRANT 3 | QUADRANT 4
              MONITOR    | KEEP
              (SPARSE)   | INFORMED
        _____|___________|_____
                    LOW INTEREST
```

### Quadrant 1: Manage Closely (High Power, High Interest)

**Characteristics:**
- Critical to initiative success
- Will actively engage in decision-making
- Can allocate resources or block progress
- Need to understand project in detail

**Examples:**
- Executive sponsor
- VP Engineering (if initiative requires engineering resources)
- VP Sales (if selling to customers)
- Key customer (for B2B initiatives)

**Engagement Strategy:**
- Regular sync meetings (weekly or bi-weekly)
- Early and frequent communication
- Involve in major decisions
- Seek input and feedback
- Escalate issues immediately
- Celebrate wins together
- Provide detailed progress updates
- Anticipate and address concerns proactively

**Communication Frequency:** Weekly or bi-weekly
**Communication Style:** Detailed, interactive, two-way dialog
**Key Focus:** Decision-making, resource allocation, risk management

### Quadrant 2: Keep Satisfied (High Power, Low Interest)

**Characteristics:**
- Have decision authority but aren't deeply involved day-to-day
- Need to maintain perception of control
- May have power to block even without direct interest
- Typically C-suite or external stakeholders

**Examples:**
- CEO (unless directly sponsoring)
- Board members
- External regulatory bodies
- Senior customers without daily involvement

**Engagement Strategy:**
- Regular high-level updates (monthly)
- Executive briefs and summaries
- Quick decisions when needed (don't over-consult)
- Keep them informed of key milestones
- Provide reassurance without excessive detail
- Escalate decisions that could affect their priorities
- Seek their guidance when it matters
- Publicly acknowledge their oversight

**Communication Frequency:** Monthly or quarterly
**Communication Style:** Executive summaries, high-level, decision-focused
**Key Focus:** Strategic alignment, resource needs, risk management

### Quadrant 3: Monitor (Low Power, Low Interest)

**Characteristics:**
- Minimal involvement needed
- Not actively engaged in decision-making
- Low risk of blocking progress
- May become more interested over time

**Examples:**
- Peripheral team members
- Minor stakeholders
- External analysts not focused on your area

**Engagement Strategy:**
- Occasional updates (quarterly)
- Include in broader communications
- Make information available if they want it
- Minimal time investment required
- Monitor for changes in interest or power
- May not need direct engagement

**Communication Frequency:** Quarterly or as needed
**Communication Style:** Email updates, status dashboards
**Key Focus:** Awareness and information availability

### Quadrant 4: Keep Informed (Low Power, High Interest)

**Characteristics:**
- Interested in outcome but lack decision authority
- May become more powerful over time
- Can be valuable advisors and advocates
- Energetic and engaged

**Examples:**
- Team members implementing the work
- Engaged customers
- Partner organizations
- Internal champions without authority

**Engagement Strategy:**
- Regular updates (bi-weekly or monthly)
- Encourage input and feedback
- Involve in working groups
- Use them as sounding boards
- Convert to advocates
- Give them visible roles in success
- Share detailed information
- Celebrate their contributions

**Communication Frequency:** Bi-weekly or monthly
**Communication Style:** Detailed, collaborative, inclusive
**Key Focus:** Engagement, feedback, advocacy

## Stakeholder Dependency Mapping

### Understanding Interdependencies

Create a matrix showing how stakeholders depend on each other:

```
         WHO DEPENDS ON WHOM
        │  Eng │ Sales │ Finance │ Product
────────┼──────┼───────┼─────────┼────────
Product │  √   │   √   │    √    │   ---
Sales   │  √   │  ---  │    √    │   √
Eng     │ ---  │   √   │    √    │   √
Finance │  √   │   √   │   ---   │   √

√ = One depends on the other for success
```

This helps identify:
- Which stakeholders must be aligned first
- Which stakeholders have leverage over others
- Where bottlenecks might exist
- Coalition opportunities

## Stakeholder Attitude Assessment

### Measuring Current Attitude

For each key stakeholder, assess their current position:

**Champion:** Actively advocates for the initiative
- Brings it up unprompted
- Defends it in meetings
- Encourages others
- Allocates resources willingly

**Supporter:** Generally supportive
- Agrees with direction
- Participates in planning
- Allocates required resources
- May have minor concerns

**Neutral:** Haven't taken a position
- Open to learning more
- May have questions
- Not actively opposing
- Engaged but not enthusiastic

**Skeptic:** Has concerns but open to persuasion
- Raises valid objections
- Wants more data
- Worried about trade-offs
- May be won over with right approach

**Opponent:** Actively against the initiative
- Raises objections frequently
- May block or obstruct
- Not easily persuaded
- May have fundamental concerns

### Tracking Attitude Changes

Document attitude changes over time:

```
STAKEHOLDER ATTITUDE TRACKING
     │ Week 1 │ Week 4 │ Week 8 │ Status
─────┼────────┼────────┼────────┼─────────
John │ Skeptic│ Neutral│Supporter│ Moving!
Mary │ Neutral│ Neutral│Neutral │ Stalled
Bob  │Opponent│ Skeptic│ Skeptic │ Progress
```

This reveals:
- Who's being persuaded by your efforts
- Where you need to apply more effort
- Where you might be losing ground
- What messaging is working

```
   CFO  VPE  VPS

         │
        E3
```

This reveals:
- Information flow paths
- Power concentrations
- Influencer networks
- Coalition opportunities

### Key Influencers vs. Decision Makers

Not everyone with power decides, and not everyone who decides has the most power:

**Decision Makers:** Have formal authority to decide
- Executive sponsor
- Steering committee
- Budget owner

**Key Influencers:** Shape decisions without formal authority
- Technical experts
- Trusted advisors
- Relationship connectors
- Subject matter experts

Often, influencing key influencers is more effective than trying to sway decision makers directly.

## Using the Framework

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Identify:** Create comprehensive stakeholder list
2. **Categorize:** Place on Power/Interest Matrix
3. **Analyze:** Develop profiles for Quadrant 1 & 2
4. **Map:** Understand influence networks and dependencies
5. **Assess:** Determine current attitude and agreement
6. **Engage:** Create tailored engagement strategies
7. **Track:** Monitor attitude and influence changes
8. **Update:** Quarterly review and revision

### When to Revisit

Stakeholder maps should be reviewed and updated:
- **Monthly:** Track attitude and engagement progress
- **Quarterly:** Update based on organizational changes
- **On Major Events:**
  - New executives join
  - Organizational restructuring
  - Market changes
  - Initiative scope changes
  - Key personnel leave
