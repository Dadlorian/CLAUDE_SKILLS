# Launch Tier Definitions & Criteria

## Overview

Launch tiers help you determine the appropriate level of effort, resources, and go-to-market investment for different types of product releases. This framework ensures you're allocating resources proportionally to business impact.

## Tier 1: Major/Transformative Launch

### Definition

A Tier 1 launch introduces a new product line, platform, or significant market expansion. These are strategic initiatives that create new business categories or meaningfully change how customers interact with your company.

### Strategic Characteristics

- **Business Impact**: Creates new revenue stream, enters new market, or transforms core product
- **Scope**: Multi-feature, cohesive product experience
- **Market Impact**: Positions company differently in market, creates competitive advantage
- **Customer Adoption**: Targets broad audience, potentially new customer segments
- **Timeline**: 8-12 weeks preparation minimum
- **Visibility**: High internal and external visibility

### Examples

**Apple iPhone (2007)**
- First smartphone from Apple, created new category
- Transformed Apple from computer company to mobile/services company
- Multi-year preparation, full executive engagement
- Global launch, mass media coverage
- Shifted mobile computing paradigm

**Slack Launch (2013)**
- Introduced new category of team communication
- Pivot from game infrastructure to communication platform
- 5+ years of internal use before launch
- Defined new market, displaced email as primary tool
- Extensive PR campaign and launch events

**Stripe Payments (2010)**
- Made payments accessible to all developers
- Simplified payment processing compared to competitors
- Transformed online commerce infrastructure
- Required developer education and ecosystem building
- Global scale from day 1

**Google Maps (2005)**
- Web-based mapping with interactive, zoomable interface
- Displaced desktop mapping solutions
- Required significant data infrastructure
- Became fundamental to location-based services

### Go-to-Market Requirements

**Marketing & Communications**:
- Press release and media outreach (100+ reporters)
- Executive announcements and keynotes
- Customer webinars and launch events
- Landing page with rich content
- Blog post or series
- Email campaigns to multiple segments
- Paid advertising across channels
- Influencer partnerships

**Sales & Account Management**:
- Sales team training and certification
- Sales incentives and competition
- Dedicated sales blitz period
- Pitch decks and demo materials
- Enterprise customer success plans
- Customer executive briefings
- Reference customer program

**Product & Support**:
- Extensive documentation and guides
- Video tutorials and walkthroughs
- Live customer support (potentially 24/7)
- Customer success playbooks
- FAQ and troubleshooting guides
- Implementation support (for enterprise)

**Resources**:
- Dedicated launch team (8-15 people)
- Executive sponsor and steering committee
- Cross-functional working groups
- Significant budget allocation
- 8-12 weeks of focused effort

### Success Metrics

```markdown
| Metric | Tier 1 Target |
|--------|---------------|
| Awareness | 50%+ of target market aware within 2 weeks |
| Activation | 30%+ of aware segment activates/adopts |
| NPS Impact | +4 points or higher |
| Revenue Impact | New revenue stream or $2M+ revenue impact |
| Retention | 60%+ Week 1 retention, 40%+ Week 4 retention |
| Press Coverage | 50+ articles in relevant publications |
| Customer Testimonials | 10+ detailed case studies/testimonials |
| Market Position | Significant shift in competitive position |
```

### Launch Decision Criteria

Launch Tier 1 if:

- Creates entirely new product category or business line
- Enters significant new market segment
- Competitive threat or strategic importance drives urgency
- Creates new revenue stream or growth trajectory
- Requires significant go-to-market investment for success
- Executive team views as strategic priority
- Multiple quarters of planning and development invested

**Do not** launch Tier 1 if:

- Uncertain about market demand or product-market fit
- Support organization not ready for scale
- Critical bugs or performance issues exist
- Team burned out or unprepared
- Messaging and positioning unclear
- Sufficient internal resources not allocated

## Tier 2: Significant Feature Launch

### Definition

A Tier 2 launch introduces a major new feature or capability to existing product, or represents a significant update that meaningfully expands product functionality. These are important for customer value and competitive positioning but don't create new products or markets.

### Strategic Characteristics

- **Business Impact**: Meaningful feature expansion, competitive response, customer retention driver
- **Scope**: Single feature or tightly related features
- **Market Impact**: Maintains competitive position, improves customer value
- **Customer Adoption**: Targets existing user base, potentially adjacent segments
- **Timeline**: 4-8 weeks preparation
- **Visibility**: Moderate internal and external visibility

### Examples

**Slack Channels & Threads**
- Major organizational feature
- Allowed teams to structure conversations
- Addressed key user pain point
- Competitive feature vs. teams in one product
- Significant adoption and engagement impact

**Figma Multiplayer**
- Real-time collaborative editing
- Major feature parity with competing tools
- Powerful competitive differentiator
- Drove significant adoption and NPS improvement
- Required substantial engineering effort

**Notion Database Linking**
- Connected databases enabling relational operations
- Transformed Notion from document database to full CMS
- Major feature expansion
- Significant user education required
- Drove new use cases and expansion

**Stripe Billing Portal**
- Self-service billing management for customers
- Reduced support burden, improved customer satisfaction
- Major feature for SaaS customers
- Moderate launch campaign, focused on existing users
- Clear ROI in support cost reduction

### Go-to-Market Requirements

**Marketing & Communications**:
- Blog post announcement
- Email campaign to relevant segments
- In-app notifications and tooltips
- Webinar or product demo
- Social media content
- Customer success outreach
- Analyst briefing (if significant)

**Sales & Account Management**:
- Sales enablement via email
- Updated pitch decks and one-pagers
- Demo environment with new feature
- Sales success playbook
- Customer success briefings
- Expansion opportunity identification

**Product & Support**:
- Documentation and help articles
- Video tutorial (3-5 minutes)
- In-app tips and onboarding
- FAQ and troubleshooting
- Support team training
- Customer success playbook

**Resources**:
- Cross-functional team (4-8 people)
- 4-8 weeks focused effort
- Moderate budget for advertising/events
- Product manager + designer + engineer leads

### Success Metrics

```markdown
| Metric | Tier 2 Target |
|--------|---------------|
| Awareness | 40%+ of target segment aware |
| Adoption | 15-30% of eligible users adopt feature |
| Usage | 60%+ of adopters using regularly |
| NPS Impact | +2-3 points improvement |
| Retention | 50%+ Day 7 retention |
| Customer Satisfaction | Positive feedback from 80%+ users |
| Revenue Impact | 5-15% impact on key business metrics |
| Support Impact | Reduces support issues or enables upsell |
```

### Launch Decision Criteria

Launch Tier 2 if:

- Major feature addressing key customer pain point
- Competitive response necessary for position
- Significant customer retention or expansion impact
- Existing customer base ready for feature
- Feature complete and well-tested
- Clear go-to-market strategy and channels
- Customer success team can support adoption

**Do not** launch Tier 2 if:

- Feature incomplete or core functionality buggy
- Unclear product-market fit for feature
- Support organization stretched too thin
- Insufficient validation with target customers
- Timing conflicts with other launches
- Internal team capacity exhausted

## Tier 3: Minor Update

### Definition

A Tier 3 launch is a minor feature, incremental improvement, or routine update to existing product. These follow standard release cadence and require minimal go-to-market effort.

### Strategic Characteristics

- **Business Impact**: Incremental improvement, bug fix, or nice-to-have feature
- **Scope**: Single small feature or improvement
- **Market Impact**: Expected cadence, maintains product quality
- **Customer Adoption**: Existing users, transparent delivery
- **Timeline**: 1-2 weeks preparation
- **Visibility**: Low internal and external visibility

### Examples

**Slack UI Polish**
- Button position change, theme improvement
- No new functionality, incremental UX improvement
- Standard release cadence
- Mentioned in release notes
- Minimal marketing effort

**Figma Performance Improvements**
- Faster loading, reduced lag, better memory efficiency
- No new features, but better experience
- Released as part of regular updates
- Mentioned in changelog
- Monitored via performance metrics

**Notion Bug Fixes**
- Mobile app fixes, performance improvements
- Regular maintenance release
- Mentioned in version notes
- Standard support availability
- Minimal marketing

**Stripe API Version Updates**
- Regular API improvements and deprecations
- Documented in API changelog
- Support for deprecated versions
- Release notes and migration guides
- Standard developer communication

### Go-to-Market Requirements

**Marketing & Communications**:
- Release notes / changelog
- In-app tooltip or banner (if visual change)
- Support article (if helpful)
- Possibly mentioned in newsletter (if significant)
- No external marketing

**Sales & Account Management**:
- No action required
- Support documentation if customer-facing

**Product & Support**:
- Support team awareness via internal note
- FAQ if commonly asked
- Documentation update if impacted

**Resources**:
- Standard release process
- No dedicated launch effort
- Minutes to hours of coordination

### Success Metrics

```markdown
| Metric | Tier 3 Target |
|--------|---------------|
| Adoption | 40%+ of users adopt over 2 weeks |
| Issues | Zero critical bugs introduced |
| Support | No increase in support volume |
| Sentiment | No negative customer feedback |
| Performance | Maintains or improves metrics |
```

### Launch Decision Criteria

Launch Tier 3 if:

- Routine feature or minor improvement
- Low risk and simple implementation
- No significant customer communication needed
- Standard release cadence
- Part of regular product iteration

**Do not** launch Tier 3 if:

- Actually significant feature (should be Tier 2)
- Significant technical risk
- Requires customer communication
- Critical to competitiveness

## Tier Selection Matrix

| Factor | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **New Product/Category** | Yes | No | No |
| **Market Expansion** | Yes | Possibly | No |
| **Major Feature** | Yes | Yes | No |
| **Incremental Feature** | No | Possibly | Yes |
| **Strategic Importance** | Very High | High | Low |
| **Target Audience Size** | Large (new segment) | Medium (existing + adjacent) | Existing users |
| **Preparation Timeline** | 8-12 weeks | 4-8 weeks | 1-2 weeks |
| **Executive Involvement** | Heavy | Moderate | Minimal |
| **Marketing Campaign** | Full multi-channel | Targeted channels | Release notes |
| **Sales Training** | Required | Recommended | Optional |
| **Support Prep** | Extensive | Moderate | Minimal |
| **Budget** | $50K-$500K+ | $5K-$50K | $0-$5K |
| **Success Metrics** | Comprehensive | Focused | Basic |
| **Post-Launch Cadence** | Daily standups, Weekly reviews | Weekly reviews | Standard process |

## Decision Framework

### Step 1: Assess Business Impact

**High Impact** (Tier 1 or 2):
- Creates new revenue stream
- Addresses key competitive threat
- High customer value/retention driver
- Strategic priority for company

**Medium Impact** (Tier 2):
- Important for customer satisfaction
- Addresses significant pain point
- Moderate competitive importance
- Clear adoption value

**Low Impact** (Tier 3):
- Nice-to-have improvement
- Regular maintenance/updates
- Minor competitive consideration
- Limited adoption value

### Step 2: Assess Scope & Effort

**Large Scope** (Tier 1):
- Multi-week engineering effort
- Cross-functional involvement
- Significant product change
- Requires significant planning

**Medium Scope** (Tier 2):
- 1-3 week engineering effort
- Product team involvement
- Clear feature scope
- Moderate planning

**Small Scope** (Tier 3):
- Days of engineering effort
- Single person/team can handle
- Straightforward change
- Standard process

### Step 3: Assess Market Readiness

**Market Validation Required** (Tier 1):
- New market or segment
- Uncertain demand
- Beta/validation needed
- Customer education required

**Market Validated** (Tier 2):
- Clear customer demand
- Competitive benchmark exists
- Target customers identified
- Messaging proven

**Market Obvious** (Tier 3):
- Expected feature or update
- No customer validation needed
- Standard improvement
- Clear value proposition

### Step 4: Assess Organizational Readiness

**Full Preparation Needed** (Tier 1):
- Multiple teams involved
- Significant coordination
- New processes/tools needed
- Training and enablement required

**Focused Preparation** (Tier 2):
- Core team alignment
- Standard tools/processes
- Sales/support enablement
- Clear ownership

**Standard Process** (Tier 3):
- Existing processes apply
- No special preparation
- Transparent to organization
- Part of regular cadence

## Tier Change Scenarios

### Escalating to Higher Tier

**When to escalate Tier 3 to Tier 2**:
- Feature adoption or impact higher than expected
- Unexpected competitive response
- Customer demand exceeds projections
- Feature unlocks new use cases

**When to escalate Tier 2 to Tier 1**:
- Feature becomes strategic priority
- Market opportunity larger than anticipated
- Competitive threat more urgent
- Executive prioritization changes

**Decision to escalate should be made 2-4 weeks before launch**. Escalating at launch time creates chaos.

### De-escalating to Lower Tier

**When to descope Tier 1 to Tier 2**:
- Scope creep threatening timeline
- Market readiness lower than expected
- Resource constraints emerging
- Phased approach makes more sense

**When to descope Tier 2 to Tier 3**:
- Feature simpler than anticipated
- Customer demand unclear
- Resource constraints severe
- Can be bundled with other updates

**De-escalation requires explicit leadership decision**. Communicate clearly with teams.

## Launch Tier Assignment Examples

### Scenario 1: New Enterprise Features

**Context**: You sell B2B SaaS product focused on SMB. You're building new enterprise features (SSO, advanced security, custom contracts) to win enterprise deals.

**Tier**: 2 (or escalate to 1 if competitive threat or strategic bet)

**Reasoning**:
- Significant feature expansion for new segment
- Clear target audience (enterprise prospects)
- Requires sales enablement and customer success prep
- Marketing focus on new segment

### Scenario 2: Mobile App Launch

**Context**: Desktop product with web app. Building first mobile app for iOS.

**Tier**: 1

**Reasoning**:
- New product/platform
- New market segment access
- Strategic importance for growth
- Requires substantial go-to-market

### Scenario 3: Performance Improvements

**Context**: Product slowing down for power users. Engineering speeds up core operations by 50%.

**Tier**: 3 (possibly 2 if it impacts customer satisfaction significantly)

**Reasoning**:
- Incremental improvement
- No new functionality
- Existing user benefit
- Release notes sufficient

### Scenario 4: Marketplace Launch

**Context**: Adding marketplace/integration ecosystem to core product.

**Tier**: 1

**Reasoning**:
- New business model
- Multi-sided ecosystem (developers, users)
- Strategic competitive moat
- Requires developer education
- Creates new revenue stream

### Scenario 5: Dark Mode

**Context**: Adding dark mode to interface.

**Tier**: 3 (possibly 2 if brand refresh or design milestone)

**Reasoning**:
- Nice-to-have feature
- Expected for modern products
- No strategic importance
- User preference feature
- Low adoption barrier

## Common Tier Mistakes

### Over-Tiering

**Mistake**: Treating Tier 3 update as Tier 1 launch
- Creates resource waste
- Burns out teams
- Misaligns organizational focus
- Dilutes actual strategic priorities

**Prevention**: Use selection matrix, get leadership alignment, reserve high-tier launches for truly strategic initiatives

### Under-Tiering

**Mistake**: Treating Tier 2 feature as Tier 3 release
- Insufficient customer communication
- Sales team unprepared
- Support team overwhelmed
- Market missed opportunity
- Adoption disappoints

**Prevention**: Assess customer impact, gather sales/support input, ask "would we invest in promoting this?"

### Misaligned Preparation

**Mistake**: Tier 1 preparation with Tier 2 resource allocation
- Launch fails due to support overwhelm
- Marketing underfunded
- Sales unprepared
- Customer expectations unmet

**Prevention**: Match resources to tier, align budgets, validate preparation levels

### Static Tier Assignment

**Mistake**: Never re-assessing tier after initial assignment
- Priorities change, tier should too
- Can lead to over or under-preparation
- Wasted resources or missed opportunities

**Prevention**: Review tier assignment 6 and 2 weeks before launch, escalate/descope as needed
