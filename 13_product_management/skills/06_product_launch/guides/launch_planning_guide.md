# 8-Week Launch Planning Guide

## Comprehensive Blueprint for Planning a Product Launch

This guide walks you through the complete 8-week launch planning process. Use this alongside the launch_checklist.md for specific tasks and the launch_plan_template.md for a detailed plan document to complete.

---

## Overview: The Launch Planning Journey

### What You'll Accomplish

By the end of 8 weeks, you'll have:

1. **Clear strategy**: Defined target audience, positioning, and go-to-market approach
2. **Validated messaging**: Tested value proposition and positioning with customers
3. **Marketing plan**: Blog, email, social, ads, PR campaigns planned and created
4. **Sales ready**: Sales team trained with collateral, demos, and talking points
5. **Support prepared**: Documentation, FAQs, and team trained
6. **Product validated**: Beta tested with real customers, issues identified and fixed
7. **Success metrics**: Clear, measurable goals and measurement dashboard
8. **Team aligned**: Cross-functional team aligned on goals, timeline, and execution

### Key Success Factors

- **Executive sponsor**: Clear executive ownership and support
- **Dedicated team**: 4-8 people focused on launch
- **Weekly sync**: Regular cross-functional coordination
- **Clear decisions**: Defined process for making decisions
- **Flexibility**: Ability to adjust based on learnings

## Phase 1: Launch Strategy & Alignment (Week 1)

### Goals for Week 1

- [ ] Define launch tier and success metrics
- [ ] Identify target audience and market segments
- [ ] Establish launch team and roles
- [ ] Get executive and cross-functional alignment
- [ ] Create launch project plan and timeline

### Activities

#### Monday: Launch Kickoff

**9am: Launch Kickoff Meeting** (1 hour)
- Participants: Launch team, executive sponsor, cross-functional leads
- Agenda:
  1. Launch overview and goals (exec sponsor, 10 min)
  2. Target audience and strategy (product manager, 15 min)
  3. Timeline and key milestones (PM, 10 min)
  4. Team structure and roles (PM, 10 min)
  5. Next steps and questions (all, 15 min)

**Post-meeting**:
- Send launch brief to all stakeholders
- Schedule weekly launch sync for recurring cadence
- Create launch Slack channel
- Set up project management tool with timeline

#### Tuesday: Strategy Definition

**Competitive & Market Analysis** (2-3 hours)
- Identify 5-10 main competitors
- Document their approach to this product category
- Identify your competitive advantages
- List potential competitive concerns
- Create competitive positioning matrix

**Target Audience Definition** (2-3 hours)
- Define primary target audience (specific personas)
- Identify secondary audiences (adjacent segments)
- Document key pain points addressed
- Define success metrics for each segment
- Create audience segmentation document

#### Wednesday: Success Metrics

**Define Launch Goals** (2-3 hours)
- Set adoption target (% of audience trying product)
- Set awareness target (% aware of launch)
- Set engagement target (% using regularly)
- Set business impact goal (revenue, retention, etc.)
- Set NPS or satisfaction target

**Create Measurement Plan** (1-2 hours)
- Define Day 1, Week 1, Week 4, Week 8 metrics
- Identify key leading indicators
- Set up measurement dashboard
- Establish data collection process
- Assign metrics owner

#### Thursday: Team & Roles

**Define Launch Team Structure** (1 hour)
- Product Manager: Overall launch strategy and execution
- Marketing Manager: Campaign creation, channel execution, communications
- Sales Manager: Sales enablement, training, competitive response
- Customer Success: Onboarding playbooks, customer support
- Engineering: Product shipping, monitoring, support
- Design: Marketing assets, landing page, email design
- Analytics: Measurement, dashboard, analysis

**Create Communication Plan** (1 hour)
- Weekly all-hands update (Slack, email)
- Daily standups during final 2 weeks
- Escalation process for decisions
- Weekly cross-functional sync (same time each week)
- Post-launch daily standups (Day 1-7)

#### Friday: Project Planning

**Create Launch Timeline** (2-3 hours)
- Map out 8-week timeline with key milestones
- Identify dependencies and critical path items
- Create Gantt chart or timeline visualization
- Assign owners to each phase
- Identify potential risks and contingencies

**Week 1 Summary** (1 hour)
- Document launch strategy brief (2-3 pages)
- Define success criteria
- Establish team structure
- Schedule weekly syncs
- Plan Week 2 activities

### Deliverables from Week 1

- **Launch Strategy Brief**: 2-3 page document covering target audience, positioning, goals
- **Competitive Analysis**: Analysis of 5-10 competitors and your positioning
- **Target Audience Definition**: Clear definition of primary and secondary audiences
- **Success Metrics**: Specific, measurable goals for launch
- **Launch Timeline**: 8-week Gantt chart with key milestones
- **Team Structure**: Defined roles and responsibilities
- **Communication Plan**: Weekly syncs, escalation process, communication cadence

### Common Week 1 Mistakes

- **Skipping team alignment**: Teams disagree on goals later
- **Vague success metrics**: Can't measure launch success
- **Poor timing**: Launching too soon without prep or too late losing momentum
- **Insufficient executive support**: Without sponsor, struggling to get resources
- **No contingency planning**: Unprepared for issues that arise

## Phase 2: Messaging & Positioning (Week 2)

### Goals for Week 2

- [ ] Develop compelling value proposition
- [ ] Create launch messaging framework
- [ ] Test positioning with target customers
- [ ] Draft all launch messaging
- [ ] Align on key talking points

### Activities

#### Understanding Customer Problems (2-3 hours)

**Customer Problem Research**:
- Conduct 3-5 customer interviews focused on the problem
- Ask: "What's the impact of this problem on your work?"
- Document specific examples and quotes
- Identify emotional and practical impacts
- Prioritize by severity and frequency

**Customer Language**:
- Note specific words/phrases customers use
- Capture customer quotes about the problem
- Use customer language in messaging (not your language)
- Validate that your understanding matches reality

#### Value Proposition Development (3-4 hours)

**Draft Value Proposition**:

```markdown
## Value Proposition Framework

For [TARGET AUDIENCE], who [NEED/PROBLEM],
[PRODUCT] is a [CATEGORY] that [PRIMARY BENEFIT].

Unlike [ALTERNATIVES], our product [KEY DIFFERENTIATOR].

### Examples:

Slack for engineering managers who are frustrated with scattered communication:
- For engineering managers, who struggle with scattered communication across email, chat, and tools
- Slack is a communication hub that brings all conversations into one searchable, organized place
- Unlike email, Slack keeps conversation context, searchable history, and team visibility

Notion for knowledge workers who waste time managing information:
- For knowledge workers, who waste time switching between spreadsheets, documents, and notes
- Notion is an all-in-one workspace that eliminates tool switching and information sprawl
- Unlike separate tools (Google Docs, Excel, Notes), Notion provides unified workspace with connected data
```

**Test with Customers**:
- Share draft value prop with 3-5 target customers
- Ask: "Does this resonate? What would you change?"
- Refine based on feedback
- Validate that key benefit is clear and compelling

#### Messaging Framework (2-3 hours)

**Create Messaging Pillars** (3-5 core messages):

```markdown
## Example Messaging Pillars (Slack)

1. **Unified Communication**: All your team conversations in one place - searchable, organized, always available

2. **Seamless Integration**: Connect your entire toolkit (GitHub, Google Drive, Salesforce, etc.) - everything together

3. **Async-First**: Make decisions and move forward without everyone in every meeting

4. **Better Culture**: See the full picture of what's happening, stay connected across teams

5. **Security & Control**: Enterprise-grade security and compliance, admin controls, data residency
```

**Develop Talking Points** for each pillar:
- Describe the benefit specifically
- Provide concrete example or use case
- Address potential objection
- Include proof point (customer quote, metric, etc.)
- Keep concise (1-2 sentences)

#### Competitive Positioning (1-2 hours)

**Create Positioning vs. Alternatives**:

```markdown
## Feature Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Our Product | Competitor A | Competitor B |
|---------|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| Real-time collab | Yes | Yes | Manual only |
| Integrations | 500+ | 100+ | 50+ |
| Offline support | Yes | No | Yes |
| Mobile app | Native | Web only | Native |
| Price per seat | $12 | $15 | $10 |

## Positioning Statement

Unlike [Competitor A] which is complex and expensive, and [Competitor B] which lacks features,
our solution provides the best balance of power, simplicity, and value.

Key differentiators:
1. [Advantage 1]
2. [Advantage 2]
3. [Advantage 3]
```

#### Key Messages & FAQ (2-3 hours)

**Create Launch Messaging Sheet**:

```markdown
## One-Page Launch Messaging

### Headline
[Main launch message - 1 sentence, benefit-focused]

### Subheading
[Why now? What's changed? - 1-2 sentences]

### Three Main Benefits
1. [Benefit 1 with proof]
2. [Benefit 2 with proof]
3. [Benefit 3 with proof]

### Why Now?
[Market trend, problem urgency, technology enablement - 2-3 sentences]

### FAQ

Q: How is this different from [Competitor]?
A: [Specific differentiation, proof points]

Q: What does this cost?
A: [Pricing, value prop, ROI]

Q: Is this ready for production use?
A: [Maturity level, customer examples, roadmap]

Q: What are the main use cases?
A: [3-5 use cases with examples]

Q: How do I get started?
A: [Path to adoption, onboarding timeline]
```

#### Audience-Specific Messaging (1-2 hours)

**Tailor Messaging for Each Segment**:

```markdown
## Audience-Specific Talking Points

### For Engineering Teams
- Focus: Technical capability, API access, automation
- Talking points: "Reduce manual work", "Integrate with your tools", "Scale with your growth"
- Objection: "We have a solution already" → "This integrates with what you have"

### For Product Managers
- Focus: User insights, decision making, collaboration
- Talking points: "Shared context", "Faster decisions", "Customer feedback visibility"
- Objection: "Too many tools" → "This replaces 5 tools you're already using"

### For Enterprise IT
- Focus: Security, compliance, administration
- Talking points: "SOC 2 certified", "SAML/SSO", "Audit logs", "Data residency"
- Objection: "Security concerns" → "Enterprise-grade security with [specific features]"
```

### Deliverables from Week 2

- **Value Proposition Statement**: Refined based on customer feedback
- **Messaging Framework**: 3-5 core pillars with talking points
- **Positioning Statement**: Clear differentiation vs. competitors
- **Competitive Analysis**: Feature comparison and positioning matrix
- **Customer-Tested Messaging**: Validated with target customers
- **FAQ Document**: 10-15 common questions with answers
- **Audience-Specific Messaging**: Tailored messaging for each segment
- **Messaging One-Pager**: Single document with all key messages

### Common Week 2 Mistakes

- **Product-focused messaging**: Talking about features instead of benefits
- **Not testing with customers**: Messaging doesn't resonate with real customers
- **Too much information**: Messaging overwhelms rather than persuades
- **Jargon-heavy**: Using company or industry jargon instead of customer language
- **Ignoring objections**: Not addressing why customers might not adopt

## Phase 3: Go-to-Market Strategy (Week 3)

### Goals for Week 3

- [ ] Finalize GTM strategy and channel mix
- [ ] Plan marketing campaigns and tactics
- [ ] Develop sales enablement approach
- [ ] Create PR and media strategy
- [ ] Build customer success playbooks

### Activities

#### GTM Channel Strategy (2 hours)

**Assess Channels**:

```markdown
## GTM Channel Assessment

| Channel | Reach | Impact | Effort | Priority |
|---------|-------|--------|--------|----------|
| **Blog** | Medium | High | Medium | 1 |
| **Email** | High | High | Low | 1 |
| **Paid Ads** | High | Medium | High | 2 |
| **Social** | Medium | Medium | Low | 2 |
| **Press** | Medium | High | Medium | 1 |
| **Sales** | Low | High | High | 1 |
| **Events** | Low | High | High | 3 |
| **Partnerships** | Low | High | Medium | 2 |
| **Community** | Medium | Medium | Medium | 2 |
```

**Channel Mix Strategy**:
- High-priority channels get most resources
- Owned channels (email, blog) are most reliable
- Earned channels (PR, influencers) have long lead time
- Paid channels amplify owned content
- Sales focus on high-value segments

#### Marketing Campaign Plan (3-4 hours)

**Create Campaign Roadmap**:

```markdown
## 8-Week Marketing Timeline

### Week 1-3: Pre-Launch Buzz
- Blog teaser posts (2-3 posts on related topics)
- Email list warmup (value content, not promotional)
- Social media: Regular content, build anticipation
- Media relationships: Start outreach, schedule interviews
- Paid ads: Build audience awareness

### Week 4-5: Beta Program
- Blog updates on what we're learning
- Email: Beta updates and user stories
- Social: Share beta progress and testimonials
- Media: Exclusive previews for selected outlets
- Paid ads: Continue building awareness

### Week 6-7: Pre-Launch (Final Week)
- Launch blog post (published day of launch)
- Email campaigns scheduled (announce, follow-up, welcome)
- Social media posts scheduled (launch day and follow-up)
- Press release and media outreach
- Paid ads: Increase budget, launch-specific messaging

### Week 8: Launch & Post-Launch
- Blog post goes live (launch day)
- Email campaigns go out (timed sequence)
- Social posts activate (launch day + daily for week 1)
- PR outreach begins
- Sales activation
- Daily updates based on adoption

### Weeks 9-16: Post-Launch
- Customer success stories and case studies
- Follow-up content addressing customer questions
- Paid ads continue (optimization based on performance)
- Social media: Engagement and community building
- Sales: Focus on expansion and upsell
```

**Campaign Tactics**:

**Blog**:
- Launch announcement blog (1000+ words, SEO optimized)
- Customer success stories (500-1000 words each)
- How-to guides and tutorials
- Competitive positioning content (if applicable)
- Industry trends and thought leadership

**Email**:
- Launch announcement email (to existing customers/subscribers)
- Welcome series for new signups (3-5 emails, spaced 1-2 days)
- Educational emails (feature deep-dive, use case, etc.)
- Customer testimonial emails
- Expansion/upsell emails (after activation)

**Social Media**:
- Launch day announcement posts (multiple platforms)
- Customer quotes and testimonials
- Feature highlights and tips
- Customer success stories and case studies
- Engagement: Respond to comments, join conversations
- Paid promotion: Amplify top performing posts

**Paid Advertising**:
- LinkedIn (B2B audience targeting)
- Google Ads (Search for relevant keywords)
- Facebook/Instagram (Demographic targeting)
- Reddit (Community-specific targeting)
- Budget allocation: Start $5-10K, increase based on performance

#### Sales Enablement (3-4 hours)

**Sales Training Agenda** (1-2 hours, in-person or recorded):
1. Product demo (15 min)
2. Positioning and value prop (10 min)
3. Competitive comparison (10 min)
4. Customer use cases (10 min)
5. Objection handling (10 min)
6. Q&A (10 min)

**Sales Collateral**:
- One-page feature overview
- Competitive comparison slide
- Sales pitch deck (15-20 slides)
- Email template for outreach
- Demo environment access and walkthrough

**Sales Playbook**:
- Qualifying questions to identify fit
- Value prop talking points for different personas
- Common objections and responses
- Use case examples and stories
- Pricing and ROI calculations (if applicable)

#### PR & Media Strategy (2-3 hours)

**Media Outreach**:
- Identify 30-50 relevant reporters and journalists
- Create pitch angle (why is this newsworthy?)
- Send exclusive to 3-5 top-tier outlets (day before public launch)
- Send broader outreach day of launch
- Follow up with media who cover

**Press Release**:
- Format: Newsworthy, quote from executive, 500-750 words
- Include: Problem statement, solution, customer quote, company background
- Distribute through press release service
- Share with media contacts

**Media Kit** (for Tier 1 launches):
- Product screenshots and demo video
- Founder/executive bios and photos
- Company background and mission
- Customer logos and testimonials
- Key stats and metrics

#### Customer Success Planning (1-2 hours)

**Customer Success Playbook**:

```markdown
## Post-Launch Customer Success Plan

### Day 1-3: Welcome & Onboarding
- Send welcome email with getting started guide
- Offer 30-min onboarding call for enterprise customers
- Create quick start checklist
- Set expectations for support availability

### Week 1-2: Activation & Learning
- Monitor activation metrics
- Proactive outreach to customers who sign up but don't activate
- Offer additional training/support if needed
- Gather early feedback through surveys

### Week 2-4: Expansion
- Identify high-engagement customers
- Offer 1-on-1 success calls
- Share advanced features and tips
- Identify expansion/upsell opportunities

### Week 4+: Retention & Advocacy
- Regular health checks with key customers
- Identify at-risk customers and intervene
- Gather customer success stories and testimonials
- Create reference customer program
```

### Deliverables from Week 3

- **GTM Strategy Document**: Channel mix, timing, budget allocation
- **Marketing Campaign Plan**: Blog, email, social, paid, PR timeline and tactics
- **Sales Enablement Materials**: Training agenda, collateral, playbook
- **Press Release**: Draft for review and refinement
- **PR/Media Plan**: Media contact list, pitch strategy, timeline
- **Customer Success Playbook**: Post-launch customer success approach
- **Budget Allocation**: Spending plan across channels
- **Performance Targets**: Expected reach, engagement, conversion by channel

### Common Week 3 Mistakes

- **Insufficient budget**: Underfunding paid advertising or underestimating need
- **Unclear channel strategy**: Trying to do everything vs. focusing on high-impact channels
- **Sales unprepared**: Not training sales team before launch
- **Press outreach too late**: Starting PR only day of launch (should be weeks before)
- **Customer success unprepared**: No plan for post-launch customer onboarding and support

## Phase 4: Product Readiness (Week 4)

### Goals for Week 4

- [ ] Confirm feature development complete and tested
- [ ] Implement analytics instrumentation
- [ ] Verify security and performance
- [ ] Create monitoring and alerting
- [ ] Establish rollback procedures

### Activities

**This phase is primarily engineering/product focused. Key activities**:

- [ ] Complete feature development and bug fixes
- [ ] QA testing across browsers, devices, platforms
- [ ] Security testing and vulnerability assessment
- [ ] Performance testing and load testing
- [ ] Analytics instrumentation for all key events
- [ ] Error monitoring and alerting setup
- [ ] Database optimization and scaling testing
- [ ] Accessibility testing (WCAG 2.1 AA standard)
- [ ] Rollback procedure testing
- [ ] Staging environment fully mirrors production
- [ ] Monitoring dashboard creation
- [ ] Runbooks for common issues
- [ ] Feature flag implementation for kill switches

**Product Manager Responsibilities**:
- Confirm feature scope and quality
- Define key events to track in analytics
- Verify documentation is complete
- Review help docs and support materials
- Create product FAQ document
- Brief customer success on common questions

### Deliverables from Week 4

- **Product Release Checklist**: All items verified and checked
- **Analytics Implementation**: Key events instrumented, dashboard created
- **Monitoring Setup**: Alerts configured, dashboard created
- **Security Audit**: Completed with no critical issues
- **Performance Benchmarks**: Metrics established for comparison
- **Release Notes**: First draft completed
- **Support Documentation**: Help articles, FAQs, troubleshooting
- **Runbooks**: Common issues and resolution procedures

## Phase 5: Beta Program (Week 5)

### Goals for Week 5

- [ ] Recruit 20-50 beta participants
- [ ] Launch closed beta with real customers
- [ ] Gather detailed feedback on product and messaging
- [ ] Identify critical issues and fix them
- [ ] Build customer champions for launch

### Activities

**This phase is covered extensively in beta_program_best_practices.md. Key activities**:

- [ ] Recruit beta participants from target segments
- [ ] Send welcome package and onboarding
- [ ] Conduct customer interviews (3-5)
- [ ] Monitor beta adoption and engagement
- [ ] Log and prioritize feedback
- [ ] Fix critical issues identified in beta
- [ ] Gather customer testimonials
- [ ] Refine messaging based on beta feedback
- [ ] Document learnings and insights
- [ ] Identify champion customers for launch

### Key Decisions from Beta

1. **Launch Readiness**: Are we ready to launch broadly?
2. **Messaging Validation**: Does our positioning resonate?
3. **Product Gaps**: What critical features are missing?
4. **Support Readiness**: Can our support handle customer volume?
5. **Go/No-Go Decision**: Formal decision to proceed or adjust timeline

### Deliverables from Week 5

- **Beta Participation Report**: Participants, adoption rate, engagement
- **Customer Feedback Summary**: Top themes, quotes, testimonials
- **Issues Log**: Critical issues fixed, remaining issues tracked
- **Beta Learnings Document**: Key insights about product and market
- **Testimonials & Quotes**: 5+ customer quotes ready for marketing
- **Go/No-Go Assessment**: Ready to launch or adjust timeline?

### Common Week 5 Mistakes

- **Too few beta participants**: Can't validate market demand
- **Wrong beta participants**: Not representative of target market
- **Poor communication**: Participants don't feel engaged
- **Ignoring feedback**: Participants disengage, feel unheard
- **Shipping too many changes**: Never stabilize for launch

## Phase 7: Launch Preparation & Rehearsal (Week 7)

### Goals for Week 7

- [ ] Conduct full launch rehearsal
- [ ] Brief all teams on launch day roles
- [ ] Create launch monitoring dashboard
- [ ] Finalize launch day logistics
- [ ] Do final QA and testing

### Activities

**Launch Readiness Check**:
- [ ] Product fully tested and stable
- [ ] All marketing content ready
- [ ] Sales team trained and ready
- [ ] Support team trained and ready
- [ ] Monitoring systems configured
- [ ] Analytics tracking verified
- [ ] Email campaigns scheduled
- [ ] Social media posts scheduled

**Launch Day Logistics** (2-3 hours):
- [ ] Confirm launch date and time (optimal for global reach)
- [ ] Schedule launch standups (6am, 12pm, 6pm PT)
- [ ] Create launch war room (Slack channel)
- [ ] Brief all team members on roles and responsibilities
- [ ] Create escalation procedures for issues
- [ ] Prepare go/no-go decision document

**Dry Run / Rehearsal** (4 hours):
- [ ] Simulate launch scenario
- [ ] Test all systems (blog publish, email send, etc.)
- [ ] Test monitoring and alerting
- [ ] Practice deployment and rollback
- [ ] Review key decisions and escalations
- [ ] Identify any gaps or issues
- [ ] Brief team on what to expect

**Communication Prep**:
- [ ] Draft internal announcement
- [ ] Draft customer announcement
- [ ] Schedule all social media posts
- [ ] Verify press list and media contacts
- [ ] Prepare executive talking points
- [ ] Create FAQ for customer questions

**Final QA** (2-3 hours):
- [ ] Test product in production-like environment
- [ ] Test signup flow end-to-end
- [ ] Test email delivery
- [ ] Test tracking and analytics
- [ ] Verify all links work
- [ ] Test on multiple browsers and devices
- [ ] Run through FAQ scenarios

### Deliverables from Week 7

- **Launch Readiness Checklist**: All items verified
- **Launch Logistics Plan**: Date, time, team roles, escalations
- **Launch Monitoring Dashboard**: Live and accessible
- **Dry Run Report**: Any issues identified and addressed
- **Launch Day Guide**: Step-by-step guide for launch execution
- **Team Briefing**: All teams understand their roles
- **Go/No-Go Criteria**: Clear decision criteria for launch

### Common Week 7 Mistakes

- **Insufficient dry run**: Not adequately testing deployment
- **Poor communication**: Teams unclear on their roles
- **Inadequate monitoring**: Can't see what's happening during launch
- **No escalation process**: No way to quickly make decisions
- **Skipping QA**: Issues discovered during launch

## Phase 8: Launch Execution & Measurement (Week 8+)

### Goals for Week 8

- [ ] Execute flawless launch
- [ ] Monitor real-time metrics and performance
- [ ] Respond to customer feedback and issues
- [ ] Share momentum and wins
- [ ] Measure success and iterate

### Launch Day Timeline

**6am PT: Morning Standup** (30 min):
- [ ] Final system checks
- [ ] Confirm all teams ready
- [ ] Review go/no-go criteria
- [ ] Make go/no-go decision

**8am PT: Launch** (Staggered over 2-3 hours):
- [ ] Publish blog post
- [ ] Send launch email
- [ ] Post social media (scheduled posts go live)
- [ ] Brief media contacts
- [ ] Brief executives
- [ ] Monitor metrics closely (15-minute cadence)

**12pm PT: Midday Standup** (30 min):
- [ ] Share metrics so far
- [ ] Address any issues
- [ ] Celebrate wins
- [ ] Prepare for afternoon

**6pm PT: Evening Standup** (30 min):
- [ ] Share day 1 results
- [ ] Thank teams
- [ ] Prepare for day 2
- [ ] Brief executives on final metrics

**Post-launch (24 hours)**:
- [ ] Monitor metrics continuously
- [ ] Respond to customer questions
- [ ] Address critical issues immediately
- [ ] Share momentum internally
- [ ] Gather early testimonials

### Week 1-2: Measurement & Iteration

**Daily Metrics Check**:
- [ ] Adoption rate vs. goal
- [ ] Traffic and conversion
- [ ] Customer sentiment (social, reviews)
- [ ] Support volume and issues
- [ ] Technical health (errors, performance)

**Weekly Cadence**:
- [ ] Stakeholder update email
- [ ] Customer interviews (1-2)
- [ ] Feedback synthesis
- [ ] Identify high-impact improvements
- [ ] Ship improvements and iterate

**Customer Feedback Loop**:
- [ ] Address common questions
- [ ] Fix high-impact bugs
- [ ] Improve onboarding based on feedback
- [ ] Gather testimonials and success stories
- [ ] Plan next phase

### Week 2-4: Optimization

**Conversion Optimization**:
- [ ] Analyze signup and activation flows
- [ ] Test messaging variations
- [ ] Optimize landing page and email
- [ ] Reduce friction in onboarding
- [ ] Measure impact of improvements

**Customer Expansion**:
- [ ] Identify power users and heavy feature users
- [ ] Reach out for expansion/upsell opportunities
- [ ] Develop customer success stories
- [ ] Plan feature requests and roadmap

**Sales Activation**:
- [ ] Share sales wins and momentum
- [ ] Brief sales on customer feedback
- [ ] Provide customer reference calls
- [ ] Plan next sales initiatives

### Week 4-8: Launch Retrospective

**Measurement Summary**:
- [ ] Did we hit adoption goals? (% of audience who tried)
- [ ] Did we hit awareness goals? (% who heard about launch)
- [ ] Did we hit engagement goals? (% using regularly)
- [ ] Did we hit business impact goals? (revenue, retention)

**What Went Well**:
- [ ] Tactics that exceeded expectations
- [ ] Team members who went above and beyond
- [ ] Processes that should be repeated
- [ ] Customer feedback highlights

**What Could Be Better**:
- [ ] Bottlenecks and delays
- [ ] Communication gaps
- [ ] Resource constraints
- [ ] Process improvements

**Learnings for Next Launch**:
- [ ] Update launch checklist and timeline
- [ ] Document lessons learned
- [ ] Refine processes based on experience
- [ ] Plan improvements for next launch
- [ ] Share knowledge across organization

### Deliverables from Week 8+

## Launch Measurement Dashboard

### Key Metrics to Track

**Awareness Metrics** (First 7 days):
- Unique visitors to landing page
- Impressions across marketing channels
- Social media reach and engagement
- Press mentions and coverage

**Adoption Metrics** (Days 1-14):
- Signups/registrations
- Feature activation rate
- % of target audience who tried
- Time to first action

**Engagement Metrics** (Weeks 1-4):
- Daily active users
- Weekly active users
- Feature usage depth
- Average session duration

**Satisfaction Metrics** (Ongoing):
- NPS score
- Customer sentiment (social listening)
- Support ticket sentiment
- Feature satisfaction

**Business Metrics** (Weeks 1-8):
- Revenue from launch
- Customer acquisition cost
- Retention rate (cohort analysis)
- Impact on North Star metric

### Dashboard Tools

- **Google Analytics**: Website traffic and behavior
- **Email Platform**: Email open/click rates
- **Social Media**: Engagement metrics
- **Product Analytics**: In-app events and usage
- **Surveys**: NPS and satisfaction
- **Custom Dashboard**: Combination of all metrics
