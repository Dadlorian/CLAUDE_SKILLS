# Go-to-Market Strategy Guide

## Creating a Comprehensive GTM Strategy for Product Launch

This guide walks you through developing a complete go-to-market strategy. Use alongside the launch_planning_guide.md for overall timeline and the launch_plan_template.md for detailed planning.

---

## What is a GTM Strategy?

A GTM (Go-to-Market) strategy is your comprehensive plan for how you'll reach, engage, and convert your target customers. It covers:

- **Who** you're targeting (audience segmentation)
- **What** you're saying (positioning and messaging)
- **Where** you're reaching them (channels and tactics)
- **How** you're converting them (sales process and customer success)
- **When** you're executing (timeline and sequencing)

A great GTM strategy aligns marketing, sales, and customer success to create a coordinated customer journey from awareness to adoption to advocacy.

### Step 1: Define Your Target Audience

**Primary Audience** (your primary focus):

```markdown
## Primary Audience Definition

### Persona
- **Name**: Marketing Manager (or specific job title)
- **Company Size**: SMB (50-500 employees)
- **Industry**: SaaS, Technology
- **Pain Points**:
  1. Scattered marketing data across tools
  2. Unable to track campaign ROI
  3. Spending too much time on manual reporting
- **Goals**:
  1. Prove marketing ROI to leadership
  2. Make data-driven marketing decisions
  3. Spend less time on admin, more on strategy
- **How They Buy**: Influenced by product, guided by business case, needs CFO approval
- **Budget**: $50K-$500K annually
- **Timeline**: Evaluates 2-3 competitors, decision in 1-2 months

### Where They Spend Time
- LinkedIn (industry news, thought leadership)
- Product Hunt (new tools discovery)
- Slack communities (peer advice)
- Blogs (HubSpot, First Round Review)
- Podcasts (marketing, business, growth)

### Current Solutions
- Spreadsheets and manual reporting (current state)
- Google Analytics + Google Sheets (patched solution)
```

### Step 2: Segment Your Audience

**Geographic Segments**:
- **Primary**: US, UK (English-speaking, higher CAC tolerance)
- **Secondary**: EU, APAC (regulatory requirements, lower price sensitivity)

**Industry Segments**:
- **Primary**: SaaS, Technology (native to digital tools)
- **Secondary**: Professional Services, Agencies (high tool adoption)
- **Tertiary**: Enterprise, Financial (regulatory requirements)

**Company Size**:
- **SMB** (50-500): Self-service, lower price point, quick decisions
- **Mid-Market** (500-5000): Sales-assisted, moderate price, 1-2 month sales cycle
- **Enterprise** (5000+): High-touch sales, custom pricing, 3-6 month sales cycle

**Behavioral Segments**:
- **Early Adopters**: Tech-savvy, willing to experiment, want to be first
- **Pragmatists**: Want proven solutions, look for case studies, need support
- **Conservatives**: Risk-averse, need security/compliance, want established company

### Step 3: Map the Customer Journey

**Awareness Stage**:
- **Trigger**: Identifies problem or gap (new role, tool breaks, expands scope)
- **Goal**: Make them aware solution exists
- **Channels**: Blog, social, PR, content marketing, webinars
- **Message**: Problem-focused, educational, trend-based

**Consideration Stage**:
- **Trigger**: Actively evaluating solutions
- **Goal**: Make them aware of your solution and differentiation
- **Channels**: Website, product comparisons, webinars, sales conversations
- **Message**: Solution-focused, benefits, proof points, ROI

**Decision Stage**:
- **Trigger**: Ready to make buying decision
- **Goal**: Win the deal
- **Channels**: Sales conversations, contracts, demos, references
- **Message**: Implementation, support, ROI, risk mitigation

**Activation/Adoption Stage**:
- **Trigger**: Purchased or signed up
- **Goal**: Get customer to realize value
- **Channels**: Onboarding, customer success, in-app guidance, support
- **Message**: Practical, how-to, success stories

**Advocacy Stage**:
- **Trigger**: Realizing value and satisfied with product
- **Goal**: Turn customer into advocate
- **Channels**: Testimonials, case studies, referral programs, customer events
- **Message**: Impact stories, customer quotes, community

## Phase 2: Positioning & Messaging

(This section assumes you've completed the Week 2 messaging work from launch_planning_guide.md)

### Step 1: Craft Your Positioning

**Positioning Statement**:

```markdown
For [TARGET AUDIENCE], who [PROBLEM/NEED],
[PRODUCT] is a [CATEGORY] that [PRIMARY BENEFIT].

Unlike [ALTERNATIVE], our solution [KEY DIFFERENTIATOR].

## Example: Amplitude Analytics

For product managers and growth teams, who struggle with fragmented user data
and slow analytics queries that slow down decision-making,
Amplitude is a behavioral analytics platform that enables instant, self-service
exploration of user behavior and cohort analysis.

Unlike traditional tools like Google Analytics which focus on website metrics
and require SQL queries, Amplitude provides a purpose-built interface for
non-technical teams to understand engagement patterns.

Unlike other analytics platforms like Mixpanel which are expensive and slow,
Amplitude is fast, affordable, and enables the speed of decision-making
that modern growth teams require.
```

### Step 2: Develop Your Value Proposition

**Customer-Focused Value Prop** (not feature-focused):

```markdown
## Bad Value Prop (Feature-Focused)
"Real-time collaborative marketing analytics with 500+ integrations
and custom event tracking for distributed teams"

## Good Value Prop (Benefit-Focused)
"Prove your marketing ROI in minutes, not weeks. See what campaigns drive
real revenue without waiting for your data team or manual reporting."

## Better Value Prop (Problem-Outcome)
"From scattered marketing data to clear ROI visibility. Help your team
make better decisions faster, and prove your value to leadership."
```

### Step 3: Develop Core Messages (3-5)

```markdown
## Core Messages

### Message 1: Speed of Insight
**Problem**: Marketing reporting takes weeks
**Solution**: Instant visibility into marketing ROI
**Proof**: "We cut reporting time from 2 weeks to 2 minutes" - Customer quote

### Message 2: Accessible to Everyone
**Problem**: Only data teams can answer analytics questions
**Solution**: Non-technical teams can ask and answer their own questions
**Proof**: "Our whole team is more data-driven now" - Customer quote

### Message 3: Transparent ROI
**Problem**: Hard to justify marketing budget and prove value
**Solution**: Crystal clear ROI visibility for every campaign and team
**Proof**: "Helped us increase marketing budget by 40%" - Customer quote

### Message 4: Integrated Ecosystem
**Problem**: Data scattered across multiple tools
**Solution**: Central hub connecting all your marketing tools
**Proof**: "Eliminated three data integration projects" - Customer quote

### Message 5: Modern & Purpose-Built
**Problem**: Old analytics tools not designed for modern marketing
**Solution**: Built for modern teams, modern workflows
**Proof**: "Finally a tool built for how we actually work" - Customer quote
```

### Step 2: Design Your Channel Mix

**Owned Channels** (directly control):
- **Blog**: Regular content (1-2 posts/week), SEO-focused
- **Email**: Nurture campaigns, newsletters, announcements
- **Website/Landing Page**: High-converting pages, clear CTAs
- **Social Media**: Company accounts (LinkedIn, Twitter primary)

**Earned Channels** (third-party coverage):
- **PR/Media**: Tech journalists, industry publications
- **Analyst Reports**: Gartner, Forrester, G2 coverage
- **Industry Communities**: Reddit, Product Hunt, Hacker News
- **Influencers**: Respected voices, thought leaders

**Paid Channels** (purchased):
- **Digital Advertising**: LinkedIn, Google Ads, Facebook, Reddit
- **Content Syndication**: Repurpose and distribute content
- **Sponsorships**: Podcasts, newsletters, industry events
- **Affiliate/Referral**: Commission-based partnerships

**Sales Channels**:
- **Direct Sales**: Sales team outreach, demos, proposals
- **Self-Service**: Landing page, free trial, onboarding
- **Sales-Assisted**: Mix of self-service and sales support

#### Blog Strategy

**Content Calendar** (weeks leading to launch):

```markdown
## Blog Content Plan

### 6-8 Weeks Before Launch: Problem Awareness
- "The Real Cost of [Problem]" - quantify problem impact
- "5 Ways Teams Are Struggling With [Problem]"

- "How [Leading Company] Solved [Problem]"
- "The [New Category] Is Changing How Teams Work"
- "What You Should Look for in [Solution Type]"
Goal: Introduce solution concepts, start positioning alternatives

### Launch Week
- "Introducing [Your Product]: [One-Line Value Prop]"
- "Why We Built [Your Product]"
Goal: Major announcement, explain vision and approach

### Weeks 2-4 After Launch
- "How [Customer A] Uses [Your Product]"
- "[Feature] Explained: Why It Matters"
- "Getting Started: [Customer Success Story]"
Goal: Customer education, customer stories, deepening engagement

### Weeks 4-8
- "Advanced Guide to [Topic]"
- "Comparison: [Your Product] vs. [Competitor]"
- "Customer Story: [Customer B] Improved [Metric] by X%"
Goal: Deepen engagement, demonstrate value, handle objections
```

**Blog Post Guidelines**:
- Length: 1000-2000 words for launch content
- SEO: Include target keyword in title, headers, meta description
- Structure: Problem → Solution → Implementation → Results
- Calls-to-action: Subtle (email signup, demo request)
- Promotion: Email list, social media, ads

#### Email Strategy

**Email Campaign Sequence**:

```markdown
## Launch Email Campaign

### Email 1: Launch Announcement (Send day of launch)
Subject: "Finally, [Benefit Your Customers Have Been Wanting]"
Audience: Existing customers, prospects, newsletter subscribers
Goal: Announce launch, drive to blog/landing page
CTA: "Learn more" → Blog post

### Email 2: What It Is & Why (Send 2 days post-launch)
Subject: "Why We Built [Product] (And Why You Might Need It)"
Audience: Opened Email 1
Goal: Explain product and vision
CTA: "Watch demo" or "Try free"

### Email 3: Customer Story (Send 5 days post-launch)
Subject: "How [Customer] Is Using [Product]"
Audience: Opened Email 1 or 2
Goal: Show real-world impact
CTA: "Read full case study" or "Try free"

### Email 4: Feature Deep-Dive (Send 8 days post-launch)
Subject: "Here's How [Feature] Works (And Why It Matters)"
Audience: Tried product or clicked CTA in previous emails
Goal: Drive deeper engagement
CTA: "Explore feature" or "Schedule demo"

### Email 5: Proof Points (Send 10 days post-launch)
Subject: "Why Teams Choose [Product]"
Audience: All launch email recipients
Goal: Address objections, provide proof
CTA: "See comparison" or "Start free trial"

### Email 6: Limited-Time Offer (Send 14 days post-launch, if applicable)
Subject: "[Early Adopter Benefit] - Ends [Date]"
Audience: Not yet converted
Goal: Drive conversion
CTA: "Claim offer" or "Start free trial"
```

**Email Best Practices**:
- Personalization: Use first name, segment by audience type
- Subject lines: Test multiple, include benefit or curiosity
- Copy: Customer-focused, conversational, scannable
- Length: 150-200 words typical
- Design: Mobile-first, clear CTA button, minimal text
- Frequency: 1-2 emails per week during launch window

```markdown
- Continue following up with interested journalists
```

## Phase 5: Sales Strategy

### Sales Process & Enablement

**Sales Process by Segment**:

```markdown
## Sales Process

### SMB Sales Cycle (1-2 weeks)
1. Awareness: Self-directed research, email/ads lead to landing page
2. Evaluation: Free trial, in-app onboarding, customer support
3. Decision: Email sales sequences, possibly 15-min call
4. Activation: Automatic onboarding, in-app success path

### Mid-Market Sales Cycle (4-6 weeks)
1. Awareness: LinkedIn ads, content, inbound lead
2. Initial Conversation: 30-min discovery call
3. Demo: 45-min live product demo, tailored to use case
4. Evaluation: Free trial, access to customer references
5. Close: Contract negotiation, SLA agreement
6. Activation: Onboarding call, training, success plan

### Enterprise Sales Cycle (3-6 months)
1. Awareness: Industry events, analyst briefings, referrals
2. Initial Conversation: 1-hour executive briefing
3. Needs Analysis: Deep-dive into requirements, use cases
4. Demo: Customized demo with engineering/product team
5. Pilot: Pilot program with 2-3 key users
6. Reference Calls: Talk to existing customers
7. Security/Legal Review: SOC 2, data residency, legal terms
8. Contract Negotiation: Terms, pricing, SLA
9. Activation: Implementation plan, training, success plan
```

**Sales Enablement Materials**:

```markdown
## Sales Collateral

### For All Sales
- **Sales One-Pager**: Product overview, key features, benefits, CTA
- **Competitive Comparison**: How you compare to 2-3 main competitors
- **ROI Calculator**: Help quantify value in their specific context
- **Pricing Fact Sheet**: Simple pricing explanation, FAQ

### For Product Demos
- **Sales Demo Deck**: 15-20 slides covering problem, solution, features, ROI
- **Demo Environment**: Full-featured sandbox for customer trials
- **Demo Video**: 3-5 min walkthrough showing key capabilities
- **Case Study**: 1-2 detailed customer stories with metrics

### For Closing
- **Case Studies**: 3-5 detailed customer success stories with metrics
- **Customer References**: List of customers willing to speak with prospects
- **Testimonials**: Quotes from happy customers
- **Analyst Reports**: Third-party validation (if available)

### For Objection Handling
- **Competitive Response**: How you differentiate vs. each competitor
- **Proof Points**: Data and metrics supporting your claims
- **Risk Mitigation**: How you reduce implementation risk
- **Trial/Pilot Offer**: How to get them started with low risk
```

## Common GTM Mistakes to Avoid

1. **One-size-fits-all messaging**: Different audiences need different messages
2. **Feature-focused messaging**: Benefits matter more than features
3. **Insufficient channel investment**: "If you build it, they won't come"
4. **Sales team misaligned**: Sales team doesn't understand strategy
5. **Poor landing page**: Conversion rate killer - invest in optimization
6. **No differentiation**: Unclear why customer should choose you
7. **Slow sales process**: Long sales cycles kill momentum
8. **Poor onboarding**: Customer acquired but doesn't realize value
9. **No measurement**: Can't tell what's working or what to optimize
10. **Inconsistent messaging**: Message varies across channels, confuses customers
