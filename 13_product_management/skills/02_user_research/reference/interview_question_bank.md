# Interview Question Bank: 100+ Research-Validated Questions

A comprehensive collection of research-tested questions for customer interviews, organized by research goal and methodology.

---

## Section 1: Background & Context (Getting Oriented)

These questions establish context, build rapport, and frame the customer's world.

### Role & Responsibility
1. What's your title and what are you responsible for?
2. How long have you been in this role?
3. What does a typical day look like for you?
4. What are your main responsibilities and priorities?
5. Who do you report to, and who reports to you?
6. What metrics do you own or influence?
7. What's your background before this role?
8. How many people work on your team or in your function?

### Current Context
9. Tell me about the company and what it does.
10. What's your company's stage (startup, growth, mature)?
11. How has your role changed in the last year?
12. What's been your biggest win in the past 6 months?
13. What's keeping you up at night about your work?
14. What's different about your team or company compared to competitors?
15. How do you spend most of your time during the week?

### Market & Industry Understanding
16. What industry are you in, and how would you describe it?
17. Who are your main customers or end-users?
18. How is your market changing right now?
19. What's your competitive landscape look like?
20. What are the biggest trends affecting your industry?

## Section 2: Problem Discovery (The Mom Test Approach)

These questions reveal actual problems without leading or assuming.

### Understanding Current State
21. Tell me about the last time you struggled with [problem area].
22. What specifically happened? Walk me through it step by step.
23. How often does this situation come up?
24. How much time does this take you?
25. What have you already tried to solve this?
26. What's the workaround you're currently using?
27. How long have you been dealing with this?
28. Does anyone else on your team face the same problem?

### Emotion & Impact
29. How does this make you feel?
30. What's the impact if this doesn't get solved?
31. What's at stake if this continues?
32. Who else is affected by this problem?
33. What would it mean to fix this?
34. How critical is this compared to other problems you face?
35. If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing in your workflow, what would it be?

### Current Workarounds
36. How are you dealing with this right now?
37. What tools or systems do you use to work around this?
38. What's broken about your current solution?
39. Why haven't you switched to [alternative solution] yet?
40. What would need to change for you to try a different approach?
41. How much time/money are you currently spending on this workaround?
42. What's manual about your current process?

## Section 3: Jobs to Be Done (JTBD Framework)

These questions reveal the actual job the customer is trying to accomplish.

### Trigger & Motivation
43. What prompted you to start looking at solutions for this?
44. What event or realization made you think, "We need to change this"?
45. How long were you dealing with this before you decided to act?
46. What would happen if you did nothing?
47. How urgent was this for you?
48. What was the trigger that pushed you to actually do something?

### Previous Solutions & Decision Criteria
49. Before you considered [solution], how were you handling this?
50. What made you decide to explore new solutions?
51. What mattered most when you were evaluating options?
52. How did you decide between the options you considered?
53. What would have stopped you from adopting a new solution?
54. How did you involve others in the decision?
55. What concerns did people have about switching?

### Implementation & Real-World Use
56. How did you decide to go with [solution]?
57. How has it been working out since you adopted it?
58. Is it delivering on what you expected?
59. What's been better than expected?
60. What's been worse or more complicated than expected?
61. How has it changed the way you work?
62. Are you using it the way you originally planned, or differently?

### Switching Costs & Satisfaction
63. If you were choosing again today, would you pick the same solution?
64. What would make you switch away from your current solution?
65. How much switching cost (time, money, disruption) would be tolerable?
66. What would a competitor need to offer to win you over?
67. How locked in are you to your current solution?
68. What would need to be true for you to consider alternatives?

## Section 4: Specific Use Cases & Scenarios

These questions help you understand how products/solutions are actually used in context.

### Recent Behavior
69. Walk me through the last time you [used product/encountered problem].
70. What were you trying to accomplish?
71. What did you do first?
72. What happened when you tried [specific step]?
73. Did it work the way you expected?
74. What surprised you?
75. What felt awkward or confusing?

### Frequency & Context
76. How often do you deal with this situation?
77. How many people are involved?
78. How time-sensitive is it when this comes up?
79. Are there seasonal patterns to when this happens?
80. What's the range of complexity you deal with?
81. When it gets really complicated, what changes?

### Constraints & Requirements
82. Are there any regulatory or compliance requirements around this?
83. Do you need to document or audit what you're doing?
84. Who needs to sign off on decisions in this area?
85. What integrations or systems need to work together?
86. Are there any non-negotiable requirements?
87. What would break your process if it didn't work?

## Section 5: Feature & Solution Evaluation

These questions reveal what actually matters in solutions (not what people say).

### What They Actually Use
88. Walk me through exactly how you use [product/feature].
89. Which features do you use regularly?
90. Which features do you rarely or never use?
91. What do you do if [feature] didn't exist?
92. How important is [specific capability] to your work?
93. What's the first thing you do when you open [product]?
94. What would you miss most if you switched away?

### Preferences & Trade-offs
95. If you had to choose between [feature A] and [feature B], which matters more?
96. What would you sacrifice to get better [capability]?
97. How much time/cost are you willing to spend for [benefit]?
98. Would you rather have [option 1] or [option 2]?
99. How much did ease-of-use vs. features matter in your decision?
100. What features looked good on paper but don't matter in practice?

## Section 6: Competitive Understanding

These questions reveal decision criteria and competitive positioning.

### Competitive Alternatives
101. What other solutions did you consider?
102. How did you find out about [competitors]?
103. What's [Competitor A]'s main advantage over [Competitor B]?
104. Why did you ultimately choose [solution] over [competitor]?
105. What's [competitor] better at?
106. If they fixed [specific gap], would you switch?
107. What would [competitor] need to do to win your business?

### Non-Obvious Alternatives
108. What products or solutions do you think are the closest alternative to [solution]?
109. How do you currently solve this problem without [our solution]?
110. Are there other products in a different category you use for this?
111. Have you seen any new or emerging solutions to this problem?

### Team & Organization
124. How would this need to work with the rest of your team?
125. Who else needs to be happy with a solution?
126. How would your team react to [type of change]?
127. Who has veto power on adoption decisions?
128. How open is your organization to new tools/processes?
129. What's your team's typical adoption curve for new tools?
130. How resistant is your team to change?

### Integration Requirements
131. What systems would this need to integrate with?
132. What data would need to flow between systems?
133. How would this fit into your existing workflow?
134. Would this require changes to how your team works?
135. How much training would your team need?
136. What's your timeline for implementation?

### Success Criteria
137. How would you know if this solution was working?
138. What metrics would you track?
139. What would success look like after 3 months?
140. What's the minimum viable benefit that would justify the switch?
141. When would you know it was worth the effort to implement?

## Using This Question Bank

### Selecting Questions for Your Interview

1. **Pick 3-5 open-ended questions** (not more)
2. **Have 3-5 follow-up probes ready** ("Tell me more about that," "Why did that happen?")
3. **Prepare background questions** based on research about their role/industry
4. **Avoid reading from a list** (creates formal atmosphere)
5. **Listen for unexpected topics** and follow those threads

### Interview Flow Template

**Opening (5 min)**
- 2-3 rapport-building background questions
- Explain what you're learning about

**Middle (20-30 min)**
- Problem discovery questions
- Specific use case questions
- Follow their threads

**Closing (5-10 min)**
- Forward-looking questions
- Relationship building

### Red Flags for Unreliable Answers

- They give generic/scripted-sounding answers
- They describe aspirational behavior, not actual behavior
- They describe what they *should* do, not what they actually do
- They agree with leading questions
- They describe intentions but not actions
- You see inconsistencies between different questions
- They contradict their own description with later statements

**Fix**: Dig deeper with "Tell me about the last time that happened" or "How do you actually handle that?"

## Question Patterns That Work

### The "Last Time" Pattern
- Moves from opinion to behavior
- Example: "How do you prioritize features?" → "Tell me about the last time you decided what to build next"

### The "Show Me" Pattern
- Moves from description to evidence
- Example: "Do you measure impact?" → "Walk me through the last report you generated"

### The "Specific Number" Pattern
- Reveals true frequency/priority
- Example: "Is this important?" → "How many hours a week does this take?"

### The "Compare" Pattern
- Reveals decision criteria
- Example: "What do you want?" → "What matters more: speed or accuracy?"

### The "Trade-off" Pattern
- Reveals real priorities
- Example: "What features do you want?" → "Would you rather have X or Y if you could only pick one?"
