# Research Methods: Quick Reference Guide

A practical overview of the main user research methods, when to use them, and how to execute quickly.

---

## 1. One-on-One Interviews

### What It Is
Structured conversation with an individual customer about their problems, behavior, and motivations.

### When to Use
- Discovering new problems or opportunities
- Understanding decision-making process
- Deep dive on specific use case
- Exploring "why" behind behavior
- Understanding emotional/contextual factors
- Early stage product discovery

### When NOT to Use
- Validating popularity (use surveys for volume)
- Testing interface/usability (use usability tests)
- Collecting statistical data (use surveys)
- Understanding broad market sentiment (use reviews + surveys)

### Quick Setup
- **Participants**: 5-10 interviews reveals major patterns
- **Duration**: 30-60 minutes
- **Recruitment**: Reach out to existing users, ask for introductions, use LinkedIn
- **Incentive**: Optional ($10-25 for 30 min; unnecessary for existing customers)
- **Location**: Phone/Zoom is fine (coffee shop is better if in-person)
- **Note-taking**: Write notes yourself (recording feels formal)

### What to Look For
- Specific recent examples (not generalizations)
- Inconsistencies between what they say and what they do
- Emotional reactions (frustration, excitement, indifference)
- Workarounds and current solutions
- Decision-making process

### Red Flags
- Generic responses ("We want it to be easier")
- Describing aspirational behavior not actual behavior
- Agreeing with your leading questions
- No specific examples

### Timeline
- **Recruiting**: 1-2 weeks
- **Scheduling**: 1-2 weeks
- **Conducting**: 1 week (if doing 5-10)
- **Synthesis**: 2-3 days
- **Total**: 3-5 weeks for full cycle

### Cost
- Low (recruiting existing customers is free; just your time)
- Incentives: 0-500 for 10 interviews

## 2. Contextual Inquiry

### What It Is
Observing customers in their actual environment (office, home, field) using tools and processes in real context.

### When to Use
- Understanding real-world workflow and context
- Discovering hidden steps or workarounds
- Revealing environmental factors (time pressure, multiple tools, interruptions)
- Designing for realistic conditions
- Understanding how people prioritize when juggling multiple tasks

### When NOT to Use
- Early problem discovery (too time-intensive)
- Testing specific UI changes
- Gathering broad patterns (need interviews or surveys)

### Quick Setup
- **Participants**: 3-5 customers
- **Duration**: 2-4 hours per observation
- **Location**: Their actual work environment
- **Setup**: Arrange to observe during representative work
- **Note-taking**: Watch and take field notes; ask clarifying questions
- **Video**: Optional (with permission)

### What to Look For
- Real constraints (time, noise, interruptions)
- Frequency of tasks (do they actually do this daily?)
- Tool switching (what else are they using?)
- Prioritization (what matters when everything matters)
- Workarounds and hacks
- Moments of frustration

### Red Flags
- They perform differently because they're being observed
- They're not doing actual work during observation
- Focusing on rare/unusual tasks

### Timeline
- **Recruiting**: 1-2 weeks (harder than interviews)
- **Scheduling**: 2-3 weeks (coordinating with their schedule)
- **Conducting**: 2-3 weeks (1-2 observations per week)
- **Synthesis**: 1 week
- **Total**: 5-8 weeks

### Cost
- Medium (your time is significant)
- Incentives: 0 (they don't need them for their own environment)
- Travel: If in-person, add cost

## 3. Usability Testing

### What It Is
Observing users attempting realistic tasks in your product/prototype without assistance.

### When to Use
- Validating that users can accomplish specific tasks
- Identifying UI/interaction problems
- Testing new designs before building
- Validating that interface is intuitive
- Comparing 2-3 design approaches

### When NOT to Use
- Testing business strategy/positioning
- Determining if users want feature (use interviews)
- Making prioritization decisions (use surveys + interviews)
- Testing without realistic tasks

### Quick Setup
- **Participants**: 5 minimum (1-2 with same role is enough for patterns)
- **Duration**: 30-60 minutes
- **Setup**: In a quiet space, with product/prototype on screen
- **Tasks**: 3-4 realistic tasks (not "explore the app")
- **Recording**: Video + screen recording (with permission)
- **Moderation**: Facilitator asks clarifying questions

### What to Look For
- **Success/failure** on key tasks
- **Time to completion** (too long = problem)
- **Errors or dead ends** (where do they go wrong?)
- **Confusion** (hesitation, trying multiple things)
- **Assumptions** (what did they expect to work?)
- **Emotional reactions** (frustrated, confident, lost)

### Red Flags
- Participant is a power user or designer themselves
- Task is vague ("explore the app")
- You're helping them too much
- Repeating similar observations across participants (diminishing returns after 5)

### Timeline
- **Recruiting**: 1 week (focus on relevant segment)
- **Setup**: 2-3 days (prepare prototypes, tasks)
- **Conducting**: 1 week (5 sessions × 1 hour)
- **Analysis**: 2-3 days (watch videos, extract patterns)
- **Total**: 2-3 weeks

### Cost
- Medium ($50-100 per participant)
- Can use unmoderated tools (UserTesting, Maze) for lower cost ($10-30/user)

## 4. Unmoderated Remote Testing

### What It Is
Users complete pre-designed tasks asynchronously (no facilitator present) using online tools.

### When to Use
- Need sample size 20-50+ users
- Testing specific flows or features
- Need quick feedback (48-72 hours)
- Budget is limited
- Testing with geographically distributed users

### When NOT to Use
- Early discovery (need conversation)
- Complex or nuanced feedback needed
- Understanding "why" behind struggles
- High-risk decisions

### Quick Setup
- **Participants**: 20-50 (more is better for confidence)
- **Duration**: 10-20 minutes per user
- **Platform**: UserTesting, Maze, Optimal Workshop, Validately
- **Tasks**: 3-5 clear, realistic tasks
- **Screening**: Target role/segment
- **Recording**: Automatic via platform

### What to Look For
- **Success rate** (% who complete task)
- **Time to completion** (too slow = problem)
- **Where they struggled** (click patterns)
- **Quoted feedback** (why they got stuck)
- **Patterns** (3+ users having same trouble = real issue)

### Red Flags
- Low completion rate (task unclear or too hard?)
- Participants not matching your target
- No quoted explanations (only click data)
- Confusing or leading task descriptions

### Timeline
- **Setup**: 2-3 days (platform, tasks, screening)
- **Recruiting & Running**: 2-3 days (continuous recruitment)
- **Analysis**: 1-2 days (patterns from responses)
- **Total**: 1 week

### Cost
- Medium ($200-2000 depending on sample size)
- Per-user: $10-30 depending on participant type

## 5. Surveys

### What It Is
Written/online questions asked to 50-1000+ people simultaneously (or batch collected).

### When to Use
- Validating prevalence of problem ("Does everyone struggle with this?")
- Measuring satisfaction or priorities across segment
- Quick polls to guide next steps
- Quantifying problem magnitude
- Identifying which segment has which problems

### When NOT to Use
- Understanding "why" (surveys can't answer nuance)
- Testing interface (wrong method)
- Discovery (conversations better)
- Understanding context

### Quick Setup
- **Participants**: 50+ for meaningful patterns
- **Duration**: 5-10 minutes (longer = lower completion)
- **Distribution**: Email list, in-app, Qualtrics/SurveyMonkey
- **Questions**: 10-15 max
- **Incentive**: Optional ($1-3 per completion if recruiting cold)

### What to Look For
- **Response rate** (25%+ is good for existing audience)
- **Patterns in responses** (where do respondents agree?)
- **Segment differences** (role A vs. role B)
- **Confidence in results** (enough responses?)
- **Open-ended responses** for themes

### Red Flags
- Low response rate (survey poorly targeted)
- Weak questions that don't differentiate
- Too many open-ended questions (hard to synthesize)
- Asked about intentions not behaviors

### Timeline
- **Design**: 2-3 days (write good questions)
- **Recruiting**: 1 week (if recruiting cold; immediate if in-app)
- **Running**: 2-3 days (to 1 week)
- **Analysis**: 1-2 days
- **Total**: 2-3 weeks (or 5 days if internal audience)

### Cost
- Low-Medium ($0-2000)
- Free if surveying existing users
- $500-2000 if recruiting from research panel

## 6. Analytics & Behavioral Data

### What It Is
Quantitative data about how users actually use your product (events, flows, metrics).

### When to Use
- Understanding actual usage patterns (not claimed behavior)
- Identifying friction points in workflows
- Measuring impact of changes
- Finding where users drop off
- Validating that features are used

### When NOT to Use
- Understanding "why" users do something (data shows what, not why)
- Discovering entirely new problems
- Early-stage discovery
- Problems users don't realize they have

### Quick Setup
- **Data needed**: Event tracking, funnel analysis, user segments
- **Tools**: Amplitude, Mixpanel, Posthog, or your built-in analytics
- **Dashboards**: Set up key funnels, user segments
- **Frequency**: Weekly review of patterns

### What to Look For
- **Drop-off points** (where do users leave?)
- **Success metrics** (which users are successful?)
- **Frequency** (how often are features actually used?)
- **Time** (how long do key tasks take?)
- **Segments** (do different user types behave differently?)

### Red Flags
- Assuming analytics explain "why" (combine with interviews)
- Focusing on vanity metrics (sign-ups) not outcome metrics (success)
- Not segmenting (aggregate data hides patterns)
- Making decisions without talking to users

### Timeline
- **Setup**: 1-4 weeks (depends on event tracking maturity)
- **Analysis**: Ongoing (weekly reviews)
- **Insights**: 2-3 weeks of data before patterns clear
- **Total**: 3-6 weeks if setting up from scratch

### Cost
- Low ($50-500/month for tools)
- Your analytics eng time (significant)

## 7. Diary Studies

### What It Is
Participants log their behavior (text, photos, video) over days/weeks with minimal structure.

### When to Use
- Understanding behavior over time (patterns across days/weeks)
- Capturing context when it happens (not relying on memory)
- Understanding frequency/seasonality
- Seeing "typical week" realities
- Natural behavior (no artificiality of session)

### When NOT to Use
- Quick answers needed (too slow)
- Clarity needed immediately (asynchronous is slow)
- Limited budget
- Complex synthesis needed

### Quick Setup
- **Participants**: 10-20 (diary studies are demanding)
- **Duration**: 7-14 days
- **Effort**: 5-10 minutes per day from participants
- **Tool**: Custom app, Google Forms, shared doc, Slack
- **Prompts**: 3-5 simple daily prompts
- **Check-in**: Occasional slack/email reminders

### What to Look For
- **Patterns across days** (what's consistent?)
- **Context** (when, where, who, what triggers behavior?)
- **Frequency** (how often does X happen?)
- **Emotional reactions** (mood, frustration, success?)
- **Workarounds** (how do they get around problems?)

### Red Flags
- Participants dropping out (burden too high)
- Log entries getting shorter (engagement declining)
- One-off entries (not enough context)
- Unusual behavior (they know they're being watched)

### Timeline
- **Recruiting**: 1-2 weeks
- **Running**: 7-14 days
- **Analysis**: 1 week
- **Total**: 3-4 weeks

### Cost
- Medium ($100-400 depending on incentives)
- Incentives: $50-100 per participant (asking for commitment)

## 8. Competitive Research

### What It Is
Systematic examination of competitor products, pricing, positioning, and customer feedback.

### When to Use
- Understanding alternatives customers consider
- Benchmarking features and pricing
- Identifying gaps in market
- Understanding market positioning
- Learning from what competitors got right/wrong

### When NOT to Use
- Understanding your own customers (talk to them instead)
- Validating your roadmap (talk to your customers)
- As substitute for customer research

### Quick Setup
- **Tools**: G2, Capterra, app stores (reviews)
- **Competitive set**: Identify 3-5 closest competitors
- **Deep dive**: Use each product as target user would
- **Research**: Read reviews, pricing pages, help docs
- **Timeline**: 2-3 hours per competitor

### What to Look For
- **Feature set** (what are they building?)
- **Pricing model** (how are they monetizing?)
- **User feedback** (what do customers love/hate?)
- **Positioning** (how are they selling?)
- **Gaps** (what's not well-served?)

### Red Flags
- Only looking at feature checklists (missing context)
- Not understanding customer complaints
- Mimicking competitors' features
- Not understanding their positioning

### Timeline
- **Setup**: 1-2 days
- **Research**: 1 week (thorough review)
- **Analysis**: 2-3 days
- **Total**: 2-3 weeks

### Cost
- Low (mostly your time)
- G2/Capterra subscriptions: $100-500/month

## 9. User Testing Prototypes

### What It Is
Testing mockups, wireframes, or high-fidelity prototypes before building.

### When to Use
- Validating design direction before development
- Choosing between 2-3 approaches
- Getting early feedback on UI/interaction
- De-risking major design changes

### When NOT to Use
- Needs real data/behavior (test working product)
- Testing with power users only (not representative)
- Testing too early (before solid concept)

### Quick Setup
- **Prototypes**: Figma, Framer, InVision, paper prototypes
- **Participants**: 3-5 users from target segment
- **Setup**: Same as usability testing
- **Tasks**: Same as usability testing

### What to Look For
- Can they navigate the prototype?
- Do they understand the concept?
- Where do they expect things to be?
- What's confusing?
- Would they use it?

### Red Flags
- Testing too refined too early (wastes time on details)
- Not being open to major changes (becomes attachment)
- Participants who are too invested (employees, enthusiasts)

### Timeline
- **Prototype**: 2-5 days (depends on fidelity)
- **Testing**: 1 week (3-5 sessions)
- **Iteration**: 1-2 weeks (refine based on feedback)
- **Total**: 2-4 weeks

### Cost
- Low ($0-500)
- If using research tools: $10-30 per participant

## Quick Decision Tree: Which Method Should I Use?

```
What do I need to learn?

├─ "What problems do users face?"
│  └─ Use: One-on-one interviews (5-10)
│
├─ "Can users accomplish this task?"
│  └─ Use: Usability test (5 users)
│
├─ "How many people have this problem?"
│  └─ Use: Survey (50+ responses)
│
├─ "What's actually happening in real context?"
│  └─ Use: Contextual inquiry (3-5) or Diary study (10-20)
│
├─ "How do users currently solve this?"
│  └─ Use: Interview (5-10) + Contextual inquiry (3-5)
│
├─ "Should we build feature X or Y?"
│  └─ Use: Interview (learn real need) then Analytics (validate)
│
├─ "Where are users dropping off?"
│  └─ Use: Analytics (funnels) then Interview (why?)
│
├─ "What do competitors offer?"
│  └─ Use: Competitive research + reviews
│
└─ "Should we launch like this?"
   └─ Use: Usability test (5) + Analytics review
```

## Combining Methods: Research Sequences

### Discovery Sequence (New Problem Space)
1. **Interviews** (5-10, 1-2 weeks): Understand problem
2. **Contextual inquiry** (3-5, 2-3 weeks): See in context
3. **Competitive research** (2-3 days): Understand alternatives

### Validation Sequence (Before Build)
1. **Interviews** (5 quick calls): Validate problem
2. **Prototype testing** (3-5 users): Validate concept
3. **Analytics** (3-6 weeks): Measure post-launch

### Optimization Sequence (Iterating on Live Product)
1. **Analytics** (weekly review): Identify friction points
2. **Interviews** (1-2/week): Understand why
3. **Usability test** (5 users): Validate fix

### Market Understanding Sequence (Competitive positioning)
1. **Competitive research**: Understand alternatives
2. **Interviews** (5-10): Understand decision criteria
3. **Survey** (50+): Validate prevalence
4. **Competitive usage testing**: Test their product

## Starting Simple

If you're new to research and overwhelmed, start here:

**Week 1**: Conduct 3 interviews with current customers
- Call customers you know
- Ask: "Tell me about the last time you struggled with [problem area]"
- Write up what you learned
- Done.

**Week 2**: Test one key workflow with 3 users
- Show them your product
- Ask them to accomplish one task
- Watch where they get stuck
- Done.

**Week 3**: Survey 20 customers
- Ask: "How often do you face [problem]?"
- Ask: "Would [solution] be helpful?"
- Done.

This gives you more insight than most teams have and costs almost nothing.
