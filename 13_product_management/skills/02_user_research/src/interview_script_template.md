# Interview Script Template: Customizable Framework

Use this template to prepare for customer interviews. Print it out or open it on your computer during the call. Don't read it verbatim—use it as a guide.

---

## Pre-Interview Prep Checklist

**Before scheduling**:
- [ ] Customer is relevant to your learning goal
- [ ] You understand their role/company
- [ ] 30-45 minutes scheduled
- [ ] No sales/pitch goal
- [ ] Clear learning goal written down

**Before the call**:
- [ ] Quiet space; no distractions
- [ ] Pen and paper ready
- [ ] Minimize other windows/tabs
- [ ] Test video/audio
- [ ] Have backup participant in case they cancel

---

## Interview Script

### Opening: Build Rapport (5 minutes)

**Your opening** (adapt to your style, don't read verbatim):

> "Thanks so much for taking time to talk today. I really appreciate it.
>
> I'm learning how [target customer type / people in your role] approach [problem area].
> I'm **not** here to pitch anything—just want to understand your experience and perspective.
>
> Here's what's important: we're testing the [product/concept], not you. There are no wrong answers.
> If something is confusing, that's valuable information. If you can't figure something out,
> that just tells us how we can improve.
>
> You can think out loud as we go, and if you get stuck, just let me know.
>
> Sound good? Any questions before we start?"

**Why this works**:
- Reassures them they're not being judged
- Sets expectation that honesty is valued
- Creates psychological safety to criticize

---

### Phase 1: Context (5-10 minutes)

**Goal**: Understand their world and situation. Ask 2-3 of these:

#### Their Role & Situation

**Choose the most relevant**:

*"Tell me about your role and what you're responsible for."*
- Get sense of: title, team size, scope, seniority
- Follow-up if needed: "How long have you been in this role?"

*"What does a typical [day/week] look like for you?"*
- Reveals: actual workflow, priorities, time allocation
- Follow-up: "How much time do you spend on [specific task]?"

*"Tell me about your team/organization."*
- Understand: company size, industry, structure
- Follow-up: "How many people work on [relevant area]?"

#### Their Recent Experience

**If learning about a specific problem**:

*"Tell me about the last time you dealt with [problem area]."*
- This triggers specific recent example (not generalizations)
- Follow-up: "Walk me through what happened step-by-step"

*"How often do you encounter [situation]?"*
- Reveals: frequency (monthly? daily?)
- Follow-up: "When was the last time?"

#### Industry/Market Context

*"How has your role changed in the past year?"*
- Reveals: what's important right now, what's shifting

*"What's the competitive landscape like in your space?"*
- Understand: what alternatives exist, what matters

**Listen for**:
- Specific details (they're describing real experience)
- Emotional language (frustration, excitement = importance)
- What they emphasize (tells you their priorities)

**Your job**: Say "Tell me more about that" when they say something interesting or vague.

---

### Phase 2: Current Behavior & Problem (15-25 minutes)

**Goal**: Understand how they currently handle the problem and what's broken.

#### How They Currently Solve It

*"How are you handling [problem area] right now?"*
- Get their actual solution (workarounds, tools, processes)
- Follow-up: "Walk me through your process step by step"

*"What tools are you currently using for [task]?"*
- Understand: current tech stack
- Follow-up: "How do those tools work together?"

#### What's Broken / Frustrations

*"What's the hardest part about [process]?"*
- Get at pain points directly
- Follow-up: "Why is that hard?"

*"What frustrates you most about [current approach]?"*
- Emotional reaction reveals importance
- Follow-up: "How often does that happen?"

*"How much time does [task] take you?"*
- Quantify the problem
- If they say "2 hours a week," that's 100 hours/year = real problem

#### Workarounds (Gold)

*"What's your workaround for [common problem]?"*
- Workarounds reveal real pain
- If they built a workaround, the problem matters

*"How do you get around [limitation]?"*
- Follow-up: "How long did it take you to figure that out?"

*"What do you do when [scenario] happens?"*
- Reveals how they actually handle edge cases

#### Decision-Making

*"How did you decide on [current solution]?"*
- What matters when they choose tools/approaches
- Follow-up: "What other options did you consider?"

*"What would make you switch to something different?"*
- Reveals switching friction and decision criteria

**Listen for**:
- Time spent (importance indicator)
- Manual steps (where is human effort required?)
- Emotional tone (frustration? resignation? excitement?)
- Workarounds (these reveal unmet needs)
- Frequency of problem (daily? monthly?)

**Remember**: Use silence. After they answer, pause 10 seconds. They'll usually add important detail.

---

### Phase 3: Desired Outcome & Motivation (5-10 minutes)

**Goal**: Understand what they're ultimately trying to accomplish (not the solution they want).

#### The Real Goal

*"What are you ultimately trying to accomplish?"*
- Get at the job to be done
- Follow-up: "Why does that matter?"

*"If [problem area] were perfect, what would that look like?"*
- Reveals their mental model of ideal state
- Follow-up: "What's most important about that?"

*"What does success look like to you?"*
- What outcome are they optimizing for?
- Follow-up: "How would you measure it?"

#### Why It Matters

*"Why is [outcome] important?"*
- Get at underlying motivation
- Follow-up: "What happens if you don't achieve that?"

*"Who else cares about [outcome]?"*
- Reveals stakeholders and their priorities

#### Constraints

*"What are your constraints or non-negotiables?"*
- Regulatory requirements? Budget limits? Integration needs?
- Follow-up: "What would break if [constraint] didn't exist?"

**Listen for**:
- Emotion when they talk about desired state
- Who else cares (stakeholders = decision complexity)
- Constraints they mention (non-negotiables)
- What they value most (when they talk about it, they care)

---

### Phase 4: Learning Validation (2-3 minutes)

**Goal**: Check that you learned what you needed; prepare for follow-up.

#### Did You Understand?

*"Let me make sure I understood correctly..."* [summarize your learning]

- "So it sounds like [key challenge] is important because [why it matters]—did I get that right?"
- Gives them chance to correct you
- Slows down to deepen understanding

#### What Did You Miss?

*"Is there anything important we didn't cover?"*

*"What would be most helpful for me to understand better?"*

*"Is there anything you'd want me to know?"*

#### Relationship Building

*"Would it be helpful if I shared what we're learning?"*

*"Can I follow up if I have more questions?"*

*"Are there other people on your team I should talk to?"*

*"How do you prefer to stay updated?"*

## After the Interview: Documentation

**Within 2 hours** of the call, write up:

```
INTERVIEW NOTES: [Name, Role, Company, Date]

LEARNING GOAL: [What you came to learn]

KEY OBSERVATIONS:
1. [Most important observation about their situation]
2. [Observation about current process/workaround]
3. [Observation about what matters to them]
4. [Emotion/tone/something surprising]

DIRECT QUOTES (only if revealing emotion or insight):
- "[Quote that reveals motivation or mental model]"

WORKAROUNDS THEY'VE BUILT:
- [Signals real pain point]

UNMET NEEDS (explicit or implied):
- [What's clearly a problem for them]

SEGMENT/CONTEXT:
- Role, company size, industry
- How representative?

SURPRISES OR CONTRADICTIONS:
- [What contradicted your assumption?]

FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS:
- [What do you still need to understand?]
```

## Interview Scenarios: Customize for Your Situation

### Scenario A: Discovery Interview (New Problem Area)

**Learning goal**: "What problems do [target segment] face with [area]?"

**Key questions to emphasize**:
- Context: "Tell me about your current process"
- Pain points: "What frustrates you?" "What takes most time?"
- Workarounds: "How do you get around [problem]?"
- Skip solution talk; focus on understanding the problem

### Scenario B: Concept Validation Interview

**Learning goal**: "Do users actually want [solution]? Would they use it?"

**Key questions to emphasize**:
- Current state: "How are you handling this now?"
- Desired state: "What would ideal look like?"
- Decision criteria: "What would matter most in choosing a solution?"
- Don't mention your idea; let them describe what they want
- Only mention your concept if they bring it up first

### Scenario C: JTBD Interview (Understanding Motivation)

**Learning goal**: "What job are they trying to accomplish? Why does it matter?"

**Key questions to emphasize**:
- Trigger: "What prompted you to start looking for solutions?"
- Context: "Walk me through the last time you [encountered problem]"
- Desired outcome: "What are you ultimately trying to accomplish?"
- Decision criteria: "When you were evaluating options, what mattered most?"

### Scenario D: Competitive Loss Interview

**Learning goal**: "Why did they choose competitor X instead of us?"

**Key questions to emphasize**:
- Their process: "Can you walk me through your decision?"
- Their evaluation: "What were you looking for?"
- What competitor did better: "What did they do that we didn't?"
- Our gaps: "What would make you reconsider?"

### Scenario E: Churn/Retention Interview

**Learning goal**: "Why did they stop using us? What would bring them back?"

**Key questions to emphasize**:
- Trigger: "What made you decide to move away?"
- What went wrong: "What wasn't working?"
- Alternatives: "What are you using instead?"
- Opportunity: "What would make you come back?"

## Interview Tips & Tricks

### The Critical Skill: Silence

After asking a question, **count to 10 before speaking**.

Most people fill silence with additional detail. That detail is often the real answer.

### How to Ask "Why" Without Sounding Like a Toddler

Instead of repeating "why?" use:
- "Help me understand..."
- "Walk me through your thinking..."
- "What was behind that decision?"
- "I'm curious about..."
- "Tell me more about that"

### Dealing with Vague Answers

If they give a generic answer ("It's complicated"):

*"Tell me about the last time that happened"*
→ Forces specific example

*"Walk me through what happened"*
→ Gets details

*"What was the biggest challenge?"*
→ Gets at pain point

### Red Flag: They're Not Being Honest

Watch for:
- Generic/scripted-sounding answers
- Describing aspirational behavior ("I should...") vs. actual behavior
- Agreeing with your leading questions
- Inconsistencies between different answers

**Fix**: Dig deeper with:
- "Tell me about the last time..."
- "Walk me through specifically how..."
- "How does that actually work in practice?"

### Building Rapport

- Remember details they mention and refer back to them
- Mirror their language/terminology
- Show genuine curiosity (don't fake it)
- Don't jump to solutions; focus on understanding
- Thank them genuinely

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Pitching Instead of Learning
- You start talking about your idea
- Remember: listening > talking
- If they ask about your solution, say: "Let me first understand [their situation]"

### ❌ Leading Questions
- "Wouldn't you want [feature]?" (they'll agree)
- "Isn't [current solution] frustrating?" (they'll say yes)
- Ask open-ended: "How's that working?"

### ❌ Asking What They Want
- "What features do you want?" (gets nice-to-haves)
- Instead: "Tell me about the last time you struggled"

### ❌ Not Taking Notes
- You forget details
- You remember memorable quote, not actual pattern
- Write notes immediately after call

### ❌ Only Recruiting Enthusiasts
- They're not representative
- Recruit mix: power users + regular users + skeptics

### ❌ Interviewing Only Happy Customers
- You miss important churn reasons
- Also interview people who churned or chose competitor

## Quick Reference: What to Capture During Call

**DO write down**:
- Specific recent examples (when, what happened)
- Emotional language (frustration, excitement)
- Workarounds they've built
- Surprising statements
- Numbers/metrics (time, frequency, cost)
- What they actually do (vs. what they say they do)

**DON'T spend time writing**:
- Full transcription (impossible while listening)
- Every word they say (focus on meaning)
- Your interpretation (capture that after)

## Interview Template Variations

### Short Form (15-20 min interview)

Good for: Quick coffee conversations, existing customers

```
1. Context: "What's been top of mind for you?" (2 min)
2. Problem: "Tell me about [recent challenge]" (5 min)
3. Current approach: "How are you handling it?" (3 min)
4. Desired state: "What would be better?" (3 min)
5. Close: "Anything else?" (2 min)
```

### Long Form (45-60 min deep dive)

Good for: New user type, complex decision process

```
1. Rapport: General conversation (5 min)
2. Background: Their role and situation (5-10 min)
3. Problem discovery: Pain points, workarounds (15-20 min)
4. Current solution: How they solve it now (5-10 min)
5. Desired outcome: What would be ideal (5-10 min)
6. Decision process: How would they choose (5 min)
7. Close: Follow-up and relationship (2-3 min)
```

## Interview Success Indicators

**Good interview** if you:
- [ ] Heard specific recent examples (not generalizations)
- [ ] Learned 2-3 things you didn't know
- [ ] Discovered at least one workaround they've built
- [ ] Understood their emotional relationship with problem
- [ ] Know what matters most when they choose solutions
- [ ] Could explain their job to someone else
- [ ] They seemed engaged and honest

**Weak interview** if:
- [ ] Got mostly generic answers
- [ ] Spent more time talking than listening
- [ ] Didn't ask follow-up questions
- [ ] Got no specific examples
- [ ] No surprises or contradictions to your hypothesis
- [ ] Could be summarized in one sentence
