### Executive Summary (3-4 sentences)

Summarize your headline learning in plain language.

**Example**:
> We conducted 5 interviews with marketing managers at Series B SaaS companies to understand their tool selection process. The key finding: multi-stakeholder approval is required in almost all cases (4 of 5), extending decision cycles from 1-2 weeks to 3-4 weeks. Additionally, feature discoverability is a major barrier to adoption—users operate at 20-40% of available features because they don't know what exists.

### Key Findings

Present 3 major findings, one paragraph each.

**Format**: [Finding name] • [Evidence] • [Implication]

---

**Finding 1: Multi-Stakeholder Approval Extends Decision Cycles**

All 5 participants required approval from at least one other person (VP, CFO, IT) before committing to a tool. Sarah's decision took 3 weeks for approval; Marcus faced IT review adding 1 month. This means decision cycles are 3-4x longer than individual preference alone. **Implication**: Sales process needs to address multi-stakeholder concerns; consider building approval/sharing workflows into product to help customers get internal buy-in.

---

**Finding 2: Feature Discoverability Is a Major Adoption Barrier**

Users operate at 20-40% of available feature set. Elena didn't know about integrations until 6 months in. Sarah said she "uses maybe 20% of features" because "I don't know what else is there." Tom couldn't find advanced reporting without help. **Root cause**: Features aren't visible in navigation; onboarding is shallow. **Implication**: Immediate opportunity to improve onboarding and in-product discovery (progressive disclosure, contextual tips, feature tours).

---

**Finding 3: Switching Costs Are Real But Tolerable**

Users reported 10-20 hours of setup/migration/training to switch. Sarah spent 15 hours + 4 weeks for data migration. Tom needed 2 weeks internal training. Despite this, all 5 considered switching if alternatives offered meaningful improvements. **Implication**: Onboarding and migration support are sales and retention tools. Consider building migration tools for common previous tools.

## 2. Research Overview

### 2.1 Objective & Research Questions

**Overall objective**:
Understand how marketing managers at Series B/C SaaS companies evaluate and adopt marketing tools; identify adoption barriers and opportunities.

**Specific research questions**:
1. What problems are they trying to solve? (What's the job?)
2. How long is their decision process? (Who's involved? What takes time?)
3. How well do they discover and use available features?
4. What causes them to switch tools?
5. What would accelerate their adoption?

### 2.2 Methodology

**Method**: Customer interviews (moderated, semi-structured, 45-60 min)

**Rationale**: Rich qualitative data about decision process, motivations, and constraints

**Recruitment criteria**:
- Role: Marketing Manager, Marketing Ops, Product Marketer, Growth Manager
- Company: Series A-C SaaS (20-200 people)
- Experience: Using marketing tools for 6+ months
- Geography: US-based

**Sample size**: 5 participants

**Timing**: Nov 5-15, 2024 (2-week period)

**Incentive**: No compensation; existing customers

### 2.3 Limitations

- **Geographic**: US only; may not apply to international markets
- **Segment**: Mid-market SaaS only; SMB and enterprise not well-represented
- **Recency**: Nov 2024; market conditions may change
- **Sample size**: 5 is statistically small; patterns validated by consistency, not volume
- **Selection bias**: All existing customers; may not represent non-adopters or competitors' customers

## 4. Research Findings: Detailed

### 4.1 Finding #1: Multi-Stakeholder Approval Extends Decision Cycles

**Finding statement**: Marketing tool purchasing decisions require multi-stakeholder approval (80% of participants), extending typical decision cycles from 1-2 weeks to 3-4 weeks.

**Evidence**:
- Sarah: VP of Marketing makes final decision (3-week approval process)
- Elena: IT security review required (added 2 weeks) + budget approval
- Tom: CEO sign-off required for purchases >$15k (1 week)
- Marcus: Procurement department handles (1-month standard process)
- Julie: Finance and IT both required to sign off

**Participant breakdown**: 4 of 5 explicitly mentioned multi-stakeholder approval; 1 (Tom) implied it by saying "CEO needs to approve"

**Impact**: Decision cycles 3-4x longer than single individual

**Root causes**:
- Budget approval requirements (all sizes)
- Integration with existing systems (larger companies)
- Security/compliance review (enterprise)
- Procurement process (mid-market+)

**Segment variation**:
- Series A (Tom): CEO approval only (faster, simpler)
- Series B (Sarah, Marcus, Julie): VP approval + finance (moderate complexity)
- Enterprise (Elena): IT + Finance + Security (most complex)

---

### 4.2 Finding #2: Feature Discoverability Is Major Adoption Barrier

**Finding statement**: Users operate at 20-40% of available features due to poor discoverability; this limits adoption and creates perception that tool lacks needed capabilities.

**Evidence**:
- Sarah: "I use maybe 20% of [platform]. I don't know what else is there."
- Elena: "Discovered integrations 6 months in; would have used them from day 1"
- Tom: "Couldn't find advanced reporting until I asked support"
- Marcus: "Half the features I'd want aren't where I'd expect them"
- Julie: "After 6 months, I'm still learning about features"

**Participant breakdown**: 5 of 5 explicitly mentioned feature discoverability challenges

**Root causes identified**:
- **Navigation**: Features not visible in main UI; buried in menus
- **Onboarding**: Training covers basic features; doesn't introduce full capability set
- **Help documentation**: Separate from product; users don't know where to look
- **Progressive disclosure missing**: All features shown at once (overwhelming); no gradual introduction

**Evidence of impact**:
- Users think tool lacks features they actually need (when features exist)
- Users underestimate product capability when evaluating
- Customers say "we use X for Y and Z for the rest" when X could do Y and Z

---

### 4.3 Finding #3: Switching Friction Exists but Isn't Absolute

**Finding statement**: Users experience 10-20 hours of switching friction (setup, migration, training), but this doesn't prevent switching if alternatives offer meaningful improvement.

**Evidence**:
- Sarah: 15 hours setup + 4 weeks data migration from previous tool
- Elena: 2 weeks internal training on new system
- Tom: "Implementation would take week or two; low barrier for us"
- Marcus: "Locked into annual contract; would switch if [X] improved"
- Julie: "Migration cost is barrier, but not dealbreaker"

**Types of friction**:
1. **Setup/implementation** (5-10 hours): Configuration, initial setup
2. **Data migration** (varies widely): Exporting from old tool, importing to new
3. **Training** (5-20 hours): Getting team up to speed
4. **User adoption** (ongoing): Changing team workflows

**Participant tolerance**:
- All 5 willing to tolerate switching cost if product delivered meaningful improvement
- No participant cited switching cost as absolute dealbreaker
- Most would switch for clear integration wins or feature gaps addressed

## 5. Segment Analysis

### 5.1 Series A/B Company Differences

**Series A (n=1)**:
- Faster decisions (1 week)
- CEO makes decision
- Limited budget constraints
- Higher risk tolerance
- Decision criteria: feature fit, ease of use

**Series B/C (n=4)**:
- Longer decisions (2-3 weeks)
- Multiple stakeholder approvals
- Budget approval processes
- Process-oriented (compliance, etc.)
- Decision criteria: integration, ease of use, vendor stability

### 5.2 Role-Based Differences

**Individual Contributor** (n=2, Tom/Julie):
- Make recommendations but don't decide
- Care about ease of use and feature fit
- Less concerned with organizational friction
- Switching decision made by manager/executive

**Manager** (n=3, Sarah/Elena/Marcus):
- Make final decision or strong influence
- Care about team adoption and training
- Concerned with organizational processes
- Multiple stakeholder concerns

## 6. Synthesis: Patterns & Insights

### 6.1 Decision-Making Pattern

```
Stage 1: Problem Recognition (1-2 weeks before evaluating)
↓
Stage 2: Evaluation (1-3 weeks)
├─ Research options
├─ Demo or trial
├─ Compare 2-3 alternatives
└─ Get internal feedback
↓
Stage 3: Approval (1-4 weeks)
├─ Get budget approval
├─ IT/security review
└─ Final stakeholder sign-off
↓
Stage 4: Implementation (1-4 weeks)
├─ Setup and configuration
├─ Data migration
├─ Team training
└─ Go-live
```

**Total: 4-13 weeks** from problem recognition to go-live

### 6.2 Feature Discoverability Impact on Adoption

```
User arrives → Limited onboarding → Uses basic features
                           ↓
                    Doesn't know about X capability
                           ↓
                    "This tool can't do X" (incorrect)
                           ↓
                    Looking for alternative that does X
                           ↓
                    Potential churn opportunity
```

## 7. Opportunities & Recommendations

### 7.1 Opportunities (Ranked by Impact)

#### Opportunity 1: Improve Feature Discoverability (HIGH IMPACT, MEDIUM EFFORT)

**Problem**: Users operate at 20-40% of features; limits perceived value and increases churn risk

**Solution approaches**:
1. Progressive disclosure onboarding (introduce capabilities over time)
2. Contextual help in workflows ("Did you know you can also...")
3. Feature tours or interactive tutorials
4. Improved navigation/categorization by user role
5. Better help documentation (in-app, not separate)

**Evidence supporting**: 5 of 5 users mentioned; directly impacts adoption

**Effort estimate**: 4-6 weeks (design + build + testing)

**Recommendation**: Prioritize for next quarter roadmap; quick win with high impact

---

#### Opportunity 2: Build Multi-Stakeholder Workflows (HIGH IMPACT, HIGH EFFORT)

**Problem**: Decision cycles extended 3-4x due to approvals; opportunity to facilitate this process

**Solution approaches**:
1. Shareable dashboards for stakeholders (show value without giving access)
2. Approval workflows or request forms (built-in)
3. ROI calculator or metrics dashboard (helps justify to finance)
4. Security/compliance documentation (helps IT review)
5. Implementation guides by stakeholder (CEO, CFO, IT perspectives)

**Evidence supporting**: 4 of 5 users mentioned; would accelerate sales cycles

**Effort estimate**: 8-12 weeks (product + design + content)

**Recommendation**: Research deeper with larger sample; consider for Q3 roadmap

---

#### Opportunity 3: Create Migration Tools for Common Previous Tools (MEDIUM IMPACT, HIGH EFFORT)

**Problem**: Users lose 10+ hours to data migration; reduces new customer onboarding experience

**Solution approaches**:
1. Pre-built import connectors for top 3 previous tools
2. Step-by-step migration guides
3. Data mapping tools
4. Migration support/consultation

**Evidence supporting**: All users mentioned migration; could reduce switching friction

**Effort estimate**: 4-8 weeks per tool (varies by complexity)

**Recommendation**: Lower priority; validate demand from sales team on most common previous tools first

---

1. **Better onboarding messaging** about what's possible
2. **In-app contextual tips** highlighting lesser-known features
3. **Help center reorganization** by user role/use case
4. **Email sequence** for new users introducing advanced capabilities over time

## How to Choose Which Template

**Use Template A** if:
- You need to share findings quickly
- Team moves fast and doesn't want lengthy docs
- You want to drive action this week
- Audience has limited time

**Use Template B** if:
- You need comprehensive documentation
- Sharing findings widely (many teams)
- Legal/compliance requires detailed records
- You need to reference findings over time
- Multiple rounds of follow-up research will reference this

**Use Template C** if:
- You need leadership buy-in
- Showing "reality" of customer situation is important
- Audience responds to storytelling
- You want to build empathy, not just data
- Shorter attention spans (executives, board)

---

## Distribution & Follow-Up

**Share research findings within 1 week** of completion (while fresh)

**Include**:
- 1-page summary (everyone)
- Video highlights (leadership)
- Full report (core product team)
- Raw notes (synthesis reference)

**Follow-up**:
- Product team discussion: "What should we do about this?"
- Update roadmap/strategy based on findings
- Make a decision: Which opportunities do we prioritize?
- Measure: Did our changes improve adoption/retention?

**Never** conduct research and not share findings. If you share it, it drives decisions.

---

## Quality Checklist

Before distributing your report:

- [ ] **Accurate**: Facts match interview data
- [ ] **Representative**: Clear about segment and limitations
- [ ] **Actionable**: Recommendations have specific next steps
- [ ] **Interesting**: Tells story that engages audience
- [ ] **Honest**: Including disconfirming data, not just confirmation
- [ ] **Clear**: Someone unfamiliar could understand it
- [ ] **Timely**: Distributed while findings are fresh
- [ ] **Attributed**: Clear who said what and sample size
