# User Research: 30-Minute Quick Start Guide

Start talking to customers weekly and transform insights into product decisions.

---

## Your 30-Minute Roadmap

- **Minutes 1-5**: Mindset shift from research project to continuous discovery
- **Minutes 6-15**: Learn to conduct better customer interviews
- **Minutes 16-25**: Set up your discovery cadence
- **Minutes 26-30**: Document and share what you learn

---

## 5 Essential Concepts Every PM Needs

### 1. Continuous Discovery Over Annual Research
Stop waiting for big research projects. Instead, embed customer conversations into your weekly rhythm (1-2 interviews per week minimum).

**Why it matters**: Real-time insights shape decisions immediately; whole team builds customer empathy; safer experimentation grounded in evidence.

**Actionable**: Schedule 1-2 customer conversations for next week. Block time before execution planning starts.

### 2. Interview Customers About Behavior, Not Future Intent
People are terrible at predicting their own behavior. They'll tell you what they think you want to hear.

**Bad questions**: "Would you use a product that...?" "Do you find this frustrating?"
**Good questions**: "Tell me about the last time you [encountered the problem]. What did you do?"

**Actionable**: Write 5 interview questions focused on past behavior, not hypothetical preferences.

### 3. The Mom Test Framework
Even your mom won't intentionally lie to you, but she'll try to be helpful. Never ask leading questions that assume your answer is correct.

**Wrong**: "Do you think our onboarding is too complex?"
**Right**: "Walk me through what happened when you first used the product. What was easy? What was confusing?"

Listen to actions, not words. People do what they're incentivized to do, not what they say they'll do.

**Actionable**: In your next interview, commit to asking only follow-up questions. Let customers guide the story.

### 4. Pattern Recognition, Not Individual Quotes
One customer's complaint isn't a problem. When three unrelated customers mention the same issue unprompted, that's a pattern worth investigating.

**Actionable**: After each interview, write one sentence: "What's the most surprising thing I learned?" After 10 interviews, look for repeating themes.

### 5. Everyone on the Team Should Talk to Customers
Research isn't gatekeeping. When engineers, designers, and PMs talk directly to customers, they understand context firsthand and make better decisions.

**Actionable**: Invite your designer and engineer to your next two customer interviews. Debrief together immediately after.

## First Steps Checklist

### Week 1: Set Up Your Discovery Practice
- [ ] Identify 5-10 current customers willing to chat (existing users, not prospects)
- [ ] Schedule your first 2-3 interviews (30-45 minutes each)
- [ ] Prepare 5-7 open-ended questions focusing on jobs, problems, current solutions
- [ ] Brief your team: "Why we're talking to customers" (this week we're learning about X)

### Week 1-2: Conduct Interviews
- [ ] Take simple notes during calls (bullet points, not transcriptions)
- [ ] Record permission: "Can I record for my notes?" (not mandatory)
- [ ] Listen for stories: "Tell me about the last time you..."
- [ ] Ask "why" at least 3 times to uncover root causes
- [ ] End with: "Who else should I talk to?" (snowball sampling)

### Week 2: Synthesis
- [ ] Within 24 hours, write down your top 3 learnings from interviews
- [ ] Share findings with your team in a brief (15 min) sync
- [ ] Identify patterns: "Do we hear this repeatedly?"
- [ ] Discuss implications: "How does this change our thinking?"

### Week 3: Action
- [ ] Use insights to inform your current roadmap priorities
- [ ] Update backlog items based on customer context
- [ ] Test one assumption from interviews with your product
- [ ] Schedule next 2 interviews (continue the cadence)

### Ongoing: Establish Weekly Rhythm
- [ ] Block 2 hours per week: 1 hour interviews, 1 hour synthesis
- [ ] Document in shared format everyone can access
- [ ] Monthly synthesis session: What are we learning across all conversations?
- [ ] Quarterly strategy review: Are our assumptions validated? What's changed?

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Recruiting Wrong Customers
Recruiting from research panels or friends gives you unrepresentative feedback. Recruit from actual product users.

**Fix**: Start with existing customers. Ask for referrals. Offer small incentive if needed.

### Mistake 2: Leading Questions
"Do you think feature X would be useful?" leads to "yes" because it's polite.

**Fix**: Ask about behavior instead. "How do you currently handle this workflow?"

### Mistake 3: Waiting for Perfect Research Setup
You don't need fancy recruiting, transcription, or software. Start now with simple interviews.

**Fix**: Email 5 customers: "I'd love to chat about how you use our product. 30 minutes, this week?" Most will say yes.

### Mistake 4: Only Interviewing Power Users
Power users are not representative. Include light users, churned customers, and people who use competitors.

**Fix**: Intentionally recruit variety: heavy users (2+ hours/week), regular users, light users, churned customers, non-customers.

### Mistake 5: Too Long Without Customer Contact
If you haven't talked to a customer in 2 weeks, you're not practicing continuous discovery.

**Fix**: Make it a personal commitment. Two conversations per week minimum. Non-negotiable.

## Interview Template You Can Use Today

### Opening (5 min)
"Thanks for taking time. I want to learn about how you [use our product/solve this problem]. There's no right answer—just your honest experience."

### Context (5 min)
- Walk me through your typical workflow
- What tools do you use currently?
- Tell me about [specific use case]

### Problem Deep Dive (10-15 min)
- What's the biggest challenge you face when you [do this task]?
- How does this impact your work/business?
- What have you tried to solve this?
- Tell me about the last time you encountered this problem

### Current Solution (5 min)
- How do you currently handle this?
- What's working well? What's frustrating?
- What would your ideal solution look like?

### Closing (2 min)
- Who else should I talk to who faces this challenge?
- Can I follow up if I have questions?

## Quick Reference: Research Methods

### 1. Problem Interviews (Best for Discovery)
- Confirm problem exists and matters
- Understand how customers currently solve it
- Uncover emotional and contextual factors

### 2. Solution Interviews (After problem validation)
- Test early prototypes or mockups
- Gather feedback on specific approaches
- Validate assumptions about preferred solution

### 3. Usability Testing (Before launch)
- Watch users interact with your product
- Identify friction points in experience
- Validate that users understand the value prop

### 4. Contextual Inquiry (Deep understanding)
- Observe customers in their actual environment
- See the full workflow, interruptions, and context
- Uncover unstated needs and workarounds

### Actions

- [ ] Test assumption with product change
