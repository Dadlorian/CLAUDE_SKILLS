# Product Marketing: 30-Minute Quick Start Guide

Create compelling positioning and messaging that drives adoption and differentiation.

---

## Your 30-Minute Roadmap

- **Minutes 1-5**: Understand positioning vs. messaging
- **Minutes 6-15**: Build your positioning statement
- **Minutes 16-25**: Create messaging hierarchy for different audiences
- **Minutes 26-30**: Define go-to-market messaging strategy

---

## 5 Essential Concepts Every PM Needs

### 1. Positioning: Own a Unique Place in Your Customer's Mind
Positioning answers: "When my target customer has a need, why should they think of us first?"

Great positioning is:
- **Specific**: Clear which customer, which problem, which solution
- **Defensible**: Hard for competitors to copy (based on real strengths)
- **Credible**: You can deliver on the promise
- **Differentiated**: Unique in a way customers care about

**Bad positioning**: "We're a flexible, powerful platform for teams"
**Good positioning**: "The central hub where modern teams organize all their work and decisions, replacing email chaos"

**Actionable**: Write your positioning statement in 1-2 sentences. Can you explain why customers should choose you over alternatives?

### 2. Messaging is How You Communicate Positioning
Positioning is the strategic foundation. Messaging is the tactical expression.

**Positioning**: "We enable SMBs to scale without hiring"
**Messaging**: "Automate your workflow," "Save 10 hours/week," "Hire half as many people"

**Actionable**: Take your positioning. Extract 3-5 key messages that support it.

### 3. Different Audiences Need Different Messages
Executives care about ROI. End users care about ease of use. Developers care about integration.

**Messaging hierarchy**:
- **Master headline**: Core value prop (used everywhere)
- **Messages by audience**: Customize for buyer, user, technical evaluator
- **Supporting proof points**: Metrics, testimonials, case studies

**Actionable**: Identify 3 audience segments. For each, write the message that resonates most.

### 4. Competitive Differentiation Comes from Owned Dimensions
Don't try to win on every dimension. Own 1-2 unique dimensions where you're meaningfully better.

**Weak differentiation**: "Our tool is faster, easier, and cheaper"
**Strong differentiation**: "The only [feature] solution built for [specific use case]"

**Actionable**: On what 1-2 dimensions are you uniquely strong? Build positioning and messaging around those.

### 5. Messaging Should be Testable
Before rolling out new messaging, test it with target customers. Do they get it? Does it resonate?

**Actionable**: Run quick survey: "I'm going to describe a product. Tell me: Does it sound useful? Would you use it?" Get reactions to your messaging.

## First Steps Checklist

### Week 1: Customer & Market Research
- [ ] Interview 10-15 target customers: "What problems do you face? How do you solve them today?"
- [ ] Interview 3-5 lost customers: "Why didn't you choose us?"
- [ ] Analyze competitor positioning: How do they position? What's their message?
- [ ] Internal team input: What do you hear from sales? What confuses customers?

### Week 2: Positioning Development
- [ ] Define target customer (specific role, company size, use case)
- [ ] Identify primary problem customer faces
- [ ] Understand how they currently solve it (existing solution or workaround)
- [ ] Identify your key differentiator: Why are you different/better?
- [ ] Draft positioning statement using template

### Week 3: Messaging Architecture
- [ ] Write master headline/tagline
- [ ] Create 3-5 core messages (supporting positioning)
- [ ] Develop message variants by audience:
  - [ ] For executives: ROI, business outcomes, cost savings
  - [ ] For end users: Ease of use, speed, delight
  - [ ] For technical: Integration, reliability, scalability
- [ ] Create proof points: Metrics, testimonials, case studies, awards

### Week 4: Go-to-Market Messaging
- [ ] Website copy (homepage headline, key sections)
- [ ] Elevator pitch (30-60 seconds, captures core positioning)
- [ ] Sales pitch (3-5 minutes, all key messages)
- [ ] Email subject line (captures interest, leads to body copy)
- [ ] Social media tone (how does positioning come across in social voice?)
- [ ] Create messaging guide: 1-2 page doc for team alignment

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Copying Competitor Positioning
You do what successful competitors do. Now you sound like them, not different.

**Fix**: Study competitors to understand the landscape, then find your unique angle. What can only you do?

### Mistake 2: Messaging Doesn't Match Product Reality
You claim to be the "fastest" but customers experience slowness.

**Fix**: Base positioning on real capabilities. If it's not true, don't claim it. Fix the product or change the message.

### Mistake 3: Too Generic
"We're a flexible, powerful platform for teams that makes work easier"

**Fix**: Be specific. What exact problem? For whom? How is it different?

### Mistake 4: No Customer Validation
You build positioning in a room, launch it, then find out customers don't care.

**Fix**: Test with 10-15 target customers before rolling out. Does your message resonate?

### Mistake 5: Inconsistent Messaging Across Channels
Website says one thing. Sales deck says another. Marketing materials say a third.

**Fix**: Create messaging guide. Ensure all teams follow same core positioning and messages.

## Positioning Statement Template

Use April Dunford's framework:

```
For [target customer] who [customer problem/need]
[Product name] is a [category]
that [unique benefit/how you solve the problem]
unlike [main alternative/competitor]
which [their limitation or approach]
our product [key differentiator]
```

### Example 1: Slack
```
For busy professionals who struggle with communication fragmentation
Slack is a workplace communication platform
that centralizes and searchably logs all conversations and decisions
unlike email (which is fragmented and hard to search)
our product organizes conversations by channel, making context searchable forever
```

### Example 2: Notion
```
For knowledge workers who juggle multiple tools
Notion is an all-in-one workspace
that combines documents, wikis, databases, and tasks in one flexible interface
unlike other tools (which force you to learn one specific use case)
our product adapts to how you work, not the other way around
```

## Messaging Hierarchy Template

```
## Master Message (Core Value Prop)
"[Company] enables [target customer] to [core outcome] by [unique approach]"

## Message Pillars (3-5 Supporting Messages)
1. **[Customer Outcome]**: [How you enable it]
   - Supporting detail: [Proof point, metric, feature]
   - Customer quote: "[Testimonial]"

2. **[Customer Outcome]**: [How you enable it]
   - Supporting detail: [Proof point, metric, feature]
   - Customer quote: "[Testimonial]"

3. **[Customer Outcome]**: [How you enable it]
   - Supporting detail: [Proof point, metric, feature]
   - Customer quote: "[Testimonial]"

## Audience-Specific Messages
### For Executives
- ROI focus: "Reduces [cost] by [amount] or enables [revenue opportunity]"
- Risk mitigation: "Enterprise-grade security, compliance, reliability"

### For End Users
- Delight focus: "So intuitive, you'll figure it out without training"
- Time-saving: "Cuts [task] time in half"

### For Technical Evaluator
- Integration focus: "Integrates with all your existing tools"
- Reliability: "99.99% uptime SLA, advanced API"

## Competitive Positioning
vs. [Competitor 1]: "Unlike [competitor], we [key differentiator]"
vs. [Competitor 2]: "While [competitor] focuses on [their strength], we focus on [your strength]"
```

## Messaging Testing Framework

### Quick Survey (5 min, 10-15 customers)
**Show them your positioning/message**:
"[Your positioning statement]"

**Ask**:
1. "What is [product] for?" (Do they get it?)
2. "Does this sound useful?" (Does it resonate?)
3. "Who should use this?" (Do they identify with target customer?)
4. "What questions do you have?" (What's confusing?)
5. "How likely are you to try it?" (1-10 scale)

**Look for**:
- If >60% "get it," you're on track
- If <50%, rework messaging
- Note what confuses people and clarify
