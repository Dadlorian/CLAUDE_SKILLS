## Part 2: Frame of Reference

### What are customers comparing you to?

**Current alternatives customers use**:
1. [e.g., Email]
2. [e.g., Microsoft Teams]
3. [e.g., Slack]

**Implicit alternatives (what they do without you)**:
1. [e.g., Meeting constantly]
2. [e.g., Using spreadsheets]

**Frame of Reference Decision**:

Which of these frames of reference is strongest for your positioning?

- [ ] **Category frame**: "We're the [category] for [segment]"
  - Example: "Slack is the communication platform for distributed teams"
  - Best for: Creating/owning new category

- [ ] **Functional alternative frame**: "We're better than [competitor] at [capability]"
  - Example: "Figma is better than Sketch at real-time collaboration"
  - Best for: Clear competitor differentiation

- [ ] **Job-based frame**: "We help you do [job] that [alternative] couldn't"
  - Example: "Notion lets you build a unified workspace that disconnected tools couldn't"
  - Best for: Different job or use case

- [ ] **Anti-frame**: "We're NOT [competitor/category]"
  - Example: "Stripe is NOT a payment processor, we're a developer platform"
  - Best for: Breaking misconceptions

## Usage Notes

**How to use this document**:
1. Fill out each section thoughtfully
2. Get feedback from cross-functional team
3. Test positioning with customers before finalizing
4. Iterate based on feedback
5. Finalize and get executive alignment
6. Brief all teams
7. Update marketing and sales materials
8. Measure effectiveness and refine

**When to update**:
- Quarterly strategic review
- When market conditions change significantly
- When customer feedback reveals misunderstandings
- When competitive positioning shifts
- When expanding to new market segments

**Share with**:
- All customer-facing teams (sales, marketing, customer success)
- Executive leadership
- Product team
- Communications/PR team
