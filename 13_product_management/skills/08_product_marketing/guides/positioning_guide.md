# Step-by-Step Positioning Guide

## Overview

This guide walks you through the process of developing positioning for your product using April Dunford's framework combined with practical PMM best practices.

## Timeline

**Total Duration**: 6-8 weeks

- Week 1-2: Research and interviews
- Week 3: Analysis and hypothesis development
- Week 4-5: Validation and refinement
- Week 6: Documentation and rollout
- Week 7-8: Internal alignment and implementation

## Phase 1: Research & Discovery (Weeks 1-2)

### Step 1.1: Gather Internal Insights

**Objective**: Understand your product and market from an internal perspective.

**Interviews to Conduct**:

**Sales Leadership** (1-2 hour interview)
- What customer segments have the most success with your product?
- What differentiates you from competitors in customer conversations?
- What are the most common objections? What's the response?
- Which competitors do you lose to most frequently?
- What's the typical sales conversation flow?
- Which customers have the highest satisfaction and retention?

**Product Leadership** (1-2 hour interview)
- What's the product vision and strategy?
- What customer problems are you solving?
- What's unique about your product approach?
- What are the near-term and long-term product roadmap priorities?
- What have customers asked for that surprised you?
- What are you intentionally NOT doing?

**Customer Success Leadership** (1-2 hour interview)
- Which customers are most successful? Why?
- Which customers churn? What are the patterns?
- What outcomes matter most to customers?
- What problems do customers experience after purchase?
- How do your best customers use the product?
- Which customer segments have highest NPS and CSAT?

**Marketing/Communications** (1-2 hour interview)
- How are we currently positioning?
- What messaging resonates? What doesn't?
- What are key messages customers remember?
- What gaps exist in our current messaging?
- What competitive situations come up most frequently?

**Output**: Summary document of internal perspectives on positioning.

### Step 1.2: Analyze Your Customer Base

**Objective**: Understand who your best customers are.

**Data to Analyze**:

**Customer Segmentation**
- Analyze by: Industry, company size, geographic region, use case
- For each segment, calculate:
  - Number of customers
  - Revenue and ACV (average contract value)
  - Growth rate (new customers per month)
  - Retention rate and churn
  - Expansion rate (revenue growth in existing accounts)
  - NPS (customer satisfaction)

**Customer Success Metrics**
- Which customer segments have highest:
  - Product adoption (feature usage)?
  - Customer satisfaction (NPS/CSAT)?
  - Retention and renewal rate?
  - Expansion revenue (upsell/cross-sell)?

**Identify Patterns**
- Which segments are most profitable?
- Which segments grow fastest?
- Which segments expand most?
- Are there any surprising segments?

**Output**: Customer segmentation analysis with data on performance by segment.

### Step 1.3: Conduct Customer Interviews

**Objective**: Understand customer jobs-to-be-done, problems, and success metrics.

**Interview Recruitment**:
- Select 15-20 customers across different segments
- Mix of new customers (0-6 months) and long-term customers
- Include customers with high satisfaction and lower satisfaction
- Include different roles and personas
- Mix of heavy users and lighter users

**Interview Structure** (60 minutes):

**Section 1: Background** (10 minutes)
- Tell me about your company
- What's your role and what are you responsible for?
- How long have you been using our product?
- How did you first learn about us?

**Section 2: The Problem** (20 minutes)
- What problems was your team experiencing before you implemented our product?
- How was your team solving this problem before?
- What weren't you happy with about the old way?
- How did you know you needed a better solution?
- Did you consider alternatives? What were they?

**Section 3: The Solution** (15 minutes)
- What made you choose our product?
- What was important in your decision?
- How are you using our product today?
- What outcomes have you achieved with our product?

**Section 4: Value & Impact** (10 minutes)
- How has our product changed how your team works?
- What's the biggest benefit you've seen?
- How would you describe what our product does to someone else?
- What would you miss most if you stopped using our product?

**Section 5: Messaging** (5 minutes)
- If I said "[current positioning statement]", would that resonate with you?
- How would you describe what we do to someone like you?
- What makes us different from [competitors]?

**Interview Tips**:
- Record interviews (with permission) for accuracy
- Take notes during interview
- Listen for the language customers use
- Notice what they emphasize and don't emphasize
- Ask follow-up questions about emotional drivers
- Avoid leading questions (suggesting answers)
- Let them think before answering (don't fill silences)

**Output**: Interview notes and common themes across customers.

### Step 1.4: Conduct Win/Loss Analysis

**Objective**: Understand what drives wins and losses in competitive situations.

**Data to Gather**:

For recent deals won:
- What was the customer's main problem?
- What alternatives were they considering?
- What made them choose you over alternatives?
- What was the main value driver in the decision?
- What almost lost the deal?

For recent deals lost:
- Why did you lose the deal?
- What did competitors offer that you don't?
- What was the main reason they chose competitor?
- Did pricing play a role?
- Was there a specific feature or capability gap?
- Would they reconsider in the future?

**How to Conduct**:
1. Have sales leadership nominate 5-10 recent wins and 5-10 recent losses
2. Conduct interviews with decision-makers from those deals
3. Look for patterns:
   - Which competitors you lose to most?
   - What reasons drive losses vs. wins?
   - Are there pattern by segment or use case?
   - What are most common objections in losses?

**Output**: Win/loss summary identifying patterns and competitive challenges.

### Step 1.5: Competitive Analysis

**Objective**: Understand positioning and messaging of key competitors.

**For Each Major Competitor, Research**:
- Website and value proposition (what's the main message?)
- Pricing page (who are they targeting? what's the model?)
- Sales one-pager or overview
- Customer reviews (G2, Capterra, etc.)
- Recent blog posts and thought leadership
- Marketing campaigns and ads
- Job postings (reveals what's in roadmap)

**Create Competitive Profile**:
- How does each competitor position?
- What are their key messages?
- What are their strengths?
- What are their weaknesses?
- How are they different from each other?
- What's the competitive landscape?

**Map Competitive Positioning**:
- Create positioning map showing where each competitor (and you) position
- Look for white space
- Identify clusters of similar positioning
- Consider if you're trying to compete where others are

**Output**: Competitive positioning map and competitive profiles.

### Step 1.6: Market & Trend Research

**Objective**: Understand market context and trends.

**Research Questions**:
- What's the market size and growth rate?
- What are major market trends?
- What are emerging customer needs?
- How is the market segmented?
- Who are the opinion leaders and influencers?
- What do analysts say about the market?

**Sources**:
- Gartner Magic Quadrant and reports
- Forrester reports
- Industry analyst reports
- Trade publications
- Industry conferences
- Academic research
- Market research reports (Statista, IBISWorld, etc.)

**Output**: Summary of market context and trends.

## Phase 2: Analysis & Hypothesis Development (Week 3)

### Step 2.1: Synthesize Research

**Objective**: Identify patterns and themes from all research.

**Create Summary Documents**:

**Customer Segment Analysis**
- Best-performing segments (growth, retention, NPS)
- Unique characteristics of best customers
- Problems specific customers face
- How different segments use product differently

**Customer Themes**
- What problems appear across interviews?
- What language do customers use?
- What are key outcomes customers achieve?
- What are emotional drivers?
- What's surprising or noteworthy?

**Competitive Landscape Summary**
- How many direct competitors?
- How do they position?
- What's the white space?
- Who's winning/losing in market?
- What's underserved?

**Win/Loss Patterns**
- What drives wins?
- What causes losses?
- Competitive threats
- Feature/capability gaps
- Messaging/perception issues

### Step 2.2: Identify Positioning Hypotheses

**Objective**: Develop 2-3 positioning hypotheses for testing.

**For Each Hypothesis, Document**:

**Hypothesis 1: [Name/Theme]**
- Customer segment: Who is this for?
- Frame of reference: What are they comparing you to?
- Unique value: What's unique about your approach?
- Why this works: Why would customers care?
- Proof: What evidence supports this positioning?
- Risks: What could go wrong with this positioning?

**Example Hypothesis 1: Real-Time Collaboration Leader**

Customer segment: Product and design teams that work together
Frame of reference: Design tools that require async feedback or file sharing
Unique value: Real-time multiplayer editing that brings entire team into design process
Why this works: Speed up design cycles, get feedback faster, eliminate rework
Proof: Case studies showing 30% faster design-to-development
Risks: Competitors could add multiplayer features, less relevant for solo designers

**Example Hypothesis 2: Team Efficiency Platform**

Customer segment: Any team that needs better collaboration
Frame of reference: Email, scattered communication tools, design software
Unique value: Unified platform that integrates design with collaboration and handoff
Why this works: Reduce context switching, keep everything in one place, faster decisions
Proof: Customers report 40% time saved on design and handoff
Risks: Broad positioning might be hard to differentiate, many competitors claim this

**Example Hypothesis 3: Design System for Scale**

Customer segment: Enterprise design teams managing design systems
Frame of reference: Disconnected design tools and spreadsheet-based design systems
Unique value: Design system management at scale with components, tokens, and consistency
Why this works: Maintain consistency across enterprise, reduce rework, empower designers
Proof: Enterprise customers managing 1000s of components efficiently
Risks: Narrow market, other tools could add design system features

**Use Dunford's Framework**:
1. Segments you serve: Who are best customers?
2. Frame of reference: What are they comparing to?
3. Unique value: What's different about your approach?
4. Proof: What evidence backs this up?

### Step 2.3: Evaluate Hypotheses

**Objective**: Assess which hypotheses are strongest.

**For Each Hypothesis, Score**:

| Criterion | Max | H1 | H2 | H3 |
|-----------|-----|----|----|-----|
| Customer demand (do customers care?) | 25 | 20 | 18 | 15 |
| Differentiation (is it unique?) | 25 | 18 | 12 | 20 |
| Defensibility (is it hard to copy?) | 20 | 15 | 8 | 18 |
| Product fit (does product support it?) | 20 | 18 | 20 | 16 |
| Team capability (can we execute?) | 10 | 8 | 10 | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** | **79** | **68** | **74** |

**Interpretation**:
- Highest score gets validated first
- All hypotheses worth testing
- Look for gaps between scores (where's differentiation?)

## Phase 3: Validation & Refinement (Weeks 4-5)

### Step 3.1: Validate with Customer Advocates

**Objective**: Test positioning hypotheses with friendly customers.

**Who to Interview**:
- 5-10 customers you know well (advocates)
- Mix of segments
- Mix of roles
- Have strong relationships with these customers

**Validation Interview** (30-45 minutes):

**Setup**:
"I want to test some new ways we're thinking about talking about our product. I'd love your honest feedback. There are no wrong answers."

**For Each Hypothesis**:
- Present positioning statement
- Ask: "If you heard [positioning statement], what would you think?"
- Ask: "Does this resonate with you? Why or why not?"
- Ask: "What would make this more/less compelling?"
- Ask: "What's missing from this description?"
- Ask: "How would you compare this to [competitors]?"

**Listen For**:
- Immediate reactions (emotional response)
- Language they use to describe it
- Whether it matches their understanding
- What they think is most important
- What seems wrong or missing
- Whether they'd describe product this way to others

**Output**: Customer feedback on positioning hypotheses.

### Step 3.2: Validate with Sales Team

**Objective**: Test positioning with people who use it in selling.

**Sales Team Feedback**:
- Would you use this positioning in a sales conversation?
- Does it make sense to you?
- Would it resonate with customers?
- What would customers ask about?
- What's the response to the unique value claim?
- How would you respond to competitive questions?
- Is there anything missing?

**Sales War Gaming**:
- Role play sales conversations using the positioning
- Notice if positioning feels natural or forced
- Identify objections and responses
- See if messaging flows naturally
- Understand what questions customers ask

**Output**: Sales feedback on positioning and messaging gaps.

### Step 3.3: Validate with Product Team

**Objective**: Ensure product can support the positioning.

**Product Team Feedback**:
- Does our product deliver on this positioning?
- What's currently in product vs. roadmap?
- Are there gaps in the product?
- How do we address product limitations?
- What's on the roadmap that supports this positioning?
- Are there risks the product can't support this?

**Output**: Product assessment of fit with positioning.

### Step 3.4: Refine Positioning

**Objective**: Improve positioning based on validation feedback.

**What to Adjust**:
- Language: Use customer language, not company language
- Frame of reference: Adjust based on what customers actually compare to
- Unique value: Clarify or reframe based on feedback
- Proof: Strengthen with specific customer quotes or metrics
- Target segment: Refine who this is for based on feedback

**Red Flags Requiring Major Changes**:
- Customers don't understand positioning
- Sales team wouldn't use it
- Product doesn't support claims
- Competitive feedback suggests weak differentiation
- Misconceptions about what you do

**Minor Refinements**:
- Word choice and language
- Emphasis on which benefits matter most
- Proof points and supporting evidence
- Examples and illustrations

## Phase 4: Documentation & Rollout (Week 6)

### Step 4.1: Document Positioning Statement

**Create Final Positioning Statement**:

"For [customer segment] who [statement of customer need], [product name] is the [frame of reference] that [unique value]. Unlike [alternative], we [proof point]."

**Example: Figma Positioning**

"For product and design teams who need to work together in real-time, Figma is the browser-based design tool that brings multiplayer editing to design collaboration. Unlike desktop design tools like Sketch and Adobe XD, Figma enables entire teams to edit the same file simultaneously without file conflicts or back-and-forth feedback cycles."

**Positioning Statement Should Be**:
- Clear and concise
- Specific to a customer segment
- Focused on benefits, not features
- Differentiated from alternatives
- Supported by product and proof
- Internally aligned

### Step 4.2: Develop Supporting Positioning Narrative

**Create 2-3 Page Narrative** that includes:

**Positioning Statement** (1 sentence)
- The core positioning statement

**Customer Segment Description** (paragraph)
- Who is this for?
- What are their characteristics?
- What's their role/responsibility?
- Why are they important to us?

**Problem Statement** (paragraph)
- What problems do they face?
- How are they currently solving it?
- What's not working about current approach?
- Why does this matter to them?

**Unique Value Proposition** (1-2 paragraphs)
- What's unique about our approach?
- How is this different from alternatives?
- Why does our approach work better?
- What results can they expect?

**Proof & Validation** (bullet points)
- Customer examples and quotes
- Metrics and outcomes customers achieve
- Analyst recognition or awards
- Market traction and growth

**Example Structure**:

---

**Positioning Statement**
Figma is the browser-based design tool that brings multiplayer editing to design collaboration.

**Customer Segment**
Product and design teams at mid-market to enterprise companies that work across distributed locations and need to iterate quickly. These teams include designers, product managers, engineers, and stakeholders who need to collaborate on design work.

**Problem**
Design teams struggle with current tools that force linear, disconnected workflows. Designers share screens or export files, stakeholders provide feedback via email or comments, engineers hunt through assets to implement designs. Every iteration requires another export, another email, another sync meeting.

**Unique Value**
Figma's multiplayer editing fundamentally changes how teams work together. Everyone with a link can see edits in real-time, leave comments, and collaborate on the same canvas simultaneously. No file conflicts. No out-of-date versions. No endless back-and-forth.

**Proof**
- Teams report 30% faster design-to-development cycle
- Design systems teams manage thousands of components without overhead
- 12M+ monthly active users trust Figma
- 3 of top 5 design-first companies use Figma

---

**Output**: Final positioning statement and supporting narrative.

### Step 4.3: Develop Key Messages

**Create 3-5 Key Messages** that support positioning:

For each message:
1. State the key message (1-2 sentences)
2. Explain the benefit
3. Provide proof point
4. Note what makes it unique

**Example: Figma Key Messages**

1. **Real-Time Multiplayer Collaboration**
   Message: "Work together on the same file simultaneously with real-time presence and editing"
   Benefit: "Speed up iterations and get faster feedback without file conflicts"
   Proof: "Customers report 30% faster design cycles"
   Differentiation: "Unlike desktop tools, Figma enables true multiplayer"

2. **Browser-Based Accessibility**
   Message: "Design from anywhere, on any device, with no installation or plugin requirements"
   Benefit: "Reduce friction and enable designers across different teams and locations"
   Proof: "Thousands of companies have all designers on Figma within weeks"
   Differentiation: "Unlike Sketch which requires Mac, Figma works everywhere"

3. **Design System at Scale**
   Message: "Manage design systems with components and design tokens that scale"
   Benefit: "Maintain design consistency across large teams without manual updates"
   Proof: "Enterprise teams manage 10,000s of components efficiently"
   Differentiation: "Figma's architecture enables design systems in ways others can't"

**Output**: 3-5 key messages with supporting details.

### Step 4.4: Create Internal Alignment Document

**For Internal Distribution**, create document that includes:

1. **Positioning Statement** (why we're changing, what's new)
2. **Customer Segment** (who is this for)
3. **Problem We Solve** (what's the customer's problem)
4. **Unique Value** (what's different about our approach)
5. **Key Messages** (how we should talk about it)
6. **Supporting Proof** (metrics, customer quotes)
7. **Competitive Differentiation** (how we compare)
8. **How to Use** (for sales, marketing, customer success)
9. **FAQ** (common questions about positioning)

**Share with**:
- All customer-facing teams (sales, marketing, CS)
- Product and leadership
- Make it easy to find and reference

**Output**: Internal alignment document and communication plan.

## Phase 5: Implementation & Launch (Weeks 6-8)

### Step 5.1: Brief Internal Teams

**Sales Team Briefing** (1-2 hours):

**Agenda**:
1. New positioning statement and why (10 min)
2. Customer segment and how to identify them (15 min)
3. Key messages and how to use them (20 min)
4. Competitive battle cards (20 min)
5. Q&A and role play (20 min)

**Provide**:
- Positioning statement handout
- Key messages one-pager
- Competitive battle cards
- Pitch deck slide with new positioning
- Sales collateral with new messaging

**Marketing Team Briefing** (1 hour):

**Agenda**:
1. New positioning and messaging framework (20 min)
2. Customer segment and personas (15 min)
3. Marketing implications (20 min)
4. Update plan for website, campaigns, content (20 min)

**Provide**:
- Positioning statement and narrative
- Key messages and supporting messages
- Customer segment and persona information
- Marketing update plan and timeline

### Step 5.2: Update Marketing Materials

**Website Updates**:
- Hero section with new positioning statement
- Key benefits using key messages
- Customer segment focus
- Social proof and proof points
- Competitive differentiation

**Sales Materials**:
- Updated pitch deck with new positioning
- Sales one-pager with key messages
- Competitive battle cards
- Feature-to-benefit mapping
- ROI/business case templates

**Collateral**:
- Email signature messaging
- LinkedIn company description
- Social media bios and messaging
- Case studies focused on key customer segments
- Webinar and event descriptions

**Content**:
- Blog posts explaining positioning
- Thought leadership around unique value
- Educational content for target segment
- Video content with key messages

### Step 5.3: Train Sales & Customer Success

**Ongoing Enablement**:
- Regular lunch-and-learns on messaging
- Battle card updates as competition evolves
- Sales contests on using new positioning
- Win/loss analysis to understand effectiveness
- Monthly messaging reinforcement

**Measure Sales Adoption**:
- Listen to sales calls
- Survey sales team on usage
- Track sales materials usage
- Measure deal velocity and win rate by segment
- Get feedback on what's working and what isn't

### Step 5.4: Monitor & Measure

**Track Positioning Effectiveness**:

**Awareness Metrics**:
- Do customers recall our positioning?
- Is new messaging showing up in conversations?
- Are we being seen as solving the problem we claim?

**Preference Metrics**:
- Has preference shifted in our favor?
- Are we winning against specific competitors?
- Are customers choosing us for the right reasons?

**Conversion Metrics**:
- Is deal velocity improving?
- Are win rates up, especially for target segment?
- Are we losing less to specific competitors?

**Customer Metrics**:
- Do customers describe us using our positioning?
- Is satisfaction and NPS improving?
- Are expansion and retention rates improving?

**Gather Feedback**:
- Quarterly customer surveys on positioning
- Sales team feedback on effectiveness
- Win/loss analysis tracking which positioning elements drove wins
- Monitor competitive response

## Common Positioning Pitfalls

### 1. **Positioning for Everyone**
Problem: Trying to appeal to all possible customers
Solution: Focus on 1-2 primary segments

### 2. **Feature-Focused Positioning**
Problem: Focusing on what product does vs. customer outcomes
Solution: Focus on benefits and customer outcomes

### 3. **Positioning Without Proof**
Problem: Making claims product doesn't support
Solution: Ensure product and customers validate positioning

### 4. **Ignoring Competition**
Problem: Positioning in isolation from competitive landscape
Solution: Understand competition and find white space

### 5. **Poor Internal Alignment**
Problem: Different teams using different positioning
Solution: Create clear, documented positioning and train teams

### 6. **Setting Unrealistic Expectations**
Problem: Expecting positioning to change things overnight
Solution: Plan for 6-12 month adoption period

### 7. **Not Updating Based on Feedback**
Problem: Positioning becomes stale as market evolves
Solution: Review quarterly and adjust as needed

## Key Takeaways

1. **Research First**: Base positioning on real customer research, not assumptions
2. **Test Hypotheses**: Validate positioning with customers before full rollout
3. **Document Clearly**: Create clear positioning statement and supporting narrative
4. **Get Alignment**: Brief all teams and get feedback
5. **Measure Impact**: Track awareness, preference, and conversion
6. **Iterate**: Update positioning based on results and market changes
7. **Be Patient**: Allow 6-12 months for positioning to take root
