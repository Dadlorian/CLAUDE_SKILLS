## 1. Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) - Quick Reference

**Core Idea:** Customers "hire" products to accomplish jobs

**Three Types of Jobs:**
- **Functional:** Task to accomplish (primary job)
- **Emotional:** How they want to feel
- **Social:** How they want to be perceived

**Application Steps:**
1. Interview customers about what they were trying to do
2. Understand the situation (context) when they needed the job done
3. Identify all alternative solutions they considered
4. Dig into why they chose one over others
5. Uncover unmet needs in current solutions

**Competitive Insight:** Compete on job-related dimensions, not product dimensions

**Example Applications:**
- Slack - **Job:** "Reduce communication chaos" → Product design follows (searchable, organized)
- Uber - **Job:** "Get reliable transportation on-demand" → Product design (ratings, real-time tracking)
- Netflix - **Job:** "Discover and access entertainment" → Product design (algorithms, content library)

## 2. Porter's Five Forces - Quick Reference

**Threat Assessment (Rating: 1-5, where 5 = severe threat)**

### Force 1: Competitive Rivalry
```
HIGH threat when:
- Many competitors of similar size
- Industry is mature/commoditized
- Exit barriers are high (infrastructure, contracts)
- Customers are indifferent (price wars)

LOW threat when:
- Few dominant players (oligopoly)
- Differentiation is strong
- Switching costs are high
- Customer loyalty is high
```

### Force 2: Threat of New Entrants
```
HIGH threat when:
- Low capital requirements
- No proprietary technology barriers
- Easy access to distribution channels
- Weak brand loyalty

LOW threat when:
- High capital requirements
- Complex technology required
- Strong network effects
- Proprietary supplier relationships
```

### Force 3: Bargaining Power of Suppliers
```
HIGH when:
- Few suppliers exist
- Switching suppliers is expensive
- Supplier can forward-integrate
- Supplier has differentiated product

LOW when:
- Many suppliers available
- Easy to substitute suppliers
- Can threaten backward-integration
- Suppliers compete on price
```

### Force 4: Bargaining Power of Customers
```
HIGH when:
- Few large customers (concentrated)
- Easy to switch products
- Customers can backward-integrate
- Product is commodity (price sensitive)

LOW when:
- Many small customers (fragmented)
- Switching costs are high
- Differentiated product (not commodity)
- Customer benefits exceed cost of switching
```

### Force 5: Threat of Substitutes
```
HIGH when:
- Alternative solutions are available
- Substitutes are cheaper/better
- Substitutes are improving faster
- Customers will accept substitutes

LOW when:
- No viable alternatives exist
- Your product has unique advantages
- Substitutes are more expensive
- High switching costs to alternatives
```

**Strategic Response:**
- Minimize threats you can't control
- Build defensibility in areas you can control
- Choose to compete where forces favor you

## 3. Blue Ocean vs Red Ocean - Quick Reference

### Red Ocean Strategy (Compete in existing space)
```
Characteristics:
- Beat competitors on same dimensions
- Lower costs, more features, better service
- Price competition drives margins down
- Zero-sum competition

Example: Airlines competing on routes, price, service
```

### Blue Ocean Strategy (Create new space)
```
Characteristics:
- Eliminate factors competitors fight over
- Reduce costs while improving value
- New dimensions customers didn't expect
- Value innovation (better + cheaper)

Example: Southwest Airlines - eliminated meals, focus, fun
         Netflix - eliminated late fees, physical stores
```

### Decision Matrix
```
Are you fighting on                Are you creating
existing dimensions?         →      new value dimensions?

    YES                              NO
    ↓                                ↓
  RED OCEAN               TRY FOR BLUE OCEAN
  (Competitive)           (Differentiation)
```

**Implementation:**
1. Identify factors industry competes on
2. Ask: Which factors should we eliminate?
3. Ask: Which factors should we reduce?
4. Ask: Which factors should we raise?
5. Ask: What factors should we create?

## 4. Strategy Canvas - Quick Reference

**Visual tool showing how you compete on different dimensions**

**Steps:**

1. **List competitive factors** (what your industry competes on)
   - Examples: Price, features, service quality, convenience, customization

2. **Rate yourself and competitors (1-5 scale)**
   - Where do you stand on each factor?

3. **Look for patterns:**
   - Where does everyone cluster? (This is the red ocean)
   - Where is there white space? (Blue ocean opportunity)
   - Where can you be dramatically different?

**Netflix Strategy Canvas (early 2000s):**

| Factor | Video Store | Netflix |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| Price | 3 | 5 (cheaper) |
| Convenience | 2 (drive to store) | 5 (mail delivery) |
| Title Selection | 3 | 4 |
| Late Fees | 1 (hated) | 5 (eliminated) |
| Instant Gratification | 5 | 1 (at first) |
| Personalization | 1 | 4 |

**Strategic Insight:** Netflix didn't win on video stores' dimensions—they excelled on different ones

## 5. Ansoff Matrix - Quick Reference

**Growth strategy tool - Select direction based on market and product**

```
                EXISTING PRODUCTS     NEW PRODUCTS
EXISTING        Penetration           Development
MARKETS         (Low Risk)            (Medium Risk)

NEW MARKETS     Development           Diversification
                (Medium Risk)         (High Risk)
```

### Market Penetration
```
Goal: Grow in current market with current products
Tactics:
- Increase customer acquisition
- Increase usage/consumption
- Win competitors' customers
- Expand within existing customer base

Example: Uber expanding in existing cities, same service
Risk: LOW (you know market and product)
```

### Product Development
```
Goal: New products for existing customers
Tactics:
- Add new features for current users
- Create new tiers/products (Premium, Enterprise)
- Expand into adjacent categories
- Develop complementary products

Example: Slack adding Slack Connect, Workflow Builder
Risk: MEDIUM (new product, known market)
```

### Market Development
```
Goal: Existing products in new markets
Tactics:
- Enter new geographic regions
- Target new customer segments
- Develop new use cases
- Adapt product for new markets

Example: Netflix expanding to Asia, India
Risk: MEDIUM (known product, new market)
```

### Diversification
```
Goal: New products for new markets
Tactics:
- Enter completely new industries
- Develop new business models
- Acquire adjacent businesses
- Compete in multiple categories

Example: Amazon expanding from books to cloud to ads
Risk: HIGH (both unknown)
Avoid unless: Core market is saturated OR significant synergy exists
```

## 6. SWOT Analysis - Quick Reference

**Simple framework for internal/external assessment**

```
INTERNAL                                EXTERNAL

Strengths                               Opportunities
- What are we good at?                  - What external trends help us?
- Unique capabilities                   - Market gaps we could fill
- Brand advantages                      - Regulatory changes (positive)
- Talented team                         - Technology breakthroughs
- Financial position                    - Customer behavior shifts

Weaknesses                              Threats
- Where are we lacking?                 - What could harm us?
- Limited resources                     - Competitive moves
- Skill gaps                            - Regulatory changes (negative)
- Geographic limitations                - Economic downturns
- Technology debt                       - Changing customer preferences
```

**Strategic Application:**

1. **Leverage:** Use strengths to capitalize on opportunities
2. **Build:** Use strengths to mitigate threats
3. **Fix:** Address weaknesses that block opportunities
4. **Defend:** Build defenses where weaknesses meet threats

## 7. McKinsey 7S Framework - Quick Reference

**For assessing organizational alignment around strategy**

```
            STRATEGY (what we do)
                    ↑
    STRUCTURE ←─────+─────→ SYSTEMS
    (hierarchy)  (processes)
          ↑           ↑
    SKILLS ←─────────→ STYLE
         ↑         (culture)
      STAFF
   (people)
          ↓
      SHARED VALUES
    (core beliefs)
```

**Element Details:**

| Element | Questions | Examples |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Strategy | Where are we going? | Market position, differentiation |
| Structure | How organized? | Hierarchy, matrix, flat, functional |
| Systems | How do we work? | Processes, tools, metrics |
| Style | How do we behave? | Culture, decision-making |
| Skills | What are we good at? | Capabilities, expertise |
| Staff | Who do we need? | Hiring, roles, talent |
| Shared Values | Why do we exist? | Mission, values, culture |

**Alignment Check:**
- All elements should point to same strategy
- Misalignment → execution problems
- Changing strategy requires changing multiple elements

## 8. VRIO Analysis - Quick Reference

**Identifies if competitive advantage is sustainable**

**Four Questions:**

1. **Valuable?** Does capability help us serve customers better?
   - YES → Continue
   - NO → Irrelevant to strategy

2. **Rare?** Do many competitors have this capability?
   - YES (rare) → Competitive advantage
   - NO (common) → No advantage

3. **Imitable?** Can competitors easily copy this capability?
   - NO (hard to copy) → Sustainable advantage
   - YES (easy to copy) → Temporary advantage

4. **Organized?** Are we organized to exploit this capability?
   - YES → Competitive advantage
   - NO → Organizational inertia (fix internally)

**Result Matrix:**

| Valuable | Rare | Imitable | Organized | Result |
|----------|------|----------|-----------|--------|
| No | - | - | - | Weakness |
| Yes | No | - | - | Parity |
| Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Temporary advantage |
| Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Sustained advantage |
| Yes | Yes | No | - | Temporary advantage |

**Strategic Implication:** Invest in capabilities that are valuable, rare, hard to imitate, and that you can organize around.

## Framework Selection Decision Tree

```
What problem are you solving?

├─ "Why aren't we winning?"
│  └─ Porter's Five Forces + VRIO
│
├─ "What should we grow next?"
│  └─ Ansoff Matrix + Jobs to be Done
│
├─ "How do we differentiate?"
│  └─ Blue Ocean + Strategy Canvas + JTBD
│
├─ "Is our organization aligned?"
│  └─ McKinsey 7S + SWOT
│
└─ "What do customers actually want?"
   └─ Jobs to be Done + Customer Research
```

## Combining Frameworks

**Example: Complete Strategic Analysis**

```
Step 1: Understand Customer Need
→ Jobs to Be Done interviews

Step 2: Assess Market & Competition
→ Porter's Five Forces
→ Competitive market sizing

Step 3: Identify Strategic Opportunity
→ Blue Ocean Canvas
→ VRIO on your advantages

Step 4: Choose Growth Direction
→ Ansoff Matrix

Step 5: Test Alignment
→ McKinsey 7S
→ SWOT (what could derail us)

Step 6: Set Strategy & OKRs
→ Based on above analysis
```

## Framework Limitations

| Framework | Strength | Limitation |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| JTBD | Reveal unmet needs | Time-intensive research |
| Porter's | Industry-level insight | Can miss disruption |
| Blue Ocean | Ideate new space | Oversimplifies competition |
| SWOT | Simple, accessible | Vague (needs structure) |
| Ansoff | Growth clarity | Doesn't address "how" |
| McKinsey 7S | Alignment check | Internal only |
| VRIO | Sustainability focus | Backward-looking |

**Best practice:** Combine multiple frameworks for comprehensive view

## Quick Framework Application Times

**1 Week Strategy Sprint:**
```
Day 1: SWOT + Porter's Five Forces (industry analysis)
Day 2-3: Jobs to Be Done (customer research synthesis)
Day 4: Strategy Canvas + Blue Ocean (differentiation)
Day 5: Ansoff Matrix (growth directions)
→ Output: Strategic direction + initial OKRs
```

**30 Minute Strategic Health Check:**
```
- McKinsey 7S (are we aligned?)
- VRIO (do we have defensible advantages?)
- Ansoff (are we in right growth direction?)
→ Output: Top 3 things to fix
```
