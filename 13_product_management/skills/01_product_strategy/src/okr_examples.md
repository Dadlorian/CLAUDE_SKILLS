# Real OKR Examples from Tech Companies

This document provides real (and reconstructed) examples of OKRs from well-known tech companies. These are based on public information, company announcements, and common knowledge about company strategy.

**Important:** These are examples and reconstructions based on public knowledge. Exact OKRs are typically internal. Use these as templates and inspiration for your own OKRs.

---

## Meta (Facebook) - 2023: "Year of Efficiency"

Meta announced a major strategic shift toward efficiency after massive hiring and spending. While they didn't publicly share OKRs, their strategy was clear:

### Likely OKRs:

```
OBJECTIVE 1: Dramatically improve capital efficiency and achieve path to profitability

Key Result 1: Reduce operating costs by 30% ($20B+ in savings)
- Original: ~$80B annual OpEx
- Target: ~$55B
- How: Headcount reduction (10,000 people), eliminating waste

Key Result 2: Grow revenue to $120B+ (20% growth despite cost cuts)
- Original: ~$115B
- Shows: Can grow and cut costs simultaneously

Key Result 3: Achieve operating margin of 25%+ (vs ~15% current)
- Shows: Profitability is core goal

Strategic insight:
- Previous years: "Growth at any cost"
- 2023: "Profitable growth"
- Represents major strategic pivot


OBJECTIVE 2: Establish Meta as AI computing leader

Key Result 1: Build largest AI training clusters globally (H100 GPUs, trillion parameter models)
- Why: Need foundation to compete with OpenAI/Google
- Signal: Major capex investment in AI despite "year of efficiency"

Key Result 2: Ship AI features used by 100M+ people
- Why: Demonstrate value of AI to users
- Examples: Meta AI assistants, recommendations

Key Result 3: Establish open-source leadership (Llama adoption)
- Why: Ecosystem play, developer mindshare
- Signal: Released Llama 2 to compete with closed models


OBJECTIVE 3: Dominate social, messaging, and creator economy

Key Result 1: Grow creator revenue distribution to $1B annually (from $700M)
- Why: Creator economy is future of content
- How: Reels bonuses, in-stream ads, subscriptions

Key Result 2: 500M+ users on Threads (new Twitter competitor)
- Why: Social dominance requires presence in chat/short-form
- Note: They pulled Threads to focus on core later

Key Result 3: Expand WhatsApp monetization (business messaging, ads)
- Why: WhatsApp is platform with 2B users but low monetization
```

**What made these OKRs effective:**
- Clear trade-off between growth and efficiency (very public)
- All three OKRs could be scored independently
- Ambitious but achievable (efficiency, growth, AND AI)
- Aligned organization around "year of efficiency" narrative

## Netflix - 2022: "Growth Resumption"

Netflix had a difficult 2022 (lost subscribers) and needed to refocus on growth and profitability. Their public strategy focused on:

### Reconstructed OKRs:

```
OBJECTIVE 1: Return to growth and prove Netflix can grow again

Key Result 1: Add 30M+ new subscribers in 2023 (after losing 2M in 2022)
- Context: After subscriber losses, show growth engine works
- Drivers: Ad-supported tier, password sharing crackdown, content

Key Result 2: Achieve positive free cash flow of $7B+
- Context: Prove business model works without growth crutch
- Drivers: Revenue + efficiency improvements

Key Result 3: Achieve 50%+ engagement with paid, shared accounts
- Context: New monetization model (password sharing)
- How: Implement paid sharing, track adoption

Strategic insight:
- Previous assumption: Subscribers = success
- New assumption: Revenue + FCF = success
- Pivot: From "subscriber obsession" to "profit obsession"


OBJECTIVE 2: Revolutionize storytelling with live events and sports

Key Result 1: Launch live sports streaming (NFL games, WWE)
- Why: New content category with huge revenue potential
- Drivers: Signed exclusive NFL partnership, WWE integration

Key Result 2: Host 4+ major live events (awards, sports, concerts)
- Why: Live drives engagement and press
- Examples: Golden Globes, WWE Raw

Key Result 3: Build live event revenue stream to meaningful %
- Why: Different economics than on-demand (higher margins)

Strategic insight:
- Content expansion into live
- Differentiation from Disney+, HBO Max
- Higher engagement and revenue potential


OBJECTIVE 3: Establish Netflix as ad platform of choice

Key Result 1: Reach 40M+ users on Netflix with Ads tier
- Context: Launched ads tier in late 2022
- Target: Meaningful revenue contribution

Key Result 2: Achieve advertising partnerships with top brands
- Drivers: Direct sales to major advertisers

Key Result 3: Build ad tech platform to compete with YouTube
- Why: Own the full stack, higher margins
- Long-term: Leverage existing 250M users for ad targeting
```

**What made these OKRs effective:**
- Showed strategic pivot (subscriber to profit)
- Addressed market concerns (growth, profitability)
- Concrete, measurable, ambitious
- Connected to business model changes

## Stripe - 2021: "The Internet's Economic Infrastructure"

Stripe's strategy is visible through public statements, hiring, and product launches. Their likely OKRs:

### Reconstructed OKRs:

```
OBJECTIVE 1: Make Stripe essential for every business globally

Key Result 1: $1 trillion+ in payment volume processed
- Context: Stripe is plumbing layer for internet commerce
- How: Expanding integrations, geographic footprint

Key Result 2: Support payments in 200+ countries
- Context: Previous: maybe 50-60 countries
- Why: Every business is global, need global payment infrastructure

Key Result 3: 100,000+ businesses actively using Stripe
- Context: Shows platform adoption at enterprise and SMB
- Measure of: Embedded in ecosystem

Strategic insight:
- Expanding TAM (all global businesses need payments)
- Building defensible moat (network effects, switching costs)


OBJECTIVE 2: Build financial products on top of payments platform

Key Result 1: Launch Stripe Treasury (banking-as-a-service)
- Why: Payment processing is table-stakes, treasury is the moat
- Drivers: Onboard 1,000+ businesses using treasury

Key Result 2: Launch Stripe Fintech Stack (cards, banking)
- Why: Own full financial stack
- Products: Issued cards, bank accounts, loans, insurance

Key Result 3: $10B+ in assets under management
- Why: Measure of scale of Stripe as financial platform
- Long-term: Stripe becomes financial infrastructure

Strategic insight:
- Vertical integration (can't rely on banks forever)
- Shift from transaction processor to financial platform


OBJECTIVE 3: Enable creators and SMBs to build global businesses

Key Result 1: Support 50M+ Creator Economy creators
- Why: New class of business (creators, freelancers)
- Products: Stripe Connect, creator payouts

Key Result 2: Stripe for SMBs has 50,000+ active customers
- Why: SMB segment has different needs than enterprise
- How: Simplified pricing, better support

Key Result 3: Enable $100B+ in creator payouts
- Why: Stripe facilitates financial movement, not just payment
```

**What made these OKRs effective:**
- Showed horizontal expansion (all businesses)
- Showed vertical expansion (payments → full fintech)
- Global ambition (200+ countries)
- TAM expansion narrative

Key Result 3: Build Figma community to 100K+ creators/resources

## Airbnb - 2010: "Belong Anywhere"

```
OBJECTIVE 1: Establish Airbnb as trusted way to find accommodations

Key Result 1: 1M+ listings globally
- Context: 2010, they had thousands
- Why: Marketplace needs critical mass to be valuable

Key Result 1: Launch in 50 countries
- Context: 2010-2012 was geographic expansion phase
- Why: Marketplace value increases with availability

Key Result 2: Reach #1 position in top 20 cities (by listings)
- Why: Can't be everywhere, own the key markets
- Metric: vs Hotels.com, Booking in major cities

Key Result 3: Establish local support teams in 10+ countries
- Why: Trust requires local presence/support
- Signal: Serious about global expansion

Strategic insight:
- Geographic expansion (different timing for different markets)
- Local presence matters (not just ship globally)


OBJECTIVE 3: Build brand as "preferred way" to travel

Key Result 1: Achieve 80% awareness in top 20 cities
- Why: Brand = growth for marketplace
- How: Content, PR, local partnerships

Key Result 2: Host loyalty program reaches 50%+ of guests
- Why: Repeat usage = stronger business
- Products: Smart pricing for hosts, flexible cancellation

Key Result 3: NPS reaches 70+ (vs hotels 45)
- Why: Emotional connection = differentiation
- Impact: Word-of-mouth growth
```

**What made these OKRs effective:**
- Trust-first mentality (early marketplace)
- Geographic ambition (but measured)
- Brand building (became emotional, not transactional)

## Slack - 2015: "Bring Focus to Your Work"

Slack's original OKRs weren't public, but based on their growth and public statements:

### Reconstructed OKRs:

```
OBJECTIVE 1: Establish Slack as essential workplace communication tool

Key Result 1: Reach 500K daily active users
- Context: 2015, probably 50-100K DAU
- Why: DAU > growth metric for communication platform

Key Result 2: Achieve 90%+ retention after 1-month for strong users
- Why: Communication tools live or die on retention
- Definition: "Strong users" = 5+ messages/day

Key Result 3: Expand to 50,000 teams globally
- Why: Teams = unit of network effects
- Metric: More teams = more value

Strategic insight:
- DAU over MAU (better metric for usage)
- Retention matters (communication tools are habitual)
- Network effects through teams


OBJECTIVE 2: Win enterprise with robust platform capabilities

Key Result 1: Support Fortune 500 security/compliance requirements
- Why: Enterprise doesn't adopt consumer tools
- Products: SAML, audit logs, encryption, data residency

Key Result 2: Establish 500+ enterprise customers with $100K+ ACV
- Why: Show can win upmarket
- Impact: Revenue and sustainability

Key Result 3: Achieve 99.99% uptime (enterprise-grade reliability)
- Why: Enterprises don't tolerate downtime
- Impact: Can compete with entrenched platforms (enterprise IM)

Strategic insight:
- Dual motion: SMB (product-led) + Enterprise (sales-led)
- Different path for each segment


OBJECTIVE 3: Build ecosystem that increases switching costs

Key Result 1: Launch App Directory with 1,000+ integrations
- Why: Every tool a customer uses should integrate with Slack
- Impact: Slack becomes hub, not spoke

Key Result 2: Reach 100M+ installed apps (across all customers)
- Why: App usage = deeper integration = higher stickiness
- Metric: Usage of integrations matters more than count

Key Result 3: Enable partners (app developers) to earn $100M+
- Why: Thriving ecosystem = moat
- Impact: 3rd parties invested in Slack success
```

**What made these OKRs effective:**
- Focused on retention (communication is habitual)
- DAU as key metric (better than subscriber count)
- Dual motions (SMB product-led, Enterprise sales-led)
- Ecosystem lock-in

Discord was gaming-centric and pivoting to general purpose communities. Reconstructed OKRs:

### Reconstructed OKRs:

```
OBJECTIVE 1: Evolve Discord from gaming to general communication platform

Key Result 1: 50%+ of new servers are non-gaming communities
- Context: Discord started 100% gaming
- Why: TAM expansion (not just gamers)

Key Result 3: Average session time increases to 4+ hours/month per DAU
- Why: Deeper engagement across more use cases

Strategic insight:
- TAM expansion (gaming → all communities)
- Keep core users (gamers) while expanding


OBJECTIVE 2: Monetize without hurting community experience

Key Result 1: Launch Nitro (premium tier) reaching 2M+ subscribers
- Why: Premium subscription model
- Pricing: Separate from ad model (keeps community pure)

Key Result 2: Launch Server Subscriptions and Boosts (creator monetization)
- Why: Enable community creators/admins to monetize
- Impact: Deeper integration into communities

Key Result 3: Achieve $100M+ annual revenue (from Nitro + partnerships)
- Why: Sustainable business, justify VC investment

Strategic insight:
- Premium + creator monetization (not ads initially)
- Preserve community experience


OBJECTIVE 3: Build trust and safety at scale

Key Result 1: Zero critical security breaches
- Why: Community platform must be trustworthy
- Impact: Trust = growth in education, enterprise

Key Result 2: Implement automated moderation for 90% of toxic content
- Why: Manual moderation doesn't scale to 150M+ users
- Impact: Keep communities healthy

Key Result 3: Launch parental controls for younger users
- Why: Youth safety is barrier to growth in education
- Signal: Serious about non-gaming segments
```

**What made these OKRs effective:**
- TAM expansion while protecting core
- Monetization without harming experience
- Trust/safety for new segments

## Key Patterns Across Real Company OKRs

```
PATTERN 1: TAM Expansion
- Successful companies expand their addressable market
- Examples: Netflix (rentals → streaming), Figma (UI design → all design),
  Discord (gaming → communities)
- Strategic insight: Sustainable growth requires bigger market

PATTERN 2: Moat Building
- Shift from "winning on features" to "defensible advantages"
- Examples: Slack (integrations), Stripe (payments → fintech), Notion (all-in-one)
- Strategic insight: Features can be copied; moats are defensible

PATTERN 3: Monetization/Profitability
- Early stage: Focus on product-market fit and growth
- Growth stage: Add revenue models and unit economics
- Mature: Focus on profitability and free cash flow
- Examples: Meta (2023: efficiency), Netflix (2022: profitability)

PATTERN 4: Power User Focus
- Successful companies focus on power users first
- Examples: Slack (daily active users), Notion (50% as primary workspace)
- Strategic insight: Power users drive network effects and retention

PATTERN 5: Ecosystem/Network Effects
- As companies mature, focus shifts to ecosystem
- Examples: Stripe (platforms), Slack (app directory), Figma (plugins)
- Strategic insight: Ecosystem = switching costs = defensibility

PATTERN 6: Trust/Safety for New Segments
- New market segments require proof of trust/safety
- Examples: Discord (parental controls for education), Airbnb (trust metrics)
- Strategic insight: Trust is often the barrier, not product

PATTERN 7: Specific, Measurable Targets
- Effective OKRs have concrete numbers
- Never: "Improve our product" or "Grow faster"
- Always: "500K DAU" or "60 NPS" or "50% retention after 1 month"

PATTERN 8: Balance Growth + Efficiency/Profitability
- Sustainable companies balance multiple objectives
- Never just "grow at any cost"
- Always: Growth + margins + retention + team
```

## Using These Examples for Your OKRs

1. **Find your stage:** Are you product-market fit phase? Growth phase? Maturity phase?
2. **Look for comparable company:** What's closest to your situation?
3. **Extract the pattern:** What are they optimizing for?
4. **Adapt to your context:** What would this look like for your business?
5. **Test the metrics:** Can you actually measure them?
6. **Share with team:** Do they believe these are achievable?

**Remember:**
- These are reconstructed, not the actual OKRs
- Context matters (company size, market, stage)
- What works for Meta might not work for a startup
- OKRs should be aspirational but achievable
- The best OKRs are ones your team believes in and commits to
