# Complete Product Strategy Document Template

## How to Use This Template

This template provides a **complete structure** for a product strategy document. Adapt sections based on your situation:
- **New product:** Use all sections
- **Existing product refresh:** Focus on sections 3-5 (market, competition, strategy)
- **Quick strategy (1-pager):** Use sections 1, 3, 5, 6 only

**Expected length:** 10-20 pages for comprehensive document

---

## PRODUCT STRATEGY DOCUMENT

### Executive Summary (1 page)

**Purpose:** Provide overview so busy executives understand strategy without reading entire document

**Include:**
- Product vision (1 sentence)
- Customer we're serving (1 sentence)
- Core problem we solve (1 sentence)
- Market opportunity ($X)
- Differentiation (1-2 sentences)
- Success metrics (top 3)
- Timeline to impact

**Example:**

```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY - [Product Name]

Vision: "Enable distributed teams to collaborate and make decisions
in real-time without the complexity of juggling multiple tools."

Customer: Product and engineering teams at mid-market SaaS companies (50-500 people)

Problem: Teams waste 30% of time on communication fragmentation and
asynchronous decision-making across email, Slack, and various project tools.

Market Opportunity: $500M addressable market (SMB collaboration software)
growing at 20% annually.

Differentiation: Purpose-built for team coordination with emphasis on
decision-logging and context preservation, vs. general communication platforms.

Success Metrics (Year 1):
- Achieve 1,000 paying customers
- $2.5M Annual Recurring Revenue
- 65 Net Promoter Score

Timeline: Private beta (Q1), Public launch (Q2), Market traction (Q3-Q4)
```

## 4. Product Strategy

### 4.1 Target Customer & Customer Problem

```
TARGET CUSTOMER:
Sarah is a Senior Product Manager at a mid-market SaaS company (150 people).
She leads a 6-person product team (product managers, designers, engineers).

CUSTOMER PROBLEM:
"Our team's coordination is broken. We use Slack for chat, Linear for tasks,
Google Docs for specs, meetings for decisions, and email for async. I spend
2 hours daily switching between tools and re-explaining decisions. We're
missing context constantly. A decision I made 2 weeks ago, no one remembers
the reasoning."

OUTCOME CUSTOMER WANTS:
"I want one place where our team can quickly coordinate, get context on
decisions, and not have to search 5 tools. I want async communication
that isn't overwhelming."

WHY IT MATTERS:
Sarah's team ships 25% fewer features than they could because 30% of time
is wasted on communication friction. Better coordination = more product
shipped = career success.
```

### 4.2 How We Solve It

```
OUR APPROACH:

Core Product Capabilities:
1. Organized Communication
   - Topic-based channels (decisions, announcements, brainstorm)
   - No notifications overload (choose what matters to you)
   - Thread organization (related discussions together)

2. Decision Logging
   - Capture why decisions were made
   - Record what was considered
   - Searchable decision history
   - Link decisions to outcomes

3. Context Preservation
   - AI summarizes long discussions
   - Relevant context surface automatically
   - Full history preserved and searchable
   - Link related conversations together

4. Integration with Tools They Use
   - Pull information from Linear, Docs, etc.
   - Push summaries back to Slack
   - Embed collaboration in their workflow
   - Don't replace their tools (augment them)

5. Mobile-First Experience
   - Quick checkin when away from desk
   - Search decisions on the go
   - Reply to decisions from phone

Why This Works:
- Designed around how they actually work (async, decision-heavy)
- Doesn't try to replace chat (integrates with it)
- Solves the #1 problem (context loss) directly
- Leverage their existing tools (easier adoption)

Why Competitors Can't:
- Slack designed for general chat, not team coordination
- Teams focused on compliance, not experience
- Other tools only solve part of problem
```

### 4.3 Differentiation

```
COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGES:

1. EXPERIENCE DESIGN
   - Built specifically for product teams (not general communication)
   - UX designed for async-first (not real-time default)
   - Bias toward finding information (powerful search)
   → Competitors: Slack optimized for chat, Teams for enterprise
   → Sustainable: Takes years to change UX DNA

2. DECISION LOGGING
   - Only product with decision-as-first-class citizen
   - Link decisions to outcomes (enables learning)
   → Competitors: Generic communication platforms
   → Sustainable: Requires rethinking architecture

3. CONTEXT AI
   - AI that understands team context (not generic LLM)
   - Learns decision patterns, team dynamics
   - Provides proactive context suggestions
   → Competitors: Adding generic chat summarization (me-too)
   → Sustainable: Network effect of more decisions = better AI

4. VERTICAL FOCUS
   - Purpose-built for product teams (their language, workflows)
   - Not trying to serve everyone
   → Competitors: Horizontal platforms
   → Sustainable: Deep domain expertise hard to replicate

5. TEAM-FIRST ADOPTION
   - Designed for bottom-up adoption (team discovers)
   - Not dependent on enterprise sales
   → Competitors: Slack (dependency trap), Teams (enterprise-dependent)
   → Sustainable: Network effects grow naturally
```

### 4.4 Go-to-Market Strategy

```
POSITIONING STATEMENT:

For product and engineering teams at mid-market companies
who struggle with communication fragmentation and context loss
[Product Name] is a team coordination platform
that logs decisions and preserves context
unlike Slack which is designed for general chat
and unlike email which is fragmented and unsearchable.

MESSAGING PILLARS:

2. "Ship more features"

   - More time building

3. "Build great products"

   - Async work enables better thinking

HOW WE'LL REACH THEM:

1. Product-led growth (free tier)
   - Team discovers and adopts

   - Focus on PMM (Product Market Manager) persona

2. Content marketing
   - Articles on async-first team coordination

3. Community
   - Build community of product managers

4. Strategic partnerships
```

## 10. Decision-Making Framework

**Use this framework to guide decisions throughout execution:**

```
STRATEGIC FILTER:

For each opportunity/decision, ask:

1. Does this serve our primary target (SMB product teams)?
   YES → Continue
   NO → Question if we should do it

2. Does this strengthen a differentiation?
   (UX, decision logging, context AI, or vertical focus)
   YES → Prioritize
   NO → Question if we should do it

3. Can we do this better than competitors?
   YES → Consider it
   NO → Only if essential, question trade-off

4. Is this before or after product-market fit?
   BEFORE → Focus on retention and core value
   AFTER → Can expand to new things

5. Are we resourced to do this well?
   YES → Go for it
   NO → Don't do it half-heartedly

If YES to 4+ of 5: Prioritize
If YES to 3 of 5: Consider carefully
If YES to 2 or fewer: Default to NO
```
