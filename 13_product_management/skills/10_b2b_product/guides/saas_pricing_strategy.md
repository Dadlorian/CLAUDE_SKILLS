## Executive Summary

SaaS pricing is one of the most critical levers for company growth. The difference between a $99/month and $299/month pricing point can mean the difference between a unicorn and a failed startup. This guide covers value-based pricing frameworks that maximize both revenue and customer satisfaction, plus practical packaging strategies used by market leaders like Slack, Figma, and Notion.

**Key Insights:**
- Value-based pricing captures customer willingness to pay
- Tier strategy should balance simplicity with upsell opportunity
- Psychological tactics increase conversion and reduce cancellation
- Price testing must be systematic and data-driven
- Different segments require different pricing approaches

## Pricing Fundamentals

### The Three Pricing Philosophies

#### 1. Cost-Plus Pricing
**Definition:** Add a markup to your production costs.

**Formula:** Price = Cost + (Cost × Markup %)

**Example:**
- Cost to deliver: $100/month
- Markup: 100%
- Price: $200/month

**Pros:**
- Simple to calculate
- Guarantees profit margin
- Used in hardware/traditional products

**Cons:**
- Ignores customer value
- Leaves money on the table
- Doesn't reflect market demand
- Risk: underpricing high-value products, overpricing low-value ones

**When to use:** Early startup validation, hard cost-heavy products

#### 2. Competitive Pricing
**Definition:** Price based on competitor rates.

**Process:**
1. Identify key competitors
2. Price at parity, below, or above them
3. Adjust based on feature differentiation

**Example:**
- Competitor A charges $99/month (basic)
- Competitor B charges $299/month (professional)
- You price at $199/month (between them)

**Pros:**
- Market validation from competitors' success
- Reduces risk of extreme mispricing
- Customers have reference points

**Cons:**
- Competitors may be wrong
- Doesn't capture your unique value
- Creates race to the bottom
- Ignores customer segments

**When to use:** Mature categories, when matching established pricing makes sense

#### 3. Value-Based Pricing
**Definition:** Price based on the value delivered to customers.

**Process:**
1. Calculate customer ROI from your product
2. Identify willingness to pay
3. Capture percentage of that value
4. Price across tiers by feature access

**Example:**
- Customer saves 10 hours/week at $50/hour = $500/week saved
- Customer willing to pay up to $200/week
- You price at $150/week = 30% of value captured

**Pros:**
- Maximizes revenue potential
- Aligns incentives with customer success
- Scales pricing with customer growth
- Justifiable to enterprise buyers

**Cons:**
- Requires deep customer research
- Harder to communicate
- May need customization per segment

**When to use:** Whenever possible - it's the most profitable approach

## Value-Based Pricing Framework

### Step 1: Calculate Economic Value to Customer (EVC)

**Formula:**
```
EVC = Reference Value ± Quantifiable Differences ± Intangible Factors
```

**Example - Project Management Tool:**

**Reference Value:** Cost of their current solution
- Project manager salary + tools: $60K/year
- Time spent on manual tasks: $15K/year
- Total current cost: $75K/year

**Quantifiable Differences (your product benefits):**
- Time saved per manager: 5 hours/week = $12,500/year
- Reduced project overruns: $8,000/year savings
- Fewer hiring needs due to efficiency: $20,000/year
- **Total benefit: +$40,500/year**

**Intangible Factors:**
- Improved team morale: +$5,000/year (estimate)
- Reduced stress: +$3,000/year (estimate)
- Better company reputation: +$2,000/year (estimate)

**Total EVC:** $75,000 + $40,500 + $10,000 = **$125,500/year**

**Pricing captured:** 10-30% of EVC
- Low estimate: $125,500 × 10% = **$12,550/year = $1,046/month**
- Mid estimate: $125,500 × 20% = **$25,100/year = $2,092/month**
- High estimate: $125,500 × 30% = **$37,650/year = $3,138/month**

### Step 2: Identify Willingness to Pay (WTP)

**Willingness to Pay = What customer will spend before switching to alternative**

**Research Methods:**

#### A. Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter
Survey customers on 4 pricing questions:

1. "At what price would this product be too inexpensive (poor quality)?"
2. "At what price would this product be a bargain?"
3. "At what price would this product be expensive but acceptable?"
4. "At what price would this product be too expensive (won't buy)?"

**Analysis:**
- Plot responses on a graph
- Find intersection of "too cheap" and "too expensive"
- This intersection = price sensitivity zone

**Example Results:**
- Too cheap: $29
- Bargain: $49
- Expensive but OK: $199
- Too expensive: $299
- **Optimal price: $99-$149**

#### B. Conjoint Analysis
Show customers product combinations and ask preferences.

**Example for Productivity Software:**
```
Product A: 5 team members, 10 GB storage, $49/month
Product B: 10 team members, 50 GB storage, $99/month
Product C: 5 team members, 50 GB storage, $79/month
Product D: 10 team members, 10 GB storage, $69/month

Which would you choose?
```

**Analysis:** Determines value of each feature increment

#### C. Direct Customer Interviews
Ask customers directly:
- "How much would you pay?"
- "What's your budget for this category?"
- "At what price would you NOT buy?"
- "What price signals quality to you?"

**Effective framing:**
- "If this existed, what would be fair price?"
- "Compared to X (alternative), what's the price delta?"

#### D. Max Differential Value (MDV)
What's the maximum premium over next-best alternative?

**Example:**
- Next-best alternative: $99/month
- Customer value from your product: 2x faster results
- Time value to customer: High (paid per hour)
- Maximum they'd pay: $150-$200/month

**Your pricing target:** $129-$179/month

### Step 3: Price Anchoring and Tier Spread

**Price Anchoring:** First price shown significantly influences perception

**Tier Spread Strategy:**

#### The "Good-Better-Best" Tiers
```
BASIC ($29/month)
├─ 1 user
├─ Basic features
├─ 1 GB storage
└─ Email support

PROFESSIONAL ($99/month) ← Price anchor tier
├─ 10 users
├─ All features
├─ 100 GB storage
└─ Priority support

ENTERPRISE ($299/month)
├─ Unlimited users
├─ All features + custom
├─ Unlimited storage
└─ Dedicated support
```

**Psychology:**
- Middle tier anchors perception of value
- Makes premium tier seem reasonable
- Makes basic tier seem affordable

**Optimal spread ratio:** 1:3:9 to 1:3.5:10 (basic to mid to premium)
- Too close: no upgrade incentive
- Too far: premium seems unreasonable

### Step 4: Feature-Value Mapping

Map which features drive value in each tier:

**High-Value Features (high willingness to pay impact):**
- Workflow automation
- Advanced analytics
- API access
- Priority support
- Custom integrations
- SSO/advanced security
- Service Level Agreements (SLAs)

**Medium-Value Features:**
- Advanced reporting
- Team collaboration tools
- Third-party integrations
- Mobile app
- Bulk operations

**Low-Value Features:**
- Dark mode
- Advanced search
- Custom branding
- Email summaries
- Basic templates

**Pricing strategy:**
- Reserve high-value features for higher tiers
- Include low-value features in all tiers (increases perceived value)
- Stagger medium-value features across tiers

## Tier Strategy and Packaging

### The Five Packaging Approaches

#### 1. Vertical Feature Gating (Most Common)
**Approach:** Gate features by tier

**Example - Design Tool:**
```
STARTER (Free)
├─ 1 project
├─ Basic shapes
├─ 3 templates
└─ Personal use only

PRO ($15/month)
├─ Unlimited projects
├─ Design system
├─ Advanced effects
├─ Team collaboration (3 people)

TEAMS ($45/month)
├─ Everything in PRO
├─ Unlimited collaborators
├─ Advanced prototyping
├─ Workspace analytics
└─ Priority support
```

**Pros:**
- Clear upgrade path
- Easy to communicate
- Customers know what they get

**Cons:**
- Can feel artificial to gate basic features
- Cannibalization risk if feature-gating doesn't drive value

#### 2. Usage-Based Pricing (Growing)
**Approach:** Price per unit of consumption

**Metrics Examples:**
- Per API call (Stripe: $0.70 per 1000 calls)
- Per user (Slack: $8-15 per active user)
- Per GB storage (AWS S3: $0.023 per GB)
- Per workflow run (Zapier: $0.25-$2 per run)
- Per seat (Figma: $12-80 per editor seat)

**Example - Email Service:**
```
Free: 100 emails/month
Pay-as-you-go: $0.10 per 1,000 emails after free tier

Customer sends 50,000/month:
- First 100 free
- Remaining 49,900 × ($0.10/1,000) = $4.99
```

**Pros:**
- Fair to customers (pay for value)
- Revenue scales with customer success
- Low barrier to entry (start free)
- Aligns incentives

**Cons:**
- Unpredictable customer costs (may cause churn)
- Requires transparent metering
- Customer acquisition cost must be high-margin
- Complex billing

**When to use:**
- Infrastructure/utility products (hosting, APIs)
- High variance in customer usage
- When usage correlates strongly with value

#### 3. Value-Based Tiers (Premium Approach)
**Approach:** Price by value segment, not features

**Example - Analytics Platform:**
```
STARTUP ($99/month)
- For founders & early teams
- 5 data sources
- 7-day data retention
- Community support

GROWTH ($499/month)
- For scaling companies
- 50 data sources
- 90-day data retention
- Priority support
- Custom reports

ENTERPRISE (Custom)
- For established corporations
- Unlimited data sources
- Unlimited retention
- Dedicated success manager
- SLA guarantee
```

**Pros:**
- Customers self-select into tiers
- High perception of value
- Clear ROI justification
- Easy to upgrade

**Cons:**
- Feature parity may confuse
- Harder to prevent downgrading
- Requires good market segmentation

#### 4. Hybrid Pricing (Emerging Best Practice)
**Approach:** Combine seat-based + usage-based

**Example - Figma's Model:**
```
Team plan: $12/editor/month
+ $4/collaborator/month (viewer-only team members)
+ Variable based on file storage
```

**Why this works:**
- Base fee funds infrastructure
- Usage charges for true marginal cost
- Aligns with customer value
- Predictability + fairness

#### 5. Add-On Pricing (Maximizes Revenue)
**Approach:** Base plan with optional premium features

**Example - Project Management Tool:**
```
BASE PLAN: $99/month
├─ 10 projects
├─ 20 team members
├─ Basic templates
└─ Email support

ADD-ONS:
├─ Advanced Automation: +$49/month
├─ Custom Reporting: +$29/month
├─ API Access: +$99/month
├─ Priority Support: +$49/month
└─ Compliance Pack: +$199/month
```

**Pros:**
- Customers only pay for needs
- Higher total revenue per customer
- Perceived customization
- Upsell opportunities

**Cons:**
- Complex pricing (confuses buyers)
- Can feel nickel-and-diming
- Implementation cost

**Best practice:** Limit to 3-5 core add-ons

## Real-World Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Slack - Network Effects Pricing

**Business Model:** Seat-based + usage-based

**Pricing Structure:**
```
Free Plan
├─ Unlimited users
├─ 10,000 most recent messages
└─ 1:1 calls

Pro Plan: $8/user/month
├─ Unlimited message history
├─ Screen sharing
├─ User administration

Business+ Plan: $12.50/user/month
├─ Everything in Pro
├─ Advanced admin controls
├─ Enhanced security

Enterprise Grid: Custom
├─ Unlimited workspaces
├─ Dedicated support
├─ Custom SLA
```

**Value-Based Pricing Elements:**

1. **The Problem They Solve:**
   - Average employee communication tool spend: $50-100/year
   - Time spent searching for information: 9.3 hours/week
   - Lost productivity from context-switching: $15K-20K/employee/year

2. **Their Value Capture:**
   - $8/user/month = $96/user/year
   - Typical company: 100 employees = $9,600/year
   - Represents <1% of value created for the organization
   - Makes it easy to justify to procurement

3. **Freemium Strategy:**
   - Free plan creates network effects
   - Users demand upgrade to access history
   - Team leads upgrade workspaces

4. **Packaging Insight:**
   - Free plan is not meant to be profitable
   - Free plan creates indispensability
   - Switching costs increase as message history grows

**Lessons for Your Pricing:**
- Design free tier to create habits and lock-in
- Use message history/data as key upgrade trigger
- Price per-seat in collaboration tools
- Position as cost savings relative to employee time

### Case Study 2: Figma - Feature-Based Value Scaling

**Pricing Structure:**
```
Free Plan
├─ Unlimited viewers
├─ 3 editable files
├─ Basic prototyping

Pro: $12/month per editor seat
├─ Unlimited files
├─ Advanced prototyping
├─ 30-day version history
├─ Team libraries

Organization: $60/month (minimum 3 seats)
├─ Everything in Pro
├─ Permissions management
├─ Shared teams
├─ Admin controls

Enterprise: Custom
├─ Everything in Organization
├─ Priority support
├─ SSO
├─ Advanced security
```

**Value-Based Elements:**

1. **Core Problem:**
   - Designer salary: $70K-120K/year
   - Time spent managing design files/handoff: 10-15 hours/week
   - Rework from design-eng miscommunication: 20-30% of work

2. **Value Creation:**
   - Figma reduces handoff time: 5+ hours/week saved = $12,500-25,000/year
   - Reduces rework: 10-15% improvement = $7,000-18,000/year
   - Total value: $20,000-43,000 per designer/year

3. **Pricing Capture:**
   - $12/month = $144/year per editor seat
   - = 0.3% to 0.7% of value created
   - Easy to justify: "Pays for itself in 1 hour of saved time"

4. **Packaging Innovation:**
   - Free plan designed for individual users (gets them hooked)
   - Pro tier captures team collaboration value
   - Viewers cost nothing (network effect: more people use Figma)
   - Higher tiers target organizational needs

**Lessons for Your Pricing:**
- Price the audience you target (Pro tier = individual contributor-facing)
- Include viewers/read-only users for free (increases user count)
- Charge for collaboration features (highest value to teams)
- Use seat pricing for transparent usage-based expansion

### Case Study 3: Notion - Free to Enterprise

**Pricing Structure:**
```
Free
├─ Unlimited blocks
├─ Upload files < 5 MB
├─ Web clipper
├─ Mobile apps
└─ Free for personal

Plus: $10/month (user)
├─ Everything in Free
├─ File uploads up to 20 MB
├─ Unlimited guests
├─ Early access to features

Business: $20/month (user)
├─ Everything in Plus
├─ Priority support
├─ Advanced analytics
├─ Workflows

Enterprise: Custom quote
├─ Everything in Business
├─ SSO
├─ Advanced security
├─ Dedicated support
```

**Value-Based Elements:**

1. **Positioning:**
   - Notion replaces: Note-taking + docs + project management + CRM + Wiki
   - Cost of tools replaced: Confluence ($8/user) + Jira ($8-20/user) + Slack ($8-15/user) = $24-43/user/month
   - Notion: $10-20/user/month = Saves 50-70%

2. **Free Tier Strategy:**
   - Free tier is genuinely unlimited
   - No message history limits or file limits (like Slack)
   - Targets individual users and small teams
   - Builds virality (free users are sales team)

3. **Upgrade Triggers:**
   - Guests feature (free doesn't include)
   - File upload size (5MB → 20MB → unlimited)
   - Advanced features (workflows, advanced analytics)
   - Support priority

4. **Enterprise Playbook:**
   - Notion Free becomes indispensable within teams
   - IT wants control → Business/Enterprise tier
   - Custom pricing for large enterprises

**Lessons for Your Pricing:**
- Generous free tier can drive viral adoption
- Price per-user for team products
- Build upgrade triggers that unlock team value
- Each tier should address different team sizes

## Price Optimization Models

### The 80/20 Pricing Rule

**Principle:** 80% of revenue often comes from 20% of price points

**Application:**
1. Analyze current customer distribution
2. Identify which tiers generate most revenue
3. Double down on those tier benefits
4. Simplify or remove underperforming tiers

**Example Analysis:**
```
Tier Distribution      Revenue Contribution
├─ Starter 45% users  ├─ 15% revenue (low ARPU)
├─ Pro 35% users      ├─ 50% revenue (sweet spot)
├─ Premium 15% users  ├─ 25% revenue (enterprise)
└─ Enterprise 5% users└─ 10% revenue (underpriced)

Action: Focus on Pro tier benefits
```

### The Pricing Elasticity Model

**Elasticity = % Change in Quantity / % Change in Price**

**Formula:**
```
If price ↑ 10% and customers ↓ 5%
Elasticity = -5% / +10% = -0.5 (inelastic - good for revenue)

If price ↑ 10% and customers ↓ 20%
Elasticity = -20% / +10% = -2.0 (elastic - bad for revenue)
```

**Decision Rule:**
- Inelastic (-0.5 to -1.0): Raise prices
- Unit elastic (-1.0): At equilibrium
- Elastic (< -1.0): Lower prices to maximize revenue

**Example Scenario:**
```
Current: 1,000 customers × $99/month = $99,000 MRR

Test 1: Raise to $109 (+10%)
├─ Result: 950 customers (-5%)
├─ New MRR: 950 × $109 = $103,550
├─ Revenue impact: +$4,550 (+4.6%)
└─ Action: Implement the increase

Test 2: Raise to $119 (+20%)
├─ Result: 850 customers (-15%)
├─ New MRR: 850 × $119 = $101,150
├─ Revenue impact: +$2,150 (+2.2%)
└─ Action: Only increase to $109, not $119
```

### The Price-Value Ladder

**Principle:** Create pricing tiers that allow customers to upgrade as they grow

**Structure:**
```
Starter ($29/mo)
  ↓ (trigger: team grows to 10)
Professional ($99/mo)
  ↓ (trigger: advanced features needed)
Business ($299/mo)
  ↓ (trigger: enterprise requirements)
Enterprise (Custom)
```

**Key Elements:**
1. **Low entry barrier:** Affordable trial tier
2. **Clear upgrade triggers:** Specific usage thresholds
3. **Natural progression:** Each tier logically leads to next
4. **Value acceleration:** Higher tiers offer disproportionate value

**Example - Productivity Tool:**
```
Tier 1 ($0): Free
├─ 1 project
├─ 3 collaborators
└─ Basic features

Upgrade trigger: 2 concurrent projects OR 8 people needing access

Tier 2 ($49): Pro
├─ Unlimited projects
├─ 20 collaborators
├─ Advanced templates
└─ Priority support

Upgrade trigger: 50+ collaborators OR advanced automation needed

Tier 3 ($199): Business
├─ Everything in Pro
├─ Workflow automation
├─ Advanced analytics
├─ Dedicated support

Upgrade trigger: Organization-wide deployment needed

Tier 4 (Custom): Enterprise
├─ Everything in Business
├─ SSO/SAML
├─ Custom SLA
├─ Account manager
```

### The Jobs-to-be-Done Pricing Framework

**Concept:** Price based on job customer is trying to accomplish

**Jobs Example - Expense Tracking Software:**

**Job 1: "Prevent unplanned expenses"**
- Customer segment: Freelancers, small business
- Willingness to pay: Low ($5-20/month)
- Features: Basic tracking, alerts
- Tier: Starter

**Job 2: "Reduce accounting overhead"**
- Customer segment: Growing teams
- Willingness to pay: Medium ($50-150/month)
- Features: Automation, reporting, integrations
- Tier: Professional

**Job 3: "Ensure compliance and audit readiness"**
- Customer segment: Enterprises
- Willingness to pay: High ($300+/month)
- Features: Advanced audit, custom workflows, compliance certifications
- Tier: Enterprise

**Implementation:**
- Market each tier to different customer job
- Emphasize job-specific benefits
- Price reflects job criticality to organization

## Psychological Pricing Tactics

### 1. The Charm Pricing Effect

**Principle:** Prices ending in 9 feel 10% cheaper than round numbers

**Examples:**
```
$99/month ← Feels ~10% cheaper than $110
$49/month ← Feels significantly cheaper than $50
$999/year ← Feels like $900 range
```

**Why it works:**
- Cognitive bias: Process left digits first
- $99 seen as "90-something" not "close to 100"
- Creates perception of deal/thoughtfulness

**Application:**
- Pro tier: $99/month (charm pricing)
- Enterprise: $500/month or $5,000/year (round numbers convey stability)
- Starter: Free or $29/month (charm pricing drives conversion)

### 2. The Decoy Effect

**Principle:** Adding expensive option makes mid-tier option look better

**Example:**
```
WITHOUT DECOY:
├─ Basic: $29/month (30% choose)
└─ Premium: $99/month (70% choose)

WITH DECOY:
├─ Basic: $29/month (15% choose)
├─ Professional: $99/month (65% choose) ← NOW MORE ATTRACTIVE
└─ Premium Plus: $149/month (20% choose)
```

**Why it works:**
- Professional tier now looks like "best value"
- Premium Plus seems expensive relative to Professional
- Moves customers toward middle tier

**Application in SaaS:**
```
Starter: $49
Pro: $149  ← Decoy between Starter and Business
Business: $299
Enterprise: Custom
```

**Result:** Most customers choose Business tier (jump from Pro seems reasonable compared to Pro→Enterprise without it)

### 3. The Anchoring Effect

**Principle:** First price shown influences all subsequent judgments

**Application - Yearly Pricing Anchor:**
```
MONTHLY: $99/month
ANNUAL: $990/year (show this first)

vs.

ANNUAL: $840/year (show this first)
MONTHLY: $99/month (perceive as premium)
```

**Psychological effect:**
- Annual $990 anchors value perception at $990
- Monthly $99 seems cheap relative to $990 anchor
- Increases monthly conversion

**Real example:**
```
SaaS Company A: Shows $99/month first
├─ Annual conversion: 30%
└─ Average annual deal: $1,188

SaaS Company B: Shows $990/year first (same deal)
├─ Annual conversion: 45%
└─ Average annual deal: $1,188 (higher close rate)
```

### 4. The Price Presentation Effect

**Principle:** How price is presented impacts perception

**Technique 1: Divide by smaller time unit**

```
❌ $1,200/year sounds expensive
✓ $100/month feels affordable
✓ $3.33/day sounds trivial
✓ $0.11/hour seems free
```

**Application for different segments:**
- Individual users: "$X/month"
- Small business: "$X/month per user"
- Enterprise: "$X/year"

**Technique 2: The cost per value unit**

```
❌ Video conferencing tool costs $200/month
✓ Video conferencing costs $2 per meeting (if 100 meetings/month)
✓ Collaboration tool costs $1 per team member per day
```

**Technique 3: Comparison to alternative**

```
❌ Project management tool costs $99/month
✓ Project management tool costs 1/3 the price of Asana
✓ Saves $5,000/month vs. hiring project coordinator
```

### 5. The Loss Aversion Pricing

**Principle:** Fear of losing something drives behavior more than hope of gaining

**Application 1: Free Trial Positioning**
```
❌ "30-day free trial"
✓ "30 days free - then you'll need to pay"
✓ "After 30 days, decide if the $99/month is worth it"
```

**Psychological effect:**
- Second framing triggers loss aversion
- Users more likely to upgrade to prevent losing access
- Increases conversion from free to paid

**Application 2: Feature Sunset**
```
"Limited-time offer: Get Pro plan for $49/month
after 30 days, price increases to $99/month"
```

**Effect:** Creates urgency through fear of paying more

**Application 3: Discount Framing**
```
❌ "Annual plan: $990/year"
✓ "Annual plan: Save 17% - only $990/year vs. $1,200/month"
✓ "Save $210/year with annual billing"
```

## Go-to-Market Pricing Strategy

### Launch Pricing vs. Long-term Pricing

**Phase 1: Product Launch (Months 0-6)**

**Goals:**
- Acquire initial customers
- Learn price sensitivity
- Validate value proposition
- Build reference customers

**Pricing Strategy:**
- Price 20-30% below market to ensure adoption
- Simple pricing (fewer tiers than long-term)
- Generous free trial (30 days minimum)
- No long-term contracts

**Example Launch Pricing:**
```
Market rate for similar tool: $300/month
Your launch price: $199/month (-33% discount)

Rationale:
├─ Ensures adoption
├─ Generates testimonials/case studies
├─ Creates switching momentum
└─ Allows rapid feature improvements
```

**Phase 2: Growth Stage (Months 6-18)**

**Goals:**
- Increase average revenue per user (ARPU)
- Expand tier options
- Develop enterprise segment
- Optimize conversion rates

**Pricing Strategy:**
- Introduce middle and premium tiers
- Raise base tier 15-20%
- Create enterprise tier
- Build add-on product

**Example Growth Pricing:**
```
Launch ($199)
├─ 6 months later: Starter ($199), Pro ($499)
├─ 12 months later: Starter ($249), Pro ($599), Enterprise (custom)
└─ 18 months later: Starter ($249), Pro ($599), Business ($999), Enterprise (custom)

ARPU increase: $199 → $350 → $425 → $500+
```

**Phase 3: Maturity (Months 18+)**

**Goals:**
- Maximize revenue
- Premium segment expansion
- Usage-based model integration
- Market domination positioning

**Pricing Strategy:**
- Raise all tiers 10% annually
- Optimize for margin not growth
- Value-based pricing dominates
- Usage-based for overages

```
├─ Priority support

Rationale:
├─ Higher willingness to pay (budgeted spend)
├─ Feature bundles for different needs
├─ Clear upgrade path
└─ Seat-based pricing
```

**Segment 3: Enterprise (100+ employees)**
```
Enterprise Plan: Custom (typically $1,000-10,000/month+)
├─ Unlimited users
├─ All features
├─ Custom integrations
├─ Dedicated success manager
├─ SLA guarantee
├─ Custom contracts

Rationale:
├─ Willingness to pay scales with size
├─ Need for dedicated support
├─ Custom requirements common
├─ Long sales cycles justify custom pricing
└─ Account managers handle negotiations
```

## Lifecycle-Based Pricing

### Customer Lifecycle Stages & Pricing

**Stage 1: Awareness & Trial (Months 0-2)**
```
Pricing: Free trial, freemium, or heavily discounted trial
Goal: Prove value, build habits
Metrics: Trial signup rate, feature adoption

Example: 30-day free trial or free tier with usage limits
```

**Stage 2: Onboarding & Evaluation (Months 2-4)**
```
Pricing: Annual discount incentive (e.g., "Lock in $99/month if you sign up for year")
Goal: Convert to paid, build commitment
Metrics: Trial-to-paid conversion, initial ARPU

Example: "Sign up for annual plan, save 20% vs. monthly"
```

**Stage 3: Active Usage (Months 4-12)**
```
Pricing: Standard pricing, maintain value delivery
Goal: Ensure customer success, prevent churn
Metrics: Monthly churn rate, NPS

Example: Standard pricing tiers, no discounts
```

**Stage 4: Expansion (Months 12+)**
```
Pricing: Upsell add-ons, encourage tier upgrade
Goal: Increase ARPU, expand land-and-expand motions
Metrics: Net revenue retention, upgrade rates

Example: "Upgrade to Business plan and unlock automation"
```

**Stage 5: Maturity/Risk of Churn (Months 24+)**
```
Pricing: Loyalty discounts, bundle offers
Goal: Retain, win back if churned
Metrics: Churn rate, customer lifetime value

Example: "As a loyal customer, we want to offer you Pro plan at 20% off"
```

**Stage 6: Churn Recovery (After cancellation)**
```
Pricing: Win-back offers, "Come back" discounts
Goal: Reactivate churned customers
Metrics: Win-back rate, reactivation revenue

Example: "We miss you! Get 50% off your first month back"
```

### Cohort-Based Pricing Analysis

**Concept:** Different cohorts (groups) of customers may have different optimal pricing

**Analysis Template:**

```
Cohort A: B2B SaaS customers
├─ Count: 45% of customer base
├─ Average ARPU: $249/month
├─ Churn rate: 2% monthly
├─ Optimal pricing: $199 → $249 → $299 (standard ladder)
└─ Recommendation: Slightly increase, value well-established

Cohort B: Agencies
├─ Count: 30% of customer base
├─ Average ARPU: $399/month
├─ Churn rate: 4% monthly
├─ Optimal pricing: Use-case specific, higher ability to pay
└─ Recommendation: Create agency-specific tier at $499-599

Cohort C: Individual freelancers
├─ Count: 25% of customer base
├─ Average ARPU: $79/month
├─ Churn rate: 6% monthly
├─ Optimal pricing: Highly price-sensitive
└─ Recommendation: Keep free tier generous, minimize upgrade friction
```

## Appendix: Quick Reference Formulas

### Economic Value Calculator
```
EVC = (Replacement cost + Quantifiable benefits + Intangible value) × 10-30% capture rate
```

### Tier Pricing Template
```
Basic: Base × 1
Mid: Base × 3-4 (price anchor)
Premium: Base × 9-10
```

### Annual Discount Target
```
Annual discount = (Monthly price × 12) × 0.85 = 15% discount incentive
Example: $99/month = $1,188/year regular = $1,000/year annual (15% off)
```

### Price Elasticity Decision
```
If elasticity > -1.0: Inelastic = Raise prices
If elasticity < -1.0: Elastic = Lower prices
```

### Pricing Power Formula
```
Pricing Power = (Switching Cost + Differentiation + Value Capture) / (Commoditization + Competition)
```

## Conclusion

Value-based pricing is both art and science. The best SaaS pricing strategies combine:

1. **Deep customer research** - Understand true willingness to pay
2. **Clear value articulation** - Make ROI obvious
3. **Thoughtful packaging** - Good/Better/Best tiers that drive value
4. **Psychological tactics** - Anchoring, charm pricing, decoys
5. **Continuous optimization** - Test, measure, adjust quarterly
6. **Segment-specific approaches** - Different pricing for different customer types

Companies like Slack, Figma, and Notion didn't get pricing right on day one. They evolved their pricing as they learned more about customer needs and willingness to pay. Your pricing should be a strategic lever you pull continuously, not a one-time decision.

The goal: Capture 20-30% of the value you create. This funds your growth while leaving customers thrilled about the ROI they receive.
