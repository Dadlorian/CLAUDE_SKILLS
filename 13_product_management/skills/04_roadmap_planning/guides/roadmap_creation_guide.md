# Roadmap Creation Guide: Building Your First Roadmap

A step-by-step guide for product managers creating their first strategic roadmap. This guide takes you from zero to a polished, stakeholder-approved roadmap in 6-8 weeks.

---

## Phase 1: Discovery & Input Gathering (Weeks 1-2)

### Week 1: Data Collection

**Goal**: Gather all the inputs needed for strategic planning.

**Day 1-2: Quantitative Data**
- [ ] Pull annual performance metrics
  - Revenue and growth numbers
  - User engagement (DAU, MAU, session length)
  - Retention curves (D1, D7, D30)
  - Churn analysis and trends
  - Feature adoption rates
- [ ] Identify key trends year-over-year
- [ ] Create simple dashboard or spreadsheet

**Day 3: Qualitative Data**
- [ ] Collect customer feedback from all sources
  - Support tickets (most common issues)
  - Feature requests (top 20)
  - NPS/CSAT feedback
  - User research findings
  - Sales calls and objections
  - Interview snapshots from customer conversations
- [ ] Organize by theme or problem area
- [ ] Identify recurring pain points

**Day 4: Competitive Intelligence**
- [ ] List top 5-10 competitors
- [ ] Note major features they've launched recently
- [ ] Identify competitive positioning gaps
- [ ] Document pricing and business model changes
- [ ] Look for industry trends

**Day 5: Internal Perspectives**
- [ ] Send survey/template to gather input from:
  - Sales team (customer needs, competitive losses)
  - Support team (common issues, top requests)
  - Engineering leadership (tech debt, performance concerns)
  - Design team (UX improvements, capability gaps)
  - Marketing (positioning, launch needs)

**Example Input Request Template**:
```markdown
## 2025 Roadmap Input Request

Please respond by [DATE] with your perspective on:

### Top 3 Customer Needs
What are the top 3 problems or needs your team hears from customers?

1. [Need] - Why important? How many customers affected?
2. [Need]
3. [Need]

### Top 3 Internal Priorities
What are the top 3 things your team wants to focus on?

1. [Priority] - Why important? Business impact?
2. [Priority]
3. [Priority]

### Major Risks / Concerns
What could go wrong in 2025 if we don't address it?

1. [Risk] - What would the impact be?

### Questions for Leadership
What should we be thinking about that we might be missing?

---

Please keep responses to 1 page max. Submit by [DATE].
```

### Week 2: Strategy Input Sessions

**Goal**: Get leadership perspective on strategic direction.

**1:1 Conversations** (30 min each with 3-5 key stakeholders):
- [ ] Schedule with:
  - CEO/Founder
  - VP Engineering
  - VP Sales
  - Head of Customer Success
  - Head of Design (if separate from product)

**Conversation Framework**:
```markdown
## Strategic Input 1:1 Template

"I'm working on developing our 2025 roadmap. I'd love to get your perspective on a few questions. This should take about 30 minutes."

### Questions

1. **Performance Review**
   - "How do you think we performed in 2024? Any wins or misses?"
   - "Where do we have momentum? Where are we losing ground?"

2. **Market Context**
   - "How has the market changed? What are you seeing with customers/competition?"
   - "Are there any external trends or forces that should influence our strategy?"

3. **Customer Perspective**
   - "What's the #1 problem customers have with our product?"
   - "What's something customers don't have access to that our competitors have?"

4. **Internal Perspective**
   - "What's one thing you'd like to see change about how we build product?"
   - "If you had a magic wand to fix one thing, what would it be?"

5. **Strategic Direction**
   - "What do you think should be our biggest focus area for 2025?"
   - "Where do you see the biggest opportunity for us?"

6. **Constraints**
   - "What constraints should we be aware of?" (budget, team size, technical debt, etc.)

Take notes during. Send thank-you note with key takeaways from their perspective.
```

**Document Your Findings**:
- [ ] Create summary document with themes from input
- [ ] Identify consensus areas and disagreements
- [ ] Note specific customer/market opportunities mentioned
- [ ] Summarize competitive landscape update

**Deliverable from Phase 1**:
- Quantitative data dashboard or summary
- Qualitative feedback compilation by theme
- Competitive landscape update
- Input summary from leadership conversations

## Phase 2: Theme Development (Weeks 3-4)

### Week 3: Strategic Theme Workshop

**Goal**: Develop 3-5 strategic themes that will guide the year.

**Pre-Work** (Send 1 week before):
- Data summary document (3-4 pages)
- Feedback synthesis (themes and patterns)
- Competitive landscape
- CEO/Founder strategic thoughts
- Market trends assessment

**Workshop** (Full day, ideally off-site):

**Participants**: CEO, VP Product, VP Engineering, VP Sales, Head of Design, any key advisors

**Agenda**:
```
8:00-8:30   Coffee & Opening
            - Why we're here (20 min)
            - Ground rules (10 min)

8:30-9:15   Performance Review
            - 2024 successes and misses (30 min)
            - Learning discussion (15 min)

9:15-10:30  Market & Customer Insights
            - Competitive landscape update (20 min)
            - Customer feedback themes (20 min)
            - Market opportunity areas (15 min)

10:30-10:45 Break

10:45-12:15 Theme Brainstorm
            - "What should we focus on?" Open brainstorm (30 min)
            - Narrow to top 6-8 themes (30 min)
            - Discuss pros/cons of each (15 min)

12:15-1:00  Lunch

1:00-2:30   Theme Refinement
            - Define each theme clearly (2 min per theme)
            - "What problem are we solving?" discussion
            - Impact assessment (customer value + business value)

2:30-3:15   Reality Check
            - Can we do all of these? Capacity discussion
            - Sequencing - which comes first?
            - Resource requirements

3:15-3:45   Decision & Alignment
            - Final theme list and order
            - Quick sync on why each matters
            - Assign owners

3:45-4:00   Next Steps & Wrap
            - What happens next?
            - When will we go deeper?
```

**Facilitator Tips**:
- **Manage time**: Use 15-min warnings for time blocks
- **Get everyone talking**: Call on quiet people
- **Capture dissent**: Note disagreements, revisit if needed
- **Record decisions**: Document theme list and rationale
- **Build consensus**: Not unanimity, but clear alignment

**Workshop Output**:
- [ ] 3-5 strategic themes (clear names, one-sentence descriptions)
- [ ] Ranking of themes (which is most important?)
- [ ] Why each theme matters (customer + business rationale)
- [ ] Owner assigned to each theme
- [ ] Rough sequencing (When will we tackle each?)

### Week 4: Theme Definition & Validation

**Goal**: Write clear, detailed definitions for each theme.

**For Each Theme, Document**:

```markdown
## Theme: [Theme Name]

### One-Sentence Definition
[Clear, compelling statement of what this theme is about]

### Problem Statement
[What customer or business problem are we solving?]

### Why This Matters
**Customer Value**:
- [How does this solve customer problems?]
- [What value do customers get?]

**Business Impact**:
- [How does this drive revenue/growth/retention?]
- [What's the financial impact?]

### Success Metrics
- [How will we measure success of this theme?]
- [What should improve by end of year?]
- [Quantified target if possible]

### Potential Solutions/Approaches
[List 5-10 ways we might approach this. Don't commit to anything yet.]
- Approach 1: [Brief description]
- Approach 2: [Brief description]
...

### Dependencies & Sequencing
- [What needs to happen first?]
- [Other teams we need to involve?]

### Rough Effort Estimate
- [Is this 3 months of work? 6 months? Ongoing?]

### Owner
[Who is responsible for this theme?]

### Next Steps
[What research or exploration needs to happen to move forward?]
```

**Validation Activities**:
- [ ] Share with key stakeholders and get feedback
- [ ] Test assumptions with customer interviews (5-10 customers)
- [ ] Pressure test with engineering on feasibility
- [ ] Validate with sales on customer importance
- [ ] Refine based on feedback

**Adjustments**:
- [ ] Add themes that came up in validation
- [ ] Combine themes that are related
- [ ] Reword based on feedback
- [ ] Adjust sequencing based on dependencies

**Deliverable from Phase 2**:
- 3-5 well-defined strategic themes
- Theme descriptions (1-page each)
- Success metrics for each theme
- Strategic theme narrative (2-3 pages explaining why these 5)

## Phase 3: Roadmap Planning (Weeks 5-6)

### Week 5: Now/Next/Later Planning

**Goal**: Translate themes into a Now/Next/Later roadmap.

**Step 1: Brainstorm Initiatives per Theme**
For each theme, brainstorm 8-12 specific initiatives or work items:

```markdown
## Theme: [Name]

### Potential Initiatives
1. [Initiative]: [1-line description of what it is and why it matters]
2. [Initiative]: [Description]
3. [Initiative]: [Description]
...
```

**Example - Enterprise Security Theme**:
```markdown
## Theme: Enterprise-Grade Security

### Potential Initiatives
1. SSO/SAML Authentication: Enable enterprise customers to use company identity
2. Role-Based Access Control: Let admins define granular permissions for users
3. Audit Logging: Track who accessed what and when (compliance requirement)
4. Data Encryption at Rest: Protect customer data with enterprise-grade encryption
5. Advanced Admin Dashboard: Give admins visibility and control over account
6. Two-Factor Authentication: Add security layer for sensitive actions
7. IP Allowlisting: Control which IP addresses can access accounts
8. SOC 2 Compliance: Achieve SOC 2 Type II certification
9. HIPAA Compliance: Support healthcare-regulated environments
10. Penetration Testing: External security assessment
```

**Step 2: Prioritize Within Theme**
For each initiative, estimate:
- **Customer Value** (1-10): How much do customers want/need this?
- **Business Impact** (1-10): Revenue, retention, acquisition impact?
- **Effort** (weeks): How long will this take?
- **Confidence** (0-100%): How confident are we in this approach?

Use RICE scoring: (Customer Value × Business Impact × Confidence) / Effort

```markdown
## Theme: Enterprise Security - Prioritized

| Initiative | Customer Value | Business Impact | Confidence | Effort | RICE Score | Priority |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|-----------|---------|-----------|----------|
| SSO/SAML | 9 | 8 | 90% | 8 weeks | 8.1 | 1 |
| Role-Based Access | 8 | 8 | 85% | 6 weeks | 9.1 | 2 |
| Audit Logging | 7 | 7 | 95% | 4 weeks | 11.9 | 3 |
| Admin Dashboard | 6 | 6 | 80% | 6 weeks | 4.8 | 4 |
| 2FA | 5 | 4 | 85% | 3 weeks | 5.7 | 5 |
| IP Allowlisting | 5 | 4 | 80% | 2 weeks | 8.0 | 6 |
| Data Encryption | 6 | 7 | 75% | 10 weeks | 3.15 | 7 |
| SOC 2 Compliance | 7 | 8 | 70% | 12 weeks | 3.27 | 8 |
```

**Step 3: Allocate to Now/Next/Later**

**NOW Allocation** (Next 3 months):
- Top priorities across all themes
- Mix of quick wins and bigger items
- Don't exceed 100% of team capacity
- Aim for 5-8 initiatives

Example:
```
NOW (Q1 2025)
├── Enterprise Security: SSO/SAML Authentication (Priority 1)
├── Enterprise Security: Role-Based Access (Priority 2)
├── Mobile Experience: Redesigned Mobile App (High value, customer requested)
├── Platform Reliability: Database Performance Optimization
└── UX Polish: Simplified First-Time User Onboarding
```

**NEXT Allocation** (Quarters 2-4):
- Next tier of priorities
- Contingent on Now completing on time
- Medium confidence
- 5-8 initiatives

**LATER Allocation** (2026+):
- Strategic themes without near-term timing
- Early-stage ideas
- Nice-to-have enhancements
- Lower confidence work

### Week 6: Build Communication Artifact

**Goal**: Create your roadmap document.

**Choose Your Format**:
- Now/Next/Later (recommended for first roadmap)
- Theme-based (if you want to emphasize strategic direction)
- Outcome-based (if you want team flexibility)
- See roadmap_formats.md for full options

**Create Your Roadmap Document**:

```markdown
# 2025 Product Roadmap

## Our Strategic Direction

[1-2 paragraph narrative explaining your strategy]

### Strategic Themes
1. [Theme 1]: [1-line description]
2. [Theme 2]: [1-line description]
3. [Theme 3]: [1-line description]
...

---

## NOW (Q1 2025)

### [Theme 1 Name]: [Initiative Name]
- **What**: [Clear, customer-focused description]
- **Why**: [Problem being solved, business impact]
- **Owner**: [Name]
- **Timeline**: [Weeks or target date]

### [Theme 2 Name]: [Initiative Name]
...

---

## NEXT (Q2-Q4 2025)

### [Theme 1 Name]: [Initiative Name]
[Description]

---

## LATER (2026+)

### [Theme 1 Name]: [Initiative Name]
[Description]

---

## Our Approach

We build roadmaps to communicate strategic direction, not to lock us into detailed timelines. As we learn and the market evolves, we'll update this roadmap. We'll do a detailed quarterly review to ensure we're making progress on these themes while staying flexible to new learnings.

## Questions?

Want to provide feedback or request something? [Process/email/form]
```

**Visual Roadmap** (Optional but Recommended):
- Create simple visual representation
- Use tools like:
  - Figma (design tool, creates beautiful visuals)
  - ProductBoard or Aha! (roadmap tools)
  - Google Sheets with colored rows
  - Miro (whiteboard tool)
- Visual should show:
  - Themes on one axis
  - Timeline on other axis
  - Major initiatives in each quadrant
  - Color coding for status or theme

**Deliverable from Phase 3**:
- Finalized roadmap document (text)
- Visual roadmap (graph or image)
- Supporting one-pagers for each theme
- Success metrics defined for each theme

## Phase 4: Stakeholder Review & Refinement (Weeks 7-8)

### Early Review Sessions

**Step 1: Leadership Review** (1 week - Week 7):
- [ ] Present to CEO/leadership team (30-45 min)
  - Strategic rationale
  - Theme overview
  - Now priorities and timeline
  - Trade-offs and "why we're saying no to X"
- [ ] Capture feedback and concerns
- [ ] Pressure test on feasibility, market fit, business impact
- [ ] Gather any adjustments needed

**Step 2: Cross-Functional Review** (1 week - Week 7):
- [ ] Engineering review
  - Technical feasibility
  - Effort estimates
  - Tech debt and infrastructure concerns
  - Sequencing from technical perspective
- [ ] Sales/CS review
  - Customer feedback and validation
  - Competitive positioning
  - What they can sell
- [ ] Design/UX review
  - Experience considerations
  - Scope and effort
  - User research validation

**Step 3: Incorporate Feedback** (Week 8):
- [ ] Document all feedback
- [ ] Determine what to incorporate vs. defer
- [ ] Make adjustments to roadmap
- [ ] Update documentation
- [ ] Confirm alignment with leadership

### Final Approval

**Approval Checklist**:
- [ ] CEO/Leadership signs off on strategy and themes
- [ ] Engineering confirms capacity and timeline feasibility
- [ ] Sales/CS confirms customer value and competitive positioning
- [ ] Design/UX confirms scope and technical execution plan
- [ ] Finance confirms budget allocation (if required)
- [ ] Legal/Compliance reviews for any compliance/security items

**Get Explicit Approval**:
- Email: "Please confirm approval of 2025 roadmap by [DATE]"
- Require explicit sign-off (not just silence = approval)
- Document who approved what and when

## Phase 5: Communication & Launch (Week 8+)

### All-Hands Presentation

**Goal**: Share strategy and roadmap with entire organization.

**Presentation** (30-45 min):
```
AGENDA

Opening (5 min)
- "We've been working on our 2025 strategy..."
- Why this process matters
- How we'll get to the roadmap

Performance Review (5 min)
- 2024 highlights and learnings
- What went well, what to improve

Strategic Direction (10 min)
- Market opportunity
- Customer problems we're solving
- Our competitive advantage

Strategic Themes (10 min)
- Why these 5 themes?
- How they connect
- What success looks like

Q1 Focus (5 min)
- What we're starting with
- Key initiatives
- What to expect in next 12 weeks

Trade-offs (3 min)
- What we're NOT doing and why
- How we'll handle requests

Questions & Feedback (7 min)
- Q&A
- Feedback form/process
```

### Stakeholder-Specific Briefings

- [ ] CEO to Board (if applicable): Business impact and strategic fit
- [ ] Sales team deep dive: Features to sell, GTM strategy, competitor positioning
- [ ] Engineering kickoff: Technical architecture, dependencies, sprint planning
- [ ] Customer success: How to talk to customers about roadmap
- [ ] Marketing: Launch plans and customer communication

### Publish & Share

- [ ] Post roadmap to:
  - Internal wiki or Notion
  - Slack #announcements
  - Email to team
  - (Optional) Customer-facing website if doing public roadmap
- [ ] Create FAQ doc anticipating common questions
- [ ] Schedule follow-up office hours/Q&A sessions

## Common Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge 1: "Why Didn't You Include [Feature] X?"

**Prevention**:
- Create a "future considerations" list showing things you evaluated but didn't prioritize
- Explain prioritization rationale clearly

**Response**:
- "We evaluated X and felt the themes we selected had higher customer impact. We'll revisit in Q2 planning."
- "X is a great idea. Can you tell us about 2-3 customers who need it? Help us prioritize."
- "X is in our Later category - something we'll explore in 2026."

### Challenge 2: Engineering Says Roadmap is Unrealistic

**Causes**:
- Didn't involve engineering early enough
- Overestimated capacity

**Solutions**:
- Go back to Phase 1 - get more engineering input
- Reduce scope - take some items from Now to Next
- Extend timeline - spread items across more quarters
- Add team - hire or reallocate resources

### Challenge 3: Market Changes Mid-Planning

**Approach**:
- Complete planning process for baseline roadmap
- Be explicit: "We're planning for X, but ready to adjust if Y happens"
- Monthly checkpoints to assess if adjustment needed
- Have contingency processes for mid-year changes

### Challenge 4: Team Feels Roadmap is Too Rigid

**Prevention**:
- Emphasize: "This is strategic direction, not a commitment to features"
- Frame Now as committed, Next as directional, Later as exploratory
- Show flexibility: "If we learn something changes this, we'll adjust"

**Fix**:
- Add 20-30% capacity for flexibility and learning
- Monthly adjustments allowed within theme
- Quarterly deep review for major changes

## Success Metrics for Your Roadmap

After 3-4 months, assess your roadmap using these metrics:

**Execution Metrics**:
- Did we ship 80%+ of Now items on time?
- Did we stay within 10% of effort estimates?
- Did team feel motivated and clear on direction?

**Organizational Metrics**:
- Do stakeholders understand why we prioritized these items?
- Did we maintain flexibility to adjust as we learned?
- Are teams aligned on strategy?

**Business Metrics**:
- Did Now initiatives move key metrics (retention, revenue, etc.)?
- Did roadmap help us make faster decisions?
- Did roadmap reduce product debates?

**Customer Metrics**:
- Did features address top customer problems?
- Did customer satisfaction increase?
- Did market reception match expectations?

---

## Next Steps: Ongoing Management

Once roadmap is approved and shared:

1. **Monthly Updates**
   - Progress on Now items
   - Metric updates
   - Adjustment proposals if needed

2. **Quarterly Reviews**
   - Deep assessment of progress
   - Learning from delivered work
   - Adjust Next/Later based on learning
   - Start planning for following quarter

3. **Annual Cycle**
   - September: Prepare for Q4 planning
   - October: Strategy workshop
   - November-December: Detailed roadmap planning
   - January: Launch and communicate

You now have a working product roadmap! 🎉
