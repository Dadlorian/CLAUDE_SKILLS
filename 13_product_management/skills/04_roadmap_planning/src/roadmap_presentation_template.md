# Roadmap Presentation Template: Presenting to Executives

A guide for presenting your roadmap effectively to executives, board members, and leadership. Includes presentation outline, talking points, and slides.

---

## Presentation Philosophy

Your goal is NOT to convince them your specific features are right. Your goal is to:
- **Show strategic thinking**: We've analyzed the market and made deliberate choices
- **Build confidence**: We know what we're doing and can execute
- **Get alignment**: We have leadership support and resource commitment
- **Enable advocacy**: They can explain the strategy to others

---

## Presentation Tips & Tricks

### Before the Presentation
1. **Practice with stakeholders first**: Get VP Eng and Sales alignment before executive presentation
2. **Pressure test your thinking**: Have someone play devil's advocate
3. **Prepare for tough questions**: Write down possible objections and your responses
4. **Know your numbers**: Be able to explain any metric on your slides
5. **Check equipment**: Test projector, audio, internet before
6. **Arrive early**: Technical issues happen; give yourself buffer

### During the Presentation
1. **Open Strong**: Hook them in first 2 minutes
2. **Tell a Story**: Don't just list features; show how strategy is connected
3. **Use Visuals**: Graphics > words. Make slides visually interesting
4. **Engage Audience**: Ask questions, pause for reaction, invite comments
5. **Show Confidence**: You know your material. Own it.
6. **Handle Questions Well**:
   - Listen fully before responding
   - Acknowledge valid points
   - Say "I don't know, let me get back to you" if needed
   - Don't get defensive
7. **Watch Your Pace**: Don't rush through. Speak slowly.
8. **Make Eye Contact**: Connect with the room, not the slides

### Handling Objections

**Objection**: "Why not do [Thing] instead?"

**Response Framework**:
1. Acknowledge it's a good idea: "That's a great point. We did evaluate [Thing]"
2. Explain trade-off: "We prioritized [Our Choice] because it has higher customer impact / revenue potential / strategic fit"
3. Show when you'll reconsider: "We'll revisit [Thing] in Q2 planning if market conditions change"

**Objection**: "This seems too ambitious. Can we really execute?"

**Response Framework**:
1. Show past execution: "In 2024, we shipped X major features on time"
2. Explain your planning: "We've done detailed capacity planning with engineering and have contingency built in"
3. Show risk mitigation: "We're doing [X] to de-risk the biggest unknowns"
4. Offer measurement: "We'll track weekly progress and adjust if we're going off track"

**Objection**: "Customers are asking for [Different Feature]. Shouldn't we do that?"

**Response Framework**:
1. Validate the request: "[Different Feature] is important, and we do hear customer demand for it"
2. Show analysis: "But when we looked at volume / revenue impact / strategic alignment, we found our current priorities drive higher value"
3. Show process: "We'll re-evaluate in Q2 planning if new data emerges"
4. Offer alternative: "In the meantime, here's what we can do to serve those customers..."

### For Product Teams (Internal)
Focus: Execution clarity, team motivation

- Later: Very high-level (strategic intent)

Focus: Customer value, competitive positioning
- Customer problems we're solving
- When features ship (sales can tell customers)
- What value proposition they enable
- How to position against competitors

### For Board/Investors
Focus: Business impact, market opportunity
- Market size and growth
- Our competitive position
- Revenue/growth impact
- Funding/resource needs

## Key Presentation Metrics

Measure your presentation effectiveness by these criteria:

✅ **Good Presentation If**:
- Audience asks clarifying questions (shows engagement)
- CEO/Leadership approves resource allocation
- Team leaves understanding "why"
- No major objections or concerns
- Follow-up questions are about execution, not strategy

❌ **Problem Presentation If**:
- Audience looks confused or checked out
- Leadership asks "why are we doing this?"
- Lots of objections to strategy
- No clear understanding of trade-offs
- Questions focus on doubting direction, not execution
