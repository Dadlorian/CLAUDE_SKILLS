# Marketing Content Writing Guide
## Elite Professional Standards for Digital Marketing Content

**Version**: 1.0
**Last Updated**: 2025-11-19
**Authority Level**: Tier-1 Professional Standards

---

## Purpose

This guide establishes professional writing standards for all digital marketing content including web copy, blog posts, email campaigns, social media posts, ad copy, landing pages, and other marketing materials. These standards are derived from industry-leading practices at organizations like HubSpot, Google, Shopify, and top content marketing agencies.

---

## Core Writing Principles

### 1. Audience-First Approach

**Primary Rule**: Every piece of content must solve a specific problem for a clearly defined audience.

**Framework**: Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)
- Understand what "job" the content needs to accomplish for the reader
- Identify the functional, emotional, and social dimensions
- Align content to the stage in the customer journey

**Application**:
```
❌ Poor: "Our software is feature-rich and powerful"
✅ Good: "Automate your invoicing in under 5 minutes, so you can focus on growing your business"

Why: The good example addresses a specific job (saving time on invoicing) and emotional benefit (focusing on growth).
```

**Audience Research Requirements**:
- Define primary and secondary personas
- Understand pain points, goals, and motivations
- Map content to buyer journey stage (awareness, consideration, decision)
- Use actual customer language from interviews, surveys, and reviews

### 2. Clarity Over Cleverness

**Primary Rule**: Clear, simple language always beats clever wordplay or jargon.

**Readability Standards**:
- Target Flesch Reading Ease score: 60-70 (8th-9th grade level for general audiences)
- Hemingway Editor score: Grade 9 or lower for most content
- Average sentence length: 15-20 words
- Paragraph length: 2-4 sentences for web content

**Simplification Techniques**:
```
❌ Poor: "Leverage our synergistic platform to actualize your strategic imperatives"
✅ Good: "Use our platform to achieve your business goals"

❌ Poor: "We utilize cutting-edge algorithmic methodologies"
✅ Good: "We use advanced algorithms" or "We use smart technology"
```

**When Technical Language Is Appropriate**:
- B2B audiences with specialized knowledge
- Technical documentation for technical users
- When using industry-standard terminology that aids precision
- Always define acronyms on first use

### 3. Benefit-Driven Messaging

**Primary Rule**: Lead with benefits, support with features.

**Formula**: Feature + Benefit + Proof

```
Feature: "AI-powered email segmentation"
Benefit: "Send more relevant emails that your subscribers actually want to read"
Proof: "Our customers see 3x higher open rates compared to blast emails"

Combined: "Our AI-powered email segmentation sends more relevant emails that your subscribers actually want to read—our customers see 3x higher open rates compared to blast emails."
```

**Benefit Hierarchy** (from Donald Miller's StoryBrand):
1. **Survival** (time, money, safety)
2. **Tribal Connection** (belonging, status, community)
3. **Self-Actualization** (personal growth, meaning)

**Examples by Type**:
```
Time Saving:
❌ "Advanced automation features"
✅ "Save 10 hours per week with automated workflows"

Money Saving:
❌ "Cost-effective solution"
✅ "Cut your customer acquisition costs by 40%"

Status/Belonging:
❌ "Join thousands of users"
✅ "Join forward-thinking marketers at companies like Stripe, Airbnb, and Shopify"
```

### 4. Specificity and Concreteness

**Primary Rule**: Use specific details, data, and examples instead of vague claims.

**Specificity Spectrum**:
```
Vague → Moderate → Specific → Ultra-Specific

"Fast results" → "Quick results" → "Results in 30 days" → "Generate your first 100 leads in 30 days"

"Many customers" → "Thousands of customers" → "12,000+ customers" → "12,487 customers across 43 countries"

"Easy to use" → "Intuitive interface" → "Set up in 5 minutes" → "Complete setup in 5 minutes without technical knowledge or coding"
```

**Data Usage**:
- Always cite sources for statistics
- Use recent data (prefer data from last 2 years)
- Round numbers appropriately ("47%" vs "46.8%")
- Provide context for numbers ("3x increase" vs "went from 100 to 300")

**Examples**:
```
❌ Poor: "We help businesses grow"
✅ Good: "We've helped 500+ B2B SaaS companies increase their trial-to-paid conversion rate by an average of 23%"

❌ Poor: "Industry-leading performance"
✅ Good: "Pages load in under 1 second, 3x faster than the industry average of 3.2 seconds (Source: Google Web Almanac 2024)"
```

## Content Type Standards

### Blog Posts

**Structure**:
1. **Headline** (60 characters or less for SEO)
   - Include primary keyword
   - Promise specific benefit or outcome
   - Use power words (proven, essential, ultimate, complete)
   - Formats that work: How-to, List, Question, Ultimate Guide

2. **Introduction** (100-150 words)
   - Hook: Start with a question, statistic, or pain point
   - Preview: What the reader will learn
   - Credibility: Why you're qualified to write this

3. **Body** (1,500-3,000 words for comprehensive posts)
   - Use H2 and H3 subheadings every 250-350 words
   - Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences)
   - Include examples, data, and visuals
   - Use bullet points and numbered lists
   - Add images, charts, or screenshots every 300-500 words

4. **Conclusion** (100-150 words)
   - Summarize key takeaways
   - Provide clear next step or call-to-action
   - Optional: Pose a question to encourage comments

**SEO Integration**:
- Primary keyword in: Title, URL, H1, first paragraph, conclusion, image alt text
- Secondary keywords: Throughout body naturally
- Internal links: 3-5 to related content
- External links: 2-3 to authoritative sources
- Meta description: 150-160 characters with primary keyword and call-to-action

**Tone**:
- Conversational but professional
- Use "you" and "your" to address reader directly
- Use "we" when speaking for your brand
- Avoid passive voice when possible
- Inject personality while maintaining credibility

**Example Blog Post Outline**:
```markdown
Title: How to Reduce Cart Abandonment by 35% (7 Proven Strategies)

Introduction:
- Hook: "69.8% of online shopping carts are abandoned before checkout—that's $18 billion in lost revenue annually."
- Preview: "In this guide, you'll learn 7 proven strategies to recover abandoned carts and increase your e-commerce revenue."
- Credibility: "These strategies are based on our analysis of 500+ e-commerce stores and backed by research from Baymard Institute."

Body:
H2: Why Customers Abandon Carts
- Unexpected costs
- Complex checkout process
- Security concerns
[Include stat: "48% abandon due to extra costs" - Baymard Institute]

H2: Strategy 1: Optimize Your Checkout Flow
- Single-page checkout
- Guest checkout option
- Progress indicators
[Include example: Shopify case study]

[... Strategies 2-7 ...]

H2: Measuring Your Cart Abandonment Rate
- Formula: (Completed purchases / Shopping carts created) × 100
- Benchmarks by industry
- Tools for tracking

Conclusion:
- "Reducing cart abandonment from 70% to 50% could double your revenue without increasing traffic."
- CTA: "Download our free checkout optimization checklist"
```

### Landing Pages

**Primary Objective**: Single, clear conversion goal

**Structure** (Above the Fold):
1. **Headline**: Value proposition in 10 words or less
2. **Subheadline**: Expand on benefit (20 words or less)
3. **Hero Image/Video**: Show product or outcome
4. **Primary CTA**: Clear, action-oriented button

**Body Elements**:
- **Benefits Section**: 3-5 key benefits with icons
- **Social Proof**: Testimonials, logos, statistics, case studies
- **Features Section**: How it works (3-5 steps or features)
- **Objection Handling**: Address common concerns (pricing, implementation, security)
- **Secondary CTA**: Repeat call-to-action
- **FAQ Section**: Address 5-7 common questions
- **Final CTA**: Last conversion opportunity

**Copy Principles**:
- Write for scanners: Use subheadings, bullets, short paragraphs
- Maintain scent: Messaging should match the ad/link that brought them here
- Remove navigation: Eliminate distractions from primary goal
- Use directional cues: Arrows, faces looking at CTA, whitespace

**CTA Best Practices**:
```
❌ Generic: "Submit", "Click Here", "Learn More"
✅ Specific: "Get My Free Guide", "Start My 14-Day Trial", "See Pricing Plans"

❌ Passive: "Get Started"
✅ Active First-Person: "Start My Free Trial", "Show Me How It Works"
```

**Length Guidelines**:
- Simple offer (free download): 500-800 words
- Mid-ticket purchase ($100-$1,000): 1,000-2,000 words
- High-ticket purchase ($1,000+): 2,000-5,000 words
- Complexity principle: More explanation needed = more copy needed

### Email Marketing

**Subject Lines** (40-50 characters for mobile):
- Personalization: Use first name when appropriate
- Clarity: Set clear expectation of content
- Urgency: When appropriate, create time-sensitivity
- Emoji: Use sparingly and when brand-appropriate (test performance)
- Avoid spam triggers: "Free", "Act Now", excessive punctuation!!!

**Examples**:
```
Welcome Email:
✅ "Welcome to [Brand], [First Name]! Here's your 15% discount"
✅ "Your [Product] journey starts here 🚀"

Newsletter:
✅ "5 growth tactics we're using this quarter"
✅ "Your weekly marketing roundup: AI, SEO, and attribution"

Promotional:
✅ "24 hours left: 30% off all plans"
✅ "Black Friday exclusive: Our biggest sale of the year"

Abandoned Cart:
✅ "Did you forget something?"
✅ "Your cart is waiting (+ 10% off to complete your order)"
```

**Email Body**:
- **Preheader Text**: 85-100 characters that complement subject line
- **Opening**: Personalized greeting, relevant to subject line
- **Body Copy**:
  - Keep paragraphs to 2-3 lines
  - Use subheadings and bullet points
  - Focus on ONE primary message or offer
  - Link to landing page for more details (don't put everything in email)
- **CTA**:
  - Use button, not just text link
  - Repeat CTA if email is long
  - Make CTA specific and action-oriented
- **Closing**:
  - Personal sign-off when appropriate
  - Include your role/title for B2B
  - Social proof or testimonial
- **Footer**:
  - Unsubscribe link (required by law)
  - Preference center link
  - Company address (CAN-SPAM requirement)

**Tone by Email Type**:
- **Welcome**: Friendly, enthusiastic, helpful
- **Newsletter**: Informative, valuable, conversational
- **Promotional**: Exciting, urgent (when appropriate), clear value
- **Transactional**: Clear, concise, helpful
- **Re-engagement**: Curious, value-focused, low pressure

**Accessibility**:
- Minimum font size: 14px for body, 16px+ preferred
- Sufficient color contrast (WCAG AA minimum)
- Alt text for all images
- Readable without images (text-only version)
- Avoid image-only emails

### Social Media Copy

**Platform-Specific Standards**:

**LinkedIn** (Professional, thought leadership):
- Length: 1,300-2,000 characters performs best for long-form
- First 2 lines are critical (before "see more")
- Hook with question, stat, or bold statement
- Use line breaks for readability
- Include 3-5 relevant hashtags
- CTA: Link in first comment or profile

**Instagram** (Visual-first, authentic):
- Caption length: 138-150 characters for optimal engagement (or go long: 2,000+ for storytelling)
- First line must hook attention
- Use line breaks and emoji for readability
- Include 11-15 relevant hashtags (in caption or first comment)
- End with call-to-action or question
- Tag relevant accounts

**Twitter/X** (Concise, real-time):
- Optimal length: 71-100 characters (even though limit is 280)
- Front-load the value
- Use 1-2 hashtags maximum
- Tag people sparingly and relevantly
- Thread for longer content (number tweets: "1/5")

**Facebook** (Community, engagement):
- Optimal length: 40-80 characters for organic reach
- Ask questions to drive comments
- Use native video when possible (performs better than links)
- Tag relevant pages
- Emoji can increase engagement

**TikTok** (Trend-driven, authentic):
- On-screen text: Large, readable, minimal
- Captions: 100 characters or less
- Use trending sounds and hashtags
- Hook in first 3 seconds
- Include CTA to follow, like, or comment

**General Social Principles**:
- Write like a human, not a brand (unless brand voice is formal)
- Ask questions to drive engagement
- Use storytelling when possible
- Include clear call-to-action
- Proofread before posting (no room for typos)
- Respond to comments promptly

### Ad Copy (Paid Search & Paid Social)

**Google Search Ads**:
- **Headline 1**: Include search keyword and main benefit (30 characters max)
- **Headline 2**: Additional benefit or unique value prop (30 characters max)
- **Headline 3**: Call-to-action or credibility signal (30 characters max)
- **Description 1**: Expand on benefit, address intent (90 characters max)
- **Description 2**: Include CTA and differentiation (90 characters max)

**Examples**:
```
Search Query: "email marketing software for small business"

Headline 1: Email Marketing for Small Business
Headline 2: Easy Automation & Templates
Headline 3: Free 14-Day Trial

Description 1: Send beautiful, targeted emails that drive results. No designer or developer needed. Trusted by 50,000+ small businesses.
Description 2: Get started in 5 minutes. Cancel anytime. See why we're rated 4.8/5 stars.
```

**Facebook/Instagram Ads**:
- **Primary Text**: 125 characters or less (before "see more")
- **Headline**: 40 characters or less
- **Description**: 30 characters or less (often hidden on mobile)
- Use emotional triggers and benefits
- Match copy to creative
- Test multiple variations

**Ad Copy Principles**:
- Match search intent or audience targeting
- Include keyword in headline when relevant (paid search)
- Lead with benefit, not feature
- Use numbers and specifics
- Create urgency when appropriate (limited time, limited supply)
- Address objections (free trial, money-back guarantee, no credit card)
- Use power words: free, proven, guaranteed, new, save, results
- A/B test everything: Headlines, descriptions, CTAs, value props

**Compliance**:
- Never make unsubstantiated claims
- Follow platform advertising policies
- Include necessary disclosures (terms apply, etc.)
- Avoid clickbait or misleading promises
- Respect trademark usage policies

## Voice and Tone

### Brand Voice (Consistent Across All Content)

**Voice Dimensions** (Choose position on each spectrum):

1. **Funny ←→ Serious**
   - Mailchimp: Funny
   - Goldman Sachs: Serious

2. **Formal ←→ Casual**
   - IBM: Formal
   - Notion: Casual

3. **Respectful ←→ Irreverent**
   - Salesforce: Respectful
   - Cards Against Humanity: Irreverent

4. **Enthusiastic ←→ Matter-of-fact**
   - HubSpot: Enthusiastic
   - Stripe: Matter-of-fact

**Defining Your Brand Voice**:
1. Identify 3-4 adjectives that describe your brand (e.g., "friendly, knowledgeable, empowering, direct")
2. For each adjective, provide do's and don'ts
3. Include examples of good vs. bad

**Example Voice Guidelines**:
```
Brand Voice: Friendly, Expert, Empowering

Friendly:
✅ Do: Use conversational language, contractions, and "you"
❌ Don't: Be overly casual or use slang that might not be understood

Expert:
✅ Do: Back claims with data, cite sources, demonstrate depth of knowledge
❌ Don't: Use jargon to sound smart or talk down to readers

Empowering:
✅ Do: Focus on what the reader can achieve, provide actionable advice
❌ Don't: Overwhelm with complexity or create dependency
```

### Tone (Varies by Context)

While voice stays consistent, tone adapts to:
- **Audience**: C-suite vs. individual contributor
- **Channel**: LinkedIn vs. TikTok
- **Stage**: Awareness vs. purchase decision
- **Situation**: Product launch vs. crisis response

**Tone Adaptation Examples**:
```
Same message, different tones:

Celebratory (product launch):
"We're thrilled to introduce our biggest update yet! 🎉"

Informative (documentation):
"This update includes three major improvements to workflow automation."

Urgent (limited-time offer):
"Don't miss out: 48 hours left to save 30%"

Apologetic (service disruption):
"We sincerely apologize for the downtime. Here's what happened and how we're making it right."
```

## Grammar and Mechanics

### Punctuation

**Commas**:
- Use Oxford comma: "We offer SEO, PPC, and social media services"
- After introductory phrases: "After analyzing 500 campaigns, we found..."
- Between independent clauses with coordinating conjunction: "We tested 10 variations, and version 5 performed best"

**Em Dashes**:
- Use for emphasis or interruption: "Our platform—used by 50,000+ marketers—makes email automation simple"
- No spaces around em dash

**Ellipses**:
- Use sparingly, primarily in quotes to show omission
- Avoid using for dramatic effect in professional content

**Exclamation Points**:
- Use sparingly (1 per piece of content maximum, or not at all)
- Appropriate for: Major announcements, celebratory moments
- Avoid in: Professional B2B content, formal communications

### Capitalization

**Title Case** (for headlines, subject lines):
- Capitalize: First word, last word, all major words
- Lowercase: Articles (a, an, the), conjunctions (and, but, or), prepositions under 5 letters (in, on, at, to, for)
- Always capitalize: Proper nouns, first word after colon

**Examples**:
```
✅ "How to Increase Email Open Rates by 35%"
✅ "The Ultimate Guide to Google Analytics 4"
✅ "10 Strategies for Scaling Your Content Marketing"
```

**Sentence Case** (for subheadings in some styles):
- Capitalize first word and proper nouns only
- Example: "Learn how to set up your first campaign"

**Product Names**:
- Follow official capitalization: Google Analytics (not Google analytics), iPhone (not IPhone)

### Numbers

**Spell Out vs. Numerals**:
- Spell out: One through nine
- Use numerals: 10 and above
- Exception: Use numerals for age, percentages, dimensions, money, time, statistics

**Examples**:
```
✅ "three strategies" but "12 tactics"
✅ "5%" not "five percent"
✅ "$50" not "fifty dollars"
✅ "30 days" not "thirty days"
✅ "3x increase" (not "three times increase")
```

**Large Numbers**:
- Use commas: 1,000 (not 1000)
- Spell out million, billion: "1.5 million users" (not "1,500,000 users")

**Percentages**:
- Always use numeral: "5%" (not "five percent")
- Use "percent" in body copy, "%" in headlines/tables for space

### Commonly Confused Words

**Effect vs. Affect**:
- Effect (noun): "The effect of personalization on open rates"
- Affect (verb): "How subject lines affect click-through rates"

**Its vs. It's**:
- Its (possessive): "The campaign exceeded its goals"
- It's (it is): "It's important to track your metrics"

**Then vs. Than**:
- Then (time): "First optimize, then test"
- Than (comparison): "Email performs better than social"

**Your vs. You're**:
- Your (possessive): "Improve your conversion rate"
- You're (you are): "You're going to see significant improvements"

**Compliment vs. Complement**:
- Compliment (praise): "The client complimented our work"
- Complement (complete): "SEO and PPC complement each other"

## Inclusive and Accessible Language

### Inclusive Writing Principles

**Gender-Neutral Language**:
```
❌ "Every marketer should track his metrics"
✅ "Every marketer should track their metrics"
✅ "Marketers should track their metrics"
```

**Avoid Assumptions**:
```
❌ "Moms looking for work-life balance"
✅ "Parents looking for work-life balance"
✅ "Professionals looking for work-life balance"
```

**Person-First Language** (especially for accessibility content):
```
❌ "Blind users"
✅ "Users who are blind" or "users with visual impairments"
```

**Cultural Sensitivity**:
- Avoid idioms that don't translate: "knock it out of the park", "circle back"
- Use international date formats: "November 19, 2024" or "19 November 2024" (not "11/19/24")
- Avoid cultural references that might not be universal

### Accessible Writing

**Plain Language**:
- Prefer common words over fancy alternatives
- "use" not "utilize"
- "help" not "facilitate"
- "buy" not "purchase" or "procure"

**Active Voice**:
```
❌ Passive: "The campaign was launched by our team"
✅ Active: "Our team launched the campaign"
```

**Clear Structure**:
- Use descriptive headings that make sense out of context
- Front-load important information in paragraphs
- Use bulleted lists for 3+ related items
- Keep sentences under 25 words when possible

**Link Text**:
```
❌ "Click here for more information"
✅ "Read our complete guide to email marketing"
```

**Image Alt Text**:
- Describe content and function
- Keep under 125 characters
- Don't start with "image of" (screen readers announce it's an image)
```
✅ "Bar chart showing 35% increase in conversions after implementing A/B testing"
```

## Editing and Proofreading

### Self-Editing Checklist

**First Pass** (Big Picture):
- [ ] Does this achieve the primary objective?
- [ ] Is the target audience clear and appropriate?
- [ ] Is the structure logical and easy to follow?
- [ ] Are there any gaps in logic or missing information?
- [ ] Is the call-to-action clear and compelling?

**Second Pass** (Paragraph Level):
- [ ] Can any paragraphs be shortened or split?
- [ ] Do paragraphs flow logically from one to the next?
- [ ] Are there unnecessary repetitions?
- [ ] Is each paragraph focused on one main idea?

**Third Pass** (Sentence Level):
- [ ] Can any sentences be shortened or simplified?
- [ ] Is the voice mostly active rather than passive?
- [ ] Are there any awkward phrasings?
- [ ] Do sentences vary in length and structure?

**Fourth Pass** (Word Level):
- [ ] Replace weak verbs (is, are, was, were) with strong action verbs
- [ ] Remove unnecessary qualifiers (very, really, quite)
- [ ] Replace vague words with specific ones
- [ ] Check for correct word usage (affect/effect, its/it's)

**Final Pass** (Proofreading):
- [ ] Spell check (but don't rely on it exclusively)
- [ ] Check all proper nouns and brand names
- [ ] Verify all numbers and statistics
- [ ] Check all links work correctly
- [ ] Read aloud to catch awkward phrasing

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Wordiness**:
```
❌ "In order to" → ✅ "To"
❌ "Due to the fact that" → ✅ "Because"
❌ "At this point in time" → ✅ "Now"
❌ "In the event that" → ✅ "If"
```

**Redundancies**:
```
❌ "Free gift" → ✅ "Gift"
❌ "Past history" → ✅ "History"
❌ "Future plans" → ✅ "Plans"
❌ "End result" → ✅ "Result"
```

**Weak Qualifiers**:
```
❌ "Very important" → ✅ "Critical" or "Essential"
❌ "Really good" → ✅ "Excellent" or specific benefit
❌ "Quite helpful" → ✅ "Helpful" or specific outcome
```

## SEO Writing Integration

### Keyword Integration

**Keyword Placement** (Priority Order):
1. Page title / H1
2. URL slug
3. First paragraph (within first 100 words)
4. Subheadings (H2, H3)
5. Image alt text
6. Meta description
7. Throughout body (natural placement)

**Keyword Density**:
- Target: 1-2% keyword density (not a strict rule)
- Focus on natural writing first
- Use semantic variations and related terms
- Avoid keyword stuffing (unnatural repetition)

**Natural Integration**:
```
❌ Keyword stuffing:
"Our email marketing software is the best email marketing software for email marketing campaigns. If you need email marketing software, our email marketing software is perfect."

✅ Natural integration:
"Our email marketing software makes it easy to create, send, and track campaigns. With advanced automation and segmentation, you can deliver personalized messages that drive results."
```

### Semantic SEO

**Topic Clusters**:
- Create pillar content (comprehensive guide)
- Develop cluster content (specific subtopics)
- Link clusters to pillar and pillar to clusters

**Related Terms** (Use naturally):
- Synonyms: "digital marketing" ↔ "online marketing"
- Related concepts: "SEO" + "organic traffic" + "search rankings"
- LSI keywords: Terms commonly associated with topic

**Search Intent Alignment**:
- **Informational**: "how to", "what is", "guide to"
  - Provide comprehensive answers
- **Navigational**: "[brand name]", "[product name]"
  - Direct to specific page
- **Transactional**: "buy", "pricing", "subscribe"
  - Clear path to conversion
- **Commercial Investigation**: "best", "vs", "review"
  - Comparison and evaluation content

## Quality Assurance

### Pre-Publish Checklist

**Content Quality**:
- [ ] Spelling and grammar checked
- [ ] Facts and statistics verified and sourced
- [ ] All links tested and working
- [ ] Images optimized and have alt text
- [ ] Readability score meets target (Hemingway, Flesch)
- [ ] Content adds genuine value to reader

**SEO**:
- [ ] Primary keyword in title, URL, H1, first paragraph
- [ ] Meta description written (150-160 characters)
- [ ] Header tags properly structured (H1 → H2 → H3)
- [ ] Internal links to 3-5 relevant pages
- [ ] External links to authoritative sources
- [ ] Images have descriptive file names and alt text

**Brand Compliance**:
- [ ] Voice and tone consistent with brand guidelines
- [ ] Messaging aligns with brand positioning
- [ ] Legal disclaimers included if required
- [ ] Competitor mentions appropriate and factual

**Conversion Optimization**:
- [ ] Clear call-to-action present
- [ ] CTA above the fold (for landing pages)
- [ ] Value proposition clear in first paragraph
- [ ] No broken conversion paths

**Accessibility**:
- [ ] Sufficient color contrast (WCAG AA minimum)
- [ ] Heading structure logical
- [ ] Links descriptive (not "click here")
- [ ] Images have alt text
- [ ] Readable font size (14px minimum)
