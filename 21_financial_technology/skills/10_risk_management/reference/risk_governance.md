# Risk Governance Framework

## Overview
Risk governance establishes the structures, processes, and systems for identifying, measuring, and managing risks. It includes roles/responsibilities, policies, oversight mechanisms, and escalation procedures.

## Governance Structure

### Board Level

#### Board of Directors
**Responsibility**: Ultimate accountability for risk management and capital adequacy.

**Key Functions**:
1. Approve risk appetite and strategy
2. Oversee senior management
3. Monitor capital and liquidity adequacy
4. Approve policies and limits
5. Review material losses and incidents
6. Ensure compliance with regulations

**Risk Committee**:
- Chartered board committee (all large banks)
- Reviews risk reports monthly/quarterly
- Approves risk policies and frameworks
- Monitors capital, stress tests, KRIs
- Escalates major risks/issues
- Recommends capital distribution decisions

#### Board Risk Committee Charter
```
Composition:
- Independent directors only (required)
- Mix of financial expertise
- Size: 3-5 members
- Chair: Often independent director

Meetings:
- Minimum quarterly (often monthly)
- Full board present or available
- Review comprehensive risk reports
- Access to risk management team

Responsibilities:
1. Assess risk appetite and strategy
2. Oversee operational risk management
3. Review liquidity and capital adequacy
4. Evaluate regulatory compliance
5. Oversee risk models and assumptions
6. Review succession plans
```

### Management Level

#### Chief Risk Officer (CRO)
**Position**: Senior executive reporting to CEO/Board

**Key Responsibilities**:
1. Design and implement risk management framework
2. Oversee all risk identification and measurement
3. Monitor risk limits and escalate breaches
4. Recommend capital levels
5. Report to board and regulators
6. Manage risk professionals
7. Ensure independence from business units

**Authority**:
- Can override business units on risk decisions
- Cannot be removed by business unit heads
- Reports independently to board
- Participates in capital allocation

**Conflicts**:
- May oppose profitable but risky strategies
- Business units may resent restrictions
- CRO job is to protect institution, not maximize profits

#### Risk Management Committee
**Chair**: Chief Risk Officer or CEO

**Members**:
- CRO (chair)
- Heads of credit risk, market risk, operational risk, liquidity risk
- Chief Financial Officer
- Treasurer
- Business unit heads (as needed)
- Internal Audit representative

**Frequency**: Weekly or as-needed

**Key Responsibilities**:
1. Monitor current risk exposures vs. limits
2. Review and approve new products
3. Assess concentration risks
4. Escalate limit breaches
5. Recommend control improvements
6. Review remediation of audit findings

#### Credit Committee
**Members**:
- Chief Risk Officer
- Credit Risk Manager
- Business Line Heads
- Regional credit managers

**Meetings**:
- Weekly for material credits
- Monthly for portfolio reviews

**Authorities**:
```
Individual Credit Decision Authority:

Amount        Authority
< $1M         Branch Manager
$1M-$5M       Regional Credit Officer
$5M-$25M      Group Credit Officer
$25M-$100M    Credit Committee (with CRO)
$100M-$500M   Credit Committee + CFO
> $500M       Board Credit Committee
```

### Risk Management Infrastructure

#### Credit Risk Management
- **Head of Credit Risk**: Reports to CRO
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Approve all credit decisions above thresholds
  - Monitor PD/LGD/EAD models
  - Track portfolio concentration
  - Manage credit limits
  - Escalate problem credits

#### Market Risk Management
- **Head of Market Risk**: Reports to CRO
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Monitor VaR, stress tests, Greeks
  - Approve trading strategies
  - Enforce position limits
  - Real-time risk monitoring
  - Independent of trading

#### Operational Risk Management
- **Head of Operational Risk**: Reports to CRO
- **Responsibilities**:
  - RCSA (Risk & Control Self-Assessment)
  - Loss event collection
  - KRI (Key Risk Indicator) monitoring
  - Model risk management
  - Operational risk capital allocation

#### Liquidity Risk Management
- **Treasurer/Head of Liquidity**: May report to CFO or CRO
- **Responsibilities**:
  - Monitor LCR, NSFR ratios
  - Manage funding strategy
  - Ensure liquidity buffers
  - Contingency funding plan
  - Stress testing

## Risk Policies

### Risk Appetite & Strategy
**Definition**: Maximum acceptable level and types of risk.

```
Risk Appetite Statement Example:

"We target a Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio of 12-14%,
above the regulatory minimum of 10.5%, to provide a buffer
for stress scenarios. We limit concentration in any single
counterparty to 5% of capital. We will not intentionally
hold positions expecting significant losses, and we maintain
liquidity to survive 30-day stressed outflow scenario."

Components:
1. Capital targets (ratios, headroom)
2. Limit framework (concentration, product)
3. Risk tolerance by type (credit, market, operational)
4. Earning volatility tolerance
5. Stress resilience (ability to survive stress)
```

### Credit Risk Policy
```
1. Lending Standards
   - Minimum underwriting requirements
   - Financial covenants
   - Documentation standards
   - Collateral requirements by product

2. Credit Approval Process
   - Authority levels by exposure
   - Required documentation
   - Approval timeline
   - Post-approval monitoring

3. Concentration Limits
   - Largest single exposure: 5% of capital
   - Top 10 exposures: 25% of capital
   - Top 20 exposures: 40% of capital
   - Industry concentrations: 15% each
   - Geographic concentrations: 20% per country

4. Rating System
   - Internal rating scale (1-20)
   - Rating definition and mapping
   - Rating review frequency
   - Downgrade requirements

5. Provisioning
   - Loan loss reserve methodology
   - IFRS 9 stage definitions
   - Reserve adequacy targets
   - Provision release policies
```

### Market Risk Policy
```
1. Market Risk Framework
   - VaR calculation methodology (historical, parametric, Monte Carlo)
   - Confidence level (95% or 99%)
   - Time horizon (1-day, 10-day)
   - Backtesting frequency

2. Trading Limits
   - Desk VaR limits
   - Product type limits
   - Greeks limits (delta, gamma, vega)
   - Position limits
   - Loss limits (daily, monthly)

3. Limit Monitoring
   - Real-time monitoring
   - Daily reporting
   - Breach escalation procedure
   - Remediation requirements

4. Stress Testing
   - Frequency (daily, weekly, monthly)
   - Scenarios (historical, hypothetical)
   - Escalation if loss > threshold

5. Model Governance
   - VaR model approval by risk committee
   - Quarterly validation
   - Backtesting results reporting
   - Model change procedures
```

### Operational Risk Policy
```
1. Risk & Control Assessment (RCSA)
   - Annual assessment by all business units
   - Risk rating (frequency × severity)
   - Control effectiveness evaluation
   - Residual risk rating
   - Remediation plans for high risks

2. Loss Event Collection
   - Threshold for recording: $5,000 (or operational impact)
   - Root cause analysis required
   - Preventive actions identified
   - Quarterly loss reporting

3. Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)
   - Monthly monitoring of KRIs
   - Traffic light thresholds (green/yellow/red)
   - Escalation if thresholds breached
   - Management action plans

4. Operational Risk Capital
   - Annual op risk capital calculation
   - Standardized approach: ~15% of gross income
   - Reviewed by risk committee
   - Included in total capital requirement

5. Third-Party Risk
   - Vendor assessment and due diligence
   - Vendor risk rating
   - Monitoring and periodic review
   - Escalation of critical vendors
```

### Liquidity Risk Policy
```
1. Liquidity Target
   - LCR > 100% (regulatory minimum)
   - Target LCR: 120-130%
   - NSFR > 100% (regulatory minimum)
   - Days of cash: 30-60 days

2. Funding Strategy
   - Diversified funding sources
   - Diversification targets by source
   - Funding term profile
   - Market access plans

3. Liquidity Buffer
   - High-quality liquid assets target
   - Buffer adequacy for stress scenario
   - Assets eligible as collateral

4. Contingency Funding Plan (CFP)
   - Triggers for plan activation
   - Response actions by severity
   - Roles and responsibilities
   - Communication protocols

5. Stress Testing
   - Monthly stress scenarios
   - Firm-specific stress scenario
   - System-wide stress scenario
   - Reverse stress testing
```

## Limit Framework

### Limit Setting
```
Process:
1. Determine Risk Appetite (Board)
2. Calculate Available Risk Budget (Risk Committee)
3. Allocate by Risk Type (credit, market, op)
4. Allocate by Business Unit
5. Establish Position Limits
6. Communicate to Business
7. Monitor Daily
8. Report Weekly
```

### Example Limit Hierarchy
```
Bank Total Capital: $1,000M

Capital Allocation:
- Credit Risk: $600M (60%)
- Market Risk: $200M (20%)
- Operational Risk: $150M (15%)
- Reserve: $50M (5%)

Credit Risk Allocation:
- Large Corporates: $300M
- Mid-Market: $150M
- Small Business: $100M
- Mortgages: $50M

Large Corporates Allocation:
- Single name: $30M max (5% of capital)
- Sector: $60M per sector max
- Sub-allocations by geography

Market Risk Allocation:
- Interest Rate: $100M (measured as DV01)
- FX Risk: $50M (delta)
- Equity Risk: $50M

Trading Desk Allocation:
- Rates Desk: $60M VaR limit
- Credit Desk: $40M VaR limit
- Equity Desk: $30M VaR limit
```

## Escalation Procedures

### Limit Breach Escalation
```
Level 1 - Yellow Alert (50% of limit):
- Trading desk notified
- Risk officer monitoring
- No immediate action required
- Daily monitoring intensified

Level 2 - Orange Alert (80% of limit):
- Risk committee notified
- Discuss position reduction
- Monitor closely
- May request remediation plan

Level 3 - Red Alert (Limit Exceeded):
- Immediate escalation to CRO
- Remediation required within trading day
- Senior management aware
- Risk committee briefed

Level 4 - Crisis (Major Breach):
- CEO/CFO notified
- Board Risk Committee briefed
- Potential enforcement action
- Regulatory notification
```

### Material Loss Escalation
```
Loss < $100k:
- Business unit manager notified
- Risk officer aware
- Documented in loss database

Loss $100k-$1M:
- CRO notified
- Risk Committee briefed
- Root cause analysis
- Preventive actions

Loss $1M-$10M:
- CEO/CFO notified
- Full board Risk Committee review
- Investigation and remediation plan
- Regulatory notification likely

Loss > $10M:
- Board notification
- CEO/CFO/CRO involved in response
- Regulatory/external parties notified
- Public disclosure likely
- Systemic review of controls
```

## Model Risk Management

### Model Governance Framework

```
1. Model Development
   - Clear business objective
   - Methodology documented
   - Development team qualifications
   - Development sample adequate

2. Model Validation
   - Independent validation team
   - Testing against development/holdout data
   - Backtesting and sensitivity analysis
   - Documented validation report

3. Approval
   - Model approved by Risk Committee
   - Condition on implementation testing
   - Documentation complete
   - Governance established

4. Implementation
   - System implementation tested
   - Output quality verified
   - User training completed
   - Live monitoring setup

5. Monitoring
   - Monthly performance review
   - Quarterly backtesting
   - Annual revalidation
   - Change tracking

6. Update/Refresh
   - Data refresh (annual minimum)
   - Model retrain if performance degrades
   - Validation for changes
   - Approval for updates

7. Retirement
   - Model superseded by better model
   - Key assumptions no longer valid
   - Regulatory requirements changed
   - Documented retirement
```

## Risk Culture & Training

### Risk Culture
- Risk management is everyone's responsibility
- Risks should be reported promptly
- Whistleblower channels for concerns
- Senior management demonstrates commitment
- Risk policies actively enforced

### Training Requirements
```
All Employees:
- Annual AML/compliance training
- Know Your Customer (KYC) basics
- Anti-bribery and corruption
- Data protection/privacy

Risk Professionals:
- Advanced risk modeling
- Regulatory requirements
- Risk calculation methodologies
- Industry updates

Business Managers:
- Limit framework
- Approval processes
- Risk reporting requirements
- Escalation procedures

Board Members:
- Risk governance overview
- Key risk metrics
- Regulatory landscape
- Industry practices
```

## Regulatory Interaction

### Regulatory Examinations
```
Annual Regulatory Examination:
- Capital adequacy review
- Risk management assessment
- Model validation review
- Compliance testing
- Loss review

Findings Classification:
- Findings: Regulatory concerns
- Observations: Best practice suggestions
- Violations: Regulatory breaches

Response Requirements:
- Written response within 30 days
- Remediation plan with timeline
- Progress updates
- Evidence of remediation
```

### Regulatory Capital Reporting
```
Quarterly Reporting:
- Capital ratios (CET1, Tier 1, Total)
- Risk-weighted assets by category
- Leverage ratio
- Liquidity ratios (LCR, NSFR)
- Large exposures

Annual Reporting:
- Full regulatory capital documentation
- Stress test results (CCAR, EBA, etc.)
- Credit risk by geography, industry
- Derivatives positions
- Full governance documentation

Timelines:
- Quarterly: 30-45 days after quarter end
- Annual: 60-90 days after year end
- Meeting regulatory deadlines critical
```
