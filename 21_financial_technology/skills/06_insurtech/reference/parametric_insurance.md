# Parametric Insurance

## Parametric Insurance Overview

Parametric insurance (also called index insurance or trigger-based insurance) is a type of insurance where claims are paid based on a pre-defined trigger event rather than actual incurred losses. Once the trigger is met, predetermined payout amounts are automatically disbursed without requiring proof of actual loss.

## Key Differences from Traditional Insurance

### Traditional Insurance
- **Loss-Basis**: Pay based on actual losses incurred
- **Assessment**: Requires assessment/investigation
- **Proof Required**: Policyholder must prove loss
- **Time to Settlement**: Can take weeks/months
- **Variability**: Payout equals actual loss

### Parametric Insurance
- **Trigger-Basis**: Pay based on pre-defined index/parameter
- **Automatic**: No assessment required
- **No Proof**: Automatic payout when trigger met
- **Fast Settlement**: Immediate or near-immediate payment
- **Fixed Payout**: Same amount for same trigger level

## Parametric Insurance Structure

### Core Components

**1. Trigger Definition**
- **Parameter**: Specific measurable metric
- **Threshold**: Value that activates payout
- **Measurement**: How parameter is measured
- **Data Source**: Where measurement comes from
- **Verification**: How trigger is verified

**2. Payout Schedule**
```
Trigger Level → Payout Amount
Wind Speed 80mph → $10,000
Wind Speed 90mph → $25,000
Wind Speed 100mph → $50,000
```

**3. Coverage Period**
- **Effective Date**: When coverage starts
- **Expiration Date**: When coverage ends
- **Event Window**: Time when triggerable events occur

### Example: Hurricane Parametric Insurance
**Trigger**: Hurricane makes landfall within 100 miles with wind speed ≥ 80mph
**Data Source**: National Hurricane Center official reports
**Payout Schedule**:
- Wind Speed 80-89mph: $100,000
- Wind Speed 90-99mph: $250,000
- Wind Speed 100-109mph: $500,000
- Wind Speed 110+mph: $1,000,000

**Payout**: Automatic within 48 hours of NHC confirmation

## Parametric Insurance Applications

### Catastrophe Insurance
**Natural Disasters**:
- **Hurricane**: Wind speed, rainfall
- **Earthquake**: Magnitude, location
- **Flood**: Water level, precipitation
- **Tornado**: Wind speed, path
- **Drought**: Rainfall amount

**Benefits**:
- Quick capital recovery after disaster
- Predictable payouts
- Lower claims adjustment costs

### Agricultural Insurance
**Crop Insurance**:
- **Drought**: Rainfall below threshold
- **Excessive Moisture**: Excessive rainfall
- **Frost**: Temperature below threshold
- **Pest**: Infestation index

**Example**:
- Trigger: Monthly rainfall < 2 inches
- Payout: $1,000 per acre
- Benefit: Quick payout enables replanting

### Weather Insurance
**Business Interruption**:
- **Temperature Extremes**: Too hot/cold affecting sales
- **Rainfall**: Excessive rain/drought
- **Wind**: High wind events
- **Snowfall**: Heavy snow events

**Example**:
- Restaurant Loss-of-Revenue Insurance
- Trigger: Temperature > 95°F for 10 consecutive days
- Payout: $5,000 per day above threshold
- Benefit: Compensate for reduced outdoor dining

### Event Insurance
**Event Cancellation**:
- **Weather**: Extreme weather preventing event
- **Participation**: Attendance threshold not met
- **Liability**: Third-party injury event
- **Revenue**: Revenue target not met

**Example**:
- Festival Rain Insurance
- Trigger: Rainfall > 3 inches during event
- Payout: $10,000 per inch above threshold
- Benefit: Quick payout to cover losses

### Travel Insurance
**Trip Disruption**:
- **Weather**: Flight cancellation due to weather
- **Delay**: Flight delayed > 4 hours
- **Airline Failure**: Airline bankruptcy
- **Terrorism**: Terrorist attack in destination

### Business Interruption
**Extended Business Interruption**:
- **Supply Chain**: Supplier disruption
- **Key Person**: Key executive death/disability
- **Pandemic**: Pandemic-related closure
- **Infrastructure**: Power outage duration

## Challenges and Limitations

### Basis Risk
**Definition**: Risk that insurance payout doesn't match actual loss

**Example**:
- Trigger: Wind speed > 80mph
- Payout: $100,000
- Actual Loss: $200,000
- Gap: $100,000 uncompensated loss

**Management**:
- Multiple triggers: Layered triggers for different scenarios
- Combination triggers: Multiple parameters
- Percentage-based: Payout scales with magnitude
- Deductible: Policyholder covers small gaps

### Coverage Gaps
- **Localized Impact**: Parameter applies to region, not specific location
- **Threshold**: Events just below threshold uncompensated
- **Indirect Loss**: Only direct loss compensated
- **Multiple Events**: Interaction of multiple events

### Moral Hazard Considerations
- **Reduced Prevention**: No incentive to mitigate loss
- **Behavior Changes**: Policyholder may take more risk
- **Fraud Risk**: Claim fraudulent trigger activation
- **Mitigation**: Tie payouts to actual underlying exposure

## Pricing Parametric Products

### Pricing Factors
- **Trigger Frequency**: Likelihood of trigger event
- **Payout Amount**: Fixed payout size
- **Coverage Period**: Duration of coverage
- **Basis Risk**: Correlation between trigger and loss
- **Expense Load**: Administrative costs

**Premium Formula**:
```
Premium = (Probability of Trigger × Payout Amount) + Expenses + Profit
```

**Example**:
```
Trigger Probability: 10% (1 in 10 chance annually)
Payout Amount: $100,000
Pure Premium: 10% × $100,000 = $10,000
Expense Load: 20%
Profit Margin: 10%
Loaded Premium: $10,000 / (1 - 0.20 - 0.10) = $14,286
```

### Actuarial Considerations
- **Historical Data**: Use historical trigger data
- **Climate Trends**: Adjust for changing climate
- **Geographic Variation**: Adjust by location
- **Dependency**: Correlation with other events
- **Tail Risk**: Extreme event likelihood
