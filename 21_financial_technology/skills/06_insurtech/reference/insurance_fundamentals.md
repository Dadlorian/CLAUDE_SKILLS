# Insurance Fundamentals

## Core Insurance Principles

### The Insurance Contract
An insurance contract (policy) is a legally binding agreement where:
- **Insurer**: Agrees to indemnify the policyholder against specified losses
- **Policyholder**: Pays a premium for the transfer of risk
- **Insurable Interest**: The policyholder must have a financial interest in the subject matter

### Key Principles

#### 1. Principle of Indemnity
- The insurer compensates the policyholder for actual losses incurred, no more, no less
- Prevents profit from misfortune
- Exception: Life insurance can be for an agreed amount

#### 2. Principle of Utmost Good Faith (Uberrima Fides)
- Both parties must act in complete honesty
- Full disclosure of material facts required
- Non-disclosure or misrepresentation can void the contract

#### 3. Principle of Insurable Interest
- Policyholder must stand to suffer financial loss from the insured event
- Must exist at time of loss
- Prevents wagering and moral hazard

#### 4. Principle of Proximate Cause
- The cause of loss must be an insured peril
- Must be the direct and continuous cause of loss
- Breaking the chain of causation can exclude coverage

#### 5. Principle of Subrogation
- Insurer steps into the shoes of the policyholder to recover losses
- Can pursue third parties responsible for the loss
- Creates right of recovery after indemnification

### Types of Insurance

#### Personal Insurance
- **Auto Insurance**: Vehicle damage, liability, and medical payments
- **Home Insurance**: Dwelling coverage, personal property, liability
- **Life Insurance**: Term, whole life, universal life, variable life
- **Health Insurance**: Medical, dental, vision coverage
- **Travel Insurance**: Trip cancellation, medical, baggage

#### Commercial Insurance
- **General Liability**: Bodily injury, property damage, advertising
- **Workers' Compensation**: Employee injury and illness coverage
- **Commercial Property**: Buildings, equipment, inventory
- **Professional Liability**: Errors and omissions, malpractice
- **Cyber Liability**: Data breach, cyber extortion, system failure

#### Specialty Insurance
- **Marine Insurance**: Ships, cargo, marine liability
- **Aviation Insurance**: Aircraft hull, liability, passengers
- **Fine Art Insurance**: High-value art and collectibles
- **Event Insurance**: Cancellation, liability for events
- **Directors & Officers**: Executive liability coverage

## Insurance Markets and Segments

### Market Structure
- **Primary Insurance Market**: Direct insurance between insurer and policyholder
- **Reinsurance Market**: Risk transfer between insurers and reinsurers
- **Insurance Brokers/Agents**: Licensed intermediaries
- **InsurTech Distributors**: Digital platforms and aggregators

### Geographic Markets
- **Domestic Markets**: Local/national insurance operations
- **International Markets**: Cross-border insurance with different regulations
- **Lloyd's of London Market**: Subscription market for specialty risks
- **Captive Insurance**: Company-owned insurers for self-insurance

## Insurance Economics

### Premium Components
- **Pure Premium**: Expected cost of losses and claims
- **Loading/Margin**: Administrative costs, profit, contingency
- **Taxes and Fees**: Insurance premium taxes, licensing fees
- **Commission**: Agent/broker compensation

```
Total Premium = Pure Premium + Loading + Taxes + Commission
```

### Loss Ratios and Profitability
- **Loss Ratio**: Incurred losses / earned premiums (target varies by line)
- **Expense Ratio**: Operating expenses / earned premiums
- **Combined Ratio**: (Losses + Expenses) / Premiums
  - >100% = underwriting loss
  - <100% = underwriting profit
- **Investment Income**: Critical for profitability in long-tail lines

### Risk Assessment

#### Underwriting Factors
- **Risk Characteristics**: Properties of the subject matter
- **Peril Exposure**: Likelihood of insured events
- **Hazards**: Conditions that increase loss probability
  - Physical hazard: Property condition (old wiring)
  - Moral hazard: Policyholder behavior (intent to commit fraud)
  - Morale hazard: Lack of care (not maintaining property)

#### Rating Approaches
- **Class Rating**: Rate based on broad categories
- **Schedule Rating**: Adjustments for specific risk factors
- **Experience Rating**: Historical loss experience
- **Merit Rating**: Discounts for loss prevention

## Policy Mechanics

### Policy Lifecycle
1. **Quote/Underwriting**: Risk assessment and pricing
2. **Issuance**: Policy creation and delivery
3. **In-Force Period**: Active coverage period
4. **Modification**: Endorsements, changes, cancellations
5. **Renewal**: Policy continuation with updated terms
6. **Claim**: Loss notification and resolution
7. **Expiration**: Policy termination

### Policy Components
- **Declarations Page**: Policyholder info, coverages, limits, premium
- **Insuring Agreement**: What is covered and conditions
- **Exclusions**: What is NOT covered
- **Conditions**: Requirements and obligations
- **Endorsements**: Modifications to standard policy

### Coverage Limits and Deductibles
- **Policy Limit**: Maximum amount insurer will pay
- **Deductible**: Amount policyholder pays before insurer coverage
- **Coinsurance**: Policyholder pays percentage of loss above deductible
- **Coverage Limit**: Maximum for specific coverage type

## Claims Process Overview

### Claim Lifecycle
1. **Notice**: Policyholder reports loss to insurer
2. **Investigation**: Verify coverage, assess damages
3. **Adjustment**: Determine amount of indemnification
4. **Approval/Denial**: Coverage decision
5. **Settlement**: Payment to policyholder or claimant
6. **Closure**: Record archival and analytics

### Claims Processing Functions
- **First Notice of Loss (FNOL)**: Initial claim intake
- **Claims Triage**: Route to appropriate handler
- **Coverage Analysis**: Determine if loss is covered
- **Loss Assessment**: Estimate claim value
- **Negotiation**: Resolve disputes with claimants
- **Closure**: Final settlement and documentation

## Insurance Distribution Channels

### Traditional Channels
- **Direct**: Insurer sells directly to customers
- **Agents**: Captive agents representing single insurer
- **Brokers**: Independent agents representing multiple insurers
- **Bancassurance**: Insurance sold through banks

### Digital Channels
- **Online Direct**: Insurer websites for self-service
- **Aggregators**: Compare and purchase policies online
- **Mobile Apps**: Download and manage policies
- **API Distribution**: B2B policy delivery and integration
- **Embedded Insurance**: Insurance integrated into other platforms

## Regulatory Framework

### Key Regulations
- **Insurance Code**: State-level insurance laws and regulations
- **Rate Approval**: Premium rates reviewed and approved by regulators
- **Solvency Requirements**: Capital and reserve mandates
- **Claims Handling**: Standards for prompt, fair handling
- **Consumer Protection**: Unfair practice restrictions

### Licensing and Compliance
- **Insurance License**: Required to transact insurance
- **Continuing Education**: Ongoing training requirements
- **Examination**: Demonstrated knowledge of insurance law
- **Appointment**: Authorization to represent insurer
- **Compliance Audits**: Regular regulatory examinations
