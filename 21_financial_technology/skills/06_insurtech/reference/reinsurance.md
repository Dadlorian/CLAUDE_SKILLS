# Reinsurance

## Reinsurance Overview

Reinsurance is insurance for insurers. It's the transfer of risk from one insurer (ceding company) to another insurer (reinsurer) for a premium. Reinsurance helps insurers manage catastrophic risk, limit losses, and improve financial stability.

## Types of Reinsurance

### Facultative Reinsurance
**Definition**: Case-by-case reinsurance for individual risks or large claims

**Characteristics**:
- Each risk is separately underwritten and negotiated
- Optional for both cedent and reinsurer
- Used for unusual or large risks
- More expensive than treaty reinsurance

**Process**:
1. Cedent offers specific risk to reinsurer
2. Reinsurer reviews and makes offer
3. Negotiate terms if needed
4. Reinsurer accepts or declines
5. Premium and coverage agreed

**Applications**:
- Large property risks
- Complex liability coverages
- Specialty/niche products
- Single large accounts

### Treaty Reinsurance
**Definition**: Automatic reinsurance agreement covering many risks over time

**Characteristics**:
- Automatic coverage (cedent and reinsurer bound)
- Covers multiple risks/policies
- Determined by formula/agreement
- Cheaper than facultative

**Treaty Types**:

#### 1. Quota Share Treaty
- Reinsurer takes fixed percentage of every risk
- Cedent retains remaining percentage
- Premiums and losses shared proportionally
- Used for risk reduction and growth

**Example**:
- 30% Quota Share Treaty
- For every $100 premium, reinsurer gets $30
- For $1M loss, reinsurer pays $300K

#### 2. Surplus Treaty
- Cedent retains fixed amount (surplus)
- Reinsurer takes excess above surplus
- Loss and premium shared proportionally
- Used for large loss protection

**Example**:
- $500K Surplus on $10M limit
- Cedent keeps first $500K of any loss
- Reinsurer covers $500K-$10M

#### 3. Excess of Loss Treaty
- Cedent retains losses up to deductible
- Reinsurer pays losses above deductible
- Non-proportional (not premium-based)
- Used for catastrophic loss protection

**Example**:
- $1M excess of $5M
- Cedent retains all losses up to $5M
- Reinsurer pays from $5M to $6M
- Reinsurer pays maximum $1M

#### 4. Stop Loss Treaty
- Protects insurer's combined ratio
- Reinsurer pays if loss ratio exceeds threshold
- Used for profitability protection
- Covers entire portfolio

**Example**:
- 90% Stop Loss
- If loss ratio exceeds 90%, reinsurer pays difference
- Loss ratio 95% triggers payout of 5% of premium

### Catastrophe (CAT) Reinsurance
**Definition**: Reinsurance for large events exceeding normal losses

**Characteristics**:
- Covers specific natural disasters
- High deductible ($250M+)
- Very expensive
- Annual contract

**Events Covered**:
- Hurricanes
- Earthquakes
- Floods
- Tornadoes
- Man-made catastrophes

## Reinsurance Pricing

### Premium Determination
- **Ceded Premium**: Gross premium transferred to reinsurer
- **Reinsurance Premium Rate**: % of ceded premium for coverage
- **Loss Ratio**: Expected losses / ceded premium
- **Profit Commission**: Bonus if loss ratio is low

**Example Calculation**:
```
Direct Premium Written: $100M
Ceded to Reinsurer: $30M (30% quota share)
Reinsurance Premium Rate: 95%
Reinsurance Premium: $30M × 95% = $28.5M

If Loss Ratio = 75%:
Expected Losses: $30M × 75% = $22.5M
Profit Commission: 10% × ($28.5M - $22.5M) = $600K
```

### Pricing Factors
- **Base Rate**: Fundamental rate for coverage
- **Loss History**: Prior loss experience
- **Trend**: Projected loss trends
- **Exposure**: Size of exposures covered
- **Competition**: Market rates for coverage

## Reinsurance Management

### Risk Selection
- **Risk Appetite**: Amount and type of risk to cede
- **Retention Levels**: Net amount insurer keeps
- **Geographic Concentration**: Control geographic exposure
- **Line of Business**: Preferred lines for reinsurance

### Program Design
- **Layering**: Multiple reinsurance agreements
- **Deductibles**: Retention amounts
- **Limits**: Coverage amounts
- **Attachment Points**: Where coverage starts

**Example Reinsurance Program**:
```
Layer 1: Quota Share 20% (covers all losses proportionally)
Layer 2: $5M Excess of Loss over $10M retention
Layer 3: $50M Excess of Loss over $15M retention
Layer 4: Cat Coverage for specific events
```

### Contract Administration
- **Premium Accounting**: Track ceded premiums
- **Loss Reporting**: Report claims to reinsurers
- **Payment Processing**: Pay reinsurance premium
- **Reconciliation**: Annual true-ups and audits
- **Renewal Management**: Renew treaties annually

## Financial Accounting for Reinsurance

### Accounting Treatment
- **Ceded Premium**: Reduce earned premium
- **Ceded Losses**: Reduce incurred losses
- **Expense Recovery**: Commission reduction
- **Profit Commission**: Contingent income

### Balance Sheet Impact
- **Premium Receivable**: Amounts due from reinsurers
- **Loss Recoveries Receivable**: Reinsurer liability for losses
- **Reinsurance Asset**: Risk transfer benefit
- **Reinsurance Liability**: Ceding commissions owed

### Income Statement Impact
- **Earned Premium**: Reduced by ceded premium
- **Incurred Losses**: Reduced by ceded losses
- **Expense Ratio**: Improved by ceding commission
- **Combined Ratio**: Improved by reinsurance benefit

## Reinsurance Underwriting

### Reinsurer Perspective
- **Risk Assessment**: Evaluate cedent's business
- **Loss Analysis**: Review cedent's prior losses
- **Underwriting Quality**: Assess cedent's underwriting
- **Financial Stability**: Evaluate cedent's finances
- **Market Conditions**: Consider market trends

### Underwriting Decisions
- **Premium Adequacy**: Ensure premium covers risk
- **Terms and Conditions**: Define coverage scope
- **Retentions**: Set appropriate retention levels
- **Exclusions**: Define what is not covered
- **Conditions**: Specify requirements

## Global Reinsurance Markets

### Reinsurance Centers
- **Bermuda**: Leading reinsurance hub (Bermuda Re)
- **London**: Lloyd's of London market
- **Switzerland**: Major reinsurers (Swiss Re, Zurich)
- **Germany**: Munich Re, Allianz
- **Singapore**: Asian reinsurance hub
- **New York**: U.S. reinsurance market

### Market Players
- **Traditional Reinsurers**: Established global reinsurers
- **Lloyd's Syndicates**: Underwriting syndicates
- **Captive Reinsurers**: Company-owned reinsurers
- **Mutual Insurers**: Mutual insurance reinsurance
- **Alternative Capital**: Hedge funds, ILS funds

## Alternative Risk Transfer (ART)

### Alternative Risk Products
- **Cat Bonds**: Securitized catastrophe risk
- **Insurance Linked Securities (ILS)**: Risk transfer via securities
- **Contingency Bonds**: Triggered by specific events
- **Weather Derivatives**: Financial instruments hedging weather

### Side-Car Reinsurance
- **Third-Party Capital**: Use external investors' capital
- **Proportional Sharing**: Investors share proportional risk
- **Specialized Lines**: Focus on specific risks
- **Time Limited**: Specific term, typically 1-3 years

## Reinsurance Regulatory Aspects

### Regulatory Requirements
- **Admitted Status**: Reinsurer must be licensed/admitted
- **Financial Strength**: Reinsurers must be financially strong
- **Collateral**: May require collateral for non-admitted reinsurers
- **Disclosure**: Must disclose reinsurance in filings
- **Credit Rating**: Reinsurers must have strong ratings

### Reinsurance Credit
- **Recoverables**: Amounts owed by reinsurers
- **Reinsurance Asset**: Benefit from reinsurance
- **Credit Risk**: Risk reinsurer won't pay
- **Concentration**: Limits on exposure to single reinsurer

## Analytics and Optimization

### Reinsurance Optimization
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Is reinsurance economical?
- **Scenario Analysis**: Outcomes under different scenarios
- **Capital Efficiency**: Reinsurance impact on capital
- **Profitability**: Impact on underwriting profit

### Metrics
- **Ceded Loss Ratio**: Losses ceded / ceded premium
- **Reinsurance Recovery Rate**: Amount of losses recovered
- **Premium Retention**: % of premium insurer keeps
- **Net Loss Ratio**: Loss ratio after reinsurance
