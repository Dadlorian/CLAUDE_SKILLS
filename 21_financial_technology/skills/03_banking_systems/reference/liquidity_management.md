# Liquidity Management in Banking

## Liquidity Management Overview

### What is Liquidity?
Liquidity refers to:
- **Ability to meet cash obligations** when due
- **Converting assets to cash** without significant loss
- **Access to funding** on reasonable terms
- **Maintaining adequate cash reserves** for operations

### Importance in Banking
```
Liquidity Risk: Bank cannot meet obligations
├── Consequences:
│   ├── Customer deposits withdrawn
│   ├── Funding costs increase
│   ├── Credit lines revoked
│   ├── Regulatory intervention
│   └── Potential collapse
│
├── Impact Examples:
│   ├── 2008 Financial Crisis: Lehman Brothers liquidity failure
│   ├── Bear Stearns: Deposit run and forced acquisition
│   └── SVB: Rapid deposit outflow due to rate shock
```

## Liquidity Risk Types

### Funding Liquidity Risk
```
Problem: Cannot raise funds to meet obligations

Causes:
├── Deposit withdrawals (customer loss of confidence)
├── Maturing wholesale funding not renewed
├── Loss of credit lines
├── Market disruptions (credit crunch)
├── Counterparty defaults
└── Operational disruptions

Mitigation:
├── Diversified funding sources
├── Maintain funding buffers
├── Build investor relationships
├── Build deposit base
└── Contingency funding plans
```

### Market Liquidity Risk
```
Problem: Cannot liquidate assets without significant loss

Causes:
├── Illiquid assets (hard to sell)
├── Forced selling in thin market
├── Asset value deterioration
├── Fire sales at discounts
└── Market disruption

Mitigation:
├── Maintain liquid asset buffer
├── Match asset/liability durations
├── Hold government securities
├── Avoid highly illiquid assets
└── Regular liquidity stress tests
```

## Regulatory Liquidity Requirements

### Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)
```
Formula:
LCR = High-Quality Liquid Assets / Net Cash Outflows ≥ 100%

Purpose:
├── Ensure 30-day survival in stressed scenario
├── Measure short-term liquidity adequacy
├── Applied to all large banks
└── Regulatory minimum: 100% (+ buffer)

Components:

High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA):
├── Level 1 (no limit, 100% value):
│   ├── Cash
│   ├── Central bank reserves
│   ├── Government securities (sovereigns, subnational)
│   └── Central bank eligible collateral
│
└── Level 2 (max 40% of total, haircuts applied):
    ├── Corporate bonds (AA- rated or higher)
    ├── Covered bonds (AA- rated or higher)
    ├── Equities (index constituents)
    └── Other securities (varying haircuts)

Net Cash Outflows (30-day stress):
├── Retail deposits: Assume 5-10% withdrawal
├── Wholesale funding: Assume 25-100% non-renewal
├── Derivative payables: Full amount
├── Contingent funding needs
└── Other contractual outflows
```

### Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)
```
Formula:
NSFR = Available Stable Funding / Required Stable Funding ≥ 100%

Purpose:
├── Ensure adequate medium/long-term funding
├── Measure 1-year funding adequacy
├── Incentivize stable funding structure
├── Prevent excessive short-term reliance
└── Regulatory minimum: 100%

Available Stable Funding (ASF):
├── Core deposits (100% or 90% of value)
├── Retail deposits (90% of value)
├── Wholesale deposits (50-0% depending on tenor)
├── Capital and equity (100%)
├── Equity liabilities (100%)
└── Other funding (various percentages)

Required Stable Funding (RSF):
├── Cash assets (0% requirement)
├── Securities (5-50% requirement)
├── Loans (50-100% requirement)
├── Other assets (100% requirement)
└── Off-balance sheet exposures (5-100%)
```

## Liquidity Monitoring

### Daily Liquidity Monitoring
```
Process:
1. Collect cash flow forecasts (next 30 days)
2. Identify major funding needs/maturities
3. Monitor deposit flows and trends
4. Track wholesale funding availability
5. Assess market conditions
6. Stress test liquidity position
7. Report to treasury/risk committee
8. Escalate if thresholds breached

Metrics Monitored:
├── LCR ratio (>100%)
├── NSFR ratio (>100%)
├── Intraday liquidity needs
├── Funding concentration
├── Deposit stability
├── Counterparty funding availability
├── Maturity ladder gaps
└── Contingency funding capacity
```

### Maturity Ladder Analysis
```
Maturity Ladder - Assets vs Liabilities (in millions):

Period        Assets    Liabilities   Cumulative Gap
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
0-7 days      $100      $500          -$400
7-14 days     $200      $300          -$500
14-30 days    $300      $250          -$450
1-3 months    $400      $400          -$450
3-6 months    $500      $300          -$250
6-12 months   $600      $250          -$100

Interpretation:
├── Negative gap = Need funding for period
├── Positive gap = Excess assets available
├── Consider: Rollover, renewal, prepayment
└── Manage with: Securitization, borrowing, deposits
```

## Liquidity Buffers and Reserves

### High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) Buffer
```
Composition:
├── Cash and central bank deposits: 20%
├── Government securities: 50%
├── Covered bonds: 15%
├── Corporate bonds: 10%
├── Equities: 5%
└── Other eligible securities: 0%

Buffer Strategy:
├── Hold 2-3 weeks of net outflows
├── Maintain >130% LCR (buffer above minimum)
├── Diversify by asset type
├── Monitor concentration
└── Regular rebalancing
```

### Reserve Requirements
```
Federal Reserve Requirements (US):
├── 0% on first $16.3M of net transaction accounts
├── 3% on next $112.1M
├── 10% on amounts over $128.4M
└── Requirements vary by institution size

Purpose:
├── Ensure adequate reserves
├── Promote monetary policy transmission
├── Safety and soundness requirement
└── Counter-cyclical adjustment possible
```

## Contingency Funding Plans

### Stress Scenario Planning
```
Scenarios Considered:
├── Idiosyncratic stress (bank-specific)
│   ├── Credit downgrade
│   ├── Fraud discovery
│   ├── Leadership change
│   └── Major operational loss
│
├── Market-wide stress
│   ├── Credit crisis
│   ├── Interest rate shock
│   ├── Currency crisis
│   └── Systemic event
│
└── Combined stress
    ├── Both idiosyncratic and market stress
    └── Severe simultaneous impacts
```

### Contingency Funding Actions
```
Escalation Levels:

Level 1 (Minor Stress):
├── Increase treasury monitoring frequency
├── Alert senior management
├── Review and update forecasts
└── Pre-position backup funding

Level 2 (Moderate Stress):
├── Activate backup funding sources
├── Increase deposit rates to attract deposits
├── Liquidate less critical securities
├── Borrow from Federal Reserve discount window
└── Increase securities lending

Level 3 (Severe Stress):
├── Activate all contingency sources
├── Aggressive deposit gathering
├── Accelerate asset sales
├── Draw emergency credit lines
├── Negotiate asset sales
└── Seek government/central bank assistance
```

## Liquidity Sources

### Primary Liquidity Sources
```
Core Customer Deposits:
├── Transaction accounts (checking)
├── Savings accounts
├── Regular depositors with stable relationships
└── Government deposits (stable)

Retail Deposits:
├── Certificates of Deposit (CDs)
├── Money market accounts
├── Individual saving vehicles
└── Generally stable if competitively priced

Customer Loans (Rollovers):
├── Loan prepayment proceeds
├── Loan maturity proceeds
├── Interest collections
└── Fee collections
```

### Secondary Liquidity Sources
```
Capital Markets Funding:
├── Unsecured debt (bonds, notes)
├── Securitization of loan portfolios
├── Repo market transactions
└── Asset sales

Interbank Funding:
├── Federal funds purchases
├── Loan participations
├── Interbank money market
└── LIBOR/SOFR-based borrowing

Central Bank Liquidity:
├── Discount window (primary credit)
├── Open market operations
├── Seasonal credit facilities
└── Emergency liquidity facilities
```

### Tertiary Liquidity Sources
```
Asset Liquidation:
├── Investment securities (may incur losses)
├── Loan sales (with discounts)
├── Asset-backed securities
└── Fire sales under stress

External Support:
├── Customer deposit increases
├── Credit line borrowing
├── Government support programs
├── Central bank lending facilities
└── Merger/acquisition by stronger institution
```

## Best Practices

### Liquidity Management
```
Recommendations:
├── Maintain >130% LCR ratio
├── Achieve >100% NSFR on all assets
├── Diversify funding sources
├── Match asset/liability durations
├── Hold adequate HQLA buffers
├── Regular stress testing (monthly)

```

### Risk Mitigation
```
Controls:
├── Deposit insurance (up to limits)

├── Financial stability monitoring
├── Liquidity facility access
├── Contingency planning
├── Regular testing and drills

```

## Conclusion
Liquidity management is critical for bank survival and safety. Banks must maintain adequate liquid assets, diversified funding sources, and robust contingency planning to meet obligations under all conditions. Regulatory requirements (LCR, NSFR) and stress testing ensure banks are prepared for adverse scenarios while maintaining sufficient lending capacity to support the real economy.
