# Account Types in Banking

## Deposit Accounts

### Current Account (Checking Account)
**Purpose**: Daily business and personal transactions
- **Features**:
  - Unlimited deposits and withdrawals
  - No interest earned
  - Check book provided
  - Debit card included
  - Online banking access
  - Multiple authorized users possible
- **Typical Holders**: Businesses, frequent traders, professionals
- **Minimum Balance**: Varies by bank ($0-$1,500)
- **Fees**: Monthly maintenance ($10-20), per-check fees, overdraft fees
- **Overdraft**: Often available as separate facility

### Savings Account
**Purpose**: Long-term savings with interest earnings
- **Features**:
  - Interest earned on balance
  - Monthly deposits and withdrawals
  - Limited monthly withdrawal limit (often 3-6 free per month)
  - Passbook or online tracking
  - Lower risk than investment accounts
  - Tax-advantaged options available
- **Typical Holders**: Individual savers, families
- **Minimum Balance**: $100-$2,500
- **Interest Rate**: 0.01-2.5% annually (varies by market)
- **Compounding**: Daily, monthly, or quarterly
- **Fees**: Minimum balance fees, excess withdrawal fees

### Money Market Deposit Account
**Purpose**: Higher interest with limited transaction access
- **Features**:
  - Higher interest than savings accounts
  - 3-6 withdrawal limit per month
  - Check writing capability (limited)
  - Tiered interest rates (higher for higher balances)
  - FDIC insured
- **Typical Holders**: Conservative investors, savers
- **Minimum Balance**: $2,500-$10,000
- **Interest Rate**: Typically 0.5-3.5% annually
- **Access**: Limited monthly transactions

### Certificates of Deposit (CDs)
**Purpose**: Fixed-term investments with fixed returns
- **Features**:
  - Fixed interest rate for term
  - Predetermined maturity date
  - No withdrawals until maturity
  - Higher rates than savings accounts
  - Terms: 3 months to 5+ years
  - Early withdrawal penalties
- **Typical Holders**: Conservative investors, retirees
- **Minimum Deposit**: $500-$100,000
- **Interest Rates**: Fixed, ranging 0.5-5% depending on term
- **Maturity**: Automatic renewal or withdrawal options
- **Penalties**: Early withdrawal typically 3-12 months interest

## Loan Accounts

### Installment Loans
**Purpose**: Fixed-amount borrowing with monthly repayments
- **Features**:
  - Lump-sum disbursement
  - Fixed monthly payments
  - Fixed interest rate (typically)
  - Predetermined loan term
  - Secured or unsecured options
- **Typical Uses**: Auto loans, personal loans, home improvement
- **Loan Terms**: 12 months to 20+ years
- **Interest Rates**: 4-25% depending on creditworthiness
- **Prepayment**: Usually allowed without penalty
- **Guarantor**: May be required for unsecured loans

### Credit Facility Accounts
**Purpose**: Flexible borrowing with revolving credit
- **Features**:
  - Approved credit limit
  - Borrow as needed up to limit
  - Interest charged only on drawn amount
  - Revolving nature (available again as repaid)
  - Monthly payments (interest + principal)
- **Typical Uses**: Business operations, personal flexibility
- **Credit Limits**: $1,000-$500,000+
- **Interest Rates**: Variable, 6-18% typically
- **Repayment**: Minimum payments required monthly
- **Draw Methods**: Checks, transfers, ATM withdrawals

### Mortgage Accounts
**Purpose**: Long-term home financing
- **Features**:
  - Large loan amounts ($50,000-$1,000,000+)
  - Long terms (15-30 years)
  - Secured by property collateral
  - Lower interest rates than unsecured loans
  - Monthly principal + interest payments
  - Prepayment options
- **Types**: Fixed-rate, adjustable-rate, hybrid
- **Interest Rates**: 3-8% depending on market and credit
- **Amortization**: Equal monthly payments over term
- **Collateral**: First lien on property
- **Insurance**: Property, title, and mortgage insurance required

### Auto Loans
**Purpose**: Vehicle financing
- **Features**:
  - Medium loan amounts ($10,000-$60,000)
  - Medium-term (3-7 years typically)
  - Secured by vehicle
  - Monthly fixed payments
  - Gap insurance often required
- **Interest Rates**: 3-10% depending on credit
- **Loan Term**: 24-84 months typically
- **Down Payment**: 10-20% typical
- **Collateral**: Title held by lender until paid off
- **Mileage/Use Restrictions**: May apply for leases

## Investment Accounts

### Brokerage Accounts
**Purpose**: Securities trading and investing
- **Features**:
  - Equity, bond, and mutual fund trading
  - Margin capabilities (borrow to invest)
  - Real-time market access
  - Dividend and interest collection
  - Tax reporting (1099 forms)
- **Account Types**: Cash account, margin account
- **Holdings**: Stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs
- **Trading**: Real-time or delayed quotes
- **Regulation**: SEC-regulated, FDIC not applicable

### Retirement Accounts
**Purpose**: Long-term retirement savings with tax benefits

#### Individual Retirement Account (IRA)
- **Features**:
  - Tax-advantaged retirement savings
  - $6,500-$7,500 annual contribution limits
  - Investment in stocks, bonds, mutual funds
  - Tax-deferred growth
  - Withdrawal restrictions (age 59.5+)
- **Types**: Traditional IRA, Roth IRA
- **Tax Treatment**: Pre-tax or after-tax contributions
- **Required Distributions**: Age 72+

#### 401(k) Plans
- **Features**:
  - Employer-sponsored retirement plan
  - $23,500 annual contribution limit
  - Employer matching common
  - Investment options selected by plan
  - Loans possible from account
- **Vesting**: Employer match typically vests over time
- **Early Withdrawal**: Penalties before age 59.5
- **Rollover**: Can roll to IRA or new employer plan

### Trust Accounts
**Purpose**: Assets held in trust for beneficiaries
- **Features**:
  - Trustee manages account on behalf of beneficiaries
  - Legal structure documented in trust document
  - Separate FDIC insurance per beneficiary
  - Estate planning tool
  - Tax-advantaged for certain trusts
- **Types**: Revocable living trusts, testamentary trusts, charitable trusts
- **Beneficiaries**: Named individuals or entities
- **Trustee**: Manages and controls assets
- **Reporting**: May require trust tax returns

## Business Accounts

### Business Checking Accounts
**Purpose**: Daily business operations and payments
- **Features**:
  - Similar to personal checking
  - Business debit card
  - Invoice payment capabilities
  - Payroll processing integration
  - Merchant services access
- **Requirements**: Business registration, EIN
- **Users**: Employees with authorized access
- **Minimum Balance**: $1,000-$5,000
- **Fees**: Higher than personal ($15-30 monthly)

### Business Savings Accounts
**Purpose**: Business savings and emergency funds
- **Features**:
  - Interest-bearing for business savings
  - Limited monthly transactions
  - Lower minimum balance than money market
  - Emergency reserve building
- **Interest Rates**: 0.01-2% depending on balance
- **Accessibility**: Limited withdrawal frequency

### Business Sweep Accounts
**Purpose**: Automated liquidity management
- **Features**:
  - Automatic sweep between accounts
  - Optimize returns and minimize fees
  - Reduce excess idle balances
  - Consolidate liquidity
- **Mechanics**: Excess funds automatically sweep to higher-yield account
- **Minimum**: Funds fall below threshold, sweep reverses
- **Purpose**: Maximize return on business cash

### Cash Management Accounts
**Purpose**: Comprehensive business cash management
- **Features**:
  - Multiple sub-accounts for organization
  - Automated balance management
  - Liquidity reporting and optimization
  - Integration with payment systems
  - Consolidated view of liquidity
- **Components**: Checking, sweep, reserve accounts
- **Reporting**: Real-time liquidity dashboard
- **Use Cases**: Large corporations, financial management

## Specialized Accounts

### Escrow Accounts
**Purpose**: Hold funds for third-party benefit
- **Features**:
  - Neutral third party holds funds
  - Released per agreement conditions
  - Common in real estate transactions
  - Protects both buyer and seller
  - Interest may accumulate
- **Use Cases**: Real estate closings, M&A transactions
- **Duration**: Until conditions met
- **Beneficiaries**: Multiple parties with conditional rights

### College Savings Plans (529 Plans)
**Purpose**: Save for education expenses
- **Features**:
  - Tax-advantaged education savings
  - $235,000 aggregate limit per beneficiary
  - Investment options (age-based or self-directed)
  - Unused funds can transfer to relatives
  - Tax-free withdrawals for qualified expenses
- **Types**: Prepaid plans, savings plans
- **Accounts**: Named beneficiary
- **Distributions**: Tax-free for education, penalized otherwise

### Health Savings Accounts (HSAs)
**Purpose**: Save for qualified medical expenses
- **Features**:
  - Triple tax advantage (deductible, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals)
  - $4,150 individual/$8,300 family limits
  - Eligible for high-deductible health plans
  - Investment options available
  - Unused funds carry over indefinitely
- **Eligibility**: Requires eligible health insurance
- **Withdrawals**: Tax-free only for qualified medical expenses
- **Portability**: Account stays with individual

## Account Features and Add-Ons

### Multi-Signature/Joint Ownership
- **Joint Tenants with Rights of Survivorship (JTWROS)**:
  - Each owner equal access
  - Surviving owner gets full account upon death
  - FDIC insured separately per owner

- **Tenants in Common (TIC)**:
  - Each owner has separate share
  - Passes to heirs upon death (not to other owner)
  - FDIC insured separately per owner

### Power of Attorney
- Third party authorized to conduct transactions
- Limited or full authority options
- Documents authority granted
- Useful for elderly or incapacitated individuals

### Authorized Users
- Third party with account access
- Limited transaction authority typically
- Does not own account
- Account holder remains liable

### Account Holds and Liens
- Bank holds funds pending verification
- Judgment lien attached to account
- Creditor claims against account
- Holds released when conditions met

## Account Consolidation

### Merging Accounts
- Customer requests account consolidation
- Funds transferred to primary account
- Secondary account closed
- Single statement, one set of fees
- Same interest rate applied

### Account Migration
- Moving to different account type
- Transfer of funds and settings
- Closure of old account
- Credit reporting updates
- Minimum balance adjustments

## Account Closure

### Voluntary Closure
- Customer requests account closure
- Final balance calculated and disbursed
- Check issued or electronic transfer
- Account marked closed
- Record retained for compliance

### Involuntary Closure
- Unclaimed property laws
- Dormancy due to inactivity
- Regulatory action
- Negative balance non-payment
- Final notice to customer

## Regulatory Protections

### FDIC Insurance
- Up to $250,000 per depositor per insured bank
- Coverage per account ownership category
- Shared accounts: $250,000 per co-owner
- Retirement accounts: Separate $250,000 coverage
- Trust accounts: $250,000 per beneficiary (up to 5)

### NCUA Insurance (Credit Unions)
- Similar coverage as FDIC
- Insures deposits at federally chartered credit unions
- Share insurance for member deposits
- Member-owned structure

## Conclusion
Banking institutions offer diverse account types to serve different customer needs—from basic checking to complex investment and trust accounts. Understanding account features, protections, and proper structure is essential for both customers and banking professionals.
