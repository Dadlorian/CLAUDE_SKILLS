# Investment Policy Statement (IPS) Guide

## Purpose and Components

An Investment Policy Statement (IPS) is a written document that outlines investment strategy, objectives, constraints, and rebalancing rules. It serves as both a governance document and a client communication tool.

## IPS Structure

### Executive Summary
- Client profile overview
- Key investment objectives
- Risk profile
- Target allocation
- Review schedule

### Client Profile Section

**Financial Situation**:
```
- Current Assets: $500,000
- Annual Income: $100,000
- Annual Expenses: $60,000
- Expected Savings Rate: 5% annually
- Time to Major Event: 15 years to retirement
```

**Investment Goals**:
```
1. Retirement
   - Amount Needed: $1,500,000
   - Timeline: 15 years
   - Priority: Essential
   - Probability of Success: 92%

2. Home Purchase
   - Amount Needed: $100,000
   - Timeline: 3 years
   - Priority: Important
   - Probability of Success: 85%
```

**Constraints**:
```
- Liquidity: $10,000 annually
- Tax Bracket: 35% marginal rate
- Investment Knowledge: Intermediate
- Account Types: Taxable and IRA
- Geographic Restrictions: US only
```

### Investment Objectives

**Return Objectives**:
```
1. Primary: Achieve 7% average annual return
   - After inflation
   - After fees and taxes
   - Over 15-year horizon

2. Secondary: Minimize tax drag
   - Through tax-loss harvesting
   - Tax-efficient fund selection
   - Strategic account placement

3. Tertiary: Provide income
   - $5,000 annually for expenses
   - From dividends and interest
   - If needed during distribution phase
```

**Risk Objectives**:
```
1. Primary: Maintain volatility within 10-12%
   - Standard deviation target
   - Annual fluctuation range

2. Secondary: Limit drawdown to -20%
   - Maximum acceptable decline
   - Based on risk tolerance

3. Tertiary: Control downside capture
   - During market declines
   - Protect downside vs benchmark
```

### Asset Allocation

**Strategic Asset Allocation (SAA)**:
```
Target Allocation:
- US Large-Cap Stocks: 40%
- International Stocks: 15%
- Bonds: 40%
- Alternatives: 5%

Rationale:
- Diversification across asset classes
- Balance growth (equities) and stability (bonds)
- International exposure for growth
- Alternatives for hedge and diversification
```

**Acceptable Ranges (Bands)**:
```
Asset Class       Target    Lower Band    Upper Band
US Equities       55%       50%           60%
International     15%       12%           18%
Fixed Income      25%       20%           30%
Cash              5%        2%            8%

Rebalancing Trigger: When any asset exceeds bands
```

### Security Selection

**Approved Securities/Funds**:
```
US Equity Exposure:
- VOO (Vanguard S&P 500): 40%
- VTV (Vanguard Value): 10%
- VUG (Vanguard Growth): 5%

International Equity:
- VEA (Vanguard EAFE): 15%

Fixed Income:
- BND (Vanguard Total Bond): 20%
- VTIP (Vanguard TIPS): 5%

Alternatives:
- VNQ (Vanguard Real Estate): 5%
```

**Selection Criteria**:
```
- Expense ratio: <0.20% for equity, <0.15% for bonds
- Assets under management: >$500 million
- Trading volume: >1 million shares daily
- Tax efficiency: Minimal turnover and gains
- Tracking error: <0.10% for index funds
```

**Exclusions** (if applicable):
```
- Tobacco companies
- Fossil fuel producers
- Human rights violators
- Controversial weapons manufacturers
```

### Rebalancing Policy

**Rebalancing Method**:
```
Type: Threshold-based with calendar review
Trigger: When any asset exceeds acceptable band
Frequency: Quarterly or more often if triggered
Timing: Avoid high-volatility periods
Tax Consideration: Harvest losses opportunistically
```

**Rebalancing Process**:
```
1. Calculate current allocation
2. Compare to target and bands
3. If triggered:
   - Identify overweight positions
   - Identify underweight positions
   - Execute trades to rebalance
   - Document changes
   - Notify client
4. If not triggered:
   - No action needed
   - Monitor for next trigger
5. Annual review (minimum)
```

### Benchmark Selection

**Primary Benchmark**: 60% S&P 500 / 40% Bloomberg Aggregate Bond Index
```
Rationale: Reflects our target 60% equity, 40% fixed income allocation
Appropriate for balanced growth strategy
Widely available and transparent
Standard benchmark for comparable strategies
```

**Performance Monitoring**:
```
Frequency: Monthly, Quarterly, Annual
Metrics: Total return, risk-adjusted returns
Attribution: Analyze allocation and selection effects
Discussion: Explain over/underperformance vs benchmark
Adjustment: Modify strategy if benchmark no longer appropriate
```

## IPS as Client Communication

### Plain Language Explanations

**What We Do**:
```
"We manage your portfolio using an automated algorithm that:

1. Invests in a diversified mix of stocks and bonds
2. Automatically rebalances quarterly to maintain your allocation
3. Uses tax-loss harvesting to reduce your tax burden
4. Monitors your progress toward financial goals
5. Adjusts your allocation as you get closer to your goals
"
```

**Why This Strategy**:
```
"This allocation was chosen because:

1. It aligns with your 15-year time horizon
2. It provides sufficient growth to meet retirement needs
3. It limits volatility to levels you indicated comfort with
4. It provides diversification across markets and sectors
5. It balances growth potential with preservation
"
```

**What to Expect**:
```
"With this allocation, you should expect:

- Average annual return: 6-7%
- Annual volatility: 10-12%
- Worst single year: approximately -20 to -30%
- Best single year: approximately +20 to +30%
- Recovery time from downturn: 1-2 years typically

These are estimates based on historical data.
Actual results will vary.
"
```

## IPS Updates and Modifications

### Annual Review Checklist

```
1. Client Situation Changes
   - Income changes: Increased or decreased?
   - Assets: Inheritance, gifts, or major purchases?
   - Goals: Changed or new goals?
   - Time horizons: Any milestones approaching?
   - Risk tolerance: Still comfortable with volatility?

2. Account Performance Review
   - Returns vs benchmark: Over/underperforming?
   - Risk metrics: Volatility as expected?
   - Asset allocation: Significantly drifted?
   - Tax impact: Effective tax rate?

3. Market Changes
   - Market valuation: Still reasonable?
   - Interest rate environment: Changed significantly?
   - Economic conditions: Implications for strategy?

4. IPS Appropriateness
   - Still suitable for current situation?
   - Objectives still relevant?
   - Allocation still optimal?
   - Modifications needed?
```

### Modification Process

**When to Modify IPS**:
```
Trigger Events:
- Major life changes (marriage, retirement, inheritance)
- Significant income change (>20%)
- Risk tolerance change
- Goal achievement or change
- Time horizon shift
- Allocation inappropriate (>10 year performance test)
```

**Modification Documentation**:
```
Date: [Date]
Client: [Name]
Modification: [Describe change]

Previous IPS Element: [What was it]
New IPS Element: [What is it now]
Rationale: [Why the change]

Client Acknowledgment: [Signature/date]
Advisor Acknowledgment: [Signature/date]
```

## Regulatory Compliance

### SEC Requirements

**Fiduciary Standards**:
```
An IPS demonstrates:
1. Understanding of client circumstances
2. Suitability of recommendations
3. Ongoing monitoring and review
4. Documentation of decisions
5. Prudent process followed
```

**Documentation Requirements**:
```
Must retain:
1. Original IPS signed by client
2. Annual updates and modifications
3. Performance monitoring records
4. Rebalancing trade records
5. Client communication logs
6. Suitability assessments
```

### ERISA Requirements (if applicable)

For accounts subject to ERISA (401k, pension plans):
```
1. Written investment policy required
2. Named fiduciary responsible
3. Regular monitoring and reporting
4. Diverse investment options
5. Annual statement to participants
6. Cost disclosure
```

## IPS Communication Strategy

### Presenting IPS to Clients

**Before Signing**:
```
1. Explain each section clearly
2. Use plain language and examples
3. Show sample portfolio performance
4. Discuss expected volatility
5. Answer all questions
6. Confirm understanding
7. Get written acknowledgment
```

**Key Talking Points**:
```
"This IPS document is YOUR strategic roadmap.

It says:
- Here's what we're trying to achieve (goals)
- Here's how we'll invest (allocation)
- Here's what to expect (returns and risk)
- Here's how often we'll review (schedule)
- Here's what could change it (triggers)

It gives you confidence that we have a plan
and we stick to it through good times and bad."
```

## Common IPS Mistakes

1. **Too Vague**: Unclear objectives and constraints
2. **Too Detailed**: Micromanaging specific holdings
3. **Unrealistic Returns**: Overly optimistic expectations
4. **Ignored**: Written but not followed
5. **Not Updated**: Stale assumptions and objectives
6. **Poor Communication**: Clients don't understand it
7. **Over-Qualified**: Excessive complexity

## Conclusion

A well-written IPS serves as both a governance tool and a client education document, providing clarity on investment strategy, appropriate expectations, and decision-making framework. Regular reviews and updates ensure continued relevance and suitability.
