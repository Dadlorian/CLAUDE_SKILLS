# Risk Assessment and Profiling Guide

## Risk Assessment Framework

Comprehensive risk assessment identifies an investor's ability and willingness to take on investment risk, resulting in an appropriate portfolio allocation.

## Risk Capacity Assessment

### Financial Capacity Components

**1. Time Horizon**
```
Score Scale: 1-10

Years to Event:
0-2 years: Score 1-2 (Very limited)
3-5 years: Score 3-5 (Moderate)
6-10 years: Score 6-8 (Extended)
10+ years: Score 8-10 (Very extended)

Application:
- Shorter horizons: Conservative allocation
- Longer horizons: Aggressive allocation
```

**2. Income Stability**
```
Score Scale: 1-10

Employment Type:
- Self-employed, variable income: 1-4
- Regular employment, stable: 5-7
- Tenured position, high security: 8-10
- Pension or annuity income: 8-10
- Multiple stable income sources: 9-10

Application:
- Stable income: Can take more risk
- Variable income: Need conservative approach
```

**3. Liquidity Needs**
```
Score Scale: 1-10

Annual Withdrawals Percentage:
- >10% per year: Score 1-2 (Must be liquid)
- 5-10% per year: Score 3-4
- 1-5% per year: Score 5-7
- <1% per year: Score 8-10 (Can weather volatility)

Application:
- High liquidity needs: Conservative
- Low liquidity needs: Aggressive
```

**4. Financial Obligations**
```
Score Scale: 1-10

Debt and Obligations:
- High leverage (>50% of assets): 1-3
- Moderate leverage (25-50%): 4-6
- Low leverage (<25%): 7-8
- No debt: 9-10

Application:
- High obligations: Conservative
- Low obligations: Aggressive
```

**5. Emergency Fund**
```
Score Scale: 1-10

Emergency Reserves:
- <3 months expenses: 1-3
- 3-6 months expenses: 4-7
- 6-12 months expenses: 8-9
- 12+ months expenses: 10

Application:
- Adequate emergency fund: Allows risk-taking
- Inadequate: Must be conservative
```

### Capacity Score Calculation

```
Total Capacity Score = (Time Horizon + Income Stability +
                       Liquidity Needs + Financial Obligations +
                       Emergency Fund) / 5

Score Interpretation:
1-3: Very Limited Risk Capacity
3-5: Limited Risk Capacity
5-7: Moderate Risk Capacity
7-9: Strong Risk Capacity
9-10: Very Strong Risk Capacity
```

## Risk Tolerance Assessment

### Psychological Components

**1. Loss Aversion Questions**

```
Question 1: Worst-Case Scenario
"Your portfolio drops 30% in a bear market. How would you feel?
a) Panic, sell immediately (1 point)
b) Concerned, might sell if worse (2-3 points)
c) Uncomfortable but hold (4-6 points)
d) Not concerned, maintain strategy (7-9 points)
e) Want to buy more (10 points)"

Question 2: Historical Loss Experience
"Have you experienced major portfolio losses?
a) Yes, sold in panic (1-2 points)
b) Yes, held through (4-6 points)
c) Yes, added during downturn (7-9 points)
d) No experience (5 points - neutral)"

Question 3: Volatility Comfort
"Annual portfolio swings of ±20% would be:
a) Unacceptable (1-2 points)
b) Concerning (3-4 points)
c) Tolerable (5-6 points)
d) Expected (7-9 points)
e) Desirable (10 points)"
```

**2. Investment Knowledge**

```
Question 4: Investment Knowledge
"Rate your investment knowledge:
a) Minimal/None (1-2 points)
b) Basic (3-5 points)
c) Intermediate (6-8 points)
d) Advanced (9-10 points)"

Application:
- Less knowledge: More conservative
- More knowledge: More aggressive possible
- Knowledge validates risk understanding
```

**3. Financial Goals Priority**

```
Question 5: Return vs Safety Priority
"Which is more important?
a) Preserve capital (1-2 points)
b) Steady income (3-4 points)
c) Balanced growth (5-6 points)
d) Capital appreciation (7-8 points)
e) Maximum growth regardless of risk (9-10 points)"

Application:
- Safety priority: Conservative
- Growth priority: Aggressive
```

### Psychological Tolerance Scoring

```
Tolerance Score = Average of psychology questions

Interpretation:
1-3: Very Risk Averse (Fear of losses dominates)
3-5: Conservative (Prefer safety over growth)
5-7: Moderate (Balanced between safety and growth)
7-9: Growth-Oriented (Accept risk for return)
9-10: Aggressive (Growth maximization)
```

## Risk Perception Assessment

### Behavioral Factors

**1. Recency Bias**
```
Question: "Current market environment is:
a) Extremely risky (bear market) (1-2 points)
b) Risky (down market) (3-4 points)
c) Normal (5-6 points)
d) Favorable (up market) (7-8 points)
e) Extremely favorable (bull market) (9-10 points)"

Correction:
- Overweight recent performance
- Adjust: Reduce score if investor too bearish,
  increase if too bullish
```

**2. Overconfidence**
```
Question: "Your ability to time markets or pick stocks:
a) Not possible (1 point)
b) Difficult for professionals (3 points)
c) Somewhat possible with research (5-6 points)
d) Likely possible with effort (7-8 points)
e) Confident I can beat market (9-10 points)"

Correction:
- Overconfidence suggests risk bias
- High scores may warrant conservative adjustment
```

**3. Herding Behavior**
```
Question: "During market rallies, do you:
a) Want to get fully invested (1-2 points)
b) Stay current on market (3-5 points)
c) Ignore hype, maintain plan (6-9 points)
d) Go against crowd (10 points)"

Interpretation:
- Herd tendency: Suggests behavioral risk
- Contrarian tendency: More disciplined
```

## Comprehensive Risk Profile

### Profile Integration

```
Overall Risk Profile = (Capacity Score × 0.6) + (Tolerance Score × 0.4)

Weighting Rationale:
- Capacity (60%): Objective, determines constraints

1-3: Conservative (Priority: Capital Preservation)
3-5: Moderate-Conservative
```

### Scenario-Based Testing

**Test 1: Market Downturn Scenario**
```
"Your $100,000 portfolio drops to $70,000 (30% loss)
in a bear market lasting 18 months. Your reaction would be:

a) Sell everything and move to cash
b) Sell enough to reduce losses
c) Hold and monitor situation
d) Maintain investments unchanged
e) Add to positions at lower prices"

Score 2x for stress-tested tolerance
```

**Test 2: Recovery Scenario**
```
"After the downturn, your portfolio recovers to $95,000
over 2 years. You feel:

a) Relieved, never again (1 point)
b) Glad but cautious (3 points)
c) Satisfied, plan was sound (6 points)
d) Disappointed didn't buy more (9 points)
e) Confident in strategy (10 points)"

Validates strategy discipline
```

## Risk Profile Updates

### Trigger Events for Reassessment

**Major Life Changes**:
- Job change or loss
- Inheritance or significant gift
- Marriage or divorce
- Birth of children
- Health issues
- Approaching retirement
- Major purchase (home, business)

**Financial Changes**:
- Income increase/decrease >20%
- Significant debt accumulation
- Emergency fund depletion
- Windfalls or losses

**Market Changes**:
- Major market downturn (>20%)
- Extended bull market (>30%)
- Significant volatility increase
- Major interest rate shift

### Reassessment Process

```python
def check_reassessment_trigger(client):
    """
    Determine if reassessment needed
    """
    triggers = []

    # Check life events
    if client.major_life_change_since_last_review():
        triggers.append('LIFE_EVENT')

    # Check financial situation
    if client.income_changed_significantly():
        triggers.append('INCOME_CHANGE')

    if client.debt_increased_significantly():
        triggers.append('DEBT_CHANGE')

    # Check market conditions
    market_return = get_ytd_market_return()
    if abs(market_return) > 0.20:
        triggers.append('MARKET_EXTREME')

    # Annual reassessment
    if time_since_last_review > 12_months:
        triggers.append('ANNUAL_REVIEW')

    if triggers:
        schedule_risk_reassessment(client, triggers)

    return triggers
```

## Risk Communication

### Client Education

**Key Messages**:
1. Risk and return are linked (higher risk = higher potential return)
2. Volatility is normal in growth portfolios
3. Time horizon affects risk tolerance
4. Diversification reduces risk
5. Discipline matters more than predictions
6. Historical perspective on downturns

### Expectation Setting

**Pre-Investment Communication**:
```
"Based on your moderate risk profile, we've recommended
a 60/40 portfolio (60% stocks, 40% bonds).

What to expect:
- Average annual return: 6-7%
- Annual volatility: 10-12%
- Worst 1-year period: -20% to -30% (historically)
- Best 1-year period: +20% to +30%
- Typical drawdown: -10% to -15%
- Recovery time: 1-2 years typically

This level of volatility is expected and normal.
Short-term fluctuations should not prompt changes
to your long-term strategy."
```

## Common Risk Assessment Mistakes

1. **Single Score Focus**: Oversimplifying complex risk picture
2. **Ignoring Changes**: Not reassessing when circumstances change
3. **Investor Overconfidence**: Suggesting higher risk than capacity allows
4. **Neglecting Emotions**: Not accounting for behavioral factors
5. **Inadequate Stress Testing**: Not validating through scenarios
6. **Poor Documentation**: Insufficient compliance records
7. **No Communication**: Failing to educate about expectations
8. **Static Profiles**: Treating risk as unchanging

## Risk Assessment Best Practices

1. **Comprehensive**: Address all dimensions of risk
2. **Documented**: Complete records for compliance
3. **Updated**: Regular reassessment for changes
4. **Validated**: Stress tested through scenarios
5. **Communicated**: Clear explanation to client
6. **Flexible**: Adjusted for life changes
7. **Data-Driven**: Based on historical evidence
8. **Appropriate**: Matched to actual portfolio

## Conclusion

Thorough risk assessment combining financial capacity and psychological tolerance creates appropriate, sustainable portfolio allocations. Regular updates and clear communication ensure suitability throughout the investment relationship.
