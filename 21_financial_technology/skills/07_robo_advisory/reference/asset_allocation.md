# Asset Allocation Strategy & Framework

## Definition and Importance

Asset allocation is the process of dividing an investment portfolio among different asset classes (equities, bonds, commodities, cash, alternatives) to balance risk and return according to investor goals and constraints. Studies show asset allocation accounts for approximately 90% of portfolio return variation over time.

## Major Asset Classes

### Equities
- **Large-Cap Stocks**: Large US companies, stable cash flows, lower volatility
- **Mid-Cap Stocks**: Medium-sized companies, growth potential, moderate risk
- **Small-Cap Stocks**: Smaller companies, higher growth potential, higher volatility
- **International Developed**: Stocks from developed markets (Europe, Japan, Australia)
- **Emerging Markets**: Stocks from developing economies (higher growth, higher risk)

### Fixed Income
- **Government Bonds**: Low-risk securities backed by government credit
- **Corporate Bonds**: Bonds issued by corporations (investment-grade and high-yield)
- **Municipal Bonds**: Tax-exempt bonds for state and local projects
- **TIPS**: Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities, inflation-adjusted returns
- **International Bonds**: Bonds denominated in foreign currencies

### Alternatives
- **Real Estate (REITs)**: Real estate investment trusts, inflation hedge
- **Commodities**: Oil, metals, agricultural products, portfolio diversifier
- **Hedge Funds**: Absolute return strategies, low correlation to markets
- **Private Equity**: Private company investments, illiquidity premium

### Cash & Money Market
- **Cash Equivalents**: Treasury bills, money market funds, high liquidity
- **Emergency Reserves**: 3-6 months of expenses for clients
- **Tactical Cash**: For rebalancing and opportunity capture

## Allocation Models

### Age-Based Allocation
- **100 - Age Rule**: 100 minus age in equities percentage
  - Example: 35-year-old gets 65% equities, 35% bonds
  - Becomes more conservative as client ages

- **110 - Age Rule**: More aggressive, suitable for longer time horizons
  - Example: 35-year-old gets 75% equities, 25% bonds

### Target-Date Allocation
- **Glide Path**: Automatic shift from aggressive to conservative over time
- **Time-Based Transition**: Predetermined reallocation schedule
- **Multi-Stage Allocation**: Different stages (accumulation, transition, distribution)
- **Market-Condition Responsive**: Adjusts based on market conditions and cycles

### Risk-Based Allocation
- **Conservative**: 20-30% equities, 70-80% fixed income/cash
- **Moderate**: 50-60% equities, 40-50% fixed income/alternatives
- **Aggressive**: 80-90% equities, 10-20% bonds/alternatives
- **Very Aggressive**: 95%+ equities, minimal defensive positions

### Goal-Based Allocation
- **Bucketing Strategy**: Separate portfolios for different time horizons
  - Short-term bucket (1-3 years): Cash and short-term bonds
  - Medium-term bucket (3-10 years): Balanced allocation
  - Long-term bucket (10+ years): Equity-focused

- **Liability-Matching**: Assets allocated to match specific liabilities or goals
  - Duration matching for bond allocations
  - Return requirements driving equity allocation

## Diversification Within Asset Classes

### Equity Diversification
- **Geographic**: US, developed international, emerging markets
- **Market Cap**: Large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap blends
- **Sector**: Technology, healthcare, financials, industrials, etc.
- **Style**: Value, growth, blend strategies
- **Factors**: Quality, dividend, momentum, low-volatility

### Fixed Income Diversification
- **Duration**: Short-term, intermediate, long-term bonds
- **Credit Quality**: Government, investment-grade, high-yield
- **Type**: Treasuries, corporates, municipals, inflation-protected
- **Geographic**: US and international bonds
- **Maturity Ladder**: Bonds maturing at different intervals

## Strategic vs. Tactical Allocation

### Strategic Allocation (SAA)
- **Definition**: Long-term target allocations based on investor goals and risk tolerance
- **Rebalancing**: Back to strategic targets at regular intervals
- **Buy-and-Hold**: Generally maintain allocations despite market movements
- **Time Horizon**: 3-5 years or longer

### Tactical Allocation (TAA)
- **Definition**: Short-term deviations from SAA based on market conditions
- **Market Timing**: Overweight undervalued assets, underweight overvalued
- **Conditions-Based**: Respond to economic cycles, valuations, technical signals
- **Time Horizon**: Days to months, typically under 1 year
- **Complexity**: Requires active monitoring and rebalancing discipline

## Implementation Considerations

### Cost Minimization
- Use low-cost index funds and ETFs
- Minimize expense ratios (target 0.1-0.5% for core holdings)
- Reduce trading costs and tax drag
- Implement tax-loss harvesting systematically

### Liquidity Management
- Ensure portfolios can meet redemptions and distributions
- Maintain adequate cash positions
- Use liquid investments for majority of portfolio
- Keep alternatives to 10-20% due to liquidity constraints

### Constraint Handling
- Tax considerations (holding periods, wash sales)
- Minimum investment requirements
- Account type restrictions (IRA, 401k, taxable)
- Concentration limits and regulatory requirements
- Illiquidity premia for alternative investments

## Performance Monitoring

- **Return Attribution**: Compare returns to expectations
- **Risk Monitoring**: Track volatility and drawdowns
- **Drift Analysis**: Monitor deviation from target allocation
- **Benchmark Comparison**: Assess outperformance/underperformance
- **Rebalancing Frequency**: Quarterly or when drift exceeds thresholds

## Common Allocation Mistakes

1. Over-concentration in single asset class or security
2. Insufficient diversification across geographies and sectors
3. Chasing performance after good years
4. Selling after poor performance (pro-cyclical behavior)
5. Ignoring taxes in allocation decisions
6. Failing to rebalance systematically
7. Using inappropriate benchmarks for comparison
