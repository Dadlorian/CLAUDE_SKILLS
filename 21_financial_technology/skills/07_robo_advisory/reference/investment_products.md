# Investment Products Overview

## Mutual Funds

### Definition and Structure
- Pooled investment vehicles managed by professional managers
- Investors own shares in the fund
- Diversified holdings across securities
- Daily pricing and trading

### Types of Mutual Funds

**By Strategy**:
- **Index Funds**: Track market indices, passive management, low costs
- **Active Funds**: Manager selects securities, higher costs, variable performance
- **Sector Funds**: Focused on specific sectors (tech, healthcare, etc.)
- **Growth/Value Funds**: Different investment styles

**By Asset Class**:
- **Equity Funds**: Stocks of different market caps and regions
- **Bond Funds**: Fixed income securities of various durations
- **Mixed/Balanced Funds**: Combination of stocks and bonds
- **Money Market Funds**: Short-term, liquid, low-risk

### Costs
- **Expense Ratios**: 0.05% - 2.0%+ annually
- **Sales Loads**: Front-load (5-6%), back-load, level load
- **12b-1 Fees**: Distribution and marketing costs
- **Trading Costs**: Bid-ask spread, market impact

### Advantages
- Professional management
- Instant diversification
- Lower minimum investments
- Daily liquidity
- Transparent pricing

### Disadvantages
- Higher costs vs ETFs
- Tax inefficiency (active trading)
- Investor dilution (late buyers fund outflows)
- Performance inconsistency
- No direct control over holdings

## Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs)

### Definition and Characteristics
- Funds that trade like stocks on exchanges
- Can trade intraday (vs mutual fund daily pricing)
- Generally more tax-efficient than mutual funds
- Lower expense ratios typically

### ETF Types

**By Investment Approach**:
- **Index ETFs**: Track market indices passively
- **Active ETFs**: Manager-selected holdings (newer, growing)
- **Smart Beta**: Rules-based selection (not pure passive or active)

**By Asset Class**:
- **Equity ETFs**: US, international, emerging markets
- **Fixed Income ETFs**: Treasury, corporate, municipal bonds
- **Commodity ETFs**: Oil, gold, agricultural products
- **Specialty ETFs**: Leveraged, inverse, sector-specific

### Cost Structure
- **Expense Ratios**: 0.03% - 1.5%
- **Trading Costs**: Bid-ask spreads (typically 0.01-0.10%)
- **No Sales Loads**: No upfront commissions
- **Lower Minimum**: Can trade 1 share

### Tax Efficiency
- **Creation/Redemption**: In-kind process avoids capital gains
- **Portfolio Turnover**: Generally lower than active funds
- **Tax-Loss Harvesting**: Easier with multiple similar ETFs
- **Trading**: Intraday trading can trigger short-term gains

### Advantages
- Low cost (expense ratios)
- Tax efficiency
- Intraday trading flexibility
- Transparency (holdings disclosed daily)
- No minimum investment

### Disadvantages
- Trading costs (bid-ask spread)
- Less professional management
- Tracking error vs index
- Complexity (many similar options)
- Possible premium/discount to NAV

## Individual Securities

### Stocks
- Direct ownership of company shares
- Variable dividend yields
- Full capital gains/losses
- Concentration risk
- Active trading possible
- Research intensive

**Advantages**:
- Direct control
- No intermediary fees
- Dividend reinvestment
- Voting rights
- Tax flexibility (holding period choice)

**Disadvantages**:
- Concentration risk
- Research intensive
- Trading costs per position
- Tax complexity
- Behavioral risks

### Bonds
- Fixed income obligations
- Predictable cash flows
- Interest rate risk
- Credit risk
- Maturity date

**Types**:
- **Government Bonds**: Treasuries, backed by US government
- **Corporate Bonds**: Issued by corporations (investment-grade or high-yield)
- **Municipal Bonds**: Tax-exempt interest for state/local projects
- **Treasury Securities**: Bills (0-1 year), Notes (1-10 years), Bonds (20-30 years)

**Advantages**:
- Predictable income
- Lower volatility
- Defined maturity
- Portfolio diversification
- Enhanced control

**Disadvantages**:
- Interest rate risk
- Credit risk
- Inflation risk
- Lower returns vs equities
- Liquidity (some bonds illiquid)

## Alternative Investment Products

### Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

**Definition**: Companies that own, operate, or finance real estate

**Types**:
- **Equity REITs**: Own real estate directly
- **Mortgage REITs**: Finance real estate
- **Hybrid REITs**: Combine equity and mortgage

**Characteristics**:
- Required to distribute 90% of income as dividends
- High current yield (3-5%)
- Inflation hedge potential
- Low correlation to stocks
- Tax-inefficient (ordinary income taxation)

**Advantages**:
- Real estate exposure
- Strong income
- Inflation protection
- Diversification
- Liquidity (publicly traded)

**Disadvantages**:
- Tax inefficiency
- Interest rate sensitive
- Dividend volatility
- Limited appreciation potential
- Inflation-driven decreases possible

### Commodities

**Types**:
- **Metals**: Gold, silver, platinum (inflation hedges)
- **Energy**: Oil, natural gas (economic sensitivity)
- **Agriculture**: Wheat, corn, soybeans (weather dependent)
- **Cryptocurrency**: Bitcoin, Ethereum (emerging asset class)

**Access Methods**:
- Commodity futures contracts
- Commodity ETFs
- Commodity mutual funds
- Commodity-linked notes
- Physical bullion (limited)

**Characteristics**:
- Return-less insurance (no income)
- Storage and insurance costs
- Low correlation to stocks
- High volatility
- Limited long-term returns

### Private Equity

**Definition**: Investments in non-publicly traded companies

**Characteristics**:
- Illiquidity (7-10 year lock-up common)
- Higher return potential
- Higher fees (2% + 20% carry)
- Significant minimum ($250k typical)
- Due diligence intensive

**Strategies**:
- **Leveraged Buyout (LBO)**: Acquire companies with debt
- **Growth Capital**: Fund expansion of profitable companies
- **Venture Capital**: Fund startup and early-stage companies
- **Distressed**: Invest in troubled companies at discount

### Hedge Funds

**Definition**: Alternative investment pools with active management

**Characteristics**:
- Limited transparency
- Sophisticated strategies
- High minimum investments ($1M+)
- Significant fees (1-2% + 20% carry)
- Limited liquidity

**Strategies**:
- **Long/Short**: Long undervalued, short overvalued
- **Market Neutral**: Balanced long/short, market-independent returns
- **Global Macro**: Bets on currency, commodity, interest rate moves
- **Event-Driven**: Merger arbitrage, distressed investing

## Direct Indexing

### Concept
Purchasing individual stocks to directly replicate an index while maintaining individual tax controls.

**Implementation**:
- Buy stocks in index proportions
- Maintain flexibility in specific holdings
- Tax-loss harvest individual positions
- Reduce tracking error

**Advantages**:
- Tax-loss harvesting optimization
- Direct control over holdings
- Avoiding concentrated positions
- Transparency
- No fund fees

**Disadvantages**:
- Higher trading costs
- Higher minimum capital
- Research and monitoring intensive
- Portfolio drift management
- Regulatory complexity

## Fractional Shares

### Characteristics
- Ownership of partial shares
- Lower minimum investments
- Enables dollar-amount investing
- Common in robo-advisors
- Dividend reinvestment

**Benefits**:
- Accessibility (lower starting amounts)
- Precision in allocations
- Elimination of cash drag
- Full diversification possible
- Lower minimum portfolio sizes

**Implementation**:
- Custodian tracks fractional ownership
- Dividend distributions split proportionally
- Rebalancing uses fractions for precision
- Tax reporting on proportional basis

## Product Selection for Robo-Advisors

### Criteria
1. **Cost**: Minimization of expense ratios and trading costs
2. **Tax Efficiency**: Lower turnover, better tax treatment
3. **Diversification**: Adequate coverage with few securities
4. **Liquidity**: Daily trading ability, minimal spreads
5. **Transparency**: Clear holdings and performance data
6. **Availability**: Availability across custodians
7. **Minimum Investment**: Low or fractional share support

### Typical Allocations

**Conservative Portfolio**:
- 30-40% US Equity ETF
- 25-35% International Equity ETF
- 25-35% Bond ETF
- 5-10% Real Estate (REIT ETF)
- 0-5% Cash

**Moderate Portfolio**:
- 50-60% US Equity ETF
- 20-25% International Equity ETF
- 20-25% Bond ETF
- 5% Real Estate
- 0-2% Cash

**Aggressive Portfolio**:
- 70-80% US Equity ETF
- 20-25% International Equity ETF
- 5-10% Bond ETF
- 5% Real Estate
- 0-2% Cash
